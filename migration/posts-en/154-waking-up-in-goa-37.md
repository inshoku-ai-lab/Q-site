## February in Goa

The stretch of days without rave parties ended with January, and once February came, the parties started all at once.

Travelers who lived for parties came back as the parties did, and the oddball density of Goa kept climbing.

The big change from the mood around New Year's was that the tourist types who had come only for the end of the year were gone.

The light, touristy air had lifted, and something more serious and more concentrated hung over the place.

Word was that the party freaks who come to Goa again and again avoid the crowded New Year's stretch and arrive in February.

February and March are the real Goa season, they said, and if you want to experience the real Goa, this is the time you have to be here.

Something even better than those overwhelming New Year's parties — I had no idea what was waiting, and my expectations swelled.

## The February Parties

Once February came and the parties began, they came in a run, one every three to five days.

They were less than half the size of New Year's, but the intensity of the people at them was unreal.

Nearly every one of the several hundred people dancing was a hardened veteran who had been traveling for years.

The crowd looked a little older than at New Year's, too.

The aura, the atmosphere, the sheer density of those parties were overwhelming, and the sense of another dimension had gone up another notch.

Everyone there had been coming to Goa for years, and they were all friends or at least familiar faces to one another; that mutual recognition showed in the air itself, giving the place a stronger sense of unity and a family feeling among the crowd.

It was like what I had felt at the New Year's parties, only sharpened and made denser.

Plainly put, it was insanely fun.

The year-end parties had moved me more than anything I had known, and now that ceiling kept getting broken, party after party.

I was floored by how good trance parties were, by how deep Goa went.

## Parties Back to Back

These absurdly good parties came every few days, and day after day I kept chasing them.

At one point there were three in a row, one every two days.

I'd take LSD, dance all night and on past noon, then eat and fall asleep.

Two nights to recover, and on the night I was recovered, another party.

Then again — LSD, dancing all night, two nights off, another party.

I was burning far more calories than I took in, and moving far more than I rested.

I was starting to feel the limits of my body, and for the next party I was thinking I would either sleep through the night and go in the morning, or go without drugs entirely.

## Nowhere to Run

The party that day, of all places, was right in front of the house I was living in.

Step out the front door, walk ten meters (thirty feet), and you'd run into someone dancing.

With it going on that close, sleeping was out of the question.

And I had not recovered enough to dance all night without drugs either.

As a last, bitter resort, I took LSD and danced.

For better or worse, a famous European DJ had come to this party, and he kept us dancing with the coolest, newest tracks.

I wanted to rest and couldn't, so I just went deeper into the dance.

There was no rest at home either, so in the end I danced until the party wound down past noon.

By that point every bit of strength was spent.

I was Joe at the end of Ashita no Joe, the boxing manga — everything given, burned down to pure white ash.
