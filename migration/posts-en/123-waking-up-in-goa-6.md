# Fun Days

We spent every day laughing, and it was genuinely fun.

Nothing in particular, really — going into town to shop and cook something good, playing cards, heading over to another friend's house.

We lived right along the beach, but we hardly ever went into the water.

Everything else was fun enough that the ocean never interested us.

# New Customs

Spending time with T and N, the two women who had come over from London, was exciting.

They were grown women with a lot of life behind them, and maybe because of the three years or so they had spent in London, they came across as sophisticated.

The thing that hit me hardest was their culture of sharing.

They had lived in a share house — one house, separate rented rooms, everything else in common — so they were used to sharing whatever they had.

Order food at a restaurant and every dish got passed around, and when one of them was thirsty and took a drink of water, she would hold the bottle out before she had even capped it — want some?

Maybe not the most hygienic thing in the world, but for sharing pleasure and tasting the same good thing together, it was perfect.

I had never run into a culture of sharing like that, and once I understood the pleasure in it, I started copying them, little by little.

The other new thing was greeting people with a hug.

The first time you saw someone that day, and when you parted, there was always a hug.

That kind of closeness was new to me too, and it took me aback, but there is real warmth in a society where every last person greets you chest to chest, and I settled into it fast, and happily.

Around that time we were always gathering at R's house to hang out.

Y, who was always with us, the two from London, and me — the five of us.

Travelers kept turning up there and moving on, one after another.

## Party Days

December came, and the parties started coming more often.

There was the weekly party at the bar, and on a beach a little way off the music ran until one in the morning every night.

And every so often a restaurant would be made over into a party space, at random.

Meeting the strange old guy at the last party had given me a glimpse of how much fun there is in cutting loose, and I wanted to cut loose too.

Figuring I would start by looking the part, I bought a pair of loud pants.

They were close to the fractal design the strange old guy had been wearing — loud warm colors, red and orange and yellow, tangled together in chaos.

The version of me that had cut loose, reborn out of the movie geek I used to be, wanted exactly that much mayhem.

Cutting loose had me riding high, but I had also taken eight times the normal dose of LSD at the last party and come through a brutal bad trip, and I was afraid to take it again.

Y's parting shot was still lodged in my chest, too: "You will never dance the way you used to again."

A line like that, thrown out to confuse me, was infuriating, and at the same time there was the fear that it might actually be true.

But alongside the anger and the worry and the fear, having seen the depths of that darkness had made me more curious instead, and I wanted to look through LSD into the deep part of consciousness.

The solution I came up with was a plan to take a very small amount of ecstasy first, ahead of time.

A tiny dose up front would hold the negative emotions down, and then even with the full force of the LSD on my mind, maybe there would be no bad trip — that was the thinking.

I got ready for the next party and waited for the time to come.
