## The Flight

One reason I hadn't flown from Goa to Bangkok, and had gone to the trouble of taking the train all the way to Calcutta instead, was that I wanted to travel overland as much as I could.

The other was that a Calcutta–Bangkok ticket was overwhelmingly cheaper than flying out of any other Indian city.

There were no cheap fares back then the way there are now, and prices swung wildly depending on which route you flew.

For a broke backpacker trying to save whatever he could, the usual thing was to come overland to Calcutta, fly to Bangkok, and go home to Japan from there.

I flew out of the airport in Calcutta and into the airport in Bangkok.

Six months of ferociously intense days in India came back to me, vivid behind my eyes.

What an outrageous country.

I was dead in the middle of the process of being remade by culture shock.

In the space of a few months, everything I had taken for normal had crumbled out from under me, and a new sense of self had begun to form.

## Bangkok

Bangkok, two years since I had last seen it, was as busy as ever.

I stayed at a guesthouse that travelers I'd met in India had all spoken well of.

Anyone who had been on the road a while kept moving on word of mouth, which funneled everybody into a handful of towns and a handful of guesthouses.

This one, on Khao San Road in the travelers' quarter, had become a place where old hands stopped to rest.

I fell in quickly with a group of Japanese staying there, and we went out to the food stalls to eat.

The display case at the stall held several kinds of Thai curry, and I ordered the chicken curry, the one that looked the most appetizing.

India had been mostly vegetarian food, so the idea was to build up some strength before going home to Japan.

Thai stall curry was more than hot enough even for someone arriving straight from India, but I managed to put the whole thing away and enjoy it.

## Stomach Pain

But the chicken curry, it turned out, had been a mistake.

Beyond a mistake — a huge mistake, or you could just call it a catastrophe.

The next morning I shot awake with a violent pain in my gut.

A few things from Calcutta crossed my mind, but this raw burning was the chicken curry from the day before, no question.

Thinking back on it now, I have the feeling that the display case at the stall had been sitting in direct sunlight.

The savage pain went on for a good half hour, and only after everything in me was out did it finally settle.

I had planned to head for a southern island that day, but I gave it up.

There was no way I could handle a bus ride.

I spent the day resting up at the guesthouse, meaning to shake off the fatigue of the trip.

The stomach pain went away, but the diarrhea never let up.

The next day I had it again, and the day after that too.

Three straight days of diarrhea, and I started to wonder: should I go to a hospital?

How do you even go to a hospital in Bangkok?

Somewhere in the middle of all that, I came around to thinking this was a message — hurry up, end the journey, go home to Japan.

That same day I went to a travel agency and booked a ticket to Kansai Airport for two days later.
