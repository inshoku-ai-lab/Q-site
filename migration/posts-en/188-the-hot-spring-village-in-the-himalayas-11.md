## C

C was one of the friends from Goa who came up to Vashisht.

Every last one of the friends I ran around with in Goa had a personality as strong and vivid as a manga character's, but even among them C's shone in a class of its own.

His attention was always turned toward his own inner world, the world of the spirit, the world of the unknown, and going deeper into it was the only thing that interested him.

That passion drew him to occult magazines like the monthly *Mu*, and inside them a handful of words in particular lit him up: the mystical, yokai, dragon gods, onmyōji — the sorcerer-diviners of the old imperial court.

## C's Style

His interests showed on the outside.

He kept to one palette: black, with a little indigo worked in.

On top of that came a ninja arrangement — black jika-tabi, the split-toed workman's boots, black kung fu pants, and a samurai-style hachimaki headband tied around his head.

The jacket varied from day to day, but it almost never strayed from the base black.

He loved the world of stealth and concealment and dressed the way a ninja dresses to vanish into shadow, but that head-to-toe black, which might have passed at midnight, stood out against the everyday India around him about as far as a thing can stand out, and it drew every eye, always.

C moved through the world in a style locked down to the last detail by that singular personality of his, and his bearing alone was usually enough to earn him a certain respect from everyone.

## C's Character

Travelers from every country would take an interest and come over to talk to him, but for all that conspicuous getup C had the covert temperament the clothes were originally designed for, and he did not much care for conversation.

He was extremely quiet, and almost never took part when several people were talking at once.

Even in a group, put something to him and an answer came back, and it was not that he disliked being around people.

One on one it went the other way: let the talk turn to the things he cared about — other dimensions, the nue and the kirin out of the old tales, crystals — and he would turn talkative on the spot.

Talkative or not, the way he spoke never got sloppy or rude; it always carried a samurai's restraint, and whatever the moment, whoever the person, he answered in keigo.

Among travelers, a lot of people dislike social conventions like rank and seniority, and the normal thing is to talk to one another in plain casual speech no matter how wide the age gap.

I saw all human beings as brothers, and I made it a principle to use plain speech with anyone, however much older, however far above me.

Any small-minded adult who could not accept my plain speech was, the way I saw it, someone I had no need to deal with.

A cheeky young punk, but that was my creed.

C was the exact opposite — gentle keigo for everyone, always, with nothing pushed on anyone — and coupled with that outstanding individuality of his, it gave off a beauty that was all of a piece.
