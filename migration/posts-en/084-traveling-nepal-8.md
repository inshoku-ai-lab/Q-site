# Ainori

I don't know it well, but there is a television show called Ainori, which apparently follows the romances that grow up inside a group traveling together.

Our own journey had something close to that kind of romantic drama in it.

One young woman and three young men, eating and sleeping in the same rooms and crossing borders together — naturally, that sort of thing was bound to come up.

I-san was a single woman of twenty-four, from Kyushu in the south of Japan.

A graduate of a journalism college, she was traveling from China to Nepal.

Her plans beyond that were not fixed.

G was a single man of twenty-two, from Chubu in central Japan.

He had taken a leave of absence from university and was traveling from China to Nepal.

Depending on how things went, he was thinking of continuing on into India.

S was a man of twenty-four, from the Chugoku region in western Japan.

He had a girlfriend back in Japan.

He had graduated from university and was traveling from China to Nepal.

He was heading home soon.

And then me — twenty, single, on my way to India alone.

# Friendship Between Travelers

I did not know it at the time, but what I came to understand by staying on the road is that between a man and a woman traveling, friendship comes more easily than it does in ordinary settled life.

Part of it is that as foreign travelers in an unfamiliar country, with no idea how anything works, you have to help each other; part of it, probably, is that your shared unease pulls you toward each other.

![Four children sitting side by side on a rock, seen from behind, looking across water toward cliffs](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog84-1.jpg)

And naturally enough, travelers are a breed you never meet in your own country, which makes them feel like your own kind.

Friendship between a man and a woman is generally a hard thing to sustain, but travelers are the exception, and friendships between men and women that run for years are not unusual.

# The Madonna

As the only woman in our group of four, I-san had the role of the madonna, the one red blossom among the green, as the phrase goes.

In the traveler's world women are vastly outnumbered, and the demand for a female presence tends to run high.

I-san had the appeal of both a journalist's independence and the softness people associate with women from Kyushu.

On top of that, her full body stirred the young men's sexual desire; that much is not hard to imagine.

S had a girlfriend in Japan and was about to go home, so I think what he felt for I-san was nothing more than warm friendship, but G was another matter.

He had traveled with her all the way from Tibet to Nepal, and with every day they spent together he had been quietly building up feelings for her.

I had no designs of my own, but having met her in Tibet and spent time with her in Pokhara, I had grown feelings for her too, without meaning to.

That I got on so naturally with I-san may not have sat well with G.

Looking back now, G does seem to have been a little cold toward me, and it also looks as though he was deliberately trying to put distance between I-san and me.

At the time I had no feel for that kind of subtlety between people; I was just enjoying my days.

# The Confession

It had been nearly two months since I left Japan, and I think the loneliness, and the unsteadiness left in me by the trouble with the small-time hood near the guesthouse, both had something to do with it.

For better or worse, I think I had stopped holding myself back about a lot of things.

In the roller-coaster swing of life on the road the feeling grew on its own, and I decided to ride the momentum and tell her.

![Two old envelopes with blue postage stamps and handwriting lying on a wooden desk beside a pen](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog84-2.jpg)

Telling a woman I was in love with her was something I had never done, and never thought myself capable of.

There was no goal behind it — not going out with her, not becoming a couple.

I had not noticed G's feelings for her either; I simply wanted to say what I felt.

Knowing nothing about the fine points of love, I wrote my simple young feelings into a letter and handed it over.

But, unfortunately, a feeling that one-sided and that simple did not get across, and all I got was a gentle no.

# Acceptance

It probably was not serious love, or a feeling with any real weight to it.

It had not felt like fooling around either, but I gave it up surprisingly easily.

I settled it in my own mind: what I felt was not serious love but friendship and sexual desire mixed together.

I don't think my heart was ready to love anyone seriously.
