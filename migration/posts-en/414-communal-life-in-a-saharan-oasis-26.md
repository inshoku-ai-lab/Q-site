## Strong Winds

Anyone who grows up in Japan knows how fierce a typhoon can be.

In Okinawa especially, typhoons are deadly enough that nobody takes them lightly.

Having grown up in a public housing project in Osaka, I haven't been through as many typhoons as people in Okinawa have, but I'd like to think I still know how frightening strong winds can be.

Then a wind beyond belief, one that overturned everything I thought I knew about strong winds, came tearing into the desert where we lived.

Something like the sandstorms you see in the movies.

## Wind Speed

The Japan Meteorological Agency grades typhoons on three thresholds: a "strong typhoon" at 33 meters per second (about 74 mph), a "very strong typhoon" at 44 meters per second (98 mph), and a "violent typhoon" at 54 meters per second (121 mph).

The desert sandstorm that hit us was blowing at 80 meters per second (about 180 mph).

Put it next to a Japanese typhoon and you can see just how brutal it was.

I found a video of an experiment with 80-meter-per-second winds, so take a look if you're interested.

## Wind Warning

During the day, there was nothing out of the ordinary, just another too-hot day in the desert.

Not a cloud in the sky, of course.

The only difference was that Gandalf, who had been listening to the radio, warned us that a ferocious windstorm was coming that day and we should be careful.

But neither I-chan nor I understood what that windstorm would really be like, and we didn't take it seriously.

As usual, we left the hut and went to sleep in our tent.

As usual, there wasn't a breath of wind, and nothing was wrong at all.

There was no point worrying about something that wasn't there, so we fell asleep without giving it another thought.
