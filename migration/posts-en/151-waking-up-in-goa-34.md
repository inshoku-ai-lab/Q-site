## The Mysterious Traveler

Goa gave me no shortage of encounters with oddballs, but B in particular had a certain saintly side to him.

He had apparently lived for a month or so at the ashram in Pune, the same one where I stayed before coming to Goa, doing yoga and meditation.

One time, at a restaurant, while we were killing the wait for our food with a deck of cards, someone started talking about tests for psychic powers.

Since there was a way to sharpen your clairvoyance using cards, we decided to try it.

You take four aces and one joker, lay them face down, and pick out which one is the joker.

You could eliminate the aces one at a time, or go straight for the joker in one shot.

We each took a turn, hitting some and missing some, nothing that rose above chance.

When B's turn came, he shut his eyes and concentrated.

Slowly, saying this one isn't it, he took out an ace.

He was right.

He took out another ace.

Right again.

Eliminating the aces one after another, he found the joker's spot on his first attempt.

Fine, fine — anyone can get it right once. A one in five chance.

On his second attempt B found the joker again without any trouble.

Fine, fine — twice in a row is still believable. A one in twenty-five chance.

But then he got it on the third attempt as well.

At this point we were at one in 125, under one percent.

We looked around at each other, our faces mixing delight, astonishment, and fear.

He went for a fourth attempt, and that one he got too, just as easily.

That made it one in 625, a 0.16 percent chance.

And then, on a fifth attempt, he got that one too, with no difficulty at all.

One chance in 3,125.

Those are not odds that come up in ordinary life.

A 0.03 percent chance.

By this point what stood out was fear rather than curiosity.

Everyone's face had gone stiff with tension.

B did not seem to mind much, and said that he himself had no real idea why he kept getting them right.

But a mood had settled over the rest of us that it might be dangerous to dig any deeper into this.

If he was a genuine psychic, some government agency would come and abduct him — we spun out childish fantasies along those lines.

Afraid that if we kept the test going we would confirm B really was a genuine psychic, we called it off.

Probably everyone had already half sensed that B was the real thing.

## B the Saint

B's calm, love-filled gaze was like a saint's, and then came an incident that made it clear he really was a man full of love.

Travel in India lies so far outside what passes for normal in Japan that plenty of people come apart mentally from the culture shock.

Goa in particular, thanks to its freedom, draws oddballs from all over the world, so the culture shock it delivers is that much stronger.

In that setting, one Japanese woman traveler became mentally ill, and things reached the point where something had to be done.

Everyone tried to help in their own way, nothing quite worked, and it got to the stage where the only thing left was for her to go home to Japan and put herself in the care of a psychiatrist or a counselor.

While we were discussing whether to contact the Japanese embassy or have her parents come and collect her, the one who stepped forward was B.

With his own return date approaching, he volunteered to take responsibility for delivering her all the way to her parents.

That act of love astonished us.

If somebody is in trouble right in front of you, you help — but going out of your way to escort a person you barely know all the way back to Japan is not something most people would think to do.

Normally you would contact the Japanese embassy and leave it at that.

Our sense that B was no ordinary man turned out to be right.
