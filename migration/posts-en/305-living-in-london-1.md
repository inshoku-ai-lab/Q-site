## Heathrow Airport

When I got to London's Heathrow Airport, my best friend N had come all the way out to the exit to meet me.

It was my first time in Europe and I could barely speak English, so that was an enormous help.

My English was so poor that whenever a situation actually called for it, I usually leaned on I-chan, who could at least get by in broken English.

I was hoping that would change during our stay in England.

I was glad to see N again after so long, and we got going on travel stories from the year and change since we had last seen each other.

While I had been in Goa this time around, N had been on Bali, and compared to India, Bali was far more built up for tourists, with not much wildness or local feeling left in it — not all that interesting, as far as N was concerned.

More than once she had gone back and forth about heading for Goa, where all her friends were, but in the end she stayed on in Bali and came to London from there.

Hearing that, I bragged, half joking, about the funniest things that had happened in Goa.

I had my own doubts about whether Goa had been the right call, but I gave N a hard time about it the way people from Osaka do, and enjoyed myself.

There is something like magic in the world of travelers, and its energy never stops shifting.

To keep hold of it takes luck and effort.

I was hoping that this move to London would turn out to be one of the right ones.

## Her Place

N gladly invited us into her own home.

Letting a young couple crash in a room of about eight tatami mats, thirteen square meters (140 square feet), must have been a real hassle, but N's heart was big enough to make it possible.

We could stay until we found a place of our own, she said, and she had already squared it with the landlord.

She was in England on a student visa, she told me, working as a waiter at a Thai restaurant.

The owner was a Japanese man married to a Thai woman, a rock-and-roll type who did not fuss over details, so even without a work visa he might let me pick up dishwashing work there, she said.

She had set up every last thing for us, and I was grateful.

As long as N was there, our stay in England looked like it would go smoothly and be fun.

My chest swelled with expectation for the bright future ahead.
