## Part-Time Work

The mountain hut job was settled, which was great, but there were still about two months before it started.

I-chan was busy with rehearsals for her theater company, while I had nothing in particular to do and just drifted around.

I was out of money either way, so I decided to get a part-time job, partly to kill time.

I'd had a comfortable run delivering for a pizza place before, so I thought it would be nice to do delivery work again. When I looked around, Gusto, the family restaurant chain, was hiring delivery staff at a branch in my neighborhood, so I applied.

This is one thing you have to hand to Osaka.

If you want work, there's no end of openings.

If I had been traveling overseas, or even living out in the Japanese countryside, I might not have found a job this easily.

Even though I'd be quitting after two months, they hired me without any trouble.

## Behind the Scenes at Gusto

The work at Gusto was simple: when a delivery order came in, I delivered it, and when there wasn't one, I grilled hamburger steaks, boiled udon, sorted spoons and forks, and so on.

The work itself was simple, but I had never imagined what went on behind the scenes at a chain restaurant, so in a way it felt like an exciting journey.

The first surprise was that nobody cooked anything.

Every dish was machine-made and frozen, and the whole job was to open the plastic bag, heat it through, put it on a plate, and send it out.

Grilling on the griddle, baking in the oven, or boiling in water were the better cases; some dishes just got zapped in the microwave.

A customer ordered, a part-timer took the contents out of the freezer, zapped them in the microwave, and arranged them on a plate.

A waitress carried it out to the customer.

That was what went on behind the scenes at Gusto, and how a chain restaurant could get food on the table so fast.
