## The Travelers of Goa

A lot of the travelers I met in India were interesting people, but the ones in Goa had personalities a cut above the rest.

Part of it is probably that ever since the sixties Goa has drawn people who stepped outside of society, and something of that rubs off on whoever shows up.

The other part is that meeting anyone takes a certain amount of time, so only the people who stay a while ever meet each other, and you basically never meet the travelers who move on right away.

People who stay a while tend to have room to spare, in their schedules and in their heads, and maybe that gives them more depth than the travelers who keep moving.

Some travelers, like Y and me, came to Goa planning from the start to stay a long time; others, like T and N, never meant to stay and sank there anyway.

By the middle of December the circle of Japanese friends had grown, and I would guess that somewhere between thirty and fifty of us knew each other's faces.

We shared an unusual sense of unity.

## A Sense of Unity

Every time there was a party, everyone gathered and we had our fun together.

We shared something like the feeling of being classmates at school.

Most of us had left on our first long journey, made our way to Goa, taken LSD for the first time, and danced at a Goa trance party for the first time.

There were a few feelings that let us recognize each other as our own kind.

Travelers from every part of the world, gathered in this corner of it, doing something this crazy and this much fun — and the joy of having found my way into it.

And the fear that the door onto the depths of consciousness — LSD — had already opened.

Those two overwhelming feelings were not the sort of thing you could easily hand to another person, so having friends right there living through the same thing was something to be grateful for, and it steadied me.

## The Fear of LSD

Everyone shared the fear of taking LSD, too.

Most of the people who had taken it many times had overdosed by mistake at some point, and most of them had been through the fear of facing their own consciousness.

Someone the fear gets to will not enjoy it, and will not want to take it in the first place.

On that count, the kind of person who comes to travel in India is usually thick-skinned about fear, and India does its share of the thickening.

That thick skin, plus the interest in the spiritual world that is also particular to travelers in India, went well with taking LSD at a Goa trance party.

Everyone felt the fear of LSD, but the curiosity outweighed it, and so everyone got past the fear and had a good time.

Some people really do lose their minds over it, so many of them had real confidence in themselves as people who could get past that fear and enjoy it.

That became a kind of strength of spirit, and it made each of those travelers shine that much brighter.

All of us felt for one another the respect of comrades who had come through the same war: "So you made it through that ordeal too? Nice work."

Most of us had gone down into the kind of mental depths I had hit on my bad trip, and we had the calm that belongs only to people who have found out.

It is the kind of nerve only someone who has faced that fear can come by.

Back then, because a revolution in consciousness had happened to me through LSD, I held a sort of chosen-people idea about myself: I had had a special experience, so I was not the same as other people any more.

Looking back, it is a ridiculous sense of being one of the elect, but for a twenty-year-old who wanted to be an artist and wanted to be special, that is how hard LSD hit.
