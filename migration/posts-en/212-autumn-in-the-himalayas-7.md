## The Bad Trip Spreads

For B too it was his first LSD, but he and the drug seemed to get along, and he was up in front of the speakers dancing flat out and loving every minute of it.

That was when F came to him for help, frantic and white as a sheet.

Picking up that something bad was going on, B got away from the speakers, found somewhere quiet, and listened to what F had to say.

Even so, F was confused, deep in panic, and in no condition to lay any of it out coherently.

B, the one doing the listening, was wrecked himself, in no condition to follow it coherently either.

In the end both of them were lost in it: F's fear infected B, and between the two of them the bad trip only picked up speed.

## The Rescue

So the two of them, confused and looking for help, came to me.

I was younger than either of them, but when it came to the number of times I had dropped LSD at parties in India, and to getting through a situation where someone had come apart on a bad trip, I was far and away the more experienced one.

Listening to them, I thought, ah, sure enough, that baba had pulled something — but there was nothing to be done about it.

With a troublesome guy, the best move is to have nothing to do with him.

This was a situation to get out of immediately, I decided, so we gathered up our scattered things and headed back to the guesthouse.

We got into an auto-rickshaw and set off for Vashisht.

It was on the other side of the valley, so we could be back at the guesthouse in about thirty minutes.

I was grateful the party had been at a hotel in town and not somewhere deep in the mountains.

Day had already broken, we had had plenty of fun, and it was about time to head back anyway.

I could have stayed and danced a few more hours, but with friends in trouble there was no choice.

My thinking was that once we were back in the safety of the guesthouse, the LSD would wear off with time and even a bad trip this extreme would pass.

In the rickshaw, F's confusion and panic kept raging, and the whole way back I just said "You're all right" over and over like an incantation.

"Give it time and the LSD wears off, and nothing dangerous can happen to you at the guesthouse," I kept telling him.

At last the rickshaw reached Vashisht, and we made it back to the guesthouse.

"We've made it this far, so you're all right now," I told them, and rolled a hash joint and offered it to them.

A smoke, I figured, would take the edge off the LSD tension and make things easier.
