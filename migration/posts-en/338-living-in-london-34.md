## The Latest in Brain Science

The book I had bought covered everything brain science knew as of 2000, and went on from there into philosophy, psychiatry, and religious accounts of consciousness.

It was the latest word twenty-one years ago, so it is surely dated by now, and I imagine a good deal of it is simply wrong, but Tachibana's stance toward knowledge, his whole attitude toward it, left a deep mark on me.

I wanted other books as well, but there was no money for them, so I spent my time reading this one over and over.

Of everything in it, the line that stayed with me was this: "of all the things a person does in life, the ones done around the age of twenty have the strongest effect on the life that comes after."

Reading that at twenty-one, I had the feeling of having read the right words at exactly the right time, and it felt as though my life had gained depth.

## Choosing Carefully

The Japan Centre, where any number of Japanese books could be had as long as I put up the money, was enormously attractive to me and at the same time frightening, like a bewitched place.

For a young traveler living hand to mouth on an illegal dishwashing job, a book at three times the Japanese price was not something to reach for lightly.

And yet at the same time, a book in Japanese was something I wanted badly enough to pay three times over for.

Supply and demand, in perfect working order.

What got me through was choosing carefully, and then more carefully still, right along the razor-thin line where supply and demand pushed against each other.

## Snufkin

The next book I got hold of was an English edition of *Finn Family Moomintroll* with a facing Japanese translation.

I had never read the Moomins, but it looked like an easy read, and enjoying a book while studying English at the same time seemed like killing two birds with one stone, so I bought it.

This was my first meeting with Snufkin, the closest thing I have to a teacher and my master of the road.

The English edition was good for studying English, but my grasp of the language was what it was, and nothing in it resonated all the way inside me; my real encounter with the Moomin books came only years later, once I began reading them in Japanese.
