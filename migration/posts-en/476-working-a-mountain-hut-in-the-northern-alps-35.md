## A Night in the Tent

About twenty minutes' walk from the hut, there was a hot spring.

The water was a cloudy, milky pale blue, and it had a strange, mysterious quality.

It isn't a color you see much in nature.

Instead of staying in the ramshackle, atmospheric old hut, I went ahead and camped in my tent beside the spring.

I pitched the tent, ate, and got everything ready for a soak.

Eat after the bath, and your body gets cold.

## The Hot Spring

There was no one around the spring, and no sign of life at the hut either.

A sign said the bath was mixed and bathers should keep their underwear on, but I figured there was no need to worry about anyone else this deep in the mountains, stripped stark naked, and jumped in.

My legs and lower back, worn out from eight hours of climbing, loosened and eased.

My sweat-soaked body came out reborn, with skin as smooth and silky as a beauty's.

The water was maybe 39 degrees Celsius (102°F) — a little on the lukewarm side, but just right for a long soak.

It was dusk when I got in, and as I soaked, the sun went down and the stars began to twinkle.

A hot spring I had reached on foot through the most beautiful scenery imaginable.

Soaking in a hot spring in the dead center of the wilderness, gazing up at a sky full of stars, felt good right down to my core.

Completely, utterly satisfied, I got out of the water, went straight into the tent, and burrowed into my sleeping bag.

On a climb you can only carry so much, so it was a bare-minimum tent, a bare-minimum mat, and a sleeping bag — not what you'd call a comfortable bed.

Still, it was plenty for resting a body that was worn out and flushed from the hot spring.

## The Next Morning

The next morning, again, I woke with the sun and got moving.

This trek came right after I'd worked myself ragged at the hut, and there had been nothing restful about it, but mountain life had toughened me up, and it was no big deal.

The day's destination was a pond down below the hut where I worked.

Its surface was as still as a mirror, and from a certain angle, apparently, you could see the summit reflected in it.

People who had been in the mountains longer than me had told me that the summit, burning in the morning sun and mirrored in the water, was so unbelievably sublime that I absolutely had to go if I ever got the chance.

As a traveler, once I'd heard talk of a place like that, I had no choice but to go.

Paying no mind to how tired I was, I strode briskly on.
