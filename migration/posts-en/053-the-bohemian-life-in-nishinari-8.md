# Material Greed

Around this time I was fiercely in the grip of a material craving: collecting recordings.

It wasn't only collecting records and renting CDs from Tsutaya, the video rental chain — I was recording the rented CDs onto MiniDisc and adding those to the collection too.

Tsutaya let each person borrow three CDs at a time for free, but on top of those three I helped myself to a great many more.

Borrow CDs in bulk, copy every one of them onto MiniDisc.

I got all of that done on the clock at the sleepy video store where I worked part-time.

With a stack of CDs taken from Tsutaya without asking and a stack of swiped MiniDiscs, I'd spend my shifts busily copying music, at a video store Tsutaya was driving out of business.

Looking back, I come across as a thoroughly rotten kid, but in my third rebellious phase I was enjoying a double agent's life, greedy for music.

# Magic Mushrooms

I was still holding a grudge about not making it to the Full Moon Party when I went to Thailand.

I had traveled all that way with my curiosity swollen to bursting, and the thing I never got to do sat inside me with nowhere to go.

What I fixated on above all was wanting to take a hallucinogenic substance and feel my brain come awake.

From the back channels of the internet I heard you could buy a magic mushroom grow kit, so I looked into it.

What they sold was the spores and nothing else — the leaf mold to raise the mushrooms in, the plastic container, all of it you had to supply yourself.

The price was thirty thousand yen for one test tube of spores, about two hundred and forty dollars. If an unknown experience could be had for thirty thousand yen, that didn't seem like a bad deal, so I decided to buy.

What I learned a few years later was that this price was an outrageous rip-off.

Magic mushrooms were completely legal in Japan until they were outlawed in 2002, so there was no legal risk whatsoever in selling either the grown mushrooms or the spores, and for spores alone, sold by mail, around two thousand yen — some sixteen dollars — would have been a fair price.

With no experience and nothing but magazine knowledge, I had no way of knowing any of that, and got fleeced, good and proper.

Not realizing for those few years that I'd been ripped off was a small mercy, at least.

# The Grow Kit

The site that sold the spores explained how to make a grow kit, so I followed it and assembled one.

I went to a hardware store and bought a clear plastic container.

The kind you'd store clothes in inside a closet, a decent size, about fifty by forty centimeters (twenty by sixteen inches).

A third of that size would actually have been fine, so I'd say I got a little carried away.

The first thing I did was sterilize the container.

Boil water in a kettle, pour it down the inside walls, kill off the bacteria.

Leave bacteria in the container and the mushrooms won't grow, apparently.

Spread leaf mold across the bottom, bury the spores just under the surface.

Dampen the soil with the spray bottle bought for the purpose.

Finally, cover the whole container with newspaper, and it's finished.

The important thing, it seems, is to let air through while keeping the moisture from escaping.
