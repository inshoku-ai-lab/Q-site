## The Karate Guys

This party was overflowing with interesting people.

Goa parties were full of interesting people too, but there were so many of them that individuals inevitably blurred together.

The Manali party, though, had about fifty people, so each personality stood out in sharp relief.

Among them were two Germans who did karate for real, and they danced like they were in the middle of a sparring match.

The rumor was that they went out to a waterfall deep in the mountains and did their karate training there every day.

It really does sound like something out of a manga, but it was as if Ryu and Ken, the characters from the video game Street Fighter, had jumped out into the real world.

The kind of oddball who goes traveling in India, handed plenty of spare time by the gap in the cost of living, can chase his own interests as far as they go.

The karate guys were one example.

They were wrecked on LSD like everyone else, throwing flying kicks into empty air and dancing hard, on and on, with infinite stamina.

## Enter C

And right about then, C — the one I introduced earlier — started to rev up.

Among ourselves we called any Japanese dancer with a personality strong enough to hold up anywhere in the world "world-class" — half in respect, half as a joke — and C was exactly that, the world-class one who represented us.

He was a samurai ninja on permanent combat footing even in ordinary life, so ninja gear was his everyday clothing, and his everyday clothing doubled as his party outfit.

At a party, though, he tied still more equipment around his waist — like the kid from the movie The Goonies, he carried a whole assortment of gadgets, ready for adventure.

And in that formidable getup, C performed a strange dance that looked like some old martial art.

He told me he had given the day's style a name: the Dance of the Tiger.

On other days, apparently, there were also the Dance of the Dragon God and the Dance of the Kirin, named for the beast out of old legend.

His body moved slowly, but it shook in small, quick motions in time with the rhythm.

He kept his center of gravity low, leaping now and then, moving around fast.

Every so often, in time with the music, he worked in a motion shaped like a tiger's paw.

Was he picturing a tiger on the hunt?

C was completely absorbed inside himself, not caring in the least about anything around him.

It had the air of the world-class about it, as advertised.
