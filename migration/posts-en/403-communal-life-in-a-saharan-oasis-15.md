## Moonlight

I-chan and I had drifted a little way from the bonfire and were watching a beautiful full moon from the dark.

Its light was sharp and yet warm, and it felt good in a way I couldn't put into words.

It even seemed a little warmer than an ordinary night.

According to something I'd read in a book, a full moon is fifty times brighter than a half moon.

The light was strong enough to make me believe it.

## Clouds

Perfectly round in a sky without a single cloud, the full moon smiled down at us.

Then a shadow crossed that smile.

A cloud had drifted over the full moon.

In a desert where the sky is always clear, that should have been impossible, and we couldn't believe our eyes.

But it was no hallucination; any way you looked at it, the moonlight was dimming.

The cloud must have been moving, because the shadow over the moon was slowly spreading.

## A Miracle

A cloud over a desert that never has a single cloud—that was a miracle! Overjoyed, we went back to the bonfire and told our friends what we'd seen.

Nobody paid any attention to what we said; they just kept watching the moon in silence.

The cloud over the full moon kept gaining ground, and it looked ready to cover the whole moon at any moment.

We watched for a while, and at last the cloud covered the moon completely.

And then, to our astonishment, the moon, blacked out by the cloud, began to turn a dark, blackish red.

That was impossible.

More impossible still, the moonlight faded and stars began to shine across the night sky.

## The Explanation

Huh? Huh? Huh?

Wait, is this a lunar eclipse?

Nothing but a lunar eclipse could logically explain what was happening.

A real lunar eclipse, right there in the desert: it threw us, but it moved us too.

When we asked our friends, it turned out some of them had known and had deliberately kept it to themselves.

Call it a surprise event.

And because we hadn't known, we got the biggest surprise of all, and the best lunar eclipse we could have asked for.
