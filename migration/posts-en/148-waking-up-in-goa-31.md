## Doubt

Goa's New Year's party, like Christmas, is one of its big draws.

A big draw, though, only among travelers in the know.

Chikyu no Arukikata and the other guidebooks Japanese travelers carried made it sound as though Goa's popularity was a thing of the past.

The beaches they actually listed were the ones that had been popular long ago and were by then full of rich old tourists.

Traveling by the guidebook, I landed first at Calangute Beach in the south, the one it recommended, but by then the travelers had moved further north.

This was a kind of information manipulation by the government, which I think did not want Japanese people experiencing what Goa really was.

From around this time, doubts about the government and the social system began to set in.

I had a stance against society back when I was living alone in Osaka too, but it was the attitude of a rebellious child.

But India, and cannabis and LSD along with it, turned that into doubt about the way society itself was built.

## Illegal

For me the biggest trigger was cannabis.

Smoke it once and you understand: it is nothing to make a fuss about.

You relax, you feel good, your appetite picks up, and music sounds better.

No real side effects either.

And yet, for some reason, illegal.

Smoking it myself, I felt the gap between the law and reality in my own body and saw how unnatural society was.

It was the same with LSD: consciousness wakes up, the senses sharpen, there is no side effect on the body, and I could not see why it was illegal.

Many years on from then, I do think LSD should be regulated by law, but at the time I simply had my doubts.

## New Year's Eve Soba

On New Year's Eve we got together to celebrate the new year and threw a Japanese food party.

Japanese food, though soy sauce was the only Japanese ingredient you could get in Goa in those days, and even so we were thrilled.

In place of New Year's soba — the buckwheat noodles you eat on the last night of the year — we made suiton, dumplings of plain flour, and in place of bonito stock we used dried local fish, and we fried tempura with fresh seafood.

With tempura suiton standing in for New Year's soba we saw the old year out and got ready for the one coming.

Next year was 1999, the end of the century.

Even without really believing it was the end of the century, the idea got into your head without your noticing.

Seeing in the last year of the millennium on the Western calendar, in Goa, felt like it had to mean something big.

It was probably the best place there was for enjoying the end of the century to the last drop.
