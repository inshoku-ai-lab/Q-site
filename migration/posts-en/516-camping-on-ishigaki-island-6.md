## Year's End

About a week after we got to the campground, the end of the year arrived.

Neither of us was seeing anyone, so the Christmas the rest of the world made such a fuss over meant nothing to us, but we did want New Year's to be a special day.

As it turned out, we didn't need to make it special ourselves. It was made special for us, whether we liked it or not.

We hadn't seen it coming at all: every shop closed for the holidays.

Restaurants, grocery stores, all of them shut.

Living out of tents, we didn't exactly have a cupboard full of food.

With that sudden, completely unexpected shutdown, we were facing a food shortage.

## Necessity Is the Mother of Invention

Still, "nothing" wasn't quite nothing. There was a little food left.

When the two of us pooled our leftovers, we had some flour, a few potatoes and onions, eggs, oil, salt, and soy sauce.

If we cooked all of it into one dish and ate it a little at a time over several days, we could probably hold out until the shops reopened after New Year's.

What we came up with after racking our brains was chopped onion and grated potato, bound with egg, flour mixed in, a pinch of salt, fried in a pan with plenty of oil.

Then soy sauce poured over the top.

It was a sort of offshoot of okonomiyaki, the savory Japanese pancake, and it was delicious and really satisfying.

We had panicked about having nothing to eat, but if you combine what you have and put your heads together, you can usually get by.

It's not much of a story, but that experience gave me a lot of confidence.

Pool what you've got, get creative, and you'll manage.

An old proverb floated up in my head: put three ordinary people together and you get the wisdom of Monju, the bodhisattva of wisdom.

## New Year's Eve

Since it was New Year's, we decided to go up to the highest point on Ishigaki Island and watch the sunrise.

Climbing a mountain in the middle of the night is pretty rough going, so the plan was to climb in the late afternoon, camp at the top, and wait for morning there.

We hiked up, found a decent spot, and spread out our sleeping bags.

That kind of flexibility is something only travelers have.

We could sleep anywhere.

Southern island or not, spending the night on a summit was cold, but what we got for it was worth the cold.
