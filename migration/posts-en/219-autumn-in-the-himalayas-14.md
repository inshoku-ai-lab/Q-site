## What Y Did

The moment Y sensed the police might be coming, he stuck the hash he had on him to the underside of a bench.

Good hash has oil in it, and it clings to whatever it touches, tacky as a kneaded eraser.

While the police had the party surrounded and were sorting everyone into those with ID and those without, he had been off doing his own thing.

One group came in through the entrance on the right side of the hotel and lined up in the main hall.

Another group came in from the left and lined up.

A policeman walked Y in from one side, and he went straight across the hall, out the exit on the opposite side, and into the courtyard.

The cops, struggling to get everyone into line, didn't much care about him stepping out into a dead-end courtyard.

And there, he said, he stayed hidden until the police left the hotel.

Once they were gone, he went back for the hash he had stuck to the bench and started walking back to Vashisht.

But our crowd was taking up the whole road at a slow walk, and since he couldn't get past, he had apparently been trailing us at a distance, watching how it played out.

## Y's Escape

After a while he came upon some Westerners who had already slipped out of the party and were trying to get back to Vashisht by auto-rickshaw, only to get caught behind the walking crowd and crawl along after it.

He talked to them, got himself a ride, and where the road widened the rickshaw got past the crowd, and that was how he made it home to Vashisht.

He kept his face hidden so as not to be spotted, of course, but we knew nothing about any of it and walked on without a care.

He was proud of how well he had played it and how fast he had moved, and at the same time he felt guilty that he alone had slipped out while his friends were suffering.

Which was exactly why he was so happy that I, his close friend, had gotten out too.

I rolled a joint, and we smoked it to the two of us getting out, and then I put on music I loved and settled in to relax.

I had danced myself out and I was tired; it was about time to rest.

Then B, who had gone to the party with us, appeared out of nowhere with a stricken look on his face, to tell us that things had taken a sudden turn.
