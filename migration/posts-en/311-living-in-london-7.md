## Privilege

T had met a traveler living in this squat house, and that was how he came to be invited in.

You can't stay there without an invitation, so it was a lucky thing.

A rent-free stay appeals to anyone, and there was a quiet competition going on beneath the surface for the right to one.

T's invitation came with a condition: two weeks and no longer.

For me as well, the whole squat house experience was something entirely new.

Life in society had always meant one thing to me — you have to keep earning money — so meeting people who earned nothing and lived perfectly ordinary lives on free rent and free food was like having the scales fall from my eyes.

The sensation of my outlook on life up to then caving in.

That what you need is not money, but a warm bed and food.

With a warm bed and food, money was not necessarily required.

It had never occurred to me that I would be set free from the whole value system of money in the dead center of one of the largest cities in the world.

## Tea

We drank tea and enjoyed the conversation in the enormous courtyard the factory grounds had been turned into.

The grounds were quite large, and further in, from the look of it, abandoned factory buildings lay scattered around.

They had more military uniforms than they knew what to do with and told me to help myself any time I wanted, but wearing a military uniform around town wasn't to my taste, so I turned them down.

Still, the number of things you could get for free was one surprise after another.

What surprised me more than anything was the telephone in the factory office.

The factory had been sealed up years before, but the office line was apparently still connected, and you could make unlimited calls anywhere in the world.

The sloppiness of government work amazed me, but apparently that's just how it goes.

And in fact, the government had been leaving this illegal occupation alone for years now.

## Curiosity

I found myself wanting to live in a squat house too, but this one was already full, and plenty of people were waiting for a spot to open.

Finding a squat house on my own was out of the question.

I was living in London on nothing but broken English to begin with, so occupying a government building by myself and living in it was a dream beyond a dream.

I-chan was fiercely against it as well — "that way of living is far too avant-garde for me, absolutely not."

Still, this way of life, living in a squat house, seems to have loosened something that had set hard in me around money, and it left a deep mark on how I saw life.
