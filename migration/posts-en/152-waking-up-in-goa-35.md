## The Hippie Old Guy

In an earlier post I wrote about C, the hippie old guy who had been living in Goa for years, and that pushy personality of his was generating trouble all over the place.

There were minor fallings-out among the Japanese, but the big trouble was that he got abducted by a group of German neo-Nazis who held anti-hippie beliefs.

Why there were neo-Nazis in Goa, a tropical paradise packed with hippies, I don't know, and I don't know what kind of quarrel you have to pick to end up abducted either, but abducted he was.

This is where B, the saint, steps in.

Fluent in English, he walked into the middle of the neo-Nazi group and got C out peacefully.

Maybe even the neo-Nazis had no answer to that peaceful manner of his.

C was scared enough to fly straight home to Japan, so we never heard it from him, and B would never go into details, so what actually happened stayed a mystery — a frightening story all the same.

## The Hard-Boiled Old Guy

The hard-boiled old guy, who I also wrote about in an earlier post, was caught up in trouble of his own.

He played the arrogant yakuza, and his shouting had gotten him frozen out by everyone, but he never reflected on it, and his brazenness only grew.

There was a young Japanese man who had lost patience with that.

He had genuinely lived in a hard world, a real hard-boiled type who had come through seriously dangerous, violent scrapes.

Small but muscular, with a mohawk, he had something about him that said this was the one person you must not cross.

Both the hard-boiled old guy and the young man were travelers, but if anything they had half a foot — or both feet — in the yakuza world.

Both made their living in the drug trade: real outlaws.

From what I heard, at a party where everyone was enjoying themselves the old guy was shouting his head off, and the young man went past his limit — he held a knife on him, threatened him, walked him out of the party, and gave him a lecture.

The old guy understood that the young man he was up against was a genuinely dangerous man in the same line of work, and he quieted down.

After that incident the old guy turned into a mild, good-natured character, but the place must have become uncomfortable for him anyway, because he left Goa soon after.

For better or worse, in Goa, where the police were soaked in bribes and useless, there was a wildness to the place like something out of a Western.
