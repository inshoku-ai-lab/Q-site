## Options

Tangier is a port town facing Europe and a scenic tourist spot as well, so it has plenty of appealing restaurants, and prices run high to match.

My partner I-chan's travel money was down to almost nothing, so we couldn't really afford to take our time sightseeing in an expensive city.

The day after we got to Tangier, we wasted no time moving on to our next destination.

## Chaouen

Next up was Chaouen, Chefchaouen on the maps, a country village in the mountains of northern Morocco.

Ordinary tourists had never heard of it, but it was famous among travelers, and everyone who had been to Morocco told you to go to Chaouen.

Apparently a lot of the buildings there were blue, and together with the deep blue of the sky they made for beautiful streets.

## Touts

Morocco lives on tourism, so there are all sorts of ways to get around, but we decided to travel by local bus, the kind the locals ride.

Our budget left us no other choice, but it was also the best way to meet Morocco as it really is.

When we got to the bus stop, sure enough, a fierce battle among the touts broke out.

We were used to scenes like this from our travels in India, so we brushed them off and found the bus we wanted.

Neither of us could speak French or Moroccan, but experience on the road gets you by anyway.

We'd been warned that Moroccan touts and beggars were incredibly persistent, but after all the traveling we'd done in India, they didn't bother us in the least.

If anything, running into this kind of fierce Third World touting for the first time in a while made it feel like coming back to life.

In London, you would almost never see anything like it.

Every last one of them was fighting to survive, throwing themselves headlong into the reality in front of them.

Getting depressed and hooked on drugs, the way Londoners did, was a luxury they didn't have.

Their vitality, joined with the fierce Moroccan sun, was blazing.
