## Opinion

LSD gives you inspiration, does no harm to the body, and carries no addiction.

"Nothing but upside — so Qryptraveller recommends it too?" You might think that. Far from it.

I don't recommend it at all, and the reason is that its effect on the mind is too strong.

As I'll write in the posts to come, I ran wild in the culture of LSD abuse, and I took a great deal out of the inspiration it gave me.

But I also have friends, several of them, whose minds LSD broke apart.

The proportion is tiny, but as the price for the good it does, the risk is far too large.

LSD works like an amplifier on the mind and the emotions, so if a person whose mind is already a little twisted takes it, the twist gets much worse, and a person carrying trauma or a phobia is driven out of their head by the fear it amplifies.

On the other side of that, for someone who can face the fear and ride out and process the information rushing in, it does a great deal for the growth of the brain and the mind.

For the greedy sort who want to burn through life fast and will take the risk to do it, it is an effective tool.

If I had to put a figure on it: a 99.9% chance of coming out the better for it, and a 0.1% chance of never coming back.

Whether that strikes you as low odds or high odds is up to you, but think about the size of what you stand to lose.

The ones who lost their minds on LSD knew it was illegal and shouldered the risk themselves, so I suppose there is nothing to be said about it, but there is no need to go charging into danger of your own accord.

Weighing dangers like that, I don't think it should be legalized at all.

Only the hard cases and the fools — the ones who want to see the other side badly enough to carry both the risk of breaking the law and the risk of their minds coming apart — should take LSD, and it shouldn't be made something just anyone can get hold of easily.

Still, it is a substance with such wonderful, enormous potential that I want it studied properly at the right research institutions and put to good use by psychiatrists and therapists who mean well.

It has dramatic therapeutic effects on depression, on obesity, on addictions of every kind, and simply outlawing it and burying it in the dark is far too much of a waste.

In the same way, it would be a fine thing if artists and religious practitioners could use it legally and safely to reach for inspiration.

I firmly believe that once LSD is studied by specialists, it will contribute enormously to the mental and spiritual advancement of the human race.

The way I see it, it is the same as weather weapons or vaccines: it can do good or do harm depending on the intent of the person using it.

And by extension the same goes for governments, and for Western medicine.

If reading my posts has made you want to experience LSD, go to a country where it is legal — the Czech Republic, say — and try it under an experienced professional.

It will be difficult if you can't speak English, but apparently there are treatments that use LSD as part of psychotherapy.

Whatever you do, please don't buy LSD cut with God knows what from God knows which dealer and test it on yourself, and please don't do what I did and dive into Goa to find out for yourself.

I make no guarantee at all for your life or your mind.

This subject is enormously important to humanity, and dangerous with it, so I am glad to be able to lay it out here, in a premium post.

Next time we go back to the stupid, dangerous, entertaining stories of LSD abuse, and I'd ask you to hold back the critical eye as much as you can.

Every kind of experience beyond good and evil comes around, links up with the rest, and turns into a great love — that large process, the one particular to travelers, is what I want to share through this blog, so I hope you'll take it in.
