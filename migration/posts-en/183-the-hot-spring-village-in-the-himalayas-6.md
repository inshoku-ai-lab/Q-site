## T

Not long after I got to Vashisht, T turned up: like R, one of the people I had spent all my time with in Goa.

With both of them, it had been months of hanging around every day, sharing our adventures and our meals, so they felt less like friends than like family, like siblings, like comrades.

Calm, quiet, and always looking after people, T played, above all, the mother role in our group. To hear the Section Chief tell it, she was one of the office ladies in our section.

She was small, not quite a hundred and fifty centimeters (just under five feet), but the ease and the size of her heart were remarkable, and Y, who wanted to be a film director, called her a little giant.

For all that bigness of heart, T had a small frame and was very delicate, in body and mind alike, with a streak of the artist in her; we had a lot in common, and she was easy to be around.

She found our guesthouse almost as soon as she reached Vashisht, and as luck would have it a room was free, so she became the newest friend under the same roof.

We celebrated the reunion, our heads full of the Himalayan adventure about to start.

## A Couple

R and T had been close in Goa as well, and each of them, on getting back to Japan, had apparently stopped by Y's place in Tokyo and crashed there for a month or so, doing nothing much.

In the house in Shibuya they did nothing in particular, cooking and enjoying themselves the way they had in Goa.

Somewhere in that close stretch of time, it seems, love grew between R and T, and they had started becoming a couple.

T was looking forward to seeing R after so long and to spending time with him.

We decided to go over to R's house the very next day.

## R's Place

The next day I took T along and went over to R's house.

Knocking on the door brought nobody out, so I called R's name, and an answer came from inside.

R came out with the same sleepy face as ever, but T's visit seemed to please him, and some life came into his face.

We went in and drank tea.

The room, sixty yen a day — about fifty cents — was the same as before, cramped and dim, wearing the look of an opium den.

The first time I came, it had felt like the house of mountain people living close to nature, but once I knew what R was doing in it, the only image I could hold was an opium den.

R was the same as always, gentle and kind, and he welcomed us warmly.

Nothing had changed since my last visit, he said: snorting brown sugar and playing Wizardry on his Game Boy.

His characters were already leveled up to an abnormal degree, something like twenty times the strength you need to beat the game.

The game is famous for how hard it is, so how much time went into that is frightening even to imagine.
