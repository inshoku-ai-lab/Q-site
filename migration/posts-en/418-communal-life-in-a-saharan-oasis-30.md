## A Rebellious Streak

My rebellious streak was insanely strong.

Part of it was probably a backlash against my repressed childhood, and the hippie traveler culture I moved into afterward must have played a big part too.

So I imagine teaching me anything was pretty hard work.

And if the one doing the teaching was some experienced older guy, I'd guess it was harder still.

That's because, somewhere in my subconscious, there was something that made me push back, without meaning to, against men much older than me.

I don't know where that came from, but I think they stood for authority, the kind you're supposed to rebel against.

## Father Figures

Growing up in a single-mother household, and with the blame my mother held, deep down, toward her ex-husband, I had nurtured a defiance toward anything paternal.

The one who softened that defiance toward older men was Gandalf.

He healed my distrust of anything paternal.

I learned to show respect to my elders, and I learned to listen to people with experience.

He was the first grown man I had ever respected in my life.

I tend to look at things from a cynical angle, so I rarely respect people.

I might like someone the way you like a friend, or look up to them, but I had never respected anyone as a human being. It was Gandalf, too, who made me change that flippant attitude at the root.

## Conscience

Whether it came from my rebellious streak or from the psytrance culture I was so into, I don't know, but I had a resistance to things like conscience, a good heart, and believing in love.

Youthful defiance and a certain coldness may have had something to do with it too.

And it was Gandalf, again, who warmed up that big-city coldness.

I can never thank Gandalf enough.
