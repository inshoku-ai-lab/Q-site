## Uneasy About Seeing Her Again

My girlfriend was arriving in a few days, and I should have been glad about it, but honestly I wasn't.

It wasn't that I disliked her — what scared me was losing the freedom to chase this life on the road by myself.

More than anything, she was coming in order to appear in the documentary Y was shooting, and the obligations that came with work like that felt like nothing but a hassle.

At twenty-one, a year and a half after finding the world of travel, the only thing that interested me was running around with the other travelers however I pleased; romance, dealing with people, relationships, all of it was simply one more nuisance.

Maybe what I should have done then was tell her the truth, end the romance and go back to being single — that might have turned out best — but with almost no experience of people, I had no sense for anything that delicate.

Into that unsettled state of mind, I-chan arrived.

## Seeing Her Again

Whatever state my head was in, I-chan came into Goa with a smile full of hope.

Acting had been her dream since childhood, and she was looking forward to working with Y, someone actually inside the film industry.

Whatever unease I had been carrying, the reunion after so long made us both happy, and the good days back in Japan came back with it.

Backpacks still in hand, the two of us headed for Y's house.

The plan was to stay at Y's place for a while and find a house we actually wanted to live in.

Last time in Goa I had stayed on a beach near the village, so this time I was thinking of getting away from the village and living out in nature.

## House Hunting

We rented a motorcycle and rode all over Goa looking for a house.

The Christmas high season was coming, and empty houses were hard to find.

We wanted something that balanced the price against the natural setting and the quiet, and at the same time a place where we could listen to music at full volume in the middle of the night.

A place that convenient doesn't turn up easily, but after several days of searching we found one that could only be called perfect.

It sat about fifteen minutes by motorcycle from where the travelers gathered — a little far compared to where most of them lived, but that was exactly why it was quiet and surrounded by nature.

The landlady was a heavyset, middle-aged Christian woman, friendly as anything, and we took to her right away.

What she rented out was a cottage about ten meters (thirty feet) from her main house — a standalone place of about twenty tatami mats, some thirty-two square meters (350 square feet).

It had a kitchen, a toilet and a shower, a garden, and privacy you could actually keep.

A place like that went for about 8,000 yen a month — roughly sixty-five dollars — if you paid three months up front, and we were overjoyed to decide that this was where we would live.
