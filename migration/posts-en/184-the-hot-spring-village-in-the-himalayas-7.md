## T's Melancholy

That day we drank our tea and went home, but starting the next day T went to R's house every day.

For T it must have been painful: she had come all that way to see the man she loved, and he had shut himself up inside his own world, cut off from everything outside it.

She went to the house every day, kept a line open between them, and worked on him patiently until she got him to move into the guesthouse where we were staying.

That made me happy too.

It meant living in the same guesthouse as the Section Chief, T and R, all of them people I was close to.

I was also starting to get friendly with the other guests.

At our place R shared a room with T, and he seemed to come back, at least a little, to something like a human life.

But he still couldn't get free of the addiction.

T grew unhappy about it, and to stay out of the way of that unhappiness R turned his days and nights around, until he was taking his brown sugar in the middle of the night.

The childishness in him that had never healed was starting to erupt.

The seasoned traveler who had looked after everyone in Goa faded into the background, and a part of him like a selfish grade-schooler came out instead.

Some of that was the brown sugar, but the relationship with T seemed to be drawing that side of him out as well.

## What's Frightening About Addiction

Writing about it this way might make brown sugar, a drug something like a weaker form of heroin, sound like a terrifying thing, but that isn't really the case.

What's frightening is the childishness and the trauma that never healed; keep something like that buried at the bottom of your heart and, given some trigger, it comes to the surface and does harm in your everyday life.

It can be alcohol, or a dependent relationship, or gambling, or work, or a hobby — anything at all.

Anything will do, as long as it gets you away from the pain in your heart.

For R it was brown sugar.

He had chosen to run from reality as far as it would go.

He was so set on it that neither T nor I could find an opening to step in.

We had no choice but to accept, fully, that there was nothing to be done, and simply keep growing the friendship between us.
