## Work

Before I set out, I had figured this would be a one-time journey: come back to Japan, skip getting a place of my own, live at my parents' house and put everything into making music.

But India had hit me so hard that I could not stop myself from going back to chase that experience further.

Which meant I needed money.

And there were no decent jobs waiting for a dumb technical high school dropout.

There are only so many ways a twenty-year-old with no schooling and no experience gets hired at good money.

## Manual Labor

After turning it over for a while, I settled on manual labor.

I wondered whether a scrawny movie geek who used to get bullied could handle physical work at all, but playing with devil sticks in Goa had been wearing away my conviction that I was no good at anything athletic, and that was enough to make me want to try.

Twenty seemed like the perfect age to go at the limits of the body, and having come through India, I had the confidence that I could probably do anything.

If anything, being skinny and unathletic was the very reason to do it — a stretch of manual labor might remake me into someone with a completely different set of values.

There are all kinds of manual labor, and out of all of it I picked the most dangerous and the best paid: tobi work, putting up scaffolding on construction sites.

The main reason was that I am afraid of heights.

Scared of heights, scrawny, no faith in my own body — walk in off the street, take the tobi job, beat the fear of heights and the sense that I was hopeless at anything physical, and leave for India again with a pile of money.

A tall order, but the person I was now ought to be able to pull it off.

It seemed worth trying.

## The Interview

Luckily, I found a scaffolding company ten minutes from the house by bicycle.

I went in for the interview and was met by a ripped, easygoing young guy.

No experience was fine, he said, and so was being skinny and bad at sports and afraid of heights.

He would toughen me up, so don't worry.

Work started the very next day.

Everyone gathered at the office at six thirty in the morning, then headed out to the site.

The job was putting up scaffolding on the roof of a four-story building.

The work wasn't especially dangerous, but heights bring fear with them, and I had no confidence in my own stamina.

## Ecstasy

For that first day — the day my fear of heights would be most switched on, and the biggest test of my stamina — I had a trick ready.

An illegal drug: ecstasy.

Ecstasy was originally made as a treatment for depression, and it forces a person into happiness.

It also has stimulants in it, so you can push past the limits of your body and keep going.

About thirty minutes before the shift started, I bit off a piece of it and braced myself for the heights.

Taking a drug and going to work on your first day is an extremely stupid thing to do, but the plan worked.

Building scaffolding while looking down from four floors up would have made the old me lose his nerve, but thanks to the ecstasy my legs never froze and I could keep moving.

If anything I was giddy, enjoying the height, working with a smile on my face, and the older hands on the crew took me in warmly.

This was going to be fine, and I felt confident about the days ahead.

It was a solution far outside anything sensible, but I was also enjoying the feeling of being off in a world of my own.
