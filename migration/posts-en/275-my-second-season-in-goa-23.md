## Crashing at Our Place

N, an oddball with a free and easy sense of money, was having far too good a time in Goa to leave, and bit by bit his travel money ran out.

Then one day he finally reached the point where he had no money at all except his plane fare home, and he could not pay his rent.

So we let him crash in our living room.

Both I-chan and I loved N for exactly who he was, so it was a decision that made sense to us.

The three of us started living together.

N was effectively penniless, so I-chan and I covered the living expenses.

Crashing at someone's place like this was not all that rare in the traveler world, and you saw it everywhere — travelers who were having too good a time to leave Goa, staying on and on with someone else looking after them.

Most travelers had been in that spot themselves — today you, tomorrow me — and with that instinct built in, there was an atmosphere of enjoying the days together whether anyone had money or not.

But it was a dangerous trap as well, and once in a while there was someone who never got out of it and wound up homeless, settled into Goa for good.

## The Three of Us

The three of us were all about the same age, we got along, and we had a good time of it day to day.

By this point the wall I had kept up inside me toward I-chan had come down completely.

Maybe N was what greased the wheels.

Our place was not especially big, but it had a large garden, and between that, the open air of Goa, and how little distance people there kept from one another, living together never became a burden.

N had no money to rent a motorcycle either, so whenever we went anywhere the three of us rode on one.

Three of us on a scooter, no helmets, of course — and in the Goa of those days none of that ran up against any traffic law.

Nobody had a license, needless to say.

Speeding, one-way streets, parking violations: none of it existed, and there was not so much as a traffic light.

All of it came down to a single phrase: everything is your own responsibility.

A simple rule — if you do not want to die, be careful.

But simple as it was, there were people who could not keep to it, and you heard about traffic accidents constantly, and every so often word of a fatal one came flying at you.

People who knew the scene said about ten Japanese died in Goa every year.

Whether that was true or not I have no idea, but it is a staggering number.

Still, the freedom was what you got in exchange for shouldering that risk and looking after yourself under it, and the people who came to Goa went in knowing the bargain and enjoyed themselves.
