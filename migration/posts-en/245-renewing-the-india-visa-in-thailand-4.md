## My Closest Friends

The day after the police squeezed a bribe out of me, R and T, the couple who had been my closest friends in Goa and in Vashisht, came over from India to Thailand.

Their plan was the same as mine: renew their Indian visas in Thailand and spend the winter in Goa.

For R there was more to it than that — he had also come to get out from under the opium addiction that had held on to him in Vashisht.

In Vashisht opium was far too easy to come by, and there was almost no trouble from the police, so nothing ever came along to break the habit.

So R crossed a border into another country, forcing himself into a place where opium was simply out of reach.

Northern Thailand borders Laos and Myanmar, and that whole area is lawless — the drug zone they call the Golden Triangle, where opium is produced in enormous quantities — so anyone who set out to find some could find it easily enough.

Crossing a border, then, did not in itself mean escaping the addiction, but R needed a reason and a place to start.

We decided to do everything in our power for our friend.

The idea was to leave the next day for an island in the south, take a vacation there, and let R sweat the addiction out of his system.

## Koh Chang

Any island would have done, but none of us felt like traveling a long way for a stay of only a few days, so we settled on Koh Chang, not far from Bangkok.

We caught the first bus of the morning, rode east for several hours, and then took a ferry out to the island.

After all those days in the Himalayan mountains in India, and then the big-city days in Delhi and Bangkok, an island in the south made the joy and the sense of release that much sharper.

A world away from the early-winter cold of the Himalayas and the snow lying over the peaks, a deep blue sky and pure white clouds shone under a fierce sun.

Once we were on the ferry, the light off the water, the sea wind and the spray hitting my face, all of it made the beauty of the journey pick up speed.

T and I were carrying on like children in the tropical air, while R, whose opium withdrawal seemed to be killing him, stayed hunched in a corner of the boat.

We were dead set on helping R, and at the same time we watched him suffer for his own doing, coldly, as a man getting no more than he deserved.
