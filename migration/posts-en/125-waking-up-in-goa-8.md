## The Oddballs of Goa

Goa had a power that drew in oddballs, and a power that broke down their frame of common sense and accelerated their oddness.

There were three older Japanese men, travelers with years on the road, whom we called the Three Yokai of Goa — yokai, the monsters out of Japanese folk tales.

Two of them were F, the homeless one, and C, both of whom I wrote about in earlier posts.

The third was H, a man of about fifty who played the part of a hard-boiled detective out of an American comic book, the kind who knows the underworld inside out.

He had in fact lived a fairly hard-boiled life, and that hardness, apparently, was what drove his wife and child away.

The tough-guy persona had sunk into him for good, and people disliked the high-handed way he treated young people and girls.

All three were trouble on a completely different scale, and we kept a reasonable distance while still spending time with them.

But the Goa scene was small and everyone was tangled up with everyone else, so keeping fully clear of them was never possible.

## The Dynamic

The balance of power among the three was strange: C, the hippie guru, stood on his own and talked to the other two as an equal.

H, the hard-boiled one, had a boss's streak in him, and whenever F, the homeless one, came to him for help, H would look after him.

F had thrown away every last shred of pride, so he fawned on H without the slightest self-consciousness and picked up the scraps.

That side of F irritated H, and he would mock him — "Hey, dog," and the like — but he kept up the friendship all the same, and I think it was because being relied on felt good.

## Gathering at the Party

The difficult old guys, the lovely travelers, the local kids, the local aunties — a Goa party is where every one of them comes together.

On the good side, everyone is connected, and there is something in it that feels like a community.

On the bad side, you also have to find a way to spend time with the troublesome ones you would rather not run into.

Being hard-boiled, H had no use for anything frivolous, and dancing around happily on ecstasy was out of the question; for him, a Goa trance party meant dropping LSD and dancing hard.

H seemed to make his living smuggling drugs, and he gave off the air of a man with nothing to fear, not the risk and not the law.

There was a genuinely mafia side to him as well, and he was seriously intimidating.

Even Y, the ex-bōsōzoku fighter, used keigo with H to a reasonable degree, to keep things from getting complicated.

What got under H's skin was the trio of girls from London.

The three of them were together again at the party for the first time in a while, chirping away and enjoying themselves, and that did not sit well with H, who started yelling, "You girls are way too loud with all that yakking!"

N, strong-willed, snapped back rather than be outdone, but the sheer menace of H's face made her take a step back.

Our mood had dropped and none of us felt like staying, and since it was a small party that was going to end early anyway, we went home early that night.

Our mood had dropped, but it was also a sign that H's stock was dropping.

H had never been much liked to begin with, and by yelling at three beautiful girls who were warm with everyone and liked by everyone, he ended up frozen out by the Japanese community in Goa.
