# My Sister's Honeymoon

The wedding went off without a hitch, and my sister and her husband left on their honeymoon — a honeymoon that also included a sightseeing trip for me, my mother, and my aunt.

At the time none of this struck me as odd, but looking back, I wish we had let my sister and her husband have the honeymoon to themselves.

The burden on her at that time must have been considerable.

There were two options for the Peru trip, the Nazca Lines and Machu Picchu, and given time and budget we settled on Machu Picchu.

Thinking about it now, it was a real waste, but not one of us had any interest in Machu Picchu.

We had no knowledge of it at all. All we knew was

"Apparently people lived up on a mountain a long time ago and then suddenly vanished"

and that was the extent of it.

The only reason for going to Machu Picchu was that it was famous.

If anything, had my mother and my aunt taken a genuine interest in Machu Picchu, we might not have been able to go at all, on the grounds that it was a heathen culture.

# Machu Picchu

From a town nearby we boarded a small Cessna and flew to Cusco, the town near Machu Picchu.

It was quite a large tourist city, teeming with travelers from all over the world, locals, and Peruvians who had come there for work.

It was Christmastime, so the streets were lively with children walking around selling cake.

It was an event out of another dimension — the whole family and our relatives sitting down together at a restaurant in a foreign tourist town — and it left every one of us giddy.

We went to bed early that night to be ready for the early train to Machu Picchu the next morning.

The train to Machu Picchu ran through winding valleys between the mountains, shaking violently, and that shaking had become its signature feature.

The train took us to the foot of the mountain, and from there it was another hour or so of being jolted around in a bus on the way to the top.

![The stone terraces and ruins of Machu Picchu with a steep green peak rising behind](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog22-1.jpg)

Once we reached the top, that famous view of Machu Picchu spread out in front of us.

Unfortunately my heart back then did not have enough feeling in it to be moved by scenery, though I do remember it felt vaguely good.

What occurs to me now is that going to that place at that particular time must have mattered, in terms of energy, in terms of the soul.

It was some kind of marker, and an initiation too.

As we walked around the ruins of Machu Picchu, my sister relayed to us, point by point, the commentary of a tour guide walking beside us.

But when someone who did not understand it explained it in fragments to people who were not interested, it did not amount to much.

To go all the way to Machu Picchu and take no interest, to be barely moved by it, is a genuine waste, but life plays that sort of trick on you again and again.

It is a common enough story — realizing how wonderful a stretch of time was only after it has passed.

Our sightseeing over, we rode the bus back down to the foot of the mountain along a zigzagging road of S-curves.

As the bus pulled away, the local vagrant children came chasing after it, calling out bye-bye.

The tourists happily took photos.

Around the next bend the children appeared again, waving bye-bye.

And again the tourists took photos.

![Six children silhouetted against a pink and orange sunset sky, some with arms raised](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog22-2.jpg)

Then another turn, and there were the children again.

Looking more closely, it seemed to be the same children chasing after us and waving.

While the bus took its time descending the S-curves, the children apparently cut straight down and waited for it so they could wave.

They must have repeated the same routine ten times or so in all.

When we finally arrived at the station at the foot of the mountain, those same children were waiting.

Grinning, they pestered the tourists for tips.

This was their job.

Chase the bus and grin.

That is what their living rests on, so they have it good.
