## Breakfast

I got up at eight in the morning and headed to the staff dining room.

Time for breakfast.

Breakfast was a solid, proper meal too, enough to give us the energy to work all day.

What I'd heard about the food always being satisfying seemed to be true.

The surprise came after the meal, once we'd had a break.

Everyone gathered in the dining room again, and the morning assembly began.

A representative from each department went over the day's work, and everyone checked it with one another.

## The Recitation

Once the assembly had run its course, we all began reciting the company creed together.

I don't remember the exact wording, but there was a list of principles along the lines of "We devote ourselves to our guests," and we all chanted them together, one after another.

This recitation floored me.

It had an eerie sense of unity, the kind that called to mind a self-help seminar or a cult.

It was just pleasant, positive words repeated over and over, but I could clearly sense the intent behind it: brainwashing, an attempt to control how the staff thought.

The words themselves were good ones, and reciting them probably did help the work run smoothly.

But I had only just arrived at this hut, and to my eyes it was simply eerie.

## "Environmental Upkeep"

After the recitation at the morning assembly, we moved on to something called "environmental upkeep."

This, too, had something about it that gave me the creeps.

At this mountain hut, every staff member spent fifteen minutes a day polishing one part of the hut.

With a rag, you polished an area about the size of a sheet of newspaper until it was spotless.

Keeping the hut clean was only natural for the staff, and once you got one spot perfectly clean, the dirty parts around it caught your eye, so you started cleaning those as well.

The whole hut ended up clean as a result, so it made sense for the company to enforce something like this down to the last detail.

But as an employee, being forced into this kind of religious practice in the name of work didn't sit well with me.

Buddhist training includes practices like scrubbing floors.

They're an important way to polish the mind, get rid of the ego, and cultivate an obedient heart.

But surely that's something you take on yourself, as spiritual training.

Being forced into the same act in exchange for a paycheck felt like brainwashing, a way for management to exploit the workers, and I didn't much like it.
