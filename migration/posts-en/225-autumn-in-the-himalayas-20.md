## Canceled

We had traveled all the way from Manali to the Parvati Valley, six hours of buses and transfers, and the party we were there for, unfortunately, did not seem to be happening.

Too much rain to organize it, apparently.

Then again, trying to hold a party in the middle of the rainy season is a mistake to begin with.

The party was off, but another friend — not the one I had come with — was already staying in a village higher up the valley, so we decided to go hang out with him.

One more Japanese man was staying there too, and he was exactly the sort of foolhardy adventurer that lives wild in the Parvati Valley: he had spent months trekking from village to village deep in the mountains in search of the very finest hash resin.

He was not the mafia type but the hippie type, a man who had traveled those mountain villages out of nothing but curiosity.

Long dreadlocks, a beard grown out any which way, hill tribe clothes with psychedelic patches on them — every inch the veteran hippie traveler.

And thankfully, he had with him the legendary top-grade stuff he had carried back from deep in the mountains, and he said he would let us smoke it.

## The Top Grade

Ordinary hash resin is called charas: you scrape the sticky pollen off your palms after handling the cannabis plants, knead it together, and press it into a single lump.

The top grade, on the other hand, goes by an English name for some reason — cream — and it is made in a special way.

The basics are the same as charas: you handle the pollen and knead it together.

Charas takes the pollen from the whole plant, but cream takes it only from the pistils that bloom at the very top.

Then you work that concentrated pollen in your hands, painstakingly, over three or four days.

Worked that fine, the fibers and particles break down, and what comes out is cream — creamy, mellow, the top of the line.

The secret of the quality is the strength of the high, of course, but also the energy of the hands that patiently work it, which is why it matters who is doing the kneading, and with what intention.

Most of the cream he had gathered from all those villages, he said, he had kneaded himself, over days, with love.

And so I found myself with a chance that would not come again — a taste of the finest hash resin in the world.
