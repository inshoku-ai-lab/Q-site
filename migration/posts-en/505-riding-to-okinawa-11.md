## Resolve

Out of sheer desperation, unwilling to turn back after coming this far, I made up my mind.

I'm right-handed, so if I held the bike on my right side, my own body would be the one on the cliff side.

So I decided to push the bike with my left hand and edge along the top of a drainage ditch forty centimeters (sixteen inches) wide, so that if anything went over, the bike would go first.

The gap was only about four meters (thirteen feet), but looking down at the collapsed road was not a pleasant feeling at all.

With this much of the road already gone, there was every chance the ditch could give way at any moment.

There was no choice now but to push on, so I steadied my breathing and started forward, slowly and fearfully.

I kept my body as close to the wall as I could, kept the bike as close to my body as I could, and moved slowly, with the utmost care.

It was pretty terrifying.

Luckily, the ditch didn't collapse, I didn't lose my balance, and I made it safely to the other side of the road.

I had beaten the curse of the closed road.

I never imagined I'd find myself on the kind of adventure where I could actually get hurt like this, but I made it through in one piece.

Just past the landslide was the top of the pass, and from there it was all downhill.

Thanking the travel gods, I sailed down the slope, feeling great.

That was the real joy of traveling by bike.

## On to Kyushu

Apart from the landslide adventure, I kept cycling through Shikoku without any real trouble, soaking in a hot spring almost every day, eating good food, and enjoying myself, but my time in Shikoku was drawing to a close.

A long, narrow peninsula stretches out from the upper left corner of Shikoku, and my plan was to ride to its tip and take a ferry across the sea.

On the other side was Oita Prefecture, in Kyushu.

Shikoku had been a first for me, and so was Kyushu.

Pedaling forward under my own power and sleeping on the bare earth: this style of travel gave me a deep sense of fulfillment I'd never felt in all my years of backpacks and cheap guesthouses.

On a bike, getting around is free, but food costs pile up instead, so in the end I was spending more than I would have lazing around a cheap guesthouse in India and eating at restaurants. Still, the satisfaction and the sense of accomplishment were in another league entirely.

I left Shikoku brimming with a good, solid confidence.
