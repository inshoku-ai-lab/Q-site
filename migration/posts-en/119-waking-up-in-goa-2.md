## The Vortex of Thought

I'm writing this travelogue more than twenty years after the fact, with everything I've experienced and understood since; back then, though, I was a twenty-year-old movie-geek brat with no experience of the world at all, and had no idea what the LSD was doing to me.

From the overdose, my brain must have been running at something like ten times its usual activity.

Sight, hearing, the feel of my own skin — every sense was sending all of its information straight into my brain with nothing to hold it back.

The first question I ran into was this: why can't I dance when I want to dance?

Now I could answer that in one line — an LSD overdose, the body unable to keep up with perception — but at the time I didn't even understand that I had overdosed.

I also didn't know that I have a sensitive body, one that overreacts to drugs.

I had taken LSD the same as everyone else, that was all, but my sensitivity was on a whole different order.

## My Body Wouldn't Move

With my thoughts spinning at that impossible speed, my body had stopped moving, and I began to worry — could this be the bad trip people talk about?

A bad trip is when LSD makes your thoughts and feelings overreact and you sink into deep worry.

LSD amplifies emotion many times over, so the worry gets several times deeper too.

Your thinking is scrambled, so what you're actually worried about hardly matters any more; all that matters is that you're carrying the feeling of worry, and that it's being amplified.

At that point nothing was wrong with me apart from my body not moving the way I wanted it to, but I had fallen into a completely pointless worry: worrying that I might be worrying.

## Kanzen Chōaku

In the middle of the party Y saw me sitting down with my head bowed, worrying, and he came over and whispered in my ear, "Kanzen chōaku, kanzen chōaku" — good rewarded, evil punished.

Looking back, Y had probably gone into worry mode on LSD himself at some point and gotten stuck on "What is good? What is evil?"

So he decided I was worrying about the same thing and gave me his advice, which missed the mark entirely.

"Huh? Kanzen chōaku? What is he talking about?" — in worry mode, I only sank deeper into confusion.

Those words set me off, and I started worrying about whether taking LSD and dancing to Goa trance was good or evil.

LSD is banned by law.

But the party is legal, and Goa trance music is legal.

Like cannabis, LSD frees the mind and lets you see again what really matters.

And yet the government bans both cannabis and LSD.

So I started worrying all over again about the gap between the good I felt from my own experience of cannabis and LSD, and the evil the government had written into law.

## The Strange Old Guy

Around then, one of the older guys at the party began to catch my attention.

I found out later that he was a Spaniard named H, who had been arrested on drug charges back home and couldn't leave the country for a while.

Now he had finally made it back to the Goa he had been coming to for years, and he was going wild at the party.

He apparently sort of spoke Japanese, and every time he caught sight of our handful of Japanese, he would raise his right hand and shout "Ohhayoo!" — good morning.

It wasn't the giddiness of drunk recklessness — he was completely lucid, just unable to contain how happy he was.

It wasn't only his movements and his energy that drew the eye; the way he looked was extraordinary to begin with.

He was wearing clothes covered in fractal patterns — geometric designs that pull your eyes in — and the colors caught the black light and glowed.

The clothes seemed to be made of some special material that lights up hard under ultraviolet.

Look at a fiercely glowing fractal pattern while you're high on LSD and it starts to come out in three dimensions, and the pattern pulling at your eyes begins to look like an opening onto another dimension whose depth goes on forever.

These clothes had obviously been made for taking LSD and dancing to Goa trance, and the purpose of dancing in them was to dazzle the eyes of the people around you and carry them into a deeper trance.

Y and I had both come to Goa to take LSD and dance to Goa trance, but H had been walking that road for years ahead of us.

He walked it without hesitation, driving straight ahead, and that abnormal way of living came out in the way he danced at the party — and the shock of it pulled me into deeper worry still.
