## Nothing but Parties

We lived our lives around parties.

Say we went to a party at night — the LSD had us too high to sleep, so we wouldn't get to sleep until the following night.

The day after that, body and brain both worn out from overuse, a limp, strengthless state would set in and hold, and there was nothing we could do.

And one more day on, my young body would repair the muscles it had driven into the ground and come back stronger, and the brain and senses and spirit that had been driven into the ground would recover through rest and come back stronger still.

There was an element of training in it too, for the body and the brain both.

The day after my body had fully recovered, or a few days after that, the next big party would be waiting.

Go to a party, get wrecked at full throttle and dance it out to the end, sleep for about two days from the exhaustion, and once recovered, go to the next party — those were the days.

With each party we met more people and connected with them, and a big circle of friends was forming.

As everyone connected with everyone else and their friendships deepened, the friendship of the whole deepened with it, until it felt something like being classmates.

Through each of those relationships we inspired one another, and deepened one another's individuality.

## G

Among all those different personalities, the one who stood out and shone was G, a guy two or three years older than me.

He had come to Goa the year before, had the kind of experience through the parties and LSD that changes a life, and rather than go back to society he had kept traveling, and this year he had come back to Goa again.

Most of us were seeing Goa for the first time, so in experience of this kind of world the gap between us was as big as the one between a first grader and a second grader.

In an earlier piece I wrote about meeting a strange Spanish old guy at the December full moon party, the one who shouted "Ohhayoo!" and had staked his life on Goa trance, and G had met that old guy — E — the year before.

G ran on high-octane energy and hit it off with the equally high-octane E, and as they grew close he went over to Spain, worked at the shop the old guy ran, and made his money there, he told us.

G had been traveling the world for about a year and a half without going home to Japan, and he wore the stylish clothes of an African tribe; with his unusual perm and the air about him, he hardly looked Japanese.

He was relentlessly bright and positive, always dancing in front of the speakers with his smile going full blast, scattering joy over everyone around him.

Even in ordinary times the laughter around him never let up, and being liked by everyone drew out everything he had.

The change that comes from travel as a way of life and the change that comes from the culture of Goa trance, put together, could make a personality this fine — it took all of us by surprise.

## Possibility

There were many other travelers as well, all sorts of them, fine people you found yourself wanting to become.

They shone dazzlingly, and I figured they must have been born under a fine star — stars, all of them.

Talking with them, I learned that many had kept traveling for two or three years, earning money abroad as they went, and they had piled up a great deal of experience, and it convinced me that the experience was what made them shine.

Talking further, they would say they had set out at twenty-three, or at twenty-seven — far later in life than I had.

Turning that over, a thought arrived: "Wait — right now I'm a fledgling who knows nothing, but if I keep traveling for a while longer, maybe I could become as cool and shining as they are."

Obviously, if you go through experience you become a person with experience, but I couldn't picture a scrawny movie nerd like me, raised indoors, turning into anything of the sort.

But seeing cool travelers right in front of me who were actually out there doing it, I filled with an enormous hope that maybe I could do it too.
