## The Next Party

We set off for the next party in high spirits.

It was another party in the same restaurant, made over for the night.

This time I went in the loud new pants I had bought.

But unlike the expensive imported clothes the strange old guy had worn, mine were cheap Indian cotton dyed with cheap dye, and under the black light in the dark they threw back no light at all; disappointingly, they were just dark pants, presence zero.

At this party I took a quarter of a tablet of ecstasy right at the start, as planned.

I thought I was taking a quarter of a normal dose, but in fact, with my sensitive body, a quarter was the normal dose.

I would not learn that until much later.

And this time I dropped half a tab of LSD.

Here too I believed I was taking half a normal dose, when in fact I was taking four times what I actually needed.

So while I thought I was going easy on myself with a quarter of ecstasy and half a tab of LSD, what I had really done was take a full dose of ecstasy and four times the LSD and get thoroughly wrecked.

With the ecstasy multiplying the happiness and the LSD deepening the mind, I was blissed out and completely gone, and I danced all night with my brain wide open.

I danced to the limit of my stamina, but my mood was running so high that I never felt tired.

On top of that, the speed-like ingredient cut into the ecstasy kept me from sleeping and kept me dancing.

## The Pleasure of Dancing

Dancing with your body perfectly synced to the music feels unbelievably good.

And it was not only the music and my own body that were in sync: because every person there had their body perfectly synced to the sound, the bodies of everyone at the party were in sync with one another.

With all those bodies in sync, a sense of oneness rose up in us, and since everyone was in a superconscious state on LSD, the whole thing gave off the air of some strange religious rite.

We were leaning on the power of drugs and music, yes, but with the fierce euphoria, a surge of activated energy, and the storm of oneness in body and mind, every last person there was overflowing with joy.

At the last party I had been convinced that everyone was on a bad trip; this time I was feeling a storm of oneness — and the party itself was no different.

The only difference was that I had taken ecstasy ahead of time.

The plan of taking the ecstasy first had clearly worked.

Wrapped in happiness, perfectly synced with the music and with the other dancers, I kept dancing.

## The Morning Sun

The night began to pale, and the sun came up.

As the light came, the faces of the people dancing near me came clearly into view.

The people who had danced beside me all night in the dark were no longer strangers; they were comrades, born onto this planet together, sharing this place.

When our eyes met as we danced, we grinned at each other.

Not polite smiles to put each other at ease — smiles that spilled out on their own because we were genuinely glad.

You are not trying to smile; you are happy, so you smile.

Just as the morning sun came slanting into the party ground, the DJ, as if he had been waiting all night for it, put on the bright, soaring track he had been saving.

Every person at the party danced like mad, grinning from ear to ear.

Whistles and shouts rang out, and everyone was swallowed up in a vortex of joy.

Even the palm trees out beyond the walls looked like they were rejoicing along with us, which is a strange thing.

## The Loud Clothes

As the light came up and we could see each other's faces, we started to see each other's clothes too.

The guy who had been dancing quietly in dark clothes in the dark was now, in daylight, the guy dancing like a maniac in loud pants.

And this was right after I had shaved my head down to bare skin.

So the picture was of a bald Asian guy who had turned up out of nowhere at the party in loud clothes and was dancing like a lunatic.

The people around me may have been looking at me as a curiosity, and my friends were in fact pointing at me and laughing, but I was riding too high to care about any of it, and I kept dancing.

In the end the party ran past noon, and every single person went home thoroughly satisfied.

It had started around ten at night, so anyone who stayed to the end had danced for something like sixteen hours.

On the walk home some local kids pointed and laughed at me, and being out in broad daylight in those loud pants suddenly felt embarrassing; I wore them that one time, at that party, and then they went into the closet for good.
