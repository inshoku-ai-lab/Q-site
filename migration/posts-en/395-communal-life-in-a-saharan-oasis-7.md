## Life in the Oasis

Daily life was simple but full.

I-chan and I slept in a tent pitched outside, so we had privacy and time to ourselves, and in that respect we seem to have had it easier than the others.

Tent life had its good points and its bad points: the privacy and the freedom were wonderful, and living right up against nature felt good.

But living right up against nature in the desert was no easy thing.

## Daytime

I'd noticed it back when we were camping at the festival, but once tent life became my everyday routine, I felt it even more: the gap in temperature between day and night.

Out in the direct sun during the day, it went past "hot" to "burning," then, given more time, to "scorched," then to "painful," until finally you reached the territory of "dangerous."

Past "dangerous," it was probably a straight line to "dead."

If there had been a black car around, you could probably have fried an egg on the hood.

The daytime was so hot it was dangerous, so we spent most of our time inside the hut.

However hot it got outside, the hut stayed at a comfortable temperature.

## Night

The days were frighteningly hot, and the nights got frighteningly cold.

We had fun comparing the temperature swing to the seasons, calling the daytime summer and the night winter: fall came at dusk, and once the sun went down, winter arrived.

When winter came, it hit us with weather that was the exact opposite of the day's.

All through the night the temperature kept dropping fast, and by dawn it was close to freezing.

Once, water left outside in a water jar was frozen solid when we looked at it in the morning.

It really did get cold enough to freeze water.

It was a pretty harsh environment, but maybe because we were young, we got through it without much trouble.

When winter came, we put on every piece of clothing we had, wrapped ourselves in layer after layer of blankets handed down from the festival, and spent the night huddled up against each other.

You could say that being a couple is what got us through that cold.
