## Negotiation

I explained that we didn't have the money to pay a proper rate, and told him we could pay for the whole stay up front if it came to about 300 yen a night — roughly two and a half dollars.

We didn't actually need a room, so we could afford to drive a hard bargain.

It probably helped that the manager had taken a liking to us, but he grudgingly accepted the 300-yen offer.

For our part, we agreed that 300 yen a day was something we could manage.

On top of that, he threw in the use of a portable gas stove that belonged to the hotel.

And so began our life in a hotel at the gateway to the desert.

## The Desert Town

We had fully braced ourselves to camp, so it was a relief that the comfortable hotel life would go on a while longer.

There was nothing new we had to take on — just enjoy life in a desert town for a while, and then the festival would start.

It wasn't what we'd planned, but it wasn't a bad idea.

We went into town and picked up a few kitchen things.

A knife and a cutting board, a pot, some spoons.

## The Town Up Close

A tourist passing through might miss them, but once you move into a town meaning to live there, you start noticing all sorts of things.

What won us over were the pickled olives, steeped in every spice imaginable.

This was where they came from, so of course they were absurdly cheap.

And since it used to be a French colony, the bread was good.

Far better than the bread you eat in India, or the bread you buy at a British supermarket.

Baked firm on the outside, moist and fluffy on the inside.

Bread like that was baked fresh every day.

It was just bread and pickled olives, but even that alone tasted incredible.
