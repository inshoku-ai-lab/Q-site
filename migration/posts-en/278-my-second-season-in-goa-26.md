## Ten P.M.

In Goa that season the police tightened their grip, and playing music at high volume after ten at night was now restricted.

Outdoor parties were tightly restricted as well, and it took something out of the ordinary for a party to happen at all.

I learned later that the Goa party scene moves in waves, the police loosening the restrictions and then tightening them again, over and over, every few years.

It comes down to the balance among the local traders, the local residents, the mafia, and the police, and the restrictions seem to be adjusted so that each side's case can be met somewhere in the middle.

But we knew none of that, and we had come to think that the end of the legendary Goa was drawing near.

The explosion of 1999, the year before, had been the last of it, and from 2000 on the police would rein it in and the whole thing would shrink away.

A lawless zone like the one we had known could not possibly be allowed to go on; everything up to then, we figured, had been a miracle, a gift of pure luck.

## Ways Around It

But travelers had not come here for clubs that shut at ten; what they wanted were wild parties in the open air, the kind that ran not just all night but for days without stopping.

So they came up with all sorts of ways to do something about the situation.

One party would be held outside the state of Goa, another on an island offshore, another on a boat.

Y, for one, said that anything held outside the heart of Goa was not a party at all and stubbornly refused to leave Goa, but I-chan and I thought a party on a boat sounded interesting and went.

## The Party on the Boat

The party was on a ferry boat that left from Panaji, the capital of the state of Goa.

There was talk that undercover police would be hidden on board and we would all be rounded up in a single sweep, but once we were actually there the mood could not have been freer, and up on the observation deck the chillums were going around one after another.

The closed space, if anything, built a sense of closeness, and gave us all the feeling of being in it together.

There was also the worry that at a party in a space that tight, out on a boat, a bad trip on LSD would leave you nowhere to escape to and things could turn strange, so we stuck mainly to ecstasy and put our focus on the fun.

The exploration of the mind with LSD could wait for another time; this time we were there to enjoy ourselves.

From the town, down the river, and out toward open water, the boat did not rock at all and everything was quiet, but once we were out at sea it started rolling hard from side to side.

No one can go against ground that moves, and we kept dancing as one, in time with the roll.

When the boat leaned right, everyone went whoa-there and hopped off to the right on one foot, and when it leaned left, we danced along with that instead, and so it went.

There was no way to dance the steady, all-out way you want to at a Goa trance party, but all of us being thrown around together, as one body, was unpleasant and fun at the same time.
