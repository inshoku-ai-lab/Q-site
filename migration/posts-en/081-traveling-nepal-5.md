# Shiva

I got to know a Nepali small-time crook who lived near our guesthouse.

He gave his name as Shiva, spoke a few words of broken Japanese, and was oddly friendly toward me.

My friends eyed him with suspicion, but he had succeeded in getting my attention.

"Taima suu?"

It was his stab at the Japanese for "You smoke weed?"

Psychedelics in general interested me — cannabis, LSD, magic mushrooms — and "Taima suu?" had the power to stop me where I stood.

A year before, on Koh Phangan in Thailand, I had eaten too much cannabis cake and paid for it badly (earlier post),

but no bad experience was ever a match for my curiosity.

Interested as I was, my English was almost nonexistent, so looking for it or buying it myself was out of the question — but if it came to me, that was another matter.

Looking back now, I am sure I bought poor-quality stuff at a ripped-off price, but it was my first time and I had no way of telling.

## Trying Cannabis

I bought a cheap smoking pipe at a souvenir shop, opened the window of the room, and smoked indoors.

When I asked my friends whether they wanted to try, the opinion was that they would pass.

In the end two out of the three tried it, but it wasn't to their liking.

Our curiosities were pointed at different things.

On Koh Phangan the overdose had me throwing up over and over, and I never understood the pleasure of it or anything else, but this time the amount was so much smaller that I could keep it under my own control.

With the Koh Phangan failure for reference, I worked up to it carefully.

Since I didn't smoke tobacco at all, at first the act of inhaling smoke was itself beyond me.

Inhaling made my throat hurt and set me coughing.

Still, book knowledge I had: I knew that holding the smoke in my lungs as long as possible after taking it in made the effect stronger.

Trying it my own way a few times, going on that knowledge, I began to get a rough sense of what the cannabis feeling was.

The first thing I felt was my body turning heavy and dull, my movements slowing down.

It got in the way of nothing in daily life, but anything that used memory or thought went badly, and reading a book felt difficult.

In exchange, emotion and sensitivity ran richer: the more I read a manga the more I identified with it, and I enjoyed it enormously.

The fine details stopped mattering, and at the same time the smallest things felt fun.

I had heard the rumors, and they were right: a while after smoking you get hungry, and food tastes many times better.

This is the phenomenon people call the munchies, and after smoking you end up eating like an idiot without meaning to.

I would smoke, walk to the Japanese restaurant nearby, stuff myself with the munchies, and lose myself in manga.

## Music and Awareness

There were all sorts of effects, but the one I enjoyed most was that the way music sounded became something else entirely.

Sounds came through more clearly, and the groove took hold of my body harder.

In songs I had heard hundreds of times, small sounds I had never caught came through, and I became aware that those sounds held an important place in the track as a whole.

This sense of awareness was another of the things cannabis got me interested in.

Smoke cannabis and the feel of your consciousness turns into something else, and you can experience things from a point of view you have never had before.

The new perspective I got that way made me aware of things I had not been seeing, and it gave me all kinds of inspiration.

Most of what I became aware of at that time was about the difference between my life in Japan up to then and life on the road.

Living in a completely different country, on a completely different sense of time and completely different values, and taking it in through a completely different consciousness — the experience of travel deepened many times over.
