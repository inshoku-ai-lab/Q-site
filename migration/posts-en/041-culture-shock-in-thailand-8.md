# The Taxi

How long had I been lying there on the gravel at the side of the road?

I must have dropped off to sleep for a while.

Dawn was breaking, the sky going pale, every bird in the jungle in full chorus.

Sleeping on gravel had left me sore, but my head was a lot clearer and the nausea was completely gone.

There was no point going to the Full Moon Party now — it would already be over.

A shame, but I gave it up and decided to head back to the guesthouse.

I went out to the road to flag down a taxi, but at that early hour there wasn't a single car on it.

After a long wait, one finally stopped.

Not a taxi. A shared minibus.

Neither the driver nor I spoke English, so we went at it in gestures, and while I was saying the name of the guesthouse over and over he came back with an offer: he knew the place, and he'd run me there for this much.

It was a little above the rate I'd heard was normal, but there was no other way back, so I grudgingly took it.

# The Ride Back

The minibus took me aboard and started back up the road we had come down the night before.

For the moment, we seemed to be on our way without any trouble.

We should have been there by then, but there was no sign at all of arriving.

When I checked with the driver, he made a sign that seemed to mean, it's fine, leave it to me.

I started imagining being taken off somewhere deep in the hills and told I wouldn't be delivered anywhere unless I paid extra, and the unease set in.

I checked again and got the same sort of response. The unease grew.

When I kept pressing him, it turned out he had no idea where the guesthouse was and had been lost and hunting for it.

Say so sooner, I thought — though it was a relief to have confirmed there was nothing malicious going on.

In the end, after stopping to ask locals again and again, we made almost a full circuit of the island and reached the guesthouse.

My friends were already back from the party and had been worried, since I still hadn't turned up.

The way they saw it, I had only been carsick and would follow in the next taxi, but I never appeared at the party, and when they got back to the guesthouse in the morning I wasn't there either.

Maybe he was robbed? Maybe something happened to him because we left him on that mountain road? They had been fretting over it.

For better or worse, in the end nothing had happened.

I had missed the Full Moon Party, my one and only chance at it.

The others, by all accounts, had the time of their lives.

A-kun, who had come down from Osaka with me, was still lost in a satisfied grin.

The worry about me seemed to have blown clean away, leaving nothing but the memories of the night before.

# North

A-kun, who had come to the island with me, announced that he wanted to try traveling on his own.

I had taken it for granted that we would go north together, but there was nothing wrong with him getting a taste of solo travel, so for now we went our separate ways.

Everything on Koh Phangan had been outside anything I knew, and the culture shock it gave me was enormous.

There was an ache in saying goodbye to the island and to the seasoned travelers I had been running around with, but the adventure ahead — northern Thailand, alone — was calling me.

A-kun had already set off north by himself, so I left Koh Phangan for Bangkok alone.

It was only retracing a route I had already traveled once, so there was nothing to be nervous about.

I bought a combined boat-and-bus ticket at a travel agency and rode the ferry and then the bus into Bangkok.

Bangkok was exactly as it had been before I left for Koh Phangan: an unbelievable density of people.

The sky was clouded over with exhaust, everyone hurrying around chasing money.

I had grown up in Osaka and loved everything about cities, but after the slowness of a southern island, the pace of the city was starting to seem a little ridiculous.
