## Charisma

Spending time with a traveler as charismatic as J was exciting and fun.

The way J told it, India is a hard place in many ways, one that doesn't let a traveler in easily, but once you're inside, the warmth becomes an addiction and you find yourself coming back over and over.

And not on the scale of a few years, but of decades: J himself had over twenty years of history with India.

Once people get in deep they get in all the way, and yet the place is hard to get close to in the first place, so the crowd that keeps coming back is full of friends and familiar faces, and he said that every arrival in India felt like a class reunion.

Even as he was talking, he would point at a traveler across the guesthouse courtyard and another story would come flying out: he'd started running into that guy about ten years ago; he'd met that woman somewhere or other fifteen years back.

## Top Secret

A network like that among travelers apparently counts for a great deal: there are places and events you cannot reach without the web of trust that builds up between people who have been traveling for years.

J was talking with a German woman he had happened to run into at the guesthouse, someone he had been crossing paths with regularly for fifteen years.

He shared what she told him: deep in the mountains of the jungle there is something like a secret paradise for travelers, a Shangri-La out of legend that only invited people can reach.

To get there you have to jump off the train that climbs into the mountains, at an unmanned mountain station where getting on or off is forbidden, and then climb a steep mountain trail for hours more.

At the top of the climb there is supposedly an enormous river that pours off the face of the cliff as a waterfall, and from the top of that waterfall you can watch the sun go down into the sea.

The bottom of that river is strewn with crystals, which purify the water and purify everything around them, and the whole place is wrapped in a pure, clean atmosphere.

The people there live completely naked, he said, doing yoga and meditating, passing their days in peace.

The concept is a little like the movie The Beach, which was filmed a few years after this — except this was a true story, and it came first.

To me and to U both, it was all far too much like a world inside a dream to sound real, but the German woman had actually been there, and she said it was the most wonderful place there is.

J, it seemed, had gotten an invitation. No invitation came our way.

Rookie travelers who could barely speak English, we were apparently not on the guest list.
