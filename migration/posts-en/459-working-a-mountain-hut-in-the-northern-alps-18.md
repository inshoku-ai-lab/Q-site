## The Manager

J, the manager of the small hut and about to become my direct boss, was a pretty wonderful older guy.

We had gotten a little closer, bit by bit, while we were at the big hut, but once I moved to the small hut and spent twenty-four hours a day with him, I began to understand what kind of person he really was.

He was funny and warmhearted, and he and M from the cable-car relay hut were something like best friends — or partners in crime.

He had the toughness of a real mountain man, but he also had the sensitive temperament of an artist.

If anything, he was an artist at heart, and seemed to be playing the tough mountain man because his position called for it.

## Training in French Cuisine

He had started out as a cook through and through, and had spent many years training in French cuisine.

After training at a very famous, top-class hotel, he went independent and opened his own restaurant.

It was apparently a small, reservation-only place that served the finest food to a handful of diners at a time — a high-end restaurant for connoisseurs.

Day after day he honed his craft, determined to master French cooking with the best equipment in the best environment.

On the side, he climbed mountains all over Japan as a hobby.

## A Turning Point

Then, in the middle of those days devoted to perfecting his cooking, a turning point came.

He began to question his own perfectly arranged environment.

It goes without saying that you can make the best food in the best environment.

If he truly wanted to master his craft, wouldn't making the best food in the worst environment bring the greatest growth?

Most people, even if that thought crossed their mind, would never actually leave a well-equipped environment for an inconvenient one.

But J wasn't most people.

His artist's soul and his hunger to grow as a cook led him to a mountain hut, the most inconvenient place to cook there is.

His working hours probably doubled, and his pay probably dropped to about a third.

But he didn't let any of that stop him, and he dove headfirst into the world of mountain huts.
