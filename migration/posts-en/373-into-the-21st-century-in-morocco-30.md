## Ramadan

Christmas had only just gone by, but in Morocco, a Muslim country, Christmas means nothing.

What matters to them is the long fast that had begun about a month earlier.

How strict the fast is apparently varies from country to country, but here in Morocco it was fairly relaxed: fasting didn't mean eating nothing at all, and from sundown until sunrise you could eat as much as you liked.

To me it looked less like fasting than a lopsided diet of late-night meals, but for them it's an important religious ritual.

Going by the old saying "When in Rome, do as the Romans do," we were doing our own imitation of Ramadan.

That meant lazing in bed until past noon, then eating nothing for the two or three hours until evening.

We ate breakfast after dark, lunch late at night, and dinner in the middle of the night — a thoroughly slack way to live.

Still, since we were keeping Ramadan, at least on paper, we were vaguely pleased with ourselves.

## The Ramadan Miracle

For us, Ramadan was just a tourist game, but for the people living their everyday lives in this town, it was serious.

For anyone doing physical labor under the blazing sun without food or water, it must have been pretty brutal.

On the last day of Ramadan, a miracle happened, as if to quench their thirst.

The town we were staying in sits at the gateway to the Sahara, and for all practical purposes it has a Saharan climate.

In other words, the sky is always cloudless and the air is bone-dry.

You never see a cloud, and rain is out of the question.

Under those conditions, a miracle happened.

Believe it or not, rain started falling on this desert town.

Looking down on the town from the hotel roof, I saw everyone who lived there come pouring out into the streets, cheering and dancing.

It was a blessing for those who had made it through Ramadan, and a gift from heaven to the harsh, parched desert.

According to the hotel manager, it was the first rain in four years.

It rains about as often as the Olympics come around.

Whether that counts as often or rarely depends on who you ask, but getting to see people's joy at the moment a once-every-four-years event landed on the last day of Ramadan made me feel grateful to the universe.
