## The End of the Eclipse

What we had taken for a cloud over the moon was actually a total lunar eclipse.

Knowing the secret didn't make it any less surprising, moving, or beautiful.

The moon glowed a dark red, and the stars that the full moon's light had been hiding just moments before all regained their strength at once.

The warm moonlight was gone now, and the cold of the desert night came back all of a sudden.

We sat by the fire and kept gazing at the dark red moon.

The only word for the sight was mystical.

The red moon was mystical, and so were the desert, the oasis, our friends, and the whole setting of our journey — all of it was full of wonder.

This natural mystery lasted longer than I expected, and things stayed like that for about an hour.

## Why the Moon Turns Red

I looked it up later: during a total lunar eclipse, the moon passes completely into the earth's shadow and goes black, but sunlight passing through the layer of atmosphere around the earth is bent, and only the red light reaches the moon and reflects back.

It makes sense, I suppose.

## The Full Moon Again

As the eclipse ended, the red drained out of the moon, and it began to look like a full moon with a piece missing.

At the same time, the stars that had grown so strong lost their strength and went back into hiding in the full moon's light.

Watching it turn back into the full moon we knew so well, we felt disappointed and relieved at the same time.

Even so, the full moon was as wonderful as ever, and the full moon party at the desert oasis went on.

The peak of the party, it seemed, had been the eclipse after all; once it was over, things shifted into a calmer kind of party.

We sat around the fire, swapping stories about our travels and our home countries.

Everyone had a prize story or two about things the rest of us had never seen or heard of, and every one of them was exciting.

Some of them were real bombshells.
