## The Wonder of Nature

I put my suspicions about S out of my mind and kept dancing, feeling good, to the ominous music.

Before long the night began to pale and we started to make out each other's faces.

So that was the getup, and that was the face, of the person who had been dancing beside me all night.

Everyone was feeling the same thing, and we congratulated each other for having made it through the long night together, answering one another with smiles.

Then, right as the sun came up, the DJ put on a high-energy track he had been saving for that exact moment.

Party after party, I came to understand that this sunrise surge was common to all of them, a standard feature of the night.

And as the standard went, everyone took ecstasy at that point.

While it was dark you dove deep into the inner world of consciousness on the LSD high; once it grew light you turned outward and rose high on the ecstasy.

In the dark most of the tracks were psychedelic, built to play with the sounds LSD puts in your ears, and after the sun came up the music turned bright and set people dancing with smiles on their faces.

And this party was outdoors, in the middle of the jungle.

With the LSD locked in perfectly, the beauty of the natural world came rushing at my eyes.

The bamboo grove that closed us in swayed as if it were breathing in time with the rhythm.

Just as travelers of every conceivable character were merging into one through the music, in sync with each other, the trees of the forest were in sync and merged into one as well.

The beauty of it was majestic and at the same time gentle and warm, and the uncanny air the bamboo grove had carried at midnight was gone, with a mystery rising in its place, the kind fairies might come out of.

## The Green Fairy

The party went off, the music kept running for hours after sunrise, and many of the people there added more LSD and pushed their bodies to the limit.

Even so, sometime after noon the police came and the party broke up.

There was apparently some plan to collect donations and keep it going, but everyone had enjoyed themselves to their satisfaction, so it ended.

The music stopped, and my friends came back to the chai stall.

Everyone was there except R.

There was no way he had ridden his motorcycle home as wrecked as he was, and it was hard to imagine R, an experienced traveler, going paranoid and wandering off somewhere.

We were still asking each other where he could have gone when R came back, dyed green from head to toe.

Green powder and dead leaves clung to every part of his clothes.

Shuffling toward us, he was spouting something that made no sense: "Yeah, I got caught up in the war between the natural and the chemical..."

And why green, of all things?

When we finally got the story out of him, it turned out that the Guevara acid that had been through the wash, far from having lost any of its strength, was as fierce as it gets, and R had spent the whole party away on a long, outrageous voyage through his own consciousness.

On top of that, the hash joints he had smoked in quantity hit him hard, and he went down somewhere in the jungle.

Somewhere along the way he had apparently been all over the fluorescent green paint meant for the decorations, and that was how he ended up green from head to toe.

Lying there on the jungle floor where he had fallen, the chemical high of the LSD and the natural high of the cannabis combined and carried him off into a deep, deep world.

While that was going on he was bitten by ants, bitten by mosquitoes, and covered in dead leaves, caught up in the war between the natural and the chemical.

That was R's baptism into Goa.
