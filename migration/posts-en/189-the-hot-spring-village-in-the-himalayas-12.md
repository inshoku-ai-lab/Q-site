## The Party

By the time I had settled into life in Vashisht and my friends had gathered there, word came around that there was going to be a Goa trance party deep in the mountains above Old Manali, the village on the other side of the valley.

Almost every one of my friends was a party person, and chasing parties was what had brought them to Vashisht in the first place.

Manali didn't have parties as often as Goa did, so this was one we had been waiting a long time for.

We got excited and started getting ready.

Getting ready didn't amount to much, since we didn't own much to begin with — as many warm clothes as we could carry, water, food — and for us the drugs mattered more than the gear anyway.

We worked every connection we had, came up with good LSD and ecstasy, and put together plenty of charas, cannabis resin.

To put it at its most extreme, we figured that as long as we had the goods — the drugs — and water, everything else would take care of itself.

Me especially: I had just spent months in Japan doing brutal physical labor, and my body had come out of it all muscle, built like Kinnikuman, so between that and being young I was afraid of nothing at all.

I was sure I could survive with nothing but my own two hands, however deep in the mountains, however harsh the country.

## Old Manali

All of us piled into a few auto-rickshaws and headed for Old Manali.

Word of the party had already spread among the rickshaw drivers, and they drove us all the way to the mouth of the mountain trail.

There was still time before dark, so it looked like we would make the climb in daylight.

The trail eased and steepened and eased again, but it never stopped going up.

The higher we climbed, the more of the distance opened out, and it came home to us how far into the middle of the vast Himalayas we were.

Across the valley lay the village of Vashisht, and behind it the high mountains ran on in a line as far as we could see.

From inside Vashisht you couldn't see those mountains behind it at all; climbing the far side of the valley made it plain that we were living at their foot.

Vashisht sits up above the town of Manali, so in my head it had always been the village at the top of the mountain, but seen from a distance like this it was down at the feet of the great Himalayan range.

It was startling to realize what a small world I had been living in.

Going out on the road, piling up experiences, having my sense of the world rewritten — it had become a pleasure of its own.
