## The Freedom of Goa

The freedom here works to bring people's talents fully alive, and many travelers take an enormous leap in their growth as human beings just by going through Goa.

At the same time, that freedom is a double-edged sword, and it amplifies the bad side just as far.

It is not rare for someone who was already mentally unwell to begin with to go over the edge and break down completely, and people who run off into extreme cult beliefs are no rarity either.

There are far-right neo-Nazis, and there are far-left hardcore punks.

And at the same time, enlightened saints and masters of yoga and meditation are all over the place.

In short, the people who gather here are as intense as people get.

That intensity may run thicker than a concentrate distilled from the top 0.01 percent of the most intense people in New York.

Goa's society is endlessly wild like that, but precisely because everyone is far too extreme, the opposite has taken root — ground that takes everything exactly as it is — and most of the time the days pass remarkably peacefully.

People buried head to foot in tattoos and piercings, the kind who could have walked out of Fist of the North Star or Mad Max, drinking fruit juice and greeting each other with easy smiles: that bizarre scene is an everyday sight.

## Where the Intensity Comes From

If you ask why people that freakishly intense started gathering in one place, the beginning may have been LSD.

Hippies had been carrying LSD into Goa in bulk since the sixties, and the place was built up by people with the highest concentration of LSD in the blood of anyone on earth.

LSD works something like an amplifier for consciousness, so travelers who took it in quantity, innocently, just for fun, drove their already extreme characters further out toward the edge, and built a culture that took that furthest edge as the average, as plain common sense.

Out of that history, being extreme counts as good in Goa, the average and the ordinary and the sensible came to be laughed at, and the extreme culture has kept picking up speed ever since, right up to the present day.

A world running in exactly the opposite direction from Japan's don't-make-waves mentality — for me and my friends, born and raised in Japan, it was far too much of a shock.
