## Not Fitting In

H had loved the Rainbow Gathering, but the culture there, where everyone shares everything, had apparently started to make him uncomfortable, so he decided to leave.

At the Rainbow Gathering, he said, people call each other brother and sister and live like a kind of surrogate family, sharing everything from food to tools.

For H, all that extreme closeness and intimacy, and the feeling of being one body and soul with everyone, sharing it all, had gotten to be too much.

From a lone-wolf rock 'n' roller, that made complete sense.

I guess he just wasn't the type to get chummy.

## Curiosity

For me, though, just hearing about the Rainbow Gathering set my curiosity burning.

Right then, I made up my mind that someday, no matter what, I would go to one.

But you couldn't just buy a ticket somewhere; word only spread from one traveler to the next, so it apparently wasn't something you could easily come across.

I had met plenty of travelers by then, and not one of them had ever mentioned it.

Even finding the event in the first place looked hard.

## Yoga Training

H's stories didn't stop there.

His yoga training deserves a mention of its own.

His travels had taken him to Rishikesh, the holy city of yoga.

A rock 'n' roller all the way, he had fierce eyes and gave off an aura that was anything but ordinary, so he naturally attracted people of the same kind and ended up hanging out with seasoned yogis.

That was how he came to meet an older Romanian man who lived in a cave and practiced yoga.

This man wasn't just another practitioner; he was the practitioners' guru.

And the yoga he practiced and taught went by the name of tantric yoga — in other words, "sex yoga."
