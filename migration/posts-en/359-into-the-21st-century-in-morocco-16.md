## The Oasis

Old Gandalf had made such a strong impression that I hadn't paid the oasis much attention, but a pale-blue oasis shining in the middle of that dry, hard, red desert was almost too beautiful, the kind of sight that makes you feel you're looking at a miracle.

There was no sign of the island where the festival was supposed to be held.

When I asked Gandalf, he said it becomes an island if it ever rains, but most of the time it's a peninsula.

Depending on how you take the photo, he said, it can look like an island.

I asked how often it rains, and he said once every four or five years.

Rain about as often as the Olympics. This was the Sahara, all right.

It lived up perfectly to the picture of a desert we all carry around.

## The Mansion

After a while, the friend came for us by car, and we set off for the house where they were all living.

The friend was a Westerner too, and couldn't get over the way we'd suddenly turned up in the desert, but welcomed us warmly.

The house where everyone was staying was about a fifteen-minute drive away.

It was an astonishingly grand mansion, built entirely in a modern Western style.

I had been picturing a lone Moroccan-style house in the desert, so it came as a big surprise.

## The People

When we went inside, there was quite a crowd.

About eighty percent were men in their twenties, maybe twenty people in all.

I hadn't met a single Westerner since leaving London, so running into this many people in a lone house in the desert startled me, and I shrank into myself.

Everyone here seemed to be new; most of them had only just met, and some didn't even know each other's names yet.

While we hung back, Gandalf introduced us to everyone, and that put me a little more at ease.

A few people spoke to me in English, but I couldn't quite follow.

Some of them adjusted to my level of English, and somehow we managed a conversation.
