## The Bamboo Forest

We split up among the motorcycles and headed for the party.

We had gotten the location out of a taxi driver beforehand.

We were getting used to Goa's party culture, and we had stopped doing stupid things like jumping the gun and dropping LSD before we even knew where the party was, then hunting for it by ear while we were wrecked.

Experience makes you better at anything.

This party was in a bamboo grove called the Bamboo Forest.

When we arrived, dense stands of bamboo and the plants you only get in the tropics were there to greet us.

The air was many times more bewitching and mysterious than at the usual bar parties.

We had started using the word "psy" as the adjective for things that were eerie and beautiful like this.

Psy is short for psychedelic, and it refers to the state of seeing things through the hallucinations LSD gives you.

And yes — the Bamboo Forest, the site of this party, was exactly that: psy.

We were excited about going to our first outdoor party.

What kind of adventure was waiting for us — what kind of miraculous, wonderful experience was waiting for us?

## Behind the Free Parties

Every party so far, at a bar or a restaurant, had been free and open, come and go as you pleased, and this one was free too.

Goa in those days was absolutely strict about parties being free.

Organizers, DJs, and the people who came were all one crowd in their own minds, and the thinking was that taking money from your own crowd was out of the question.

Most of the organizers were hippie mafia, living the hedonist's life on the road and making their money smuggling drugs at the same time.

For men like that Goa was something close to holy ground, and putting on free parties for their crowd in Goa was a point of pride and a pleasure.

So we got our fun for free, and by way of thanks we got as wrecked as we possibly could and danced as hard as we possibly could.

Us having the craziest possible time was itself a joy to them — men who had gone wild when they were young and could not go so wild now that they were older.

## The Trip

The music was pounding through the place, a good-sized crowd had gathered, and everyone was dancing like mad.

We reined in our impatience and headed for the chai stall.

This was a much bigger party than the ones before it, and the number of chai stalls run by the local aunties was on a different scale entirely.

We spotted Japanese friends who lived over on another beach, so we staked out a spot at the same chai stall.

We ordered chai and rolled a joint.

I was itching to get up and dance right that second, but I held back and drank my chai.

Around the time the chai was finished, we took the LSD we had ready.

Last time I had taken half a tab and gone more than far enough — at the peak my body locked up and would not move — so this time I decided to halve it again and take a quarter.

The ecstasy, not a quarter but an eighth.

I thought I had cut myself back quite a lot, but even so I was taking double the LSD and half the ecstasy, and it was clearly an overdose.

My friends took the stuff they had ready at the same moment.

Taking it at the same time mattered in another sense as well — a trip is a journey, and we were making it together.

Hearts swelling at the adventure ahead of us, we headed for the dance floor.
