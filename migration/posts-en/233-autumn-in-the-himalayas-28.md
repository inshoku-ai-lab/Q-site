## The Buy

My roommate O was in Vashisht for the second time, and he knew the local farmers who grew high-quality cannabis.

O made the introduction, and we went out to the farms to hang out and size up the product.

This was a region where cannabis was the local specialty, and it lived up to that: superb quality at cheap prices, a perfect chance to buy in quantity.

They even had cream, the top grade, and the quality of it took me by surprise.

It was every bit as good as anything out of the Parvati region.

I put most of what I had saved up working in Japan into the buy, and came away with about a kilo (two pounds) of hash resin in all.

## LSD

On top of that, I managed to get a hundred tabs of LSD from a friend who had come over from Europe — far more than enough for me and my friends to have our fun with in Goa, with plenty left over to turn into money and keep me on the road.

The friend who sold it to me had already been traveling the world for years, moving drugs as he went, and he was proud of the nerve it took for me to step into that world at twenty-one, and he cheered me on.

Escaping society to live in the world of travel was a decision that demanded a certain resolve from us.

Today I know any number of ways to make money and keep traveling, but back then I could not think of a single one besides carrying drugs.

That was not ignorance, and it was not inexperience — it came from a failure to imagine what was possible.

With no life experience behind me, I could not think of anything but copying my friends.

## Crime

Needless to say, this was a serious crime, far too reckless and far too stupid, and I was proud of having chosen this road.

The way I saw it, this was a risk that had to be taken to live the way I truly wanted, and the life it bought me would be worth the resolve I had shouldered.

In fact — and I did not understand this at the time — the risk was far greater than I thought, and once caught, I would be spending several years in an Indian prison.

But at the same time, I knew exactly how corrupt Indian police officers were, and even if I were caught, handing everything I owned and every bit of money I had over to the officer would be enough to make him look the other way.

In my head, I was not staking my freedom to win a greater freedom; I was staking my possessions and my money to win it.

Young and stupid and reckless, I did not want money. What I wanted was the possibility of freedom.
