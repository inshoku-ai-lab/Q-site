## The Next Stage

The day after the full moon, we took it easy in the shade under the tarp.

That night we went to sleep as soon as it got dark and slept soundly until the sun came up, working the tiredness out of our bodies.

We had a long stretch of traveling ahead of us, so getting into good shape came first.

The next day we took down the tent, packed away the camping gear, and headed into town.

It was a fair walk to the road, but once we reached it, hitchhiking was easy: a car stopped for us right away and took us all the way into town.

## The Manager

In town, we checked into our usual hotel.

The hotel manager, too, looked surprised as he welcomed us back.

We'd turned up three weeks before the festival started and stayed at his hotel for weeks doing nothing in particular, and now here we were again, more than a month after it had ended.

On top of that, we'd been out around the festival grounds the whole time, so no wonder he was surprised.

We'd never imagined things would turn out like this, either.

As it turned out, we spent most of our time in Morocco right here in Ouarzazate, a remote town at the gateway to the Sahara.

## Hammam

People talk about washing off the grime of the road, but we were filthy on another level.

We hadn't showered in weeks, and the sand, the dust and the direct sun had dried us out to a crisp.

On top of that, we'd been living in flip-flops the whole time, so my heels had gone rock hard and cracked.

They didn't look like the feet of a twenty-two-year-old.

To loosen up bodies as shriveled as dried fish, we went to a hammam, an Arab-style sauna.

The moment we stepped inside, hot steam full of the smell of herbs came rushing at us.

The fierce heat and steam seemed to soak our dried-out bodies soft all at once.

My whole body drank in the water, and I felt as if I'd come back to life.

I was ready, it seemed, to set out on the road.
