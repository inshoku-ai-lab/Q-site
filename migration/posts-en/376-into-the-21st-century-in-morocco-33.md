## Day One of the Festival

We got to the festival grounds and set up camp, but the start had been pushed back, and we waited and waited and nothing happened.

Knowing that every structure on the site had been flattened only a few days earlier, I just waited and kept my complaints to myself.

Guests who had been to Europe's top-quality festivals were grumbling, though.

And some things really didn't match what the flyer had promised.

Drinking water, for one, was supposed to be free and unlimited, and instead it cost money.

In the desert, water is a lifeline, and when you're dancing and sweating under a hot sun, it matters several times over.

I wanted to complain about that one myself, but since I was involved in running the festival, I swallowed my grievances and got on with it.

## It Begins

After a delay of about five hours, the first day's stage finally got going.

They were so far behind schedule that the opening ceremony and the rest were cut, and the festival opened with live music.

The first act was a live band that layered percussion over techno beats, and they had the crowd fired up from the very start.

We danced too, sweating hard and loving it.

At this rate, it was going to be a good festival.

I had come to this festival as a guest, strictly speaking, but I was already in far too deep to simply enjoy it.

Whether the other guests were having a good time mattered far more to me than whether I was, and I couldn't stop checking.

As far as I could see, there were smiles everywhere, so I could breathe a little easier.

## Camp

Because of the delays, the first day wrapped up without much happening, but it promised good things for the days ahead, and we went back to our tent satisfied.

When night came and I looked up, the sky was crowded with stars.

Stars that city lights had hidden get their shine back in the middle of the desert.

The stars glittering in the night sky and the tents, each glowing with its own small light, made a beautiful picture of contrasts.
