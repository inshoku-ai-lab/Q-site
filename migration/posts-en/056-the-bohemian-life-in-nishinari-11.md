# Trainspotting

I have reacted to movies with all kinds of poison in them, but the ones that hit my view of life hardest were the sensational portraits of decadent, violent young people living in the present day.

A film called La Haine — Hatred, in its Japanese release — pushed me into cutting my hair down to a buzz close to a skinhead.

A few months later came Trainspotting, a movie in much the same vein, about the decadent daily life of shaven-headed working-class kids, and it hit me just as hard.

By the time it opened my head was already shaved, and I was pleased with myself, feeling like I had gotten out ahead of the times.

Every time a friend teased me for copying the lead in Trainspotting, I brought up La Haine and argued my case: I was ahead of the times.

My feelings for Trainspotting ran high enough that I papered a whole wall of my cramped studio apartment with a giant poster, about 1.2 by 1.7 meters (four by five and a half feet), and sat there admiring it.

The movie takes place in Edinburgh, Scotland.

Its protagonists: heroin addicts living at the bottom of society.

The lead falls into heroin as an escape from a collapsed daily life overrun with violence and crime.

To get out of the addiction he commits crimes, and the crimes fail, and the failure pulls him deeper in — a downward spiral that keeps going.

Then, trying to break out of that, he commits a bigger crime still and gets clear of the spiral by betraying his friends; it is a story about some fairly rotten human nature.

Here is the trailer. [https://youtu.be/eMC68qlZnKg](https://youtu.be/eMC68qlZnKg)

I watched it laying my own life over the life of that broken urban working-class lead.

It draws the misery of these completely wrecked characters as pop and stylish through and through, and it felt like an anthem cheering me on.

Watching the lead crawl up out of that garbage heap of an environment like a cockroach encouraged me enormously.

The music was exciting and cool as well, and the soundtrack, which seemed to sum up the British pop, rock, and techno scenes, got its own share of attention.

It hit every button I had for film and for music; I think I was always going to fall for this one.

# KIDS

Another movie in the same current that I loved was KIDS, about the daily life of American skateboarder kids.

It was directed by Larry Clark, an up-and-coming photographer, and was praised for its high artistry.

Along with that praise, its realistic depiction of the uninhibited sexuality of teenage boys and girls, and of kids sinking into drugs and alcohol, became a major target of criticism and set off a large debate; in the United States it was barred to anyone under seventeen.

The trailer. [https://youtu.be/r0J8y0-YKMQ](https://youtu.be/r0J8y0-YKMQ)

Like La Haine and Trainspotting, this one too is built around the collapsed morals of the young, and it overflows with violence.

The first time I saw it, how thoroughly it went against social morality was a shock, and it lit up everything rebellious in me.

I thought the rough, cool, defiant style of the characters looked good, and I wanted something like it for myself.

The film industry runs all kinds of brainwashing and propaganda, and I suspect these decadent youth movies I fell for also carried an intent to steer people's minds.

The frightening part is that this movie was distributed by Disney.

And not under the Disney name — they went to the trouble of setting up a separate company so the criticism would not land on Disney, and distributed it through that.

Disney, distributing a movie that stirs up the loose sexuality of teenage boys and girls... The darkness here runs deep.
