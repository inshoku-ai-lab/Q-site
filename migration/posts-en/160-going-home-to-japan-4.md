## The Flight to Japan

At last it was time to go home to Japan.

A flight from the airport in Bangkok to Kansai Airport.

I couldn't wait to eat Japanese food until I was stuffed, couldn't wait to read the latest volumes of the manga that had kept coming out while I was away.

There was a whole list of other things on my mind too — see friends from my old part-time job and tell them about my adventures in India, make music with my brother again.

But before any of that, I had to get my health back.

The diarrhea in Bangkok had been so bad that at the airport I reported myself to the quarantine desk and had them run a test.

It came to nothing much in the end — just food poisoning, apparently.

A relief, for now.

Through customs, and finally into Japan.

From the very first step onto Japanese ground, something already felt different from before.

Japan hadn't changed, of course. I had.

There was more confidence in the way I carried myself than there had been, and every step felt stronger.

## Grime

From Kansai Airport, a train and then a bus, back to the house where my mother lived.

When I got there, my mother and grandfather celebrated my safe return.

I unpacked first, then got in the bath.

The first bath in seven and a half months felt unbelievably good.

The grime of the road was far worse than I'd guessed — I washed my body before getting into the tub, and the water still went filthy all at once.

A strange oily film I had never seen before floated on the surface.

My clothes had been fairly ordinary by Indian standards, but measured against Japanese ones they apparently looked pretty shabby.

In India you wash your laundry by hand in a tub of water, and there's only so much dirt that will come out.

Even so, straight off the line it feels clean enough — it just can't compete with a machine.

The frayed seams and stains on my shirts, which had never bothered me in India, stood out next to the clothes people wore in Japan.

My mother ran my laundry for me, and the blackness of the water coming out startled her.

She had to wash it twice, she said.

## Japanese Food

My mother had my favorite waiting for me: negitoro-don, minced fatty tuna and green onion over rice.

In Goa I'd enjoyed my share of pretend Japanese food, soy sauce and all, but tuna and wasabi are things you only get in Japan.

Japanese rice soaked in wasabi and soy sauce slid down my throat.

For a while after landing in Japan, I did nothing but rest.

I went around greeting relatives, hung out with my brother, went to see friends from my old part-time job.

Japan after so long was fun, and the fear and tension I'd felt toward it while I was in India seemed like it had been worry over nothing.

I had been submerged so deep in a world out of another dimension that I'd been afraid I might never make it back to life in Japan.

But it would be a while yet before that fear took solid form.
