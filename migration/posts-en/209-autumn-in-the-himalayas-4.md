## New Friends

A new pair of Japanese guys turned up at the guesthouse.

Best friends since they were small, the two of them, and pure jock energy.

Apparently they had in fact graduated from one of those universities known for their athletic clubs.

The older one was B: sharp, good at looking after people, and genuinely grown-up as a person.

The other was F, who played little brother to B, sloppy and clingy but likable.

Jocks that they were, they were full of energy and sociable, and in no time they were on easy terms with everyone.

The flip side was that the long-stay travelers in Vashisht, who liked the place quiet, found them hard to take.

All of us went to the hot springs, went out to eat, went to the waterfall, filling every day with things to do and having a good time, until one day, there was an incident.

## The Indoor Party

The rainy season was still going, the rain starting and stopping.

I had just been thinking there was no way an outdoor trance party could happen in weather like this when word of a party came in.

Someone had rented out a whole hotel in the village of Old Manali, on the other side of the valley from Vashisht, and the party was going to be held there.

That made sense: the rain couldn't touch it, and even inside a village the noise would be less of a problem.

There would be none of the joy of dancing out in the middle of nature, which was a shame, but for a hotel with hardly any tourists in the rainy season it couldn't be a bad deal either.

It made the travelers happy, it made the hotel owner happy, and it put no burden on the locals: a win-win-win.

In high spirits we rode one auto-rickshaw after another and headed for the party venue.

Part of it would be inside the building and part of it out in the courtyard, so rain or shine we could enjoy it in the best possible conditions.

There was a cover charge, unheard of for a Goa trance party in India, but the money was going to the hotel for the use of the place, so there was no room to complain.

The organizers weren't in it to make money in the first place, so the entry fee was cheap anyway.

Hardly any Goa trance party in India was put together for money; nearly all of them came out of a pure spirit of service to the community, out of wanting to enjoy yourself and wanting your friends to enjoy themselves.

This party was made out of that same spirit of service, and it was full of a vibration of love completely unlike the parties made out of the spirit of exploitation, out of wanting to make money.
