## Quiet Days

Leh was a beautiful town, quiet and peaceful and calm.

Maybe that came from Tibetan Buddhist values, rooted in the people who lived there, or maybe from the shortage of oxygen at that high altitude, which slowed everyone down.

Or maybe a whole mix of reasons was at work. Either way, the place was unhurried.

That may have suited older travelers, or families, or couples, but for me — just turned twenty-one and roaming the world for a thrill — it was nowhere near enough.

The scenery was insanely beautiful, the weather always clear and pleasant, the town clean and orderly, the people kind and gentle in their manner, and whatever job you asked of them, they did it properly.

On top of that, Ladakhis must have a gift for cooking, because every restaurant we ate in was astonishingly good.

It was paradise, nothing but good things, but the hard-edged thrill I was after wasn't there, and the high prices in town were grinding me down.

I walked out to the temples and wandered around town, but sightseeing while pinching every rupee was not much fun, and I got bored fast.

Having traveled in Tibet already probably had something to do with it — Tibetan culture no longer excited me that much.

C, who loved the occult, was delighted, making his careful way one by one through the shops that sold Tibetan Buddhist goods and unusual crystals, while N — the Section Chief, fourteen years older than me — was at an age to enjoy a calm atmosphere, had a little money to spare, and was savoring the high quality of the quiet in Leh.

## Manali

In the end, less than a week in, I decided to head back to Manali.

Clean, tidy, comfortable, but expensive — I could not settle into a life like that.

I wanted to live somewhere cheap, in the middle of chaos, with my days full of thrills.

In Manali's quiet I had felt the chaos I wanted, and a stillness that was not the comfortable kind but something cosmic and mystical.

The others said they wanted to stay a while longer, so I decided to go back on my own, ahead of them.

Newly twenty-one, I had no interest in paying good money to sightsee in comfort.

What interested me was blending in among poor Indians, on adventures that were dangerous and thrilling and real.

I jumped on a local bus without a reservation and headed for Manali.
