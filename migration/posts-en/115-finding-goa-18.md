# A Letter to My Brother

A while after arriving in Goa, I decided this place was pretty interesting, and started wishing my brother could come out and enjoy it too.

Normally, finding India interesting as you travel through it doesn't put the idea of calling in your own brother into your head, and even if you called, he wouldn't come all the way to India.

But I wasn't normal, and a fun idea came to me, so I wrote my brother a postcard.

In the address I put nothing but the word JAPAN and a string of numbers — no city, no ward, no town.

Japan's postal codes had just gone from five digits to seven, so a letter would reach you with nothing but the street number and the apartment number.

The plan was to use that new system to invite my brother to Goa.

There was an art movement in the early twentieth century called Dadaism, and apparently its people used to write on dried squid or on leaves and send them back and forth as postcards, as a form of expression.

I was borrowing a little of that.

Here is what I wrote:

"There are orderly countries like Japan, where a postcard finds you on numbers alone, and there are countries like India, where not one single thing runs in an orderly way. Come and have an experience you've never had."

That one postcard and its short message moved my brother.

The moment he read it he told his wife, "I'm going to India for a bit" — or so the story goes.

A seriously funky brother, and I'm proud of him.

# My Brother's First Trip Abroad

He turned up in Goa astonishingly soon after I mailed the card.

His first time abroad, ever.

Picking India for a first trip abroad takes some nerve, but apparently he figured that if he didn't go while his little brother was there, the chance would never come again.

He came with his friend W.

W was married too, hair bleached a loud brown, but a working adult, more or less.

It amazes me that the two of them could take time off work that suddenly, and that their wives let them go.

I met them at the airport and had a slightly nicer hotel ready.

My brother and I hadn't grown up together, but we shared the same obsessive tastes in culture — fellow fanatics who happened to be blood relatives, and best friends.

Until a few months before, we'd been meeting once a week to make music together.

It made me very happy to see him again after so long.

# The Boombox

I love music, and yet for this trip I'd chosen to bring nothing to play it on.

Even so I couldn't hold out, and I'd bought a boombox in Nepal, and its size, its weight and its bad sound had worn me down.

So I asked my brother to buy a boombox and bring it with him.

The normal request would have been food from home, but music mattered more to me.

What he bought was a Sony, made in Japan.

About fifty centimeters (twenty inches) wide, far too big for travel, but it ran on batteries, so it worked outdoors, and it sounded good and got properly loud.

The kind of box you picture a Black hip-hop guy carrying on his shoulder as he strolls down the street.

I also had him dub about ten of the very best from my huge music collection onto cassette and bring those.

# Manga Obsessives

We set the boombox up in the room right away and put music on, loud and clean.

Living in Goa, a town made of music, I was still surprised to find how starved for music I'd been.

The songs I knew by heart soaked all the way in.

My brother and I gave each other a report on the last few months.

Mine was the story of crossing Tibet; his was a progress report on the serialized manga we both loved.

In the few months I'd been out of Japan, every one of those stories had moved a long way on.

By the time I got home a few months later, they'd have moved further still.

Travel abroad and the serials advance without you noticing, and not having to fret through the wait for each new volume was, if anything, a pleasure.

My friends in Goa were amazed at the manga-nerd conversation going on between my brother and me.

Brothers that in sync are not common, and hearing that we hadn't grown up together and had only been best friends for a few years amazed them more.
