## Setting Off

I set off fully prepared and brimming with confidence.

What felt coolest about starting a bicycle trip was walking out the front door of my family home.

The journey didn't begin at an airport. It began at my own front door.

I packed my gear into my bike bags and left the house.

In the bike shed of the public housing project, I loaded everything onto the bike, waved to my mother, and started pedaling.

You don't get a much more down-to-earth departure than that, I thought, pleased with myself.

## To Wakayama

My first destination was the port of Wakayama.

From there I'd take a ferry across to Tokushima.

The plan was to ride along the southern coast of Shikoku, the smallest of Japan's four main islands, and then cross over to Kyushu.

After that, I'd catch a ferry from Kagoshima to Okinawa. That was about as detailed as the plan got.

I had a general direction, but no fixed route.

Still, I had a good map, and as long as I had the best gear, I figured I could handle whatever came up.

## The Cold

I left in early December, so the cold was already my biggest obstacle.

I had to get to Okinawa fast, or the cold would do me in.

With that on my mind, I pushed on.

I'd left home late on the first day, so I had already given up on reaching the port of Wakayama.

I needed to find somewhere to sleep along the way.

A light rain started partway there, so I stopped to rest at a covered bus stop on a country road.

It was a bus stop deep in the mountains, with not a single person or car going by.

I ate the boxed lunch I'd brought and rested, but the drizzle kept up and I wasn't in the mood to pitch a tent, so I decided to spread out my sleeping bag right there in the bus stop and sleep.

Unrolling a sleeping bag at a bus stop half a day's ride from my family home felt strange, to say the least, but I'd set out to travel by bicycle, and I wanted to do it with real grit, so I went through with it and slept rough at the bus stop.

As I was drifting off, all kinds of thoughts ran through my head: someone might come and chase me off, or the police might show up and treat me like a vagrant. But the exhaustion from half a day of nonstop pedaling won out.

As it turned out, nothing happened at all. I slept soundly and woke before the sun came up.
