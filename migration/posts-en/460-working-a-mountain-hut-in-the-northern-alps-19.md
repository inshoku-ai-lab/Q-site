## J's Background

He put that background to use and was hired as head chef at a mountain hut from day one.

He wasn't just an excellent cook. He was smart and had good instincts, so he handled the work well, earned the president's trust, and was eventually put in charge of a hut of his own.

Getting there took a fair amount of negotiating with the president.

J apparently won him over with the argument that making full use of his talents would be good for the business of running the huts, and that was how he got to be a manager.

## What Set the Hut Apart

The president runs several huts, but at J's suggestion this small one was marketed as "the finest mountain hut, for those in the know."

J put all his talent into planning the menus, and every trick in the book went into making the food as good as it could possibly be in conditions about as limited as conditions get.

Because he pulled all of that off strictly within budget, the president liked him and apparently held him in real respect.

## Kitchen Training

As luck would have it, and it was very good luck, I was going to learn the basics of cooking directly from a manager who had mastered the heart of French cuisine.

I'd been living on my own and cooking for myself since I was eighteen, and I cooked a lot of my own meals while traveling, so I could cook well enough, but the fundamentals were something I knew nothing about.

About the only training I'd ever had was learning to hold a kitchen knife in home ec in elementary school.

J taught me one thing at a time, in order.

He didn't dump it all on me at once but went step by step, only as much as I could handle, and I'm truly grateful for that.

## The Onion

The first thing he confronted me with was an onion.

Peel this, he said.

The way a person peels an onion, apparently, tells you their level as a cook.

I did as I was told and peeled it my own way.

I cut off the top and bottom ends and peeled away the thin brown skin.

It would be flattering to call it fast.

Then J showed me how it's done.

After cutting off the top, he stuck the knife into the root end, gave it a neat twist, and took out only the hard core of the root.

Next he made one vertical slit down the body of the onion and stripped away the thin brown skin together with the first layer of onion.

The whole thing took an instant.

This, J said, is how a professional peels an onion.

Minimum time and effort, maximum yield of the best part.

And if you repeat that one simple motion over and over, you get to where you can peel onions fast without even looking at them.

It was only an onion. And yet it wasn't only an onion.
