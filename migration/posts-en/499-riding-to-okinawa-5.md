## Freedom of Movement

On an ordinary trip, you'd walk to the nearest bus stop and check the timetable, or climb into a taxi, but my way of getting around was a bicycle.

No waiting for buses, no paying for taxis.

I checked the map for my direction and, taking my time, started pedaling.

Frankly, the roads in December were pretty cold, but keep pedaling in the sunshine and within fifteen minutes you start to sweat.

Some people say touring by bike in the cold is crazy, but pedaling warms you up, so winter might actually suit a bike trip better than summer.

## Udon

After pedaling for a while, you get tired, and you get hungry.

Right on cue, an udon shop appeared in front of me.

An old-fashioned udon shop in the woods along the highway.

If I wasn't going to rest here, where was I going to rest? The setup was perfect.

When I stepped inside, a woman who seemed to be the proprietress gave me a warm welcome.

I settled my overheated body onto the raised tatami seating and ordered the udon set meal.

A run-of-the-mill choice, but probably the best one for really tasting what Shikoku does well.

Next to the table, a basket was piled high with mandarin oranges.

Beneath it was a sign: "Please help yourself."

I had never seen service that generous.

This was a different league from the free pickled ginger at Yoshinoya, the beef-bowl chain.

When you're worn out from cycling, few things hit the spot like a fresh mandarin.

While I waited for the udon set, I ate a mountain of them.

The mandarins alone may already have paid for the meal.

Right around the time I'd had my fill of mandarins, the udon set I'd been waiting for arrived.

I grabbed my chopsticks and shoveled the steaming udon into my mouth.

It really was a cut above any udon I'd eaten before.

The flavor of the noodles themselves might not have been all that different, but the chewiness and the way they went down were something else entirely.

Back when I lived alone in Osaka, there was a stretch when I lived on nothing but cheap udon ([an earlier post](/posts/on-my-own-in-osakas-flophouse-district-1-vagabond-chronicles-028)), so eating the real thing here in Shikoku, udon's home turf, meant a lot to me.
