## Baggage Check

The bus stopped at the checkpoint, and police officers came aboard.

They started at the front and worked their way back, checking the foreign travelers one by one.

They seemed to be sizing up each foreigner's attitude and deciding from that whether to check him.

Slow and openly intimidating, one of the officers worked his way back to the rear seats where we were sitting.

My friend U, with his long dreadlocks and the piercing through his jaw, stood out as a rebel, so they went for him first.

But we had disposed of anything that might look suspicious, perfectly and completely, so we were calm, and nothing about us drew suspicion.

Inside, my heart was pounding, but I steadied myself by believing, utterly, that in a situation like this there was no way they would search us that thoroughly.

In the end there was no trouble at all: the officers got off the bus, and it pulled out for Delhi.

## Delhi

When morning came, we were in Delhi.

In Vashisht the snow had been piling up on the mountains in the distance and the mornings and evenings had turned bitterly cold, but Delhi had not gotten to winter yet.

Short sleeves were fine during the day, and a single long-sleeved shirt was enough morning and night.

The air smeared with exhaust and the streets overflowing with garbage were nothing to feel good about, but not having to dread the cold was a relief.

In Delhi we stayed at a place called the Bright Guest House.

Back in Vashisht we had heard through the traveler network that there was an interesting guesthouse there.

Even though it stood right in the middle of the street where travelers gathered, a traveler's freedom was guaranteed inside, and smoking a chillum in the guesthouse was supposedly no problem at all.

The first thing that surprised me when we stepped inside was how many veteran travelers were there.

They were people you could tell at a glance had been traveling hard for years, and they carried real intensity.

You could see from the way they looked that they had stepped outside ordinary society and were living free, and it was stylish, and it was cool.

When they saw us come in with backpacks on our shoulders, they welcomed us with easy smiles.

Of course not one of them knew a thing about us, but from the moment we met they were as warm as the closest of friends.

This is one of the greatest things about the world of travelers: people who know what it is to walk into a place where they are strangers can accept a stranger walking in among them.

From the moment we arrived, the place felt as comfortable as coming home.

I had been dreading the noise of the big city, but with this, I could relax.
