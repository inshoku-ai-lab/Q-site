# To Kathmandu

After soaking the fatigue of the road out of me in the hot springs of that village on the Tibetan border, I decided to head for the capital, Kathmandu.

Nepal is a mountain country, so wherever else you might be going, you have to come through Kathmandu first or you get nowhere.

Luckily it wasn't far, and a few hours on a bus put me there.

I had heard the pollution in Kathmandu was terrible, and it was exactly that: when the bus came up onto the hill that looks down over the city, the sky above it, and only above it, was clouded with exhaust.

Nothing like the clear air of Tibet or Tatopani.

The bus pulled in at the Kathmandu bus stand.

The crush of people was exactly the kind of thing I had predicted I would find in Nepal and India.

To the locals it was an ordinary scene, and I'm sure there was an order inside the chaos, but to me all of it looked like chaos.

Cows wandering through the bus stand, touts shouting each other down, bus horns, passengers shoving to board ahead of one another, rows of food carts.

The puddles across the big terminal were chaos too — every passing bus threw up a spray of mud mixed with cow dung and piss — and so were the middle-aged women in bright traditional dress with enormous loads balanced on their heads.

All of it looked like chaos to me, but the steam rising after the rain, the press of body heat, and the smell of some strange incense made an atmosphere entirely its own, and it seemed to fuse the whole thing together.

# Freak Street

Looking for a room for the night, I skipped Thamel, where the tourists gather, and headed for Jhochhen — better known as Freak Street — where the hardcore travelers were said to collect.

My standard for finding a room was clear: the cheapest place, and nothing else.

Comfort didn't matter to me, and neither did getting around to the sights; what mattered was keeping my spending as low as possible so I could stay on the road longer.

That way I would take in more of life, I thought, and it would all feed the art I wanted to make later.

After making the rounds of a few guesthouses and haggling at each one, I got a staff room from one of them for almost nothing.

It was a narrow room on the sixth floor, the top of a building with no elevator — not easy, but cheap.

I left my pack in the room and went out to walk the city.

A few minutes away on foot was Durbar Square, lined with old temples and Buddhist statues.

The old capital was full of steam after the rain, and it gave the place a mood all its own.

The 2015 earthquake brought many of those temples down, so none of it can be seen now, but back then the beauty of the old capital was something remarkable.

Young couples and foreign travelers sat on the stone steps of the temples, drinking chai.

I followed their lead, ordered a chai at the stall across the way, and sat on the steps to drink it.

It was the first time I had been alone in a month, since meeting the exchange students on the train in Xi'an.

Here, I knew no one.

Traveling alone, drinking chai alone in a foreign landscape — there was a real charm in it.

# The Rainy Season

The bad weather in Nepal kept on.

I had no concept of a rainy season and thought it was simply raining a lot, but in Nepal the whole summer is monsoon.

Alone for the first time in a long while, and unable to go outside, I was bored.

I hadn't brought anything to kill time with on my own.

Right about then I bought a scrap of LSD paper off a shady man who struck up a conversation with me in town.

The paper had supposedly been soaked in the drug itself.

One of the aims of this life on the road was to try LSD, so I went back to my room in high spirits and took it.

Back when I was living alone in Osaka I had grown magic mushrooms myself and eaten them, so I figured I had some idea of what that kind of altered state was like.

But this LSD did nothing, no matter how long I waited.

After a while I had to accept the fact that the shady man had cheated me.

Failed on Koh Phangan, failed in Osaka, and now failed here in Kathmandu.

My curiosity about psychedelics only grew, and I swore to myself that I would never give up.
