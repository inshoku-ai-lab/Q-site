## Facing Things

At this party, every one of us was facing something.

I-chan had set her dream of working as an actress aside for the time being and come to London.

She was getting plenty out of her days here and enjoying them, but with the dream set aside she had begun to feel as if the world were leaving her behind, and she carried a restless, pent-up gloom.

Maybe enjoying this party — fun, and at the same time holding some kind of emptiness in it — was what pulled the trigger on all that looking inward.

N, the happy-go-lucky reggae lover, was simply having a good time, but D was facing something too.

What he was facing got at something essential.

It was this: maybe this party was not, in fact, the one we had been aiming for — Return to the Source, the night DJ Tsuyoshi organized.

## The Mistake

There was no mistaking that all of us were enjoying ourselves, but something felt off, and every one of us could tell that something was wrong.

D in particular had had this party in his sights since before he came to London, so it weighed on him more than on the rest of us.

The music never turned into psytrance no matter how long we waited, and the crowd did not feel quite right either.

It was fun, no mistake about that — but was this really DJ Tsuyoshi's party?

Nowhere on the ticket was the name Return to the Source printed.

This was supposed to be a properly organized big event with a history behind it, and what we had was a cheap ticket, a flimsy scrap of fluorescent paper with the price and the location printed on it.

The entry fee, as I recall, had been pretty cheap to begin with.

## D's Struggle

He was seriously beginning to think that this party was not the one we had been aiming for.

He had lost his passport, spent his time in Dhaka under what amounted to house arrest, watched the Glastonbury Festival slip away in front of him, and now, on top of all that, he had missed Return to the Source as well, the event that was supposed to make up for it.

*It's fun, but...* That was the feeling eating at him, and there was nothing to be done with it.

Everyone had paid the entry fee to get in, and each of us was seriously high; going out to look for another party in that state was not remotely possible.

From the moment we dropped the LSD, right at the start of the party, we had no choice left but to enjoy the place for all it was worth.

Whether the party turned out to be good or not, there was no choice, and we had braced ourselves for that — but it had never once crossed our minds that we might have come to the wrong party.

It was all too late by then...
