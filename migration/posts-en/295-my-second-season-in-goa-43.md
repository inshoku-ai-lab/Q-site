## Time Off

Once the unexpected week-long rebirth ritual brought on by a huge dose of LSD was over, I tried to live as quietly as I could.

It was the end of April, just before the rainy season, the temperature close to forty degrees Celsius (a hundred and four Fahrenheit), a steaming heat hanging over everything.

Most of my Japanese friends had moved on to the next place, and the only ones left in Goa were my very closest friends.

Across the whole of Goa there were hardly any travelers to be seen; those still there were not so much travelers as old hippies who had moved the base of their lives here.

## Nine Bar

There were no more outdoor parties.

Even if there had been, no one was around to come to them, so there was not much point.

Even so, the sound lunatics whose brains Goa trance had wrecked stayed on in Goa.

Where they gathered was a place called Nine Bar, which in practice felt less like a bar for drinking than a club for dancing.

It was open only during the dry season, so there was no roof to speak of and the whole place was half open-air.

Nine Bar sat on high ground looking down at the sea, with the far edge of the bar right at the lip of a cliff, and twenty meters (sixty-five feet) straight down you could see the rough waves of the Arabian Sea breaking against the rock face.

From there, dead center on the horizon, an enormous sun went down.

## An Unspoken Understanding

The music at Nine Bar started up about two hours before that spectacular sunset.

The LSD experience a few days earlier had taken me somewhere outside of time, and in Goa — a place built up by people who had been through much the same thing — the sense of time was there in name only.

For travelers, time was set not by the clock but by the sun.

As dusk came on, everyone gathered at Nine Bar, passed chillums around while watching the sun sink into the horizon, and danced to music at full blast until they reached a trance state.

Places like this in Goa were mostly in bed with the top of the police force: in return for a cut of the take handed up to them, the police stayed out of whatever went on inside the bar.

It was an unspoken understanding among the travelers, the people running the bar, the police, and the local residents.

For better or worse, every side's interests lined up beautifully, and on an exquisitely balanced castle built on sand they had raised a phantom paradise.
