# A Music Duo with My Brother

Around the same time I started living on my own, the band my brother had kept going for years broke up.

He had played bass in an experimental rock band, but differences in musical direction and each member's own circumstances had apparently made it impossible to carry on.

Around this time he seemed to want to make music that fused the techno that was steadily going mainstream with the rock he had been playing.

Having just moved out on my own, I had time and a place, and techno interested me; forming a brothers' duo with me looked fun to him, and he invited me to make music together.

Might there be music that only brothers with a peculiar upbringing and strange taste in music could make?

If we were going to do it at all, then we would push our own individuality as far as it would go and create something interesting that had never existed before.

This new game, full of possibility, set our hearts racing.

# The First Instrument I Ever Bought

In the electronic music scene of 1996, composing on a computer was only just emerging as a method, and plenty of people still composed by connecting gear like synthesizers, samplers, and sequencers with MIDI cables.

My brother owned a sequencer, a machine that automatically drives equipment such as synthesizers, which produce electronic tones, and samplers, which produce sampled sounds.

![A dim music studio desk with drum pads, a mixer, a keyboard and a laptop](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog31-1.jpg)

He also had all sorts of cables and effects units, so as long as I bought a sampler, in theory we could start making the music we wanted to make, he said.

So I decided to buy the piece of gear called a sampler, which at the time cost a full thirty thousand yen (about two hundred and eighty dollars) even secondhand.

That the first instrument I ever bought was a sampler rather than a guitar struck me as cool, as though it symbolized a new generation.

Back then I did not know the joy of playing an instrument, so I had this notion:

"A sampler is an instrument that can record and use any sound at all, so in a sense it is like getting your hands on every instrument there is."

Or so I thought.

The idea was to load the sampler with sounds I had recorded and samples lifted from all kinds of songs, then make music by having the sequencer trigger them in order.

# Composing Sessions

We decided to hold our composing sessions at my place every Tuesday night.

From my brother's place to mine was about a forty-minute drive.

On Tuesdays he would eat dinner early and come over.

Back when I was a Jehovah's Witness, Tuesday was the day I went to the meeting and studied the Bible, but that had been replaced by making music with my brother, an adherent of the Church of Subculture.

We wanted to take the sample-driven music of the acts we were huge fans of, like Cibo Matto and the Chemical Brothers, interpret it in our own way, and express it.

We began by connecting the sampler and the sequencer and simply making them produce sound, and through trial and error we got as far as attempting to compose on the sequencer.

After repeating this for several weeks, something vaguely song-like began to take shape.

But compared with what we were aiming at, it was not even close.

Having already realized the limits of what could be made with a sampler and a sequencer alone, we started considering a move to composing on a computer.

# Macintosh

As it happened, buying a "Macintosh computer" had become the fashion among my part-time coworkers, under the influence of N, whom I mentioned earlier, so I rode the wave and took the plunge.

![A vintage beige all-in-one desktop computer with a floppy drive and small screen showing a window](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog31-2.jpg)

One hundred and fifty thousand yen (about fourteen hundred dollars) in total.

It was, of course, "the biggest purchase of my life."

My older sister had once bought a home computer called an MSX for a high school class, so I had touched one, if only barely, but for all practical purposes this was my first time handling a personal computer.

It was a time when even a word like "copy-paste," everyday vocabulary now, did not exist.

Using the computer was nothing but confusion at first, but once I realized that no matter how recklessly I handled it, "resetting it puts it back the way it was," I pressed every button without fear and mastered it in no time.

A good example, I think, of succeeding by turning fear into curiosity.
