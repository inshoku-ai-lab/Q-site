## The Leather Jacket

Winter was coming on, and I was riding my bicycle to work under a cold sky.

The owner's wife at the place where I worked was worried about how poor I was and how cold it had gotten, and told me she would give me a leather jacket.

A leather jacket was not my style in the slightest, but I am not so graceless as to turn down something a person wants to give me.

The way she told it, it had originally been the owner's — her Japanese husband's — from when he was young, and he had not worn it once in decades. "It's a perfect fit on you, so I'll give it to you."

It was a leather jacket by a brand called Chrome Hearts, she said.

I had no interest in any of that, so I paid it no mind.

## Reselling

The owner had handed it over because he had no use for it, but that did not make it any more to my taste, so I never wore it and it sat in the closet gathering dust.

But now that Morocco was next, I could hardly haul a leather jacket somewhere warm, so something had to be done with it.

I had been selling off the odds and ends I picked up in London through the "Free and For Sale" section of the local listings magazine for Japanese residents, so I decided to sell the jacket there too.

Since I was never going to wear it and had no real idea what it was worth, I put it up for around three thousand yen — about twenty-five dollars.

The next day a message came in. A question: "Is it genuine?"

I answered that I thought so, and after that, nothing.

Another day, someone else got in touch, with a question: "Why is it so cheap?"

I answered that there was no particular reason, and again, nothing.

## Worth Something

Around this point I started to wonder whether this leather jacket might actually be worth a good deal of money.

So I took the listing down for the moment and decided to go to an internet café the next day to look up the price.

To my surprise, a Chrome Hearts leather jacket turned out to be the kind of thing bikers who ride Harley-Davidsons look at with envy, and apparently its trademark was the silver buttons with the cross design.

Then I looked up the list price of the model I had been given, and it floored me.

Brand new, the thing was said to go for as much as eight hundred thousand yen — about sixty-four hundred dollars.

It had never once crossed my mind that I had been handed anything that expensive, and the owner's wife surely had not known what the jacket was worth either.

If her husband ever found out about this, he might be furious.

But it had already been given to me, so it was mine.

I figured I would sell it on Yahoo Auctions or somewhere like it whenever I next made it to Japan, and decided to hang onto it.

When I set off for Morocco, the plan was to leave it at my friend N's place.
