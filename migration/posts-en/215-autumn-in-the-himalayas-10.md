## Self-Censorship

The stories in my travel writing from this period all end up being ones where I have no choice but to censor myself.

My apologies to readers of the free posts, and to anyone with no interest in drug stories, but I want to share my own experience as close to how it actually was as possible, so please bear with me a little longer.

As I've written before, a psytrance party in India is in practice a dance event run on psychedelic drugs, and legally speaking it is about as illegal as it gets.

Some countries don't regulate it at all, but in India at the time it was not permitted in the slightest, and if the police found it, the party was shut down on the spot.

But this was India, a country that accepts everything.

In a place this loose, this full of gaps, things had a way of working out.

That is exactly why the parties kept being thrown even though they were illegal, and why travelers kept coming.

Tourism grew up around the money those travelers spent, and the people earning that money had a say with the police.

That was how illegal parties went on being held, over and over, without causing much trouble.

## The Next Party

Last time I wrote about the party where F got into a fight with a sadhu and had a Hadouken fired at him, and not long after that there was another one.

This party, like the one before it, was held in the courtyard of a hotel.

Done that way, the unsettled weather of the rainy season was no problem, and nobody had to haul speakers deep into the mountains.

It suited the hotel, whose guests had thinned out with the rains; it suited the travelers, who wanted a party; and it suited the locals, who wanted the travelers to leave their money behind.

The ones who did not want an illegal drug party thrown out in the open at a hotel in the middle of town were the local police.

Cracking down on illegal things was how they saved face and did their jobs.

And if they couldn't crack down, their real feeling was probably that they wanted at least to collect a bribe.

All of those calculations, ideals, and appetites collided at this party.

And what came of it took the shape of a catastrophe.
