## R's House

R came out through the narrow wooden door of a house built the traditional way, mud walls and a stone roof.

He must have just woken up — he looked dazed.

Even so, we hugged each other and celebrated the reunion.

He offered to make tea, so I went inside to wait.

It was the middle of the day, but little light reached the room and it stayed dim.

He turned on a bare bulb to make up for it.

Apparently he was renting a single room inside a big house.

The house R had lived in back in Goa had white walls that threw the sunlight back in a glare, and it stood on its own with a wide yard, which only added to the sense of freedom there.

This house in Vashisht was cramped and dim and dirty, the exact opposite of the one in Goa.

Then again, a beach in the south in the dry season and a village deep in the mountains in the rainy season were never going to feel the same.

Still, finding a room this far out of the way was an achievement of its own.

It was a fair distance from the hot springs, from where his friends stayed, and from the restaurants.

I had figured the rent would be cheap, but the actual price surprised me even so.

R's room cost sixty yen a night — about fifty cents.

Eighteen hundred yen a month, about fifteen dollars — an astonishing number.

So I put the simple question to him: cheap is wonderful, but living somewhere this remote and this dull, what are you even traveling for?

The answer that came back surprised me even more.

## Brown Sugar

R said, "Ah, well, I've gotten hooked on brown, you know. I snort brown all day and play Wizardry on my Game Boy."

Since none of that is likely to make sense, let me go through it one piece at a time: brown is brown sugar, one stage in the processing of opium, the illegal narcotic, and middling in strength.

From the weakest on up there is raw opium, opium, brown sugar, and heroin, and with each step of refinement it hits harder, it is more addictive, and it costs more.

Snort the brown powder up your nose and every physical and psychological anxiety and discomfort vanishes, and for a few hours you can float in a happy mood without doing anything at all.

A Game Boy is a small battery-powered game console, and Wizardry is a viciously difficult 3D role-playing game made for obsessives.

So R had shut himself away alone deep in the mountains, gotten hooked on opium, and thrown himself into nothing but grinding levels in a game.

It had never crossed my mind that a friend who mattered to me would be holed up in an opium den destroying himself, and it hit me hard.
