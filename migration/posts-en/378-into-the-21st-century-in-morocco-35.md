## Janis Joplin's Kids

The woman I had rated above Janis Joplin had four children in tow.

All girls, ranging from three to eleven years old.

I don't know whether it's true or not, but they say women with strong energy tend to give birth to girls, so as to pass their strong-woman genes down to the next generation.

Looking at her, I was completely convinced.

The strong-woman genes had been passed down to the children perfectly, and the daughters were every bit as tough and wild as their mother.

They were the living embodiment of somebody's words: "It's all right if they're little rascals, as long as they grow up strong."

Each of them carried the genes of a different father, so their faces were different, and so was the color of their skin, and even their race.

## Fire Dance

Apart from the youngest, who was three, they all had two things in common: every one of them wore dreadlocks, and every one of them did fire shows as a hobby.

A seven-year-old girl in dreads setting fire to gasoline-soaked poi (a kind of juggling you see at circuses and such) and dancing as she swung them around and around was a sight with an impact like nothing else.

When she finished her act, she handed off to the nine-year-old.

The nine-year-old danced two years' worth better than the seven-year-old.

Last came the eleven-year-old's fire dance, and she was in a different league from the other two.

These girls could have set off that very day and made a living traveling the world.

## Raised Outside Society

They had never been to school; for that matter, they had never belonged to society at all.

They seemed to have grown up purely inside the world of travelers and the world of hippies.

As for studying, I doubt they had done anything beyond reading, writing, and arithmetic.

But they had already learned the things that matter in life.

Getting along with friends, cooking good food, earning money freely by doing what they loved.

Expressing their feelings freely, and expressing love.

Their powerful presence was enough to convince me that school was unnecessary.

What matters is learning how to live.

Not studying whatever somebody says matters.

They had stepped completely outside society and yet were living as happily as anyone could, and their existence succeeded in setting my mind free.
