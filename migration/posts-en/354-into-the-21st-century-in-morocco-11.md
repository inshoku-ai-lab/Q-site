## The Manager

Money was truly tight, so a long stay at the hotel was never part of the plan.

We'd bought a tent for just this moment, and the idea was to go somewhere outside town and camp.

I hadn't camped since a summer vacation trip with family and relatives back in elementary school, but surely it was something we could manage if we tried.

In Morocco you can almost never get by in English, though at the key tourist spots you can.

In this case, that meant the hotel manager.

We had only stayed two days, but he had already welcomed us warmly, and we'd become friends.

I told him about the festival starting in three weeks, explained that we were thinking of camping somewhere until it began, and asked him to let us know if he knew a good place.

## An Offer

When we said we wanted to camp, the manager's eyes went wide.

Foreign tourists might camp at an oasis in the Sahara, but no foreigner ever lived in a tent on the edge of town for weeks just to save on living costs, and he seemed to be in something like culture shock.

By the standards of a Muslim country, a woman living out in a tent on the edge of town was unthinkable.

He was genuinely worried about our safety.

The night I asked him, once he was done with his work as manager, he came to the room we were staying in and put an offer to us.

The room was about 800 yen a night, roughly six and a half dollars, but if we paid for the whole stay in one go, he would knock it down to about 500 yen, around four dollars, so why not skip the camping and stay at his hotel?

It was a steep discount, the kind of offer most people would gladly take, but we were different.

We simply didn't have the money to pay, and we were curious about camping.
