## Tickets

Preparations for Morocco were coming along steadily.

This would be the first time I had traveled within Europe, and I had been dreading the fares, but when I looked into tickets, I was amazed at how cheap they were.

The internet café I usually went to, easyInternet, ran a budget airline called easyJet, and flying around Europe on easyJet, you could travel for prices that were hard to believe.

A one-way ticket from London to Madrid in Spain was about 7,000 yen — roughly fifty-six dollars — an absurdly low price by the standards of 2000.

On top of that, I booked an express bus from Madrid down to Algeciras, the southernmost town in Spain, and that came to about 3,500 yen, around twenty-eight dollars.

I hadn't booked it, but the ferry from Algeciras in Spain to Tangier in Morocco was also about 3,500 yen, which by my math meant I could get all the way to Morocco for about 14,000 yen one way — around a hundred and twelve dollars.

Traveling in a Third World country like Morocco doesn't take much money, but I had assumed that getting there by way of Europe would cost a lot in fares, so I was very happily wrong.

## Getting Ready

Not that there was much to get ready: we had come to London as travelers, unpacked our backpacks, and started our lives here, so all we had to do was put everything back in.

We had been living on very little, so we hadn't bought much over the past six months, and we were traveling light.

I cleared out the small household odds and ends, selling some and giving the rest away through the listings magazine.

After Morocco, the plan was to come back to London, live here again, and focus on making music.

And even if I changed my mind and ended up going somewhere else, I intended to come back to London at least once, so rather than get rid of anything I might need again, I decided to leave it at N's place.

That way, getting ready for the trip took less than a week.
