## The Hut

We met up at the hotel, all piled into a shared taxi, and headed out to the hut at the oasis.

For now, we were the first ones there; more people would join us over time, and in the end, apparently, about ten of us would be living together.

I had no idea what kind of life was waiting for us.

The hut that would be our home base was about five meters by ten (16 by 33 feet).

It was apparently built of packed earth, and it kept us safe from the fierce daytime sun, the cold of the night, and the desert sandstorms.

There were no windows or anything like that, so it was really just a shelter, but I was grateful for anything that protected us from the harsh desert climate.

We dropped our bags inside the hut for the time being and went off to explore the area.

## The Oasis

This spot was upstream from the lake where the festival had been, a place where a little stream from the distant mountains came seeping out of the ground.

The stream flowed into the lake about two kilometers (a mile and a quarter) away.

In my mind, an oasis had always been a green paradise, thick with palm trees, in the middle of a desert of drifting, pure white sand.

But the oasis where we were going to stay was more like a handful of palm trees clinging to life on a spring that seeped out of a hard, red-earth wasteland, nothing like the oasis teeming with life I had pictured.

Even so, just having water in the middle of the desert was an enormous joy.

Farther up the stream was the spot where the water welled up out of the ground.

I scooped some up in my hands and drank.

The moment it touched my tongue, a jolt ran through me.

What was this?

It was way too good!

Minerals from the mountains had dissolved into the water, giving it a faint saltiness and a smooth, mellow taste full of minerals.

We took the water's off-the-charts taste as the best possible sign.
