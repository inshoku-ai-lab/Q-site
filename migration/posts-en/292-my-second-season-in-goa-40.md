## Negotiation

Y was talking to the officers and the DJs, feeling out whether there was any way to keep the party going.

A year earlier in Goa the police had been loose, and at a small party you could hand the officer who came around on patrol a bribe of about 3,000 yen — some twenty-five dollars — and keep going all night.

But this year it had all gone strict at once, and we were in a hard world where no sound could be made after ten at night.

Even so, bribes still carried the day, which is India for better or worse: whatever the situation, it can be worked out if you negotiate.

Y understood that perfectly, and as a smooth talker from the film world, he latched on and would not let go.

For the officers, breaking the law meant penalties waiting for them, so they would not hand out permission easily.

In the end the deal came together: a bribe of about 100,000 yen — around eight hundred dollars — to keep the party going for the night.

That was something like two hundred times the starting pay of a rank-and-file Goa policeman, and even split among several of them, a serious amount was going into their pockets.

Put it the other way around, and it means the law this year had gotten strict enough that nothing short of a bribe that size was worth their while.

## Sense of Money

Y's head was already gone on LSD, and his thinking had run off somewhere far too extreme.

Today's party was too magnificent to let the day end, he reckoned, and he was ready to put up 100,000 yen out of his own travel money.

100,000 yen was enough for us to live in Goa for three months and cut loose the whole time.

Before coming to Goa, Y had made easy money smuggling hash into Japan, so to him money was there for anyone willing to take the risk.

That, I think, is exactly why he wanted to stake everything on this one moment.

But the rest of our friends, wrecked on LSD as they were, still had their eyes on reality.

Burning 100,000 yen on a single night's party was absurd, they told him, and they talked him around until he agreed and let it go.

And so the curtain came down on our landmark experience, out past every dimension.
