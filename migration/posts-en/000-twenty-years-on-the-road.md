# Nice to Meet You

Hello. My name is Qryptraveller.

I'm a troubadour, and I've been a nomad for twenty-odd years. The closest thing I have to a teacher is Snufkin — the wanderer from the Moomin books, who travels light and leaves town every autumn without telling anyone.

You probably won't believe this, but I really have been moving from country to country, without stopping, for more than twenty years.

Which probably has you thinking I came from money.

That's the usual guess, isn't it? Nobody spends a lifetime roaming the world and living for the fun of it unless there's money behind them somewhere, or a comfortable upbringing behind the money.

Here's the truth. I was born poor in Japan and raised by my mother on her own. No education, no credentials, no steady job, no savings, nothing to my name. To this day I'm not on the resident registry — the government's list of who lives where, which every Japanese adult is supposed to be on — which means no health insurance card and no driver's license. No wife and no children either. (That was where things stood in early 2020, when I started writing this. Life is steadier now.)

What I had when I set out, still a teenager, was a little money saved, a small backpack, curiosity about to boil over, no sense of danger left at all — curiosity had burned it clean out of me — and a raw thirst for freedom. That was the whole inventory.

At the start I couldn't speak a word of English. I knew nothing useful, had done nothing, had never belonged anywhere. I made all of it up as I went, and I floundered, and it hurt. But I couldn't say no to my own curiosity, so I kept doing what I could, where I was, the way I wanted to. It turned into over two decades of going wherever I please, and I still haven't stopped.

More than half my life has gone into this, and I've lost count of the wild and funny things that have happened along the way.

I'd stack it up against most movies.

- Raised by my mother in a poor, strict Jehovah's Witness household
- A quiet kid at school. The one who got picked on
- At seventeen, sick of all of it, walked out of the religion, out of school, out of Japanese society, and went to Peru
- From age nineteen, on the road without a break
- At twenty, in Goa on India's west coast, had my eyes opened to the world of the hippie nomads
- Lived alone and naked on an uninhabited island at the far edge of Japan
- At twenty-five, got rid of everything I couldn't carry on my back and gave my life to the road
- Opened small Japanese restaurants in cities all over the world
- Nearly died of tuberculosis; four months in an isolation ward in Israel
- Traveled like a king for six months with nothing in my pockets
- Became one with the universe in Jerusalem
- Designed organic hemp clothing and sold it at festivals
- Someone I knew conned me into taking on two million yen in bank debt — about eighteen thousand dollars — and clawed my way out
- Accepted bitcoin at my own online shop in 2011
- Led sound-healing workshops at psytrance festivals in country after country
- Rode a bicycle around the world for two and a half years
- Put together a twenty-six-person bicycle caravan of broke hippies and toured Central America performing circus shows
- Founded a hippie community with friends in the Costa Rican jungle and lived there naked
- Busked my way from country to country on the handpan and wound up on Romanian television
- Bought crypto, made a fortune, lost all of it, went right on investing
- Ran a Twitter account dragging the world's dark corners into the light, grew it past twenty thousand followers, then got suspended and throttled over and over
- When the Covid lockdowns killed street performance, started sourcing silver for buyers on commission — it went far better than expected, and it's what pays for this writing

And that's just the tip of the iceberg. I have more stories than I could tell in a lifetime.

I'll set them down here, one at a time.

- **I want you to know that a person like me is allowed to exist.**
- **I want to prove that you can spend your whole life on the move and living for the fun of it with no money, no knowledge, no experience, no schooling, and nobody behind you.**
- **I want you to see that we are free, all of us, and that whatever we can picture, we can make real.**

That's what drives me to write this.

What I've lived through is so far outside the ordinary that I think humanity gains something just from knowing it happened. So I'm setting my own life down in the open, out of what feels close to duty.

I hope it becomes one of the small pleasures of your day.
