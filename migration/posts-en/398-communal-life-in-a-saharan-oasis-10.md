## Meals

Once everyone had started getting up, someone would make breakfast.

There was no rotation or anything like that — whoever got hungry first would cook.

So if nobody felt like it, breakfast sometimes turned into lunch, but nobody was racing the clock, so it wasn't much of a problem.

In principle, everyone was responsible for whatever work needed doing, but the cooking usually fell to Y or O, the two who were confident in the kitchen.

The rest of us pitched in by washing the dishes after meals, cleaning up, or going into town to buy food.

Gandalf paid for the food and served as a kind of teacher to all of us.

## The Garden

As a group, we had no clear idea of what we wanted to do, but C started a garden next to the hut.

It's true — how wonderful it would be to grow our own food instead of just spending money to get by from one day to the next.

C tilled a patch of ground about two meters (six and a half feet) square, and we tried planting carrot tops, fruit seeds, and whatever else, but by the next day they had dried to a crisp, instant mummies.

Imagine trying to make a garden in a desert of red rubble under blazing, direct sunlight.

Ground baked rock-hard, where nothing lived.

Well, to put it bluntly, making a garden in a place like that is impossible.

C's ambition, far too reckless and without a hope of coming true, was dead within a few days.

## C

C was a French traveler who worked on a farm back in France and lived close to nature, but he wasn't a crop man — he was a livestock man.

He had spent much of his life living with animals.

He worked on the ranch in summer and traveled in winter.

This winter, he had chosen to see in the turn of the century at a festival in Morocco.

The people who come to psytrance and Goa trance festivals are usually futuristic, cutting-edge cultural types, but C was their complete opposite, a thoroughly simple country man.

That was part of why Gandalf had taken a liking to him and invited him to join our communal life here in the desert.
