## The Outdoor Party

The first one to get wrecked was R.

Having taken that slightly damp scrap of paper — the LSD he had run through the wash in his pants pocket, wrapped in plastic — he was startled by how much harder it hit than he had expected.

The paper was damp, but the compound in it was still intact, and even after the wash it came on at its usual full strength.

What he had taken, though, was Guevara, the brand printed with Che Guevara's portrait, the same brand as the LSD that had set off the revolution in my own head, and it was very strong stuff.

R's easygoing oddball streak came out even stronger than usual, and he muttered away at something that was half joke nobody could follow, half conversation with himself, until after a while he got up and walked off toward the dance floor.

The LSD was coming on for me too by then, so I headed for the dance floor.

The floor was full of travelers I had never seen before, eerie and oddball, writhing as they danced.

Over the past few days the number of travelers, especially the ones who came for the parties, seemed to have doubled.

I found out later that a lot of the seasoned Goa freaks never go to the bar parties at all — outdoor parties only.

Every getup imaginable was there, individuality spilling over on all sides, the characters drawn so sharply it was like standing inside a manga or a movie.

Guys who could have walked out of the manga Fist of the North Star or the Mad Max movies — tattooed head to foot, pierced head to foot, mohawks and dreadlocks, hard-looking and stylish at once — were dancing like maniacs with smiles on their hard faces.

There were also plenty of back-to-nature types, young and old, men and women, dressed like Snufkin out of the Moomin books or like sixties old-school hippies.

Some of them had gone back to nature so thoroughly that they were dressed like homeless men.

And of course, real homeless men like F were taking part too.

There were real adventurers as well, travelers dressed like mountaineers or like cowboys.

There were plenty who looked like big-city techno freaks, in tight clothes and sharp sunglasses with the corners swept up.

And plenty of the seriously psychedelic ones, people who seemed to have staked their whole lives on Goa trance, in fractal-patterned clothes that lit up under the black light.

There was even a blonde mother with her child in tow, in a white dress and high heels and bright red lipstick.

Every soul there was expressing its own individuality at full power, and together they made a chaos that belonged to another dimension.

The interesting part was that none of it was costume, worn for the night — that was simply how all of them dressed every day.

The Fist of the North Star villain types really did ride motorcycles that looked like they came out of the manga, the hippie types really were camped out living in the jungle, the adventurers really had come here by way of some enormous adventure, and the techno people really did live in luxury apartments.

And all these ultimate individuals, every one of them wrecked on LSD, were dancing to the same rhythm.

Overwhelmingly disparate as these personalities were, there was a warmth to the place that accepted each other's craziness, and everyone danced as one.

Everyone was on LSD, so nobody knew what crazy thing they themselves might do.

That was exactly why there was ground here for accepting whatever crazy thing anyone did, and why, because you knew you would be accepted, there was ground here where you could get wrecked without worry.

Here, whether you were so far gone that your eyes rolled back and you danced with a strange look on your face, or you went back to nature and rolled around in the mud, or you were moved to tears by something the LSD had shown you, everyone would let it go with an "Ah, yeah, that happens. Classic Goa."

That atmosphere — those hard getups and yet a warmth and gentleness underneath — drew everyone into a world deeper still.
