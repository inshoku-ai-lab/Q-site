## The Next Day

The next day I went into town, pulled about 30,000 yen in cash from an ATM — around 240 dollars — hid it in the hotel, and went to meet the waiter empty-handed.

Just in case: if anyone jumped us, there would be nothing to take.

A different young guy was waiting at the restaurant, and we were told to go with him.

We followed him through the village of Chaouen for about ten minutes.

He led us to a perfectly ordinary house, its walls a beautiful blue like all the others.

## The Old Guy

Inside, a small man of about fifty, tanned dark by the sun, sat enthroned, and he greeted us in English with a "Welcome."

He spoke only broken English, but compared to French or Moroccan, neither of which we spoke a word of, it was an enormous relief.

Back then my own English was broken too, so my partner, I-chan, took on the negotiating in English that was a notch above broken.

He had a gentle air about him and didn't look like the type to cheat us, but I stayed on my guard all the same.

## The Deal

Without wasting any time, he brought his prized hash out from a shelf in the back.

The hash you can get in other countries is generally compressed rock-hard by machine to keep down the costs of exporting and importing it.

What he showed us, though, was nothing more than cannabis pollen pressed together by hand, and it crumbled apart when you touched it.

Looking pleased, he gestured for me to smell it, and when I held it close, an indescribably fragrant cannabis aroma shot straight up my nose.

One sniff, and my face broke into a grin.

This was top-grade stuff, no question.

He and I looked at each other and smiled.

It seemed we had connected, one cannabis lover to another.

When I actually smoked some, the smell of fresh cannabis pollen was wonderful, and the high was exquisite — it lifted me up while still having real depth.

There was no going wrong with this.
