# The Day of the Full Moon

The day finally came.

We had come all this way for that day's Full Moon Party.

An experience I knew nothing about was waiting for me, and my heart was pounding.

At the party I was planning to try some kind of hallucinogen.

My curiosity was ready to burst.

A few days before, at a campfire, I had smoked cannabis for the first time — mixed with tobacco and rolled up in paper — but probably because I'm not a smoker, I never quite got the hang of it, and it barely did anything.

This time I had a cannabis cake, bought from someone who worked at a restaurant. Eaten straight, it would work even if I had no idea how to smoke.

On top of that, I had gotten an ecstasy tablet from a traveler staying at the guesthouse next door.

According to something I'd read in a magazine, there are no hallucinations with it, but you get incredibly happy and want to keep dancing forever.

It was the day of the full moon, and the whole guesthouse was buzzing more than usual.

# The Cannabis Cake

At dusk, I split the cake with A-kun, who had come along with me, to get ready for the party.

It apparently took a while to kick in, so the idea was to eat early and be ready in time.

Our beach was at the north end of the island, and the party was in the south, a fair distance away.

Sharing a taxi would make it cheap, so we arranged for a big one.

We had been driving through the jungle for a while when I began to feel sick.

It got so bad that I had them stop the taxi.

The moment I stepped out, the nausea got away from me and I threw up at the side of the road.

My head was spinning.

The nausea wasn't carsickness, it seemed — the cake was simply too strong.

The nausea would not stop. I sat at the edge of the road for a while and waited for it to pass, but it showed no sign of passing.

The taxi waited nearly twenty minutes, but there were other passengers, and it couldn't wait forever.

But if I got back in the car now, the nausea would finish me off, so that wasn't an option either.

I talked it over with my friends, and we decided to leave me there and let the taxi go on ahead.

# Wandering

Lying down at the side of the road would look far too suspicious, so I went to find somewhere to rest.

It would have been better if the taxi had stopped near the beach, but it had stopped on a hilltop partway along the mountain road. To reach the beach I would have to go about three hundred meters (a thousand feet) down a steep mountain trail.

In the state I was in, going down that trail wasn't going to happen.

There was a house up on the hill, so I knocked, thinking I could ask to lie down on the balcony for a while.

Knocking on a stranger's door without hesitation may be a talent I owe to growing up in a Jehovah's Witness household.

A young couple came out and spoke to me in English, but I couldn't speak a word of it.

The conversation went nowhere, and all I had become was some creepy stranger making no sense at their door, with no way to explain myself. So I gave up and went to rest on a mountain path off the side of the road.

The dizziness kept on, but the nausea had eased off a little.

Even so, moving was still out of the question, so I lay down right there on the ground.

The gravel dug into me, but at least nobody was bothering me and I wasn't bothering anybody.

The night wind was a little cold, though not more than I could stand.

Lying there and looking up at the sky, I saw the full moon, blazing.

Unbelievable...

I had come all the way from Japan to this island for this one day, and here I was, keeled over on the way to the party.

And the nausea still hadn't stopped.

Not speaking any English, I had no idea how to get to the party, and no clear idea how to get back to the guesthouse either.

For all the beauty of the moon overhead and the moonlit sea spread out below me, I was pretty miserable.
