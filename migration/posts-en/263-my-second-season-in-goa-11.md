## New Year's

After Christmas comes the New Year's party.

I couldn't quite get myself up to peak condition, but I was trying to enjoy it for all I was worth.

At last year's party I had danced eighteen hours straight and brought in the New Year in the best way possible, but this year, with my mood refusing to lift, I had no confidence I could get that high again.

Y still had no inspiration for making the film.

The main reason was that Goa had become less interesting than the year before, but there was another one: he was afraid that showing a secret paradise like Goa to the world would destroy it.

Since the sixties this land had been a paradise of freedom that the old-time hippies had nurtured with their own hands, and to him, turning it into a film felt like an act of betrayal — like writing a tell-all about your own family and making money off it.

Feelings like that shackled him too, and he couldn't push through to the inspiration.

Y was in that kind of slump, but he was muscling his way over the challenge with the powerful positive thinking that came naturally to him, and the sheer thickness of his life experience.

What mattered to him was tearing through the peak of his life at full speed, and shooting a film was one of the ways to do it.

But as long as the inspiration wouldn't come, his way over the slump was to enjoy every last bit of his time in Goa.

## The Party

True to form, we went into the party thoroughly and perfectly prepared.

For us a Goa trance party was by then a religious rite, and if someone called us a cult there was no way to deny it.

Carrying some kind of dissatisfaction and unease deep down, we were trying to sublimate all of it by running full speed in an extreme direction.

That meant getting utterly high at the party and enjoying it more completely than anyone else.

Do that, and you are set free from every worry and can reach the state of "this is fine as it is."

And for Y, the way to get there was to take LSD past the limit of what he could hold.
