## A Massive Party

I finish the joint, bite off the smallest sliver of an ecstasy tablet, and wash the bitterness down with chai.

A piece of LSD about eight millimeters (a third of an inch) across is already too small to divide into eighths by hand, so I had cut mine up at home with scissors beforehand.

I take one of those slivers and head for the dance floor.

An unbelievable number of people are packed in tight, dancing like mad.

I'm keyed up as high as I go, so I push through the crowd to the front of the speakers.

The spot right in front of the speakers, where you can dance in the full blast of the volume, is fought over — everybody wants to be there.

Right there, a white guy with dreadlocks, maybe a hundred and ninety centimeters (six foot three) tall, is dancing at twice the speed of the already fast Goa trance.

I have no idea what's what any more, but the force coming off him is beyond anything sane, and he shines with a fierce charisma.

Around him a crowd has gathered, dancing with the same whole-body, whole-soul force, resonating with that ferocious momentum of his.

Speakers so huge that nothing I have ever seen compares are pouring out a roar that shakes the ground.

What strikes me is the hem of my shorts, trembling in answer to the bass.

Enormous sound hurls enormous energy at us.

Every single person answers that enormous energy from the music and hurls enormous energy back through the dance.

The last party had its own magnificent frenzy, but this one goes further still.

I could never have imagined that a frenzy like this existed in the world, still less that I would be standing in the middle of it.

Only six months ago I was a film nerd working the overnight shift at Tsutaya, the video rental chain, and now here I am dancing like a lunatic in the dead center of a crowd of crazy travelers gathered from all over the world.

Dancing like that to the music was what released the shackles I had kept on myself for years.

My body had been fitted into a frame without my knowing it and had only ever moved within a limited range, and through that explosive dancing its limits were broken open.

That in turn led to the release of the heart, and then of the mind, and then of the soul.

## Runner's High

What I discovered at this party was that an eighth is the right dose of LSD for me.

At that dose the speed of my consciousness never outran the speed of my body, and I danced on in the best condition possible.

At earlier parties I had never danced the whole night through — somewhere in the middle my body would seize up for an hour or two — but this time I danced from the start without a break.

Doing that added runner's high, the high that comes from keeping up hard exercise for hours, and I reached a high on a completely different order from anything before.

It was the greatest high and the greatest joy of my entire twenty years of life.

It was joy at a concentration something like a hundred times the strongest high I had ever felt in the life I had before I went traveling, joy with enough force to rewrite my own existence from the ground up.

Joy so fierce that the shine of this one moment was too bright, and my whole past life faded behind it.

Only about two weeks earlier I had looked into the abyss of the darkness in my life, felt like I had been born again, and shaved my head.

And now, two weeks after that, I was feeling the greatest joy of my life.

The greatest darkness and the greatest joy of a life, both inside two weeks.

The place where something that outrageous could happen was right here: Goa, in India.
