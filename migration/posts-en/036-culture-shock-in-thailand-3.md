# Sri Thanu

The ferry reached the port, and we switched to a taxi.

Our destination was a district called Sri Thanu, the middle of nowhere, with nothing but jungle and beach.

Rather than the middle of nowhere, it might be more accurate to call it nature in the raw.

Along the beach stood a few guesthouses that doubled as restaurants, each with several bungalows built on its grounds.

Apart from a single small general store there was nothing at all, which made it the perfect place to enjoy a beach on a southern island.

Nothing like a house or an inn in Japan, the bungalows lined up under the trees along the beach were better described as shacks or storage sheds.

They could be locked, technically, though there was not much point.

Worrying about thieves seemed entirely unnecessary.

At roughly three hundred yen (about three dollars) a night, they drew travelers from all over the world who wanted to settle in for a long stay and take it easy.

A few days out of Japan, and already I had arrived at the very center of the travelers' world.

# The Sea

Both the bungalows and the restaurant were built rough and plain, with nothing beyond the bare minimum.

There were no possessions, but everything else was there in abundance.

From the bungalow to the beach was about twenty meters (sixty-five feet).

Pure white sand and deep blue sky.

![Turquoise sea washing onto pale sand, weathered driftwood in the foreground and a green headland behind](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog36-1.jpg)

Even in March it was already midsummer, and the seawater was warm.

Dropping our bags in the bungalow, we changed into swim trunks and threw ourselves into the sea.

Clear water, bursting spray, sunlight pouring down.

A sense of exhilaration I had never felt before ran through my whole body.

Freedom, release, nature.

For a movie geek with a warped heart who had come from the exhaust-hazed center of Osaka in winter, it was a firsthand experience far too shocking, far too bright.

# Beach Life

Days were spent swaying in a hammock with a book, swimming in the sea when it got hot, going to the restaurant to eat when I got hungry.

According to the veteran traveler I-san, guesthouses like this keep room rates low and make their money at the restaurant, so eating inside the guesthouse rather than in town is a way of supporting them.

Either way, the place sat too far into the island's interior for a trip into town to be easy, so eating at the guesthouse was unavoidable — the catch being that the restaurant had only one employee, and on top of that he was frequently away.

Hungry, with nobody around and no idea what to do, I watched my friend T stride straight into the restaurant and start making himself fried rice without asking anyone.

According to T, there was no problem so long as you paid the menu price afterward.

![Chopped vegetables frying in a large steel wok on an outdoor stove, tongs resting on the rim](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog36-2.jpg)

He cooked it deftly and ate it with obvious relish.

The values on display here were quite different from what I had grown up with as common sense in Japan.

The casual ease of the more seasoned travelers came as a shock, but since imitating it was clearly beyond me, I waited for the employee to come back and ordered.

# The Head-Shaving Ceremony

Having come all this way to somewhere warm, I decided to shave my buzz cut, which had started to grow out, back down to bare skin.

Handing scissors and a safety razor to A, who had come along with us, I had him perform the head-shaving ceremony.

Shaving another person's head, though, may have been too much for an amateur.

Partway through he botched it and cut my scalp, and the ceremony was suspended.

Some of the hair was short, some still long, some patches shaved clean and some bleeding.

Blood seeped from the scalp showing between the mottled patches of hair, and I ended up looking like a monster.

My friends were howling with laughter, but for me it was no laughing matter.

With nothing to be done until the wound healed, I left it alone and went swimming in the sea, and this time the scalp on the closely cut sections caught the direct sun, burned, and swelled up red.

A few days later the skin began to peel, and the monster look kept getting worse.

The skin visible between those mottled patches was red and swollen, flaking off, in a state you could not bear to look at.

Since a haircut was impossible until the skin finished peeling, even the friends who had been laughing at first decided this was not a look fit for public view, and so I had no choice but to spend my days with a towel wrapped around my head.
