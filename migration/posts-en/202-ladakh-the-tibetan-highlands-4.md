## The Road Gave Way

The traffic, stalled by the bad road, slowly began to move again.

The jam came from having to detour around a section of road blocked by fallen rock.

Luckily the road ran along a river rather than across a mountainside, so we could pull off toward the water and keep moving.

Once we were past the rockslide, the traffic flowed smoothly.

## Spectacular Country

The bus drove on through staggering scenery.

The mountainsides were bare pale-brown earth, asserting the dignity of the land.

The sun blazed down on it, with no air to block the light.

The altitude was high, the air thin and dry as well, so the sunlight came in sharp and the blue of the sky was unreal.

A blue so deep it looked almost navy.

The world was split in two: the deep blue of the sky and the pale brown of the earth.

Put simply, look up and it was blue, look down and it was pale brown.

That view went on for hours on end.

Things close by slid past fast, things far off moved slowly.

The line of mountains far in the distance seemed not to move at all.

Even so, after a few hours the landscape did gradually change.

What came up in front of that line of mountains was an enormous lake that mirrored the blue of the sky exactly and threw back a blue more beautiful than any I had ever seen.

The country all around was high desert, so there was probably little life in the water, and it was clear beyond belief, clear right down to the bottom.

## Behind Schedule

We were supposed to reach Leh, the largest city in Ladakh, that night, but the morning's traffic jam meant another night on the road.

This place too, like the night before, sat in the dead middle of the desert plateau, with no buildings anywhere around.

A full sky of stars, thanks to air that thin and that dry.

So many stars that I could no longer pick out the Big Dipper or Orion.

Wrapped in a thick quilt like the one from the night before, heavy and a little smelly, I got through another night on the desert plateau.

The next day, a while after the bus set off, another beautiful lake came into view, and beside it I could see someone walking.

Maybe we were getting closer to a town, but there was still no hint of a building or a village around us, and seeing someone just walking there, in a world with no civilization for tens of kilometers (dozens of miles) in any direction, struck me with a strange sense of having slipped into another dimension.

Where on earth had he come from, and where was he going?

He was a guy in his fifties or so, traveling light, one small bag in his hand.

Hard to believe this was Himalayan mountain country at 4,000 meters (13,000 feet), with not one building for tens of kilometers in any direction.

He was walking the road by the lake as if it were any other day.
