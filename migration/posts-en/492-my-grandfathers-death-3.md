## One Blow After Another

The news of my uncle's suicide, my grandfather's death, and the breakup with I-chan all came at the same time.

All of it happened within a single month.

My mind kept circling back to I-chan, and whatever room was left went to my grandfather's death and my uncle's suicide.

If they had come one at a time, they would have been easier to bear.

But coming all at once, they knocked me flat.

## A Reason

I wanted someone to blame, someone to take my anger out on, but there was nowhere to aim it.

Still, I needed a reason to be this low, so I made a target of the lie I-chan had told me about why she was leaving.

I needed something I couldn't forgive, something I could condemn absolutely.

I wanted a target that would justify how miserable I felt.

Now I can see that it was the best choice she could make after agonizing over it, but at twenty-three, I couldn't accept it.

## The Funeral

It was in that rock-bottom state that my grandfather's funeral was held.

My mother's apartment in the public housing project was right across from the community center, so arranging the funeral was easy.

We got everything ready going back and forth between two places less than twenty meters (sixty-five feet) apart.

My grandfather used to sweep the neighborhood when he was alive, so the neighbors thought well of him, and many of them came to the funeral.

But no one seems to have come from far away.

For that matter, I doubt my mother and the others even contacted his old acquaintances.

The one person who did come from far away was someone I never expected.

"Far away" meant about twenty minutes by car, and the person who showed up was my father.

My brother had probably let him know.

In general, even after a divorce, it isn't all that unusual for a man to show up at his ex-wife's father's funeral.

But in my family, my mother rejected my father absolutely, and I couldn't imagine him showing his face in front of her.

My father and my grandfather had both grown up in a doya district in Osaka, so they had known each other well from the start.

Later my grandfather became his father-in-law, and he was the grandfather of my father's own children, so of course my father would come to his funeral.

But nearly twenty years after the divorce, my mother apparently still hadn't come to terms with it, and she kept turning her back on him.

I was amazed at how stubborn the people in my family could be.

It showed me just how deep family drama can run.
