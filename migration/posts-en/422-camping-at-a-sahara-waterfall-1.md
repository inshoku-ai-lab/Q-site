## The Waterfall

With the truck gone, it was our turn to do some traveling of our own.

We each shouldered our backpacks, along with the food left over from our communal life, and set off on foot toward a waterfall near the lake.

After about thirty minutes of walking, we reached the falls.

Waterfalls come in all kinds, and this one was a feeble thing — a stream dribbling down a rock face about three meters (ten feet) high.

A waterfall with a lot of water has its own kind of beauty, and a waterfall with very little water has its own kind of beauty.

This one stood in the middle of the desert, which gave it a rare kind of beauty.

The water ran into a large lake, but everything around the falls was bone-dry.

So the water trickling down the rock and spreading its moisture over a wide area was the only source of life for the plants and animals around it.

## The Water

It was the same water that flowed through the stream near where we had been living together, with a slight trace of salt and a lot of minerals.

It tasted incredibly good, and the living things around it seemed to agree: creatures adapted to this unusual water made their home there.

Life living in salty water in the middle of the desert.

It wouldn't have been surprising if some of it was a species nobody had discovered yet.

Mosses, water plants, weeds, tiny aquatic creatures, and so on.

The whole ecosystem seemed to be self-contained within about thirty meters (a hundred feet) of the falls.

A tiny, tiny universe was unfolding right there.

## Camp

We decided to camp at the foot of the falls, where the plunge pool would be.

There was a patch of flat ground just the right size, perfect for pitching a tent.

Once we had put up the tarp Y had brought, it looked like we could get through the days without suffering in the direct sun.

A new chapter of our life in the desert had begun.
