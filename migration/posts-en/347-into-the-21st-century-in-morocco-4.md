## The Bus Station

Changing from one local bus to the next, we made it to the village of Chaouen.

And sure enough, the touts came at us in a rush here too.

For some reason, long-distance bus stations all over the world have the same rough, seedy air, and this one was no different.

Hard-looking, gangster-type muscle men were throwing their weight around and staring people down.

Over in a corner of the station, a boy of maybe ten or eleven had a cigarette hanging from his lips.

Another boy looked like he was sniffing paint thinner out of a bag.

We were shocked at how far gone it all was, but there was nothing we could do about it, so we set off on foot toward the village.

## The Village of Chaouen

The village sat on the side of a mountain.

Just as we'd heard, the streets went on and on in blue, and the beauty of it stopped us in our tracks.

The crisp, dry air deepened the blue of the sky, and it played in harmony with the pale blue-white town throwing back the strong sunlight.

Walking up the slope with our backpacks on, we felt like we had become part of that beautiful village, and it felt wonderful.

Still, hunting for a room while hauling our bags was hard work, so we took a cheap place that looked good enough and decided to stay there.

This being a village out in the country, it looked like we could stay for far less than in Tangier.

The plan was to spend a few days here and recover from the last few days on the road.

## Tourist Spot

Most people had never heard of the village, but among travelers it was well known, and restaurants lined its streets.

All of them were stylish little places with a nice feel to them.

At the one we went into, the waiter spoke English — this was a tourist spot, after all.

Since arriving in Morocco, we hadn't been able to make ourselves understood at all, so it brought home all over again what a blessing it is just to share a language with someone.

We asked what he recommended, ordered it, and settled in to wait.
