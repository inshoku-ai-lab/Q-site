# Joy Inside the Suffering

My life as a grade schooler was, at bottom, built on suffering.

Looking back now at photos from those days, I see myself glaring hard at the camera, and the anger and the suffering I carried inside are reflected there.

At school I was the weak one and got pushed around; going home meant being forced into religious activity.

There was no escaping into television or other amusements, and no weekends either.

Days off, for me, were national holidays and spring break, summer break, winter break, and nothing else.

Seen from an adult's point of view, having long breaks like that is not so bad, but for me as a grade schooler, not being able to play on Saturdays and Sundays was agony.

Finding almost nothing enjoyable and almost everything painful, I shut my heart down bit by bit, out of some instinct for self-defense, I think.

By closing off my heart, I stopped feeling emotions, and escaped the suffering.

This self-defense strategy worked, and in exchange for not feeling joy, I cut the suffering down enormously.

The repressed energy was never released outward as emotion or as action; it circulated, I think, inside myself.

The main outlet for that energy was reading books.

Nobody interfered, and without feeling any suffering, I could fly off into the world of imagination.

The school library and the public library were my sanctuary.

As long as I was reading alone, everything was going fine.

# Poverty

Our household income was only my mother's part-time earnings, and since she supported two children without taking public assistance from the government, we were dirt poor.

My mother worked herself to the bone, and having thought through what was best for the family, devoted herself to Jehovah's Witness activity, but that devotion deepened our poverty further.

Poor as we were, my mother kept us in clean clothes, fed us healthy meals, and kept our home comfortable, and her effort was truly something remarkable.

There are several funny stories that play up how poor we were, but sharing them here feels like an insult to my mother's effort, so I will leave them out.

Fortunately or unfortunately, my grade school served a public housing development, home to relatively poor working-class families, so I never felt especially poor.

It was only after becoming an adult and experiencing the affluent world that I realized the household I grew up in had been desperately poor.

Not knowing is also a kind of blessing.

Since we were poor, begging for toys or asking to be taken out somewhere was out of the question.

# Grandmother

Toys were something I got by visiting my grandmother's house every long break, where she bought them for me.

My mother did not like me visiting my grandmother's house, since there was friction between them as daughter-in-law and mother-in-law, but for me it was the big event of each of the three long breaks a year.

Every time I went, my grandmother made my favorite fried chicken and Chinese chive soup and served them to me.

She gave me pocket money, and bought me toys at the department store.

Every visit I got to see my father too, and that was a joy.

# The Yearly Camping Trip

Once a year, at Obon, the whole extended family went camping, and that satisfied the children's craving for an outing.

Stories from my childhood are mostly dark ones, but this camping trip was one of the few enjoyable memories.

I say the whole extended family, but the core members were the three of us — my mother, my older sister, and me — plus my mother's older sister and her husband and my mother's older brother, six in all.

On top of that, my older brother or my mother's father would join depending on the year.

It was not a matter of going to a campground and camping there, but of going to a nearby island with few people and camping on a deserted beach, or going deep into the mountains and camping on an empty patch of land.

Playing in the sea, gathering shellfish at low tide, barbecuing.

Picking wild vegetables and fruit in the mountains.

The reason we camped out in nature had to do with Jehovah's Witnesses as well.

Rather than going to a worldly amusement park ruled by evil, Jehovah's Witnesses were encouraged to admire the great outdoors, God's creation, as recreation.

I think it is wonderful.

These camping memories shine brilliantly in my recollection, and they went on influencing me greatly even after I became an adult.

It never occurred to me that childhood camping would turn out to lay the groundwork for my life.
