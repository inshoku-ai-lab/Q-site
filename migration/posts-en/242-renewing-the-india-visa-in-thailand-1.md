## Customs

Getting on a plane and crossing a border with that much risk on me was an absurd thing to do, but at the time it was a hurdle I had to clear.

I also carried around a fantasy that taking on risk tempered your spirit and turned you into a real man.

U and I were both young — twenty-one — and we wanted the risk to make us into tougher, stronger travelers.

There is no question that it gave me the nerve to stay calm in genuinely dangerous situations, but looking back now, level-headed, at the risks I was carrying then, it seems ridiculous and it still gives me chills.

Customs at the Thai airport was exactly what you would expect from the biggest tourist country in Asia — no inspection at all, straight through.

I had known we would get in easily, but pulling it off still felt good.

We headed for Khao San Road, where the travelers in Bangkok gather.

## Where We Stayed

At U's suggestion, we decided to stay in separate places.

That way, if one of us got caught, the other would not be dragged into it as his roommate.

Figuring that police checks would be tight on Khao San Road itself, we took rooms a short distance away.

U stayed in an old wooden place in the old downtown, and I took a modern guesthouse nearby.

The idea may have been the right call, or it may have been a mistake.

Because something happened.

## The Incident

That day we did the standard Bangkok tourist things — shopping, a Thai massage — and afterward went to U's room and smoked cannabis together.

I had more than enough good hash on me, so I got thoroughly wrecked before heading back to my own place.

U's place to mine was about a three-minute walk, and since it was only a few minutes on foot, I did not bother hiding the hash — it went into my bag like anything else.

That three-minute lapse was what did me in.

The street was close to the center of town, but at night no light reached it, and it ran long and straight with hardly anyone on it.

It was narrow, maybe two meters (six feet) across, and not a pleasant place to be.

Walking down it high, I saw two men on a motorcycle coming straight at me.

Dark as it was, when I looked closer I could make out two burly Thai cops with their eyes fixed on me.

It was a narrow, straight road with no corner to turn down; whatever I did, I was going to run right into them.

And I was stoned out of my mind on top of that, which made me about as suspicious as a person could look.

Sure enough, they stopped the motorcycle right beside me.
