## The Rebellious Spirit

Charging money at a Goa party was a betrayal of the rebellious spirit that set itself against the whole social system, but we had not lost ours.

We crept through the jungle behind the venue and got in from the back.

To the party organizers we were nothing but a nuisance; to us it was an important act, one that kept intact the rebellious spirit sacred to a party.

The entry fee came to about 160 yen — a dollar and a quarter, a laughable amount of money. But paying nothing to get into a Goa party was, to us, a serious matter.

## The Party

It was my first party in Goa in a year, and over something as small as the organizers collecting an entry fee, I could never quite get into it.

It was the same for everyone else there, and the old-time hippies, who could feel the beginning of the end of Goa, looked deflated somehow.

The ones who looked like they were seeing Goa for the first time, on the other hand, cared nothing about any of it and were having a great time, which in turn made them look lame, and an invisible divide opened up inside me.

To anyone outside it, all of this really is trivial. To us it was essential.

## I-chan's Party

I-chan had come out to Goa the year before, but she arrived before the party season began, so this was her first party, and her first time on LSD.

The year before, LSD had opened a great awakening of consciousness in me, and I had gone ahead and expected, selfishly, that I-chan would feel something of the same thing if she dropped it.

But her experience at this party fell flat.

That was obvious enough, really.

Neither Y nor I could quite get swept up in the party, and that faint dissatisfaction carried over to everyone around us.

And I-chan had not come to the party out of any interest of her own — she was there to go along with her boyfriend and for what she hoped the film work would be — and she had no real interest in LSD.

If anything, the fervor I had for LSD only put her off, and what she felt, I think, was nothing but fear.
