## Days in Ouarzazate

We passed our days happily, without any real plan.

Just living in a completely unfamiliar town, in a completely different culture, was about the most fun there is, even doing nothing.

Of course, it was fun because this desert town with nothing in it turned out to be a surprisingly good place.

The people were simple and warm, and they welcomed us with smiles.

I heard later that, apparently, one of the teachings of Islam tells you to go all out in showing hospitality to foreigners.

## Getting Noticed

After we'd been there a while, the people of the town started to recognize us.

The town sits at the entrance to the desert, so it's a crossroads for travelers heading into the desert and travelers coming back out.

But hardly anyone stays long at the entrance itself.

Most people leave town in a day or two.

There's no reason to stay for days on end the way we were.

On top of that, a young Asian couple nobody could figure out.

Asians barely came there in the first place, and the ones who did were rich tourists stopping by for a few hours.

A young Asian couple was something nobody ever saw.

And for some reason, we fit right in.

So the townspeople started talking to us.

## Friends

But we couldn't really understand each other's words, so we connected with nothing but gestures and our faces.

We made friends with the old guy at the olive shop, and with the old guy at the vegetable stand.

The one we got closest to was the young guy at the souvenir shop, who could speak English.

He must have been bored in the desert town too, because meeting us seemed to give him a welcome jolt.

Whenever we dropped by, he treated us to mint tea and taught us how to make tagine and couscous.

In a strange land, being able to talk to someone takes down the walls around your heart.
