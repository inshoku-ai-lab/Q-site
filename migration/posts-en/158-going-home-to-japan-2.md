## Calcutta

The train from Bombay left without any trouble, and after a twenty-four-hour haul I finally reached Calcutta.

Goa had been one relentlessly active day after another, so the ride made for a good rest, but twenty-four hours lying flat in a sleeper car does leave the body creaking.

In Calcutta I stayed at the Paragon Guest House, the same place as last time.

I half expected to run into the friend from that visit, the Kyoto University student who had stayed on there for nine months, but apparently he had finally gone home to Japan.

## Interesting Travelers

Even so, this guesthouse still seemed built to collect interesting travelers, and a few of them were the real thing.

D, a Japanese guy a few years older than me, had thick dreadlocks hanging down to his back, and his sun-blackened face was hard; there was nothing ordinary about the air he gave off.

He had already been on the road about three years, working around the world and doing street performances to pay his way as he went.

His act, of all things, was devil sticks — the same juggling I had been practicing in Goa — and he had performed it on the street everywhere he went.

He had started in Britain, crossed Europe, come through the Middle East, and made it all the way here to Calcutta.

When he performed on the street in the Middle East, nobody there had ever seen devil sticks, so it drew a huge response: crowds gathered, he was invited into one house after another, and his days were one feast after another.

I can't imagine what that was actually like, but it isn't hard to imagine how wildly exciting it must have been.

## Ketamine

One reason the Paragon Guest House was popular with travelers must have been how tolerant it was about drugs.

My guess is that the owner had a police official in the family, or was paying bribes.

We would pass a joint around on the guesthouse balcony and enjoy the company of the other travelers, and one of them had a drug called ketamine.

Ketamine is a powerful anesthetic for horses, and refining it turns it into something you can take as a drug.

In India it was sold over the counter at any pharmacy, and because it does almost no damage to the body, the people who like it buy it, make it themselves, and use it to excess.

He was fond of the stuff and made his own, and he supplied us for free.

Take it and your body goes completely limp, while your mind, altered but still lucid, drops into a state something like sleep paralysis.

Some people apparently have mystical experiences, or leave their bodies.

Since I wanted every experience I could get, to make the journey deeper, I gladly took him up on it.

## The Experience

The liquid is evaporated down into a powder, and you snort the powder through a straw.

It's the scene from the movies, the one where somebody rolls up a hundred-dollar bill to snort cocaine.

The instant it went in, every bit of strength dropped out of my body, and there was nothing to do but lie down.

The view from my right eye and the view from my left mixed together, and nothing would come into focus.

I had been expecting some special experience, but it ended without amounting to much.

I tried it once more on another day, and it was more or less the same — nothing much.

People who like it seem to really like it, but it stirred no interest in me at all.
