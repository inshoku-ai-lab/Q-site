# A Fleeting Love

There was a cute high school girl who came in to work at the pizza place about twice a week.

Charmed as I was by how pretty she was, I never had the nerve to actually do anything, and a bit of small talk during a shift was about the limit of it.

One reason I could not act was that, before starting the pizza place job, I had barely spoken to a girl at all and was shy about it.

The other was that, having grown up in a fatherless Jehovah's Witness household — a peculiar upbringing — I carried a strange taboo and trauma around getting close to a woman.

Because of that taboo, I had come to thoroughly hide anything with a hint of sex about it whenever my mother was around.

A great distortion, inherent in my heart.

On the weekday daytime shifts, the movie-loving married woman and the older girl who played in a band were both friends of mine, and since one had a husband and the other a boyfriend, I felt nothing in particular toward them and could be friendly; but with a younger girl I actually cared about, self-consciousness took over and holding a conversation was beyond me.

I was intensely preoccupied with her and could take no action at all.

Even as I held the thought "I want to try confessing how I feel," I could do nothing, and spent my days stewing.

Then one day, the guy who had just become store manager, despite being disliked by everyone, showed up at work in a car with her.

Seeing it, I understood the situation, and understood at the same time that I was not "ready in my heart" to grow close to a woman.

![A woman sitting with clasped hands pressed to her forehead, face hidden, in dim light](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog27-1.jpg)

The trauma and the taboo hanging over me would not disappear as long as I lived in my family's home.

Becoming independent of my mother and starting my own life came first.

# Birthday

Summer came and I turned eighteen.

It was an age that carried all sorts of social meanings, but for me it meant the age at which I could live on my own.

The age at which one is socially permitted to act without a parent's consent.

The age at which one can rent an apartment and choose a job.

It was a milestone eighteenth birthday, but I kept it a secret from the others at work.

Jehovah's Witness doctrine holds that no one but God is to be celebrated, so up to then I had never celebrated my own birthday, nor been to anyone else's birthday party.

So I had no idea how to celebrate myself, or how to be happy while attention was on me.

My eighteenth birthday was something I had waited and waited for, but I was not ready to act on living alone right away.

With the excuse "I want to save up a little more money" ready, I kept on at the comfortable pizza place job.

# My Sister Comes Home, and the House Hunt

When autumn came, my sister returned to Japan.

Her husband's work in Peru was not going well, and it seemed the aim was to regroup in Japan.

With my sister's help, I decided to look for a place to live on my own.

Having no idea how a thing like a rental contract even worked, I found her return to Japan reassuring.

I had a rough vision of what I wanted to do once I started living alone.

For that, renting a cheap room in central Osaka mattered.

![A person with a backpack walking down a narrow rain-wet alley lit by red neon signs](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog27-2.jpg)

The neighborhood I had my eye on was Nishinari Ward, Osaka City.

This is the flophouse district where my father and mother grew up, the one I wrote about in the second entry of the Vagabond Chronicles, and it is also the neighborhood where my grandfather, who now lives with us, lived until a few years ago.

While my grandfather lived there I would visit several times a year, every long school holiday, so it was a neighborhood I knew well.

I liked the disorder of that neighborhood and the smell of life rising off it.

It had an old working-class humanity that did not exist in the strict religious life I was raised in or in the suburban housing blocks.

My sister and I made the rounds of the real estate agents and looked at several apartments.

Since we found a reasonably priced one-room apartment not far from where my grandfather had lived, I decided to make that my new home.

From December, I would be living alone in the new place.

Had I stayed in high school, it would have been the end of the second term of my third year.

Getting out into the world and gaining my freedom a little earlier than my classmates, I was excited, and felt a sense of superiority.
