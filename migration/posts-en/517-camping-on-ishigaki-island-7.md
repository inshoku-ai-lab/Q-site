## Dawn

If we slept too soundly we'd miss the first sunrise of the year, so H and I settled in careful not to let each other oversleep.

We needn't have worried.

Dawn on a windy summit was brutally cold, and we were both awake before the night was over.

Far below the summit, the sky beyond the edge of the sea slowly began to turn pale.

By the time it was faintly light, a few other hikers had made their way up.

The first sunrise of the year, seen from the highest spot around.

Everyone had the same idea.

The hikers were startled to find the two of us wrapped in sleeping bags like a couple of beggars, but we didn't care.

We smiled, said hello back, and waited with them for the sun to come up.

## The First Sunrise

As the sun began to rise, we noticed that the horizon was covered in cloud.

Even when the sun came up out of the water, we wouldn't be able to see it.

It would come into view once it climbed high enough, but at the same time the clouds overhead were moving toward the horizon.

At this rate, the clouds above us would close the gap before the sun could clear the clouds on the horizon and show its face.

We waited for the sunrise, thinking any second now, and silently begged the clouds overhead not to head for the horizon yet.

## A Miracle

Then a miracle happened.

Just before the clouds overhead reached the horizon and shut everything out, while there was still a gap about two suns wide, the sun appeared.

The timing was perfect, like something out of a movie, and everyone on the summit erupted.

To climb all the way to the summit in the dead of night and then not get to see the sunrise would have been too much to bear.

A moment's difference and we might not have seen it at all, which made the joy all the sweeter.

## Cleansing

I was still caught up in lingering feelings for the girlfriend I'd split up with at the mountain hut two months before, and in resentment toward her, and though my days were fun enough on the surface, deep down I was carrying a real ache.

But the first sunrise of the year, arriving at the end of all that overblown drama, was beautiful, literally shining, and it felt as if it had thrown light on the shadows in my heart and cleansed them all away.

I was all right now, for the moment anyway.

Part of the point of this trip had been to heal a broken heart, and maybe, to some extent, it had done that.
