## Farewell to Chaouen

In Chaouen we rested from the road, ate a lot of good food, and did what we'd come for, so we decided to move on to Ouarzazate, the home base for the real purpose of this trip.

Ouarzazate is a town at the gateway to the Sahara.

Apparently a festival was going to be held at an oasis a little way outside it.

I-chan was out of money and couldn't really travel, so the idea was to go to Ouarzazate for now, live as cheaply as we could, and wait for the festival to start.

If we still had money left after the festival, I was thinking we'd go around and do some sightseeing.

## The Long-Distance Bus

At a rough-looking long-distance bus station, we found a bus bound for Ouarzazate and got on.

There are no trains in Morocco, so getting around overland means taking the bus.

With no other option there was nothing to be done about it, but I hated buses.

Because I get carsick.

To keep from getting sick, I had to prepare: skip breakfast, get a good night's sleep, that kind of thing.

This time I had taken the long ride lightly, and I'd just stuffed myself on one last delicious breakfast in Chaouen.

On top of that, we were leaving a mountain town, so the road twisted and turned.

As usual I got violently carsick, threw up several times along the way, and barely made it to Ouarzazate.

There can't be many travelers who get as carsick as I do, but I've resigned myself to it as fate.

## Ouarzazate

When I got off the bus I was unsteady on my feet, so we rested for a while in a café at the bus stop and set off only once I'd recovered.

Taking a taxi into town was not an option for us.

However weak I felt, all we could do was hoist our backpacks and walk.

We walked under the hot desert sun, heavy packs on our backs.

There was a fairly large local hotel in town, so we decided to stay there.
