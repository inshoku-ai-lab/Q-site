# A Job With Nothing to Do

The new job at the video store was genuinely easy.

About one customer every thirty minutes. It was rare to have more than one person in the store at a time.

What a video store clerk actually does — beep the barcodes, take the money, put the returned videos back on the shelves — came to about ten minutes of work in every hour.

The other fifty minutes were completely free, and nobody complained about whatever I did with them.

# Reading

I squeezed everything I could out of those hours, and most of it went into reading.

What obsessed me back then was how to sharpen my own sensibility as efficiently and as far as possible, and reading seemed efficient, since a book hands you information at a high density.

Within bicycle range of the house there was a big four-story branch of the Osaka Municipal Library, full of obscure titles you would never find in a small-town library, and it stoked my hunger for knowledge.

Going there once every two weeks was one of my favorite habits, and every visit had me keyed up over the new books I would meet.

Whatever had been held down in me as a Jehovah's Witness child seems to have come back out as an explosion of curiosity.

Cut off from information for so long, I had an enormous appetite for it, for knowledge of any kind, and I took everything in at a furious rate.

I was soaking my brain in information right up to its limit, and it processed all of it without the slightest strain, so my brain in those days must have been running at a remarkable pitch.

What I liked to read was Jung on the psyche, books of philosophy, books about the sixties counterculture.

Something in them pulled at me even though I could not have said what, and I read with real interest.

Interested, yes, but in fact I understood none of it.

Even so, brushing up against things I did not understand was, I think, what got the understanding started.

Anything spiritual held a strong pull for me, and I had drifted into a set of values where the physical and the material were uncool.

It was warped — I had come to see my own scrawny, unhealthy frailness as something cool and stylish.

# G

G, an old bandmate of my brother's, worked at the video store too.

He was a genuinely interesting guy, living about as freely as a person can, an artist who had stepped well outside anything society would call normal.

The deviation was considerable, and being around someone flying that far out widened, I think, the range of what I could imagine doing with my own life.

He was a gifted guitarist, somewhat known in Osaka's underground experimental music scene.

But making a living in underground experimental music is close to impossible, so he got by on part-time work.

He had the day shift and I took over from him in the evening.

G had no interest whatsoever in the work, so he did not even do the ten minutes an hour it required.

What he did instead was play guitar, nonstop.

A workplace where you can play guitar freely is a wonderful thing, and his powers of concentration were wonderful too.

Day after day he kept working on his compositions, endlessly, and never got bored with it.

I would come in for the evening shift to find that G had done nothing at all and the videos customers had returned were piled into a mountain.

He always shelved the whole pile in the last few minutes and went home, and I found something almost moving in the sheer cleanness of his indifference to the job.

He overslept and turned up after opening time constantly — on the sloppiness front he was a social lost cause — but this job was very kind to him.

K, the assistant manager, covered for his carelessness, and the place had a warmth in it that made room for misfits.

The video store — the Forum — had a small community of its own: K the assistant manager, G the guitarist, and G's girlfriend, who did not work there but was hanging around constantly.

They were close, and they were in the store whether or not they were on shift.

It was a warm, familiar sort of workplace, the kind you only get in the old working-class part of a big city.

I soaked up a comfort you can only find in a city.
