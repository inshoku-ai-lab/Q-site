## The Plan for Goa

My girlfriend I-chan was a traveler, but she was concentrating on acting with a theater company, fighting to make that dream come true.

I couldn't get Goa out of my head, and I wanted to go back to India right away.

The two of us wanted to go in different directions, and then Y, who I had gotten close to in Goa, turned up as the puzzle piece that fit the gap perfectly.

I-chan and Y had met each other in Goa, as it happened.

Y worked in the film business, and he was set on going back to Goa the next year to shoot a film about the place; he was already taking steady steps toward making that dream real.

## The Same Direction

Right there, everything lined up: I wanted to travel in India, Y wanted to shoot a film in Goa, I-chan wanted to act in a film, and I-chan and I wanted to be together.

We were happy that all of it pointed the same way, and we decided to move toward making each of those dreams real.

I concentrated on saving money, Y concentrated on getting ready to shoot, and I-chan concentrated on practicing her acting.

It was all moving along smoothly toward the dream.

## The Guys Under Me

I kept at the scaffolding job to build up my savings.

About two months into the job, someone new came in under me.

Basically, the only guys who take this job are yankees, former or current.

The new kid lived near the office, and the sense that he didn't fit into society seeped out of him.

For a while he came in and worked seriously, but early mornings seemed to be torture for him, and after about two weeks he just stopped showing up.

A while after that, another new guy showed up.

He was a year younger than me, and one look at his getup told you he was a serious yankee.

This time the boss asked me personally to teach him the ropes.

R, the new guy, was polite, listened to what I told him, was quick on his feet, and worked hard.

A while after that, another one showed up.

He had a strange history behind him: R's crew had bullied him, but by playing the obedient errand boy long enough he won the favor of R, the leader, and was drafted in as an underling.

He was slow-witted and beaten down, and his eyes were dead.

Still, either the errand-boy slot suited him or he couldn't picture any other option, because he kept playing the obedient errand boy.

With two of them under me now, I handed out the work and trained them, sometimes screaming, sometimes yelling, sometimes losing my temper.

It was a character the old me could never have played, and yet I had grown into it without effort.

Both of them did what I said, and I played the senior and threw myself into the work.
