## Vashisht's Local Specialty

Travelers came to Vashisht for the hot springs, but that was not the only thing they were after.

The Himalayan hills around Manali were famous for their fine hash, and plenty of travelers came for the cannabis too.

Most of the ones who came only for the cannabis stayed in Old Manali, upriver from the town of Manali, while the ones who wanted the hot springs as well came to Vashisht.

Japanese travelers, of course, all went to Vashisht, so word got around among travelers that this was where the Japanese gathered.

Even at the height of the season, though, that meant fewer than twenty people, so if anything it was a traveler's landmark known only to those in the know.

Apple growing had always been the big local industry in this region, but back in the sixties, when hippies were pouring into India, some locals who saw the business in catering to them started growing cannabis mixed in among the apple trees, and from there it spread.

Apple trees stand over two meters (six and a half feet) tall, and by planting cannabis, which stays under two meters, in the gaps between them, the growers doubled their income.

Word led to more word, hippie-minded travelers came in numbers, and the rest of hippie culture began to flourish along with them.

## Parties in Manali

Travelers who had spent the season partying in Goa went looking for cooler air in the Himalayan mountains of northern India around April, when the heat became too much.

They scattered to towns all over the Himalayas, and Manali was one of the biggest of the towns that travelers came up to from Goa.

Once enough party freaks had collected there, they wanted parties in Manali and not only in Goa, so they hauled speakers, amps, a DJ setup, and a generator deep into the mountains themselves and started throwing them.

I have no idea how far back that went, but by 1999 it seemed to be happening regularly, every year.

Some of the travelers who loved Goa trance and loved India went back and forth between Goa and Manali for years on end.

They were the core of it, and they had made a culture of their own.

Seen from one side, it valued a connection with nature, pushed into unexplored parts of consciousness, and threw the possibilities of the individual wide open.

Seen from another, that freedom of action and thought went too far, and there were plenty of times when it caused trouble for the local people.
