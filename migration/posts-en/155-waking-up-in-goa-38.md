## The Limit of My Strength

Utterly wrung out, I went home.

Home was a ten-second walk away, which I suppose was the one lucky thing about it.

That exhaustion was, literally, past my limit.

I had clearly overdone it.

Already close to my limit before the party, I forced myself awake with LSD and danced on, and somewhere far past that limit I ran completely dry.

Maybe a cold germ got in while I was that weakened, or maybe some virus I had been carrying all along was switched on by the extreme exhaustion — I don't know — but from the moment I got home my condition went downhill fast.

I slept two nights straight after that, but my strength didn't come back; I had a fever and a cough.

Part of it was smoking too many joints, and part of it was Goa's dust and dry air.

And of course I had taken too many drugs.

The harm in this case, I think, came from the tobacco in the joints and from whatever the LSD was cut with — speed and the like.

LSD itself does no harm to the body, and hash on its own does no real damage.

The bite of ecstasy I had taken was such a tiny amount that it was no problem either.

But the tobacco in the unfiltered joints and whatever was cut into the LSD had pushed my body too far.

And the biggest problem, I think, was crashing at a point far past my limit.

## Rest

That party, the most fun I had ever had, was the peak, and from there I started rolling downhill.

My body seemed to have started detoxing.

Back in Japan I hadn't smoked, hadn't drunk alcohol, hadn't even touched coffee, and since coming to Goa the unfiltered tobacco in the joints I smoked every day had been a heavy load on my young, clean body.

The worst of the sickness passed in a few days, but I had been pushing myself for so long that my strength wouldn't come all the way back.

The strain on my body must have been enormous.

It was the natural price for being reckless and pushing too fast.

The parties kept coming as often as ever, and I went out to them, but I felt bad enough that I couldn't really enjoy the dancing.

The frustration gnawed at me.

## The Circle of Friends

Still, at the parties there were plenty of friends I got along with, and just talking with them was a lot of fun.

We passed joints around out of politeness, but I coughed too much to smoke.

There was nothing to do but sit it out.

The circle of our close-knit Japanese group kept getting bigger.

For me that circle was an easy place to be, and the feeling of being accepted there let me fly with an easy mind.

Though I flew too far and crashed...

We weren't the only ones glad of the Japanese circle; the other nationalities at the parties were glad of it too.

"You can tell right away that the Japanese are polite, cheerful, and close with each other.

"When a Japanese group shows up at a party, the mood lifts all at once and the place takes off.

"When you see the Japanese group at a party, you know for sure it's going to be a good one."

That was the kind of thing I was hearing.

## Change of Plans

Plenty of people had plans to fly home or move on, but Goa was too much fun and nobody could get themselves out of it.

It felt like Urashima Taro, the fisherman from the old tale, so taken with the palace under the sea that he could not make himself leave.

By this point it had become ordinary among my friends to scrap all their travel plans and spend the whole time in Goa, to tear up the plane ticket home, to borrow money from someone and stretch the stay out longer.

Part of that was simply that it was too much fun to leave, but part of it was that LSD expanded their consciousness, and people for whom only material and social things had mattered until then started to value feeling and experience and friendship instead.

I was one of them — I had decided to stay in Goa until the money ran all the way out.

My favorite people were having the time of their lives in my favorite place, and being the only one not there was something I could not accept.

The sheer presence of Goa was overwhelming, and any choice other than Goa seemed pointless.
