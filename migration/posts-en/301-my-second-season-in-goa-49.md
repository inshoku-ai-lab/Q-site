## Growth

Our days in Goa this time never went the easy way, and they put us through quite a bit of psychological drama, but in the end not one of my close friends got hurt, nobody caused any real trouble, and we got to the end of the season safely.

Y's film and his DJing, I-chan's dream of acting — none of it came to anything, and with A-san it went as far as his film dream collapsing and his mind going a little strange, so what piles up from this season is a fair stock of miserable memories, and on top of that T and R had a thief break into their house.

Even so, in a place like Goa — a wild, thrilling amusement park where risk itself is the ride — just escaping with your body in one piece and your freedom intact put you ahead.

Over those five months I had stacked up mountains of intense, turbulent experience, more of it than seemed reasonable, and it left me feeling I had grown a lot as a traveler.

## The Last Days

We spent our final days there giving Goa one last go.

We ate all the fish curry we could, the kind only Goa makes, simmered in coconut and chili; we drank lassi at the good lassi shop by the bus stop in town; and we put away strawberry shakes and fruit salad at the fruit place where the travelers gather.

In high season the lines are long enough to make eating there a chore, but by the middle of May there wasn't another customer in sight.

It felt like we had all the delicious parts of Goa to ourselves, and we were happy about it.

## Surprises

A few surprising things happened in those last days.

One was that the day after we decided to give up the house, while we were out on the motorcycle, a local middle-aged woman I had never laid eyes on before shouted at us, "Hear you're moving out. Travel safe."

I have no idea who she could have been, but we were living in an area with hardly any foreigners, so our faces must have been known, and maybe we had become famous without ever noticing.

Being sent off with warm words made me happy and, at the same time, a little unnerved.

The other surprise came at night, riding through town.

The motorcycle's headlight caught an animal about the size of a cat running along in front of us.

Looking closer, it seemed to have some small animal in its mouth as it ran.

Probably a cat carrying a rat.

The motorcycle was the faster of the two, so we gained on it little by little.

Close enough at last to make it out, what we had in front of us was an enormous rat running at motorcycle speed with a kitten in its mouth.

Even we were floored by the sight.

My unquestioned assumption that a rat's natural enemy is a cat was blown clear off into the distance, and in its place came a lesson in the depth of that great land, India.
