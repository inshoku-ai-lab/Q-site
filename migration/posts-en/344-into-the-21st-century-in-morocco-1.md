## Algeciras

The overnight bus pulled into Algeciras, the southernmost city in Spain.

It was still Europe, but far warmer than London.

It was early in the morning, yet the light was already intense, and everything felt powerfully alive.

I would have liked to see something of Spain before moving on to Morocco, but we didn't have much money for the trip, so we decided to hold back in Europe and head straight for Morocco.

From the center of town, we caught a bus to the terminal for the ferries to Morocco.

## Travel Funds

Over the past six months, washing dishes, I had saved about 200,000 yen, roughly sixteen hundred dollars.

Considering I'd put it together on the rock-bottom pay of illegal work, barely what you could call minimum wage, that was a pretty good sum.

With that much, if I set my mind to it, I could probably travel around Africa for more than six months.

The one whose travel money had run out wasn't me but my partner, I-chan.

She had lived in London without working, so the savings she'd built up years before were gone.

She could have worked in London, but she was afraid that once she took a job there, she would settle into London life and her dream of acting in Japan would fall apart, so she couldn't bring herself to do it.

That was why our trip through Morocco was going to be a penny-pinching, rock-bottom-budget affair, on the level of easyJet.

## The Ferry

Being the point where Africa and Europe meet, the ferry terminal was packed with people of every race.

This was still Europe, but the atmosphere was already Africa.

The ferry left a little after noon.

The plan was to reach the African continent by evening.

In the bright afternoon sunlight, the ferry pressed steadily on.

It was early December, so even in Africa it was a little chilly, but the Mediterranean wind felt good.

We were crossing the Strait of Gibraltar, from the continent of Europe to the continent of Africa.

Our destination was a turn-of-the-century festival on an island floating in an oasis in the Sahara Desert.

We were drunk on the feeling of a journey on this grand a scale.
