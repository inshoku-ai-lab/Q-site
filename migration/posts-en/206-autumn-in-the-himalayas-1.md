## Manali Again

The town of Manali, when I came back to it after a string of bus connections, was wet with rain.

Coming back to rainy-season Manali less than a week out from the clear skies of Leh, I regretted it a little, but even so, my spirits were bright.

For no reason I could name, it felt like I had come back to where I was supposed to be.

A rickshaw took me up to the village of Vashisht, and back to the guesthouse where I had stayed before.

Luckily a room was open, and I took it.

My room was on the second floor from the top; what I really wanted was the top floor, but the top floor was popular and always taken.

From here the same days as before would begin again: hot springs and music.

## A Roommate

The next day K, who was staying on the top floor, came to me with an offer: did I want to be his roommate?

The friend who had been sharing the top-floor room with him had set off for somewhere else, so one bed was empty, and to keep his own costs down he was looking for a new roommate.

Nothing gave me more pleasure than spending time with friends and listening to music, and I had been eyeing a shot at the top floor all along.

The offer was exactly what I had been wishing for, so I said yes on the spot.

The top floor had a rooftop terrace about five meters (sixteen feet) square, with the line of the Himalayas in view from it.

Sharing a place that good cost about 150 yen a day — about a dollar twenty — and for that I was truly grateful.

## Life on the Rooftop

Life on the roof was going very well.

The terrace had just enough room to be perfect for moving around, so every day I practiced devil sticks, a form of juggling I had started getting into in Goa.

The advantage of traveling in India is that you have time to spare.

With nothing but time on my hands, I listened to music and kept the sticks spinning, and spinning.

In my school years I was convinced I was hopeless at anything physical, so I never exercised at all, and never knew the pleasure of using my body.

But scaffolding work had put muscle on me, and my body moved wherever I told it to, and that was how I learned that moving was fun.

Playing with devil sticks kept opening up what my body could do, and I couldn't get enough of it.

Coming to enjoy the very thing I had convinced myself I was bad at, I was starting to think that if you could just get rid of the belief itself, there was nothing a human being couldn't do.

If I had been good at sports from the start, I would never have arrived at that, so even having been bad at them came to seem like a gift.
