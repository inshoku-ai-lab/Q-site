## The Cramped Room

I-chan and I shared a room of about eight tatami mats, thirteen square meters (140 square feet).

The kitchen and the bathroom we shared with the landlord's family.

D was crashing in that eight-mat room.

That came to about two and a half mats of living space per person, a pretty tightly packed way to live.

We were friends who had traveled India together and lived in Goa cutting loose hippie-style, so being right on top of each other was something we were used to up to a point, and cramped as it was, we were having a good time.

## T Arrives

And into all of that came T.

T, like me, was in the high one-seventies in centimeters (close to five foot ten), which is tall, and unlike me he had some meat on him, so he was a pretty big guy.

That made four of us sharing the eight-mat room.

Two mats apiece, by the arithmetic.

To put it plainly, that was seriously cramped.

But we decided to get through those conditions by closing the distance between our hearts.

In the traveler's world, being put up at someone's place is an ordinary thing, and between travelers there is no need to hold back.

There is also an unspoken understanding that if you have a place of your own and a traveler comes to stay, you do not turn them away.

The spirit of helping each other has real roots there.

## Dinner

Back in Goa, I-chan and I had only crossed paths with T a handful of times, so this was close to a first meeting, but the way things flow among travelers, we hit it off right away.

We cooked a huge, good dinner and welcomed him in.

The stories from Glastonbury Festival came pouring out, the adventures and the things that had gone wrong.

But T seemed to feel awkward about telling D how much fun he had had.

While T was up at the peak, D had been crawling along at rock bottom, so that was natural enough.

D kept saying, "It's fine, it's fine, don't worry about it," but he was half in tears, and it looked as though his eyes were welling up.

The current T was riding and the current D was riding were perfect opposites.

But right here, a disaster came down on T — the kind that reverses a current.
