## More Stories

We were still reeling from the butterfly story, goose bumps and all, and S went straight on to the next one.

Once, a blizzard blew up on the mountain, and a woman hiker went missing in it.

With the storm that violent, a search would have risked losing the rescuers too, so nobody could go out until the blizzard had passed.

They found her dead in the middle of the trail.

And she wasn't just dead. The body was stark naked, without a stitch on.

The search party talked it over with the police, and they started an investigation into whether she might have been raped by staff from one of the nearby huts.

But the autopsy found no sign of rape at all, and the investigation never went ahead.

What seems to have happened is that she got lost in the blizzard, was so terrified she panicked, and began to hallucinate.

Maybe she was seeing herself getting into a hot spring, and that was why she had stripped naked.

## On the Edge

S, who had told us all kinds of fascinating mountain stories, stayed at our hut for about three days, going around fixing the trails nearby.

By then I'd gotten completely used to life in the mountains, and I could stand right at the edge of a cliff without any fear at all, perfectly calm.

It was hard to believe that until about two years before, I'd had something of a fear of heights.

Every day we had about two hours off around midday, and I usually spent my break behind the hut, where only staff were allowed to go.

Back there was a seriously dangerous cliff, with a drop of about a thousand meters (3,300 feet).

The drop was so enormous that when a rainbow came out as the sky cleared after rain, we could see it not as a half circle but as a full circle.

Normally you can't see a rainbow below the horizon, but with the horizon so far below us, we could see the bottom half of the rainbow too.

## Instruments

My favorite way to spend the break was to sit right at the edge of that cliff and play music.

I played a darbuka I'd bought in Morocco, a drum with a ceramic body and camel skin stretched over it.

The sound bounced off the mountains and echoed through the whole valley.

I also made a didgeridoo, the Aboriginal Australian instrument, by joining lengths of PVC pipe, and played that too.

I wasn't much good at either the drum or the didgeridoo, but playing from the top of the cliff down toward the valley floor felt wonderful.
