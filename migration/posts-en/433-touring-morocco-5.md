## C's Secret

There in the airport, of all places, C let us in on his best-kept secret.

Hidden inside the sandals he had on were two kilos (about four and a half pounds) of hashish (cannabis resin).

One kilo in each sandal.

He had hollowed out the inside of the thick rubber soles and packed them with sealed-up hash.

It had been Gandalf's idea.

## Gandalf's Secret Trade

For years, Gandalf had worked as a hash courier and used the money he made to fund his environmental activism.

When I heard about it in Tangier, it came as a shock.

I'd had my own presumptuous wish that people like Gandalf, who worked for the good of the planet, would do it legally.

But Gandalf, it seemed, would rather take the risk and get things done efficiently, for the planet's sake.

## An Unexpected Turn

What follows is a postscript from three years later.

Gandalf was completely serious about his cause, and for years afterward he kept running hash to make money and spending it on environmental work.

Then one day, by some fluke, his smuggling came to light and he was arrested.

Spain is tolerant of cannabis.

Every Spaniard smokes it, and chances are the police who arrested Gandalf, and the judge and prosecutor who tried him, smoke it too, or have at some point in their lives.

Peter Tosh, Bob Marley's musical partner, has a song called "Legalize It," with lyrics along the lines of "the doctors, the police, the judges, they all smoke it, so hurry up and legalize it," and that is Spain exactly.

But being tolerant doesn't mean you won't be charged.

With an amount that large, depending on how the trial went, he was probably looking at years in prison.

## Gandalf's Case

But being judged against his will by society's laws was not something Gandalf could accept.

He decided to fight for his rights in court.

He was carrying cannabis, which does no harm to the body or to society, to raise money for protecting the planet's environment.

He was breaking the law, but he wasn't doing anything wrong.

He had nothing to be ashamed of, and the problem wasn't him, it was the law. That was his argument.

From where Gandalf stood, he was completely right.

The trial went on for several months.

There were apparently plenty of twists and turns along the way, but in the end, Gandalf won an acquittal.

His principles had won.

Hearing this story brought home to me, as vividly as anything could, how important it is to believe in yourself and see it through.

Gandalf really was a wizard after all!
