# The Downward Spiral, Studying

Through elementary school I had enough brains that my test scores stayed high without studying.

Once junior high started, though, that stopped working, and the subjects I could not handle on prior knowledge and quick thinking alone — English among them — collapsed in no time at all.

My mother, a devoted Jehovah's Witness, held that as long as I was studying the Bible, schoolwork did not matter, so I fell in line and did not study, dropping to the bottom of the class in every subject.

Going to university was not encouraged in the teachings of Jehovah's Witnesses.

The reason was that at university you learn independence and are exposed to the temptations of the wicked world, so we were expected to take a part-time job straight out of high school and devote as much time as possible to religious activity.

Besides, I assumed we were too poor for me to go to university.

I did not know scholarships existed. My mother, I believe, deliberately kept it from me, and my teachers never brought up scholarships because my grades were bad.

Had I known about the scholarship system back then, my life would have turned out completely different.

If I had known there was a chance of getting to university, studying my way in would have been an escape route from the Jehovah's Witnesses, and I would have studied like my life depended on it.

I liked using my head so much that in elementary school I wrote "I want to be a scientist" as my dream for the future, so an entirely different set of possibilities would have opened up, I think.

Having given up on the possibility of university, I could find no meaning whatsoever in studying.

Because adolescent rebellion arrived right around then, I would even take a self-mocking pleasure in getting bad marks on tests.

Since I refused to study at all, English was a total blank to me, and there was no way to answer anything on the tests.

Not that I understood any of it, but the alphabet I could write, so I once filled the answer boxes from the top down with a, b, c, d, got exactly one right, and scored one point.

That English would ever be necessary to me in the future was beyond my imagining.

These days I speak it fluently, so there is really no telling what will change, or how.

My scores in the other subjects were low too, somewhere between single digits and fifty points.

The total across five subjects was around a hundred and fifty on a bad day, around two hundred and fifty on a good one.

Not studying was a choice I had made myself, and yet, being high-strung, I felt stress over not knowing the answers, and during tests it brought on violent nervous diarrhea.

In the middle of every single test I got the teacher's permission and went to the toilet.

# The Downward Spiral, Bullying, Illness

In my elementary school years I was bullied, and in my own turn I bullied kids weaker still, but from junior high on I came to realize how idiotic and awful my behavior had been, and stopped bullying anyone weaker than me.

What set it off I do not know, but something in me had grown up, I think.

Even so, the bullying from the stronger ones continued.

It was not violent bullying that left actual injuries, or money extorted from me, but something insidious, more like an abuse of power.

![](https://qryptraveller.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog7-1-1.jpg)

Talking to my parents or my teachers might have been the thing to do, but with my heart closed off, the idea of confiding in anyone never so much as occurred to me.

The stress of it was heavy, and more and more often, when it came time to leave for school, my stomach hurt and I could not get off the toilet.

Using that stomach pain as my reason, I began staying home from school.

Around this time I learned that if symptoms of illness showed up, I could escape from whatever was painful.

This habit of illness would do enormous damage to my life later on.

Whenever there was something I did not want to do but had to, the nervous stomach pain kicked in.

![](https://qryptraveller.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog7-2.jpg)

On top of that, a convenient low-grade fever started showing up, and illness became my means of defense.

From this period on I stayed home from school about once a week on average.

Refusing to attend school was not permitted, but being sickly was.

Weekends meant Jehovah's Witness activities I had to take part in, so there were no days off for me, and the symptoms I manufactured myself were the one salvation that let me rest.

Three times a week I attended Jehovah's Witness meetings, and the pattern each time was the same: my stomach would hurt before we left, I would beg to be allowed to stay home, and I would be forced to go anyway.

My stomach hurt during tests, and my stomach hurt before school.

Being sickly was a convenient trait for me, and without realizing it I made use of it, and without realizing it I came firmly to believe that I was a sickly person.
