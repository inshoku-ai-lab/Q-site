# Magic Mushrooms

Out of a longing for the sixties counterculture, M's influence, the pull of techno music, and an interest in hallucinogens, I was growing magic mushrooms indoors.

Every few days I misted them with a spray bottle, and since nothing ever responded I was half ready to give up on the whole thing — and then the moment finally came.

About three months after I started, mushrooms suddenly began shooting up out of the mycelium bed.

I had waited so long for them that my attachment was abnormal, and plain mushrooms looked lovable to me.

I waited a few more days, and once about three had come up, I decided to take them.

Knowing nothing at the time about how fungi actually live, I picked the two that had formed and threw the grow kit out, when in fact, if I had left it alone, I could have harvested dozens.

Had I wanted to, I could probably have kept it multiplying almost indefinitely.

In that sense, thirty thousand yen — about two hundred and forty dollars — for the mycelium bed rather than for the mushrooms themselves is a price I can half accept. Still a rip-off, and insanely overpriced.

Ignorance costs you more than you would think.

# The Session

I did the prep work properly and decided to take them alone, in the middle of the night on a day off from my part-time job.

Plenty of people apparently take them with a few others, since going in alone the first time makes them nervous, but a world of my own is where I feel safest, so I chose a solo session.

I had heard they were good in miso soup, so I put the mushrooms in miso soup and drank it down.

Not what you would call bad, but not good either.

You don't eat them for the flavor, so this was about what it should be.

The hallucinations were supposed to come on in about thirty minutes, so with high hopes I put on the Beatles film Yellow Submarine.

The Beatles made it in 1968, built around "All You Need Is Love" as its central message, and it is highly regarded for its artful, pop-art-saturated animation and for taking a revolution in consciousness as its subject.

Beneath the surface, one of its hallmarks is imagery that plays up the hallucinations of LSD and mushrooms, and I was hoping that if I took mushrooms and watched Yellow Submarine, things a normal viewing could never pick up would start to come through.

A while into the movie, a crawling, prickling sensation came welling up around my chest.

I waited to see what kind of extraordinary experience, what unknown hallucination, was coming.

Something in my senses was clearly different.

I kept watching, full of expectation, and the next thing I knew, I had fallen asleep.

When I opened my eyes it was already morning.

My head was already clear...

Screwed it up again!!!

I had spent thirty thousand yen and three months getting ready, hoping for hallucinations, for my brain to crack open, and once again I had let the chance slip.

Without ever finding out what magic mushrooms feel like, without the hallucinations, without the awakening, without the true meaning of Yellow Submarine, the long-awaited session was over.

Nothing came of it that I had been hoping for, and it ended as a letdown.

That settled it — I swore to myself I would go to Goa, in India, and take LSD.

I wanted the real thing.

Curiosity ruled my life.

# Looking Back

Looking at it now, that session was, in its way, a success.

The mushrooms unquestionably had an effect; it was my first time, so I simply could not recognize it.

The dose, though, was too small, I think. With about six of them, even a first time would have registered.

A mild effect had come on, my nerves were running hypersensitive, and then the imagery of Yellow Submarine, which works directly on the optic nerve, seems to have put me into a kind of hypnotic, entranced state, and I fell asleep.

I woke up in the morning feeling clear-headed, but I think that was the mushrooms still working, that the clarity was that sharpened-nerve effect.

There was a real enough effect, but it was not what I was after back then, so my dissatisfaction just kept building.
