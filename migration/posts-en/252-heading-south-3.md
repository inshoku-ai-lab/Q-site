## Arrival

The thirty-six-hour train ride ended without trouble, and I made it to Goa.

A complete reversal from the snow mountains of the Himalayas: the light here was tropical, all the way through.

Sunlight sharp enough to hurt your eyes, a deep blue sky, and bare red earth made a landscape that could only be Goa.

A shiver went through me at the thought of being back in Goa.

The feeling carried the omnipotence of a fish put back into water, and the release a convict feels walking out of prison into the free world.

The sheer freedom of my last time in Goa was stored in my brain and in my cells, and one look at that red earth brought the whole sensation back.

This time I had savings enough to stay in Goa as long as I liked, and my visa was still good for another six months.

When I imagined how deep this journey could go, the depth of it made me shudder, but my mind was made up.

I meant to dive as deep as I could and fly as high as I could.

## Health

In the space of a month I had been through snow mountains and the tropics, twice each.

My body felt startled by it, but it adapted, and I came through without getting sick.

India crawls with every strong germ there is, but a long enough stay builds resistance, and they no longer knocked me down easily.

The constant diarrhea of my first trip to India was behind me, and I had come around to an ordinary life where you only get it from eating something strange.

A harsh environment trains the body hard.

That may not be the same thing as health, but there's no question I had gotten tougher.

Experiences like this make me suspect that living somewhere good for your health doesn't necessarily lead to health.

Which is healthier — a vegetable raised in a greenhouse on all the fertilizer it can take, or a weed growing wild? That's the question.

## Anjuna Beach

From the station I took one bus after another out to Anjuna Beach.

My best friend Y was waiting for me to arrive.

He had reached Goa about two weeks earlier and had everything ready.

For now, before settling on a room of my own, I would stay at his place.

Unlike the first time I came to Goa, this was a town I knew my way around and had lived in, and everything felt convenient and easy.
