## The Local Legend

Work at a mountain hut long enough and your circle of hut people keeps growing.

The ties with the neighboring huts run especially close.

We help each other out when it's needed, and when it comes to that, we'll give each other a bed and a meal.

Trail maintenance above all isn't one hut's problem. It belongs to every hut in the area, so on that front the cooperation is tight.

That's how S came to our hut.

To look at him, S was a small old man, but he'd been working in mountain huts for forty years, a veteran among veterans.

There wasn't a soul in the mountain hut world who didn't defer to him.

## The Sherpas

S was attached to a large hut near ours and worked only on trail maintenance.

The work was so dangerous that even seasoned hut staff and experienced climbers couldn't do it, and nobody could handle the jobs S took on.

Short of hands, S brought elite Sherpas over to Japan — the mountain people who live in the Himalayas.

They're the best mountain people in the world, guiding top climbers from every country through the Himalayas and joining Everest expeditions.

According to the Sherpas, the 3,000-meter (10,000-foot) peaks we were living among don't count as mountains by Sherpa standards. They call them hills.

Their whole yardstick is different.

S said that only people on the level of an elite Sherpa could help with the work he wanted to do, and I could see why.

## Today's Job

S came to stay at our hut with two of these Sherpa powerhouses in tow.

Little S with his two giant Sherpas looked like a martial arts master out of a manga, trailed by his disciples.

S and J, our manager, went way back, and you could see in J's manner how deeply he respected him.

The job they'd done that day was blasting away a rockfall.

A few days earlier, a huge boulder had come down off the mountain and blocked most of the trail.

No amount of muscle was going to move it.

They set dynamite under the boulder and let the blast knock it five hundred meters (1,600 feet) down the mountain. As trail work goes, it doesn't get much bolder.
