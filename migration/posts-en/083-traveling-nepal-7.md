## Down

Riding down a Nepali mountain road on the back of the motorcycle, still high, was pleasant enough in itself, but my head was in no state for it.

Angry, frustrated with nowhere to put the frustration, low with my own spinelessness, I started back toward the guesthouse where I was staying.

Shiva delivered me safely, and there we parted.

The ten thousand yen he had taken — about eighty dollars — I never got back.

Flattened, I went and drowned my sorrows, in hash rather than drink.

To get away from the ugly mood, I smoked and smoked and tried to lose myself.

The mushrooms and the hash stacked on top of each other and took me further out, but there was nothing fun or interesting in it — I just sank, straight into a bad trip.

Hungry, I went as usual to Anil Momo, the Japanese place, for dinner.

I almost never drink, but just this once I ordered a beer.

Flying on mushrooms, an idiotic amount of hash in me, and then a few beers on top.

I couldn't bear how stupid and how pathetic I had been.

A few beers in, the friends I was traveling with came into Anil Momo.

That is as far as my memory goes; after that the drugs multiplied one another and blew the rest away.

## The Next Morning

In the morning I woke up in my own bed.

It was already past noon, and there was no one else in the room.

My head was splitting.

The headache was what brought the day before back to me.

I had done something unbelievably stupid, and then drunk myself into the ground on top of it.

Lying on the bed, I went back over the day before, turned it over and over, and thought hard about what I had done.

Outside it looked like rain, but staying in the room would only take me further down, so I decided to go out and walk.

I went to the busy strip where the travelers gathered and wandered around.

Wanting to do something, anything, that would change the mood, I bought a few music tapes at a bootleg cassette shop.

At about two hundred and fifty yen a tape — around two dollars — it was no great sum, but on the kind of dirt-cheap travel I was doing, saving where I could, it was not something I could go on buying forever.

Today was a special case, though, so I got hold of a few favorites to console myself.

From the strip back to the guesthouse where I was staying was about a thirty-minute walk.

The rain came on the way back.

It grew heavier and heavier, and I trudged along soaked to the skin.

Luckily, Nepal in August is warm even for a country of mountains, so there was no worry about catching cold.

Walking through the rain like a soaked, worn-out rag struck me as an accurate picture of my situation, and being beaten by rain felt like exactly the treatment I deserved.

## Asking for Help

Back at the guesthouse, my friends had returned.

When I asked about the night before, they said I had been in a bad state, wild and miserable, and that they had looked after me.

They had also, it seemed, reported my whole idiotic performance to the guesthouse owner and asked his advice.

According to what they were told, Shiva was the nuisance of the entire Damside area, with a record of trouble behind him.

What saved me was that, troublemaker or not, Shiva was in a weak position and could not go against the guesthouse owner.

So it was settled that the owner would go to Shiva's house and get the money back.

Shiva's house, as it turned out, was no more than fifty meters (a hundred and sixty feet) from the guesthouse, which made the whole thing an entirely local, almost domestic affair.

The owner had probably cleared up a great many of Shiva's problems before.

He got the cash back from Shiva and brought it to me.

Part of it had already been spent, so it was not the full amount, but I was told to make do with that.

According to the owner, Shiva had a wife and children to support, and between that and his own character, very little in his life was going well.

The owner gave me a hard talking-to about what I had done, and with that the matter was closed.

Everything had gone back where it belonged, but nothing about my idiocy or my spinelessness had changed, and the shame I carried away was more than the old saying about leaving your shame behind on the road could cover.

And so the curtain came down on my "fourth time lucky" psychedelic experience: a complete failure.
