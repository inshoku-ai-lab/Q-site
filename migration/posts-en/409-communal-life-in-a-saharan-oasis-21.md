## Roles in Communal Life

When everyone lives together, each person takes on a role of their own.

I hauled the heavy water jar every day, and others cooked, went out for supplies, chipped in money, acted as a bridge to the locals, and so on.

But there was one person among us who was struggling to find a role.

R, an Englishwoman.

Her sensitivity and honesty worked against her, and they were creating friction in our communal life.

She had no confidence in her cooking, so she didn't cook; she had no confidence in her stamina, so she didn't go out for supplies; and washing the dishes after meals felt sexist to her, so she didn't want to do that either.

At the same time, she was frank about every complaint and every want she had, so the people living in the hut often found her a pain.

She could feel the friction too and wanted to move on somewhere, but her money had already run out, and she had nothing left to travel on.

Nor did she have the nerve to hitchhike all the way back to her own country the way O would have, so she was stuck, with no way out.

## Romance: R's Chapter

R was beautiful, with a feminine delicacy and a sophisticated big-city sense of style, so under the right circumstances she could have let her own light shine, had plenty of admirers, and lived a happy, fun life.

But here, living communally in the Sahara Desert, her personality was backfiring on her.

In that weakened state, the one R found to lean on was C, who was about her age.

R had been watching what was going on between O and A-kun, envious of them and, at the same time, annoyed by the noise in the middle of the night.

That was where C came in.

R took every chance she got to stay close to C and be especially kind to him, and the two of them grew friendly.

And C was hardly going to mind a woman being kind to him.
