# **Bangkok**

We set out at the beginning of March.

Osaka had only just come out of winter, and before the buds of spring came out it was chilly.

I met my friend at Tennoji Station, and we headed for Kansai Airport.

Our hearts were racing at the unknown adventure about to begin.

We got through customs and the rest of it easily, and the plane landed safely in Bangkok, the capital of Thailand.

The first thing that surprised me on stepping out of the airport was the difference in climate.

It was the beginning of March, and I had never imagined it could be this hot.

The unexpected humid heat made it feel even more like traveling.

Relying on the backpacker guidebook I had brought, "Chikyu no Arukikata," we got on a public bus.

Our destination was the area called Khao San, said to be the gateway for backpackers traveling through Asia.

The word was that it was best to go from the airport to this neighborhood first, buy your bus tickets and do your shopping, and then head out to the southern islands and elsewhere.

We got off the bus and followed the map to the street called Khao San Road, where the density of people shot up all at once.

Every ethnicity, every age group overflowed onto the street, full of energy.

Food stalls lined the roadside, the vendors calling out for us to come and eat at their place.

The souvenir shops, for their part, had display shelves jutting far out of the storefronts and occupying the sidewalk.

Into all that came the taxi touts and the hotel touts, each one trying to be first to take a tourist off somewhere.

![Three brightly coloured tuk-tuks parked side by side, each with a yellow TAXI sign on the roof](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog35-1.jpg)

We had heard that following touts like these was dangerous, so we ignored their calls and pressed on toward the guesthouse we were aiming for.

Thanks to my friend A, who spoke some English, we got through the check-in procedure and bought tickets for the next day's departure to Koh Phangan.

Since we planned to pass through Bangkok many more times after this, the idea was to put off sightseeing in the city and get ourselves to the southern paradise straight away.

# Heading South

Aiming for Koh Phangan, we boarded a night bus.

In complete contrast to the daytime heat of the capital, the air conditioning inside the night bus was set to maximum, past cool and into cold.

Having come from Japan in early spring we had warm clothes with us, which saved us, but anyone traveling only in Southeast Asia without them would catch a cold from that level of air conditioning almost without fail.

Many of the passengers stuffed towels into the cold-air vents to block the chill.

Once it comes to that, you have to wonder what the air conditioning is even for.

The bus we took was a sleeper, so there was space to stretch out my legs and lie completely flat, and since it ran straight south down the highway there was little swaying, and I slept surprisingly well.

Come morning, we had reached a port on the mainland side near Koh Phangan.

There we transferred to the ferry for the island.

# An Encounter on the Ferry

On this ferry, we met some interesting Japanese people.

Normally in Japan, even riding the same bus, you would not strike up a conversation and become friendly, but being the only Japanese among the roughly two hundred people on board made it easy to speak to each other.

![A white passenger ferry crossing grey water past a wooded shoreline, sailboats nearby under an overcast sky](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog35-2.jpg)

One was I-san, shaved bald; the other was T, long-haired.

In age they were a good deal older than us, somewhere in their late twenties, perhaps.

Their skin was tanned a deep brown, un-Japanese, and in loose T-shirts and baggy Thai fisherman pants, they looked like seasoned travelers, relaxed.

They were a type I had never met before, extraordinarily easygoing and open and friendly.

The opposite of us: pale film nerds, just arrived from Japan in early spring and tense in an unfamiliar environment.

As an aside, my love of movies had gone so far that I had taped aluminum foil over every window in the house to block the sunlight completely, which made my skin all the whiter.

Listening to their story, I gathered that they had traveled all over Asia any number of times, and that they had already been to Koh Phangan several times before.

They would work a few months at a factory making cars, save money, then travel a few months in a country where prices were cheap, and they had apparently kept that life going for several years.

I had heard rumors that people living that way existed, and had figured I would meet some if I traveled in Thailand, but the encounter came sooner than expected.

They had a favorite guesthouse they went to every year, away from the island's busy quarter, quiet, with a private beach, and on top of that the room rate was quite cheap.

The traveler's word of mouth, straight from the source, as it were.

Interested, we asked whether we could come along, and they agreed readily, so we decided to go with them to the travelers' guesthouse they were bound for.
