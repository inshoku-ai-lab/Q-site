# The Road to India

I meant to get to India without flying, by sea and over land.

From what I could find out, the Chinese visa could be arranged on board the ship itself, so there was nothing to prepare in advance.

I bought the ferry ticket through a travel agency.

Departure in mid-July. One month before my twentieth birthday.

Leaving before I turned twenty — the age you legally become an adult in Japan — was an important symbolic event for me.

A sleeper ferry called the Shin Ganjin would sail out of Kobe and take three days to reach Shanghai.

The ferry was twenty thousand yen, about a hundred and sixty dollars, and the visa another ten thousand on top of that, which made it far, far cheaper than flying at the time.

# Length of the Trip

My older sister was back in Japan from Peru, and I told her I was giving up my apartment and going traveling.

The plan: a boat to China, on to India, back after three months, then living at my family's place.

Apart from the travelers I'd met on the Thailand trip, not one of my friends from work had ever traveled for three months, and I felt a certain superiority in being a teenager about to take a trip that far outside what counted as normal.

When I told my sister it would be three months, I'm sure I had a smug look on my face without knowing it.

But my sister had lived abroad for years, far beyond anything my smug face could reach, and three months overseas apparently struck her as much too short.

What came back from my sister was a question.

"You're clearing out your apartment to travel free, and you're only going for three months?"

She sounded genuinely surprised.

Hearing it put that way, I had to admit she was right.

There was no reason at all why I had to be back in three months.

No reason, and yet without noticing I had built a frame around it and called it "the plan."

Taking my sister's cue, I changed the length of the trip from three months to open-ended.

As long as the money held out, I would take my time and look at the world.

Raised in a poor household and living poor on my own, I could economize in my sleep.

I thought my single greatest strength was that a plain, frugal life didn't bother me, and I intended to use that strength to move my life forward on favorable terms.

# Underage

My twentieth birthday was getting close.

Not much longer of being handled leniently by the law as a minor.

A punk kid who thought antisocial things were cool, I wanted to do something illegal while I was still underage.

It's an extremely stupid story, and I'm not sure it belongs in The Vagabond Chronicles, which is meant to be an account of my life so far, but it's funny, so I'll share it.

Back then I was hooked on Hotline, an underground file-sharing program on the internet.

All kinds of pirated software and porn images circulated there.

Among the files going around were what people called aikora — idol collage nudes, a pop idol's face fitted onto a nude photo of some other woman.

They came from individual otaku who composited an idol's face and a nude photo in Photoshop and shared the results in underground net communities.

These days, searching "aikora" online apparently brings up ordinary results, but back then the stuff was treated as strictly underground, and I think most of the people trading the files understood aikora to be illegal.

Through N, one of the star clerks on the Tsutaya night shift, I had come to love idols, and Ryoko Hirosue above all.

Hot-blooded punk teenager and introverted otaku at the same time, I collected aikora of Ryoko Hirosue and other idols.

The hobby went further: figuring it would also be good practice for getting the hang of a computer, I tried making a few myself.

# The Scheme

Then one day — I've forgotten how I came across it — I found a company that ran a kind of online platform for building adult sites and making money off them.

Registration was free, and as I remember it, the company skimmed a margin off what users paid and passed the rest to whoever had built the site.

In my last stretch as a minor, I came up with a scheme that made use of the opportunity.

The plan: before I turned twenty, build an illegal (as I believed) aikora site and set it up to earn money on its own while I left it alone.

If it ever became a legal problem, it was something done by a minor.

And if the trouble came up after I turned twenty and came back to Japan, I hadn't been in the country, so — safe.

That was my thinking.

Building the website was easy.

A website in 1998 was nothing like one now, very simple, a matter of setting some text, some images, and a background.

I uploaded the aikora I had onto the platform, wrote some text, and it was done.

I didn't really expect to make any money, but doing something illegal while still underage made me happy.

That was how I confirmed I was free, I think.
