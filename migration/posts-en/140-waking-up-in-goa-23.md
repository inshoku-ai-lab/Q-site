## Personalities in Bloom

The combination of Goa, parties, and LSD woke up a lot of personalities.

Every last one of us had been through the depths of darkness and the best high of our lives, and drawing inspiration from people who traveled the world, we were smashing through our own limits.

There was fertile ground for welcoming change and taking pleasure in it, and all of us were going through big changes in a short span of time.

Weeks of nonstop revelry with our energy never coming down — none of us had ever experienced anything like it before, and it set our personalities loose.

Everyone's look got louder by degrees, in step with how far they had let themselves go.

One guy had the left half of his hair dyed gold and the right half green, and his friend had dyed his short hair bright red, both of them enjoying the loudness and the attention.

## Christmas

Christmas Day finally came.

Goa, the only Christian state in India, celebrates Christmas on a grand scale, and taking their cue from that, Goa's trance people celebrated it on a grand scale too.

The number of tourists doubled yet again, the streets overflowed with people, every hotel and rental house was full, and even the motorcycle rentals had run dry.

Today's party, I hear, is at yet another place — somewhere called Paraiso, on a beach close enough to walk to from where I live.

It's meant to start with everyone watching the sun sink below the horizon.

As always, we get ourselves perfectly ready and head for the party.

The sound carries all the way to my house, a few minutes' walk away.

The closer we get to Paraiso, the louder the sound, the heavier the motorcycle traffic, the more alive the air.

By the time we're close, the whole area has turned into a motorcycle parking lot, with no trace left anywhere of what it had been.

## Paraiso

Unlike the open-air parties up to then, Paraiso felt half open-air.

It made use of the beach at the water's edge and the cliff shooting up from it — a rare place that had both the beauty of nature and the convenience of something built.

The entrance was at the top of the cliff, and going down the stairs cut into the rock you passed a few small halls along the way, and at the bottom there was a big dance floor built out of sturdy timber.

Farther down, the beach appeared.

The only way to reach this beach was through the building, which made it something like a private beach.

On the open ground on the inland side of the beach, chai stalls stood in a long row, packed with people.

## Goa Gil

As always, we sat down at a chai stall, drank chai, and smoked a joint.

Once we were ready, I bit off a chip of ecstasy and dropped half a tab of LSD.

This time it was a kind of LSD called Dragon.

Its image was brash and full-throttle, and that got me excited.

Why half a tab, four times the usual, instead of the eighth of a tab that was the right dose for me: this party was apparently running twenty-four hours straight, which left more than enough time to satisfy me no matter how wrecked I got.

I wanted to use the chance to test my own limits.

The DJ for this party, word was, would be Goa Gil, an old hippie who was something of a Goa heavyweight.

Up on the stage, which you looked up at from the dance floor, an old man with long dreadlocks hanging to his waist was DJing in fractal-patterned clothes that glowed under the black light — the same kind E, the "Ohhayoo" guy, had been wearing.

Beside the DJ table stood a statue of Shiva, the god who presides over the creation and destruction of the universe, and around it were laid out minerals of every color, bird feathers, and the like.

It seemed to be his own kind of altar; he kept incense burning on it and was offering up blessings.

For him, DJing was a religious event, a sacred rite of passage for reaching the awakening of the brain that LSD brings.

What that meant, I would come to understand through this party.
