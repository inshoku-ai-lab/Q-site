## The Next Morning

I woke with the sun and took down the tent.

I didn't want the neighbors seeing me camped out in a pachinko parlor's parking lot, and getting questioned by the police would be a pain.

The ramen shop wouldn't open for a while yet, so I bought breakfast at a nearby convenience store and rested in a park.

I'd been up since six in the morning, and having to wait until eleven for the ramen shop to open only made me angrier.

But getting angry wasn't going to change anything, so I killed some time, took money out of an ATM, and went to the ramen shop to pay for the night before.

I was angry, but I had no right to hold it against him, so I paid without a fuss. The manager who took the money was a different man.

Maybe he'd told his family what happened the day before, and they'd gotten on him for being so petty.

I had no idea what had changed, but he looked sorry as he handed back my driver's license.

I walked out with an air that said I wanted nothing more to do with a place like this, got on the bike, and pedaled on.

## Miyazaki Prefecture

Thanks to one ramen shop manager, I'd come away with a bad image of Oita Prefecture, but once I crossed the border into Miyazaki Prefecture, I was able to reset it.

Of course, the people of Oita had nothing to do with that image, and neither did the people of Miyazaki. It was just something I'd made up in my own head.

I kept pedaling south, and around Miyazaki City the whole mood shifted again, even more.

Being farther south than Oita, it was bound to be warmer, but the plain the city sits on also faces south, which gave the whole place an open, warm feel.

I hadn't done anything special, but for no real reason I was in a good mood, and the negative image of Kyushu that had started to form in my head was wiped away completely.

The next day I'd be going over a mountain pass, so I called it a night early.
