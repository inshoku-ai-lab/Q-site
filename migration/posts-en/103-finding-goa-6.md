# A Knack for Finding Cheap Rooms

My talent for finding cheap lodging seemed to be quite exceptional -- even in Anjuna, I managed to find a dirt-cheap room for 150 yen -- about a dollar twenty -- a day.

All told, ever since arriving in Nepal and India, I'd managed to find lodging in nearly every town for around 50 rupees.

The one exception was Pune, but I was put up there for free one night, so on balance it came out about the same.

I was burning with passion for not spending money.

Given my style of travel, there was no set date I had to be back in Japan by, so the less money I spent, the longer I could stay in India.

I'd been feeling my character grow by leaps and bounds since setting out on this trip, and I wanted to make the most of the chance.

Once I got back to Japan, I planned to live a life centered on making music.

I figured I'd probably never come to India again, so I wanted to enjoy it as much as possible and make it nourishment for my life.

The room was the same as before: a cramped single, shared toilet and shower, bare-bones, but it had everything I needed.

There was no nice courtyard like the one in Calangute, but since I wanted to be out running around, that was enough.

What I really wanted was just a bed and a room I could lock.

My friends' places were also just a few minutes' walk away, so I could drop in easily.

Things looked like they were about to get fun.

Unlike the quiet, merely comfortable life in Calangute, there was a lot going on in Anjuna.

It was the end of November, but travelers had already begun to trickle in here and there, and the place felt like things were about to get interesting.

There also seemed to be more restaurants for foreign travelers than in Calangute.

The biggest difference was probably the large flea market held every Wednesday on Anjuna Beach.

Not just Indians, but plenty of foreign travelers ran stalls there too, and the selection overflowed with personality.

But what mattered to us wasn't shopping at the flea market -- it was the rave held afterward at a bar along the beach near the market.

We'd had M arrange LSD for us for that day and waited for Wednesday.

The party was supposed to start at sunset, but we got ahead of ourselves completely and took the LSD before we'd even left the house.

We were too excited to wait.

In the past I'd failed four times already, so I psyched myself up telling myself the fifth time would be the charm.

We rode double on the bike toward the bar.

But by this point we'd already smoked several joints of hash resin and were completely stoned, and on top of that the LSD was starting to take effect.

Once we started riding, I realized we were so out of it we barely knew where we were headed.

Our current location was unclear, and so was our destination.

The only thing that was clear was that we wanted to get wasted and dance.

The fifth time's charm was already wobbling.

For now, I just focused on driving safely, and we started searching for the party by following the faint sound we could hear.

The sound was definitely coming from somewhere in the distance, but it overlapped with the bike's engine noise, so it wasn't clear where exactly it was coming from.

Every so often we'd cut the bike's engine to check the sound, then head in that direction.

Whenever we lost the thread again, we'd cut the engine, listen for the sound, and head toward it.

While we were doing this, the LSD's auditory hallucinations grew stronger, until I couldn't tell the difference between the bike's engine and a speaker's bass.

In the end, even with the engine off, the sound of other bikes and the sound of speakers got all mixed together, and I stopped even understanding what we were doing.

Each time, I had to concentrate as hard as I possibly could to tell the hallucinated sound from the real one.

Toward the sound, toward the sound -- we searched single-mindedly, like an insect drawn to a light.

We must have wandered lost for about an hour; lost, then lost again, we finally managed to find the bar.

It was only afterward that I realized this bar was about a five-minute walk from our house -- nowhere near a place that should have taken an hour to reach by bike.

Having gotten so thoroughly and needlessly lost before finding the party, we felt the joy hit several times harder.

We cheered wildly.

By the time we'd parked the bike in the lot and were heading toward the bar, I was already completely fired up, and I took off running for the bar with both arms raised, shouting.
