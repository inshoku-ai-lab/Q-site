## Almost Time

As the festival drew closer, foreign travelers began to turn up, a few at a time, in the hotel and around town.

Most of the people who pass through this town are on their way to a Sahara tour, but now the ones I kept noticing were travelers who had, by the look of them, come for the festival.

They were so different in age, in appearance, and in vibe that there was no mistaking them.

You could feel the excitement coming off every one of them, with the festival just around the corner.

Travelers were arriving from all over the world, but I never saw a single East Asian among them.

## Y

That was when Y showed up.

An Asian man with short hair, neatly dressed.

The moment our eyes met, I had a hunch and spoke to him in Japanese.

Sure enough, he was Japanese, and both of us relaxed a little as we started talking in our own language.

He'd been working as a writer in Tokyo, it turned out, doing cultural pieces for a few magazines.

Starting with this turn-of-the-century festival, he told me, he was going to travel the world and write a travelogue.

A young man with big ambitions.

## First Trip

Y had lived in Canada on a working holiday, so he was used to being abroad and comfortable in English, but he had never traveled as a backpacker.

Back in Japan, he'd kept working as a writer, and getting into psytrance and the culture around it had drawn him deeper and deeper into hippie culture and the culture of travel; then, with his thirtieth birthday just ahead, he'd left Japan and set out on the road.

It was probably the same feeling I'd had when I left Japan just before turning twenty.

Morocco, apparently, was his very first step as a backpacker, and you could see it in him: nerves, determination, and anticipation all mixed together.

That was the beginning of a friendship with Y that would last for years.
