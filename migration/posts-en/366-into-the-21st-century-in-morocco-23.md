## Connections

As the day of the festival drew closer, the hotel where we were staying began to fill up with travelers.

Nothing like this had ever been held in this desert town, of course, and it was apparently going to be the first electronic music festival in Moroccan history.

The travelers were mostly coming from all over Europe.

Little by little I got to know more and more people: people I met through the volunteering, travelers staying at the same hotel, residents of the nearby desert villages, all sorts of people.

## Openness

As more travelers arrived for the festival, parties started breaking out in the big dormitory room.

Not planned parties, though. A traveler with an instrument would start a jam session, another traveler who played music would hear it and join in, then another traveler would hear that, and so on; they just happened on their own.

Most of the travelers gathered here were, of course, strangers to one another.

There were couples and groups of friends, but plenty of people traveling alone, too.

Complete strangers talked to each other in English colored by the accents of their home countries, and enjoyed the conversation and the music.

The atmosphere was wide open, nobody held back with anyone, and a sense of camaraderie among travelers who had never met before surrounded us.

## Shyness

Being there, I realized that my shyness about speaking English had disappeared.

Until then, whenever a situation called for English, I'd freeze up, say, "I can't speak English," and hide behind I-chan.

But living in London had gradually eased my nerves about English, and reading a Moomin novel in its English edition had somehow started to bring down the wall between me and the language.

And now, at these parties, I watched everyone talking in accented, broken English, and my senses began settling into that English-speaking world.

Speaking English had always been something to fear, but now the joy of communicating in a language I had never been able to speak won out.
