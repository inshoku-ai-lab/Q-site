# Back to Japan

Our enjoyable trip finished, we returned to my sister's house.

My mother planned to stay on another month or so, enjoying life in Peru.

My aunt, who had a husband to look after, planned to go straight back to Japan.

When I first came to Peru I had made no particular plans, but the boredom of those days had worn me down so badly that I decided to go home together with my aunt when she went.

Friendships had started to form here and I was fitting in reasonably well, but there was no reason for me to be in Peru in the first place.

I had no idea what I would do once in Japan, but the boredom was more than I could bear any longer.

Besides, going home at this particular moment meant I would have the house in Japan to myself, and would be spared spending all day with my mother here.

With the wedding and the trip behind us, the preparations for departure went ahead in the middle of all the bustle.

In what little time was left, I packed my things and made the rounds saying goodbye to the people I knew.

The busyness, the change of climate, the lack of sleep, the fatigue — it must have all come together, because on the morning of departure I did not feel well.

Since it seemed a bit like a cold, I took Pabron or some other Japanese over-the-counter cold medicine.

It being an important travel day, I took one tablet more than usual.

Simple arithmetic: the more the better.

# Carsick

My sister would see us as far as the airport in the capital, Lima.

Saying goodbye to my mother and the rest, we headed for Lima by bus.

As the bus wound along the mountain roads of the Andes, I gradually began to feel ill.

Prone to carsickness, I had medicine ready, and took it.

Time passed and the carsickness did not improve much, and separately from that, this time the symptoms of altitude sickness set in.

My head ached terribly. It hurt so much that I took the altitude sickness medicine I had prepared.

The bus kept running along the winding mountain road.

![Passengers standing and seated inside a dim city bus, viewed down the aisle](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog23-1.jpg)

The symptoms did not improve at all, my condition got steadily worse, and between the carsickness and the cold I started throwing everything up.

On top of that came the side effects of the medicine, and the nausea was more unnatural than ever.

Further still, the symptoms of altitude sickness combined with it, a violent headache crashing over me.

Throwing up from carsickness is common enough, but what was different this time was that altitude sickness and the side effects of medicine had combined with the cold I already had.

Through the vomiting, an extraordinary detoxification and purification was taking place.

Crying as I brought up even my stomach acid, feeling the altitude headache, breathless in the thin air, I hyperventilated until my hands and feet locked into strange shapes.

My aunt looked after me with everything she had.

The mountain road went on for hours, and with the side effects of the medicine on top, I kept vomiting until my stomach acid ran dry.

After arriving at the airport, unsteady on my feet, I was taken straight to its infirmary.

There, drinking water to flush out my stomach, I threw the water up too.

In this state it would be difficult even to board the plane to Japan, they said, and I was given a big injection in the backside.

What it was they injected I do not know, but the nausea, the cramping, the cold symptoms, and the exhaustion all vanished.

Whether the lesson is that chemical drugs are to be watched out for or that they work very well, I cannot tell, but the symptoms were gone and I was able to board the plane without trouble.

This time I would not be alone; the trip back to Japan, with my aunt, would take several days.

For my aunt, not having to travel home by herself was probably reassuring, I think.

# Living in Peru

That was how my first experience abroad, in Peru, came to an end.

![A person leaping through the air between two rocky ledges against a blue cloudy sky](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog23-2.jpg)

Having stayed a little over two months, I do not think I could claim to have really experienced Peru, and if asked whether I enjoyed it, all I can say is, well... — but even so, living in a way completely different from anything before, experiencing life in another country, became a great source of confidence.

The inferiority complex of having known nothing but the life of a Jehovah's Witness, of never having properly lived, was eased a little by this.

Having grown a little, I set off on the road back to Japan.
