**The Full Moon on Koh Phangan**

We had not come to Koh Phangan just for a beach vacation.

Needless to say, we enjoyed the beach vacation to the fullest, but our main purpose in coming to the island was to take part in the Full Moon Party, said to be held every month on the night of the full moon.

A techno event called a rave is held on a beach in the south of Koh Phangan.

Enormous speakers stand in a row along the shore, DJs blast techno of every kind, and people dance all night long — that is the sort of event it is.

This is a slightly misleading way to put it, but the history of techno music is deeply bound up with the history of rave parties, and the history of rave parties is inseparable from hallucinogenic substances such as cannabis, LSD, MDMA, and magic mushrooms.

The genre of techno called psychedelic trance in particular is composed so that the music's effect peaks when you listen and dance to it on LSD.

At the Full Moon Party on Koh Phangan that type of music was especially favored and played, and travelers who loved it gathered there, took hallucinogens, and danced through the night.

I had learned about all this from an underground magazine called Abunai Ichigō (Dangerous No. 1), which I introduced in an earlier entry, but Koh Phangan was famous in the techno world as well, and A-kun, being a techno fan, knew about it too.

Knowing that history, loving techno beyond measure and composing it ourselves, we had constantly wondered whether making techno without ever having experienced a rave party or LSD wasn't missing something essential.

That said, there was no way to attend anything like that in Japan (in fact it apparently did exist underground), and no way to get hold of those substances either.

Curious techno musicians that we were, we were fascinated by the idea of taking hallucinogens at a rave party and dancing like mad to the techno we loved, and we had come all the way from Japan for an experience we knew nothing about.

From what I had read in books, dancing all night at a rave in beautiful natural surroundings brought on a kind of awakening to something wild and instinctive, and there was talk of oneness with the universe and of mystical experience.

Back then I had no idea what any of that meant, but not understanding it only piqued my curiosity all the more.

# **Drugs**

Discussing this subject in public in Japanese is quite difficult, but the story of my life so far cannot be told while avoiding it, so I want to take the occasion of the full version of the article to talk about it seriously.

Before going any further, I want to start by breaking down a prejudice a great many Japanese people are caught in.

The first thing I want you to know is that every substance, taken into the body, has some effect on body and mind.

This is not limited to medicine: every plant, animal, mineral, metal, chemical, whatever it may be, has some effect on body and mind once you take it into your body.

Most substances have a harmful effect when taken into the body, but some have a favorable one, and those are called health foods or superfoods.

Some substances have a favorable effect against illness, and those are called medicine.

Some substances have a favorable effect on the mind, and those are called psychoactive drugs, or drugs, or medicine.

And most substances produce side effects when taken in excess, and past a certain point they kill you.

That is equally true of sleeping pills and cold medicine, of salt and sugar, of methamphetamine and heroin.

And the standard for whether a thing is legal or illegal is laid down by whoever rules at the time.

Generally, whatever the law of the day has made illegal is perceived as evil, and whatever the law of the day permits is perceived as fine.

An easy example is alcohol.

It was a substance held to be evil during Prohibition in America, as it is in the Islamic world.

Even in modern Japan, the problems of overconsumption and addiction never end.

But because it is legal, culturally it is not treated as a problem.

The relationship between anticancer drugs and cannabis may be just as clear an example.

Anticancer drugs are used legally despite their enormous side effects, while cannabis, originally legal in Japan and possessing a curative effect against cancer, has been illegal in Japan ever since MacArthur's rule.

This ties into medical vested interests and other things that complicate the story, so I will deliberately set it aside here, but I think it is a good example for questioning the standard of legal and illegal.

To sum up so far: every substance taken into the body has some action and some side effect on body and mind, and the ones whose effects are strong become legal or illegal to suit the convenience of whoever rules at the time.

And through the rulers' propaganda, the general public's sense of good and evil about each substance shifts accordingly.

# **Drugs, Good and Evil**

So drugs are good, then? Some will think that, but that is not what I am saying here; the point is that they can become good or evil depending on the intention and the values of the user.

So what is a good use of drugs?

There are all sorts of definitions of good, but as a provisional one here, let it be whatever has a favorable effect on the user's body and mind and a good influence on society as well.

The opposite is evil.

Take alcohol.

As the old phrase has it, sake is the chief of a hundred medicines, and there is an opinion that a glassful of sake with the evening meal is good for the health.

On the other hand, there are people who become alcoholics and destroy their lives, and there are vicious types who get someone blind drunk on purpose in order to assault them.

Take antibiotics.

After emergency surgery following an accident or the like, prescribing antibiotics can keep infection and inflammation down and encourage healing.

On the other hand, taking antibiotics constantly whenever you catch a cold kills off the bacteria throughout your body and can lead to a more serious illness.

Take cannabis.

Inhaled through a vaporizer, the device that turns it into vapor, or eaten in food, it can be consumed with no harm to the body, and it brings relaxation, improved immunity, relief from depression, increased appetite, feelings of closeness and euphoria.

On the other hand, depending on the method of consumption it damages the lungs and liver, and the side effects include being unfit to drive a car while intoxicated, a decline in the will to work, and getting fat from eating too much.

Take LSD.

On the good side, prescribed under the supervision of an experienced doctor or counselor, it can give an understanding of life in a larger frame by way of the subconscious, lead to the treatment of trauma or alcoholism, become the trigger for a spiritual awakening, or bring about psychological independence, and depending on the intention it can be an important tool for awakening human consciousness.

On the other hand, an inexperienced person who takes a large amount can fall into confusion and suffer a mental breakdown. It has also been used in brainwashing operations by the CIA, and by Aum Shinrikyo, the doomsday cult behind the Tokyo subway gas attack, on its own followers, and one of its characteristics is that in the hands of a malicious user it can be put to thoroughly evil use.

Because LSD has such an enormous effect on the mind, deep conspiracies from many directions have been woven around it, and it is not something that can be covered in a few words, but it is a key substance in the history of humanity, so I want to explain it when the time is right.

Take heroin.

Its power to kill pain and remove suffering is strong, so it shows its worth when used as an emergency refuge for terminal patients, for people waiting to die, in emergencies such as a serious accident, or for people in psychological pain great enough to want to kill themselves that instant.

On the other hand, habitual use encourages escape from reality, and through severe addiction it produces people who will commit crimes to get the money to buy heroin.

The examples are endless, but I think you can see that many of the substances called drugs become good or evil depending on the point of view.

What matters, in short, is the method of use and the will of the user: if the rulers of the day enact laws in good faith and the substance is prescribed under the supervision of a doctor with good intentions, it becomes a marvelous tool, and if the opposite is true, it develops into addiction, crime, mafia problems, every kind of trouble there is.

# **A Personal Opinion**

My personal opinion is that some of the hallucinogens, such as cannabis, LSD, magic mushrooms, peyote, and ayahuasca, are a gift to humanity, and that depending on the method of use and the consciousness of the user, they can be put to uses that are extremely valuable to us.

Some will hear this as my recommending drugs and feel uncomfortable, but it is nothing of the sort; the point is that by going beyond the sense of good and evil planted in us by the rulers of the day and looking from a neutral perspective, we can use each substance wisely, putting its good parts to work and avoiding its bad ones.

As for my values right now, I think it is best not to use any drug or chemical at all, and the ideal is to eat food grown without pesticides and to take no alcohol, no tobacco, no cold medicine.

Spiritual exploration through the shamanic medicine that the shamans of South America use brings very favorable effects for humanity, so long as it is used with intention — that is what I believe.

In the entries to come, I want to share all of it exactly as it was.

I would be glad if you read on from an objective viewpoint, one that goes beyond the values of good and evil.
