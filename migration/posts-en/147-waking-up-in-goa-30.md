## Possibilities

What I would do after going home to Japan sat at the back of my mind, and as I turned it over without really thinking about it, a wonderful inspiration arrived.

It was a thought that had crossed my mind before — that if I kept traveling like this, I might become as cool as the seasoned travelers — and now it came back as an inspiration, far more real than before.

Rent in Goa ran about five thousand yen a month, some forty dollars. Food came to around ten thousand yen, about eighty dollars, and even renting a motorcycle on my own was another ten thousand or so.

Add the drug money on top of that, and thirty thousand yen a month — about two hundred and forty dollars — was enough to live on and have the best time possible.

A hundred thousand yen, some eight hundred dollars, would buy three months of that; two hundred thousand, half a year.

Even with the airfare added in, wouldn't three hundred thousand yen — about twenty-four hundred dollars — let me spend half of every year, no, more like nine months of it, living for the fun of it?

Work in Japan for a while and three hundred thousand yen was an amount I could somehow put together.

Thinking it through that way, the future opened up all at once.

## The Next Destination

Now that I had woken up to that possibility, a story I had heard earlier started to nag at me — the one about Manali, a town in the Indian Himalayas.

It was a Himalayan hill station, somewhere to get out of the heat, it had hot springs, and travelers came from all over the world, apparently, to throw trance parties deep in the mountains.

Among the travelers in Goa, the India-lovers and the party-lovers in particular were said to have lived that way for years — Goa in the winter, the Himalayas in the summer, back and forth.

Hot springs being what they are to Japanese, a lot of Japanese had collected there too, and the chance to meet that many interesting people was part of the appeal.

The town was big on growing apples, and they planted cannabis in among the apple trees as a mixed crop, so charas — the good Indian hash resin — went for less than half what it cost in Goa.

Cannabis is illegal, of course, but in India that sort of thing doesn't count for much.

Depending on the state, it is the legal alcohol you have to keep out of sight.

Living costs and prices up in the Himalayas ran about half of Goa's, and with no luxuries, ten thousand yen a month was supposed to cover soaking in the hot springs every day, smoking good cannabis, and days that were nothing but parties.

I had thought Goa was paradise, and hearing that another paradise just like it existed somewhere else got me excited.

Once this journey was over I had meant to stay at my parents' house and build a life around making music, but the interest in travel and trance that had just been born in me overwhelmed that plan.

Having seen this world, I could not go back to pretending I hadn't — I felt like the frog in the well who had learned there was an ocean.

Manali in the summer, then — and with that decided, the possibilities of my own life felt like they would keep opening wide from here on.

With the near future pointed in a direction, I found myself wanting to enjoy the present even more.
