## An Encounter

When I woke up in the morning, the air was warmer still.

It no longer felt anything like the Japan I knew.

Wandering around the ferry, I saw a man with long hair and a beard coming toward me from the other end.

I had long hair and a beard myself.

One look at either of us and you could tell we were travelers.

As if it were the most natural thing in the world, we walked up to each other and said hello.

Just from the way we looked, each of us could see that the other was a traveler, and one with a background in something like hippie culture at that.

Within minutes we were talking about the road, running through where we'd each been.

He—H—said he had just come back from India.

The moment India came up, we hit it off.

You may wonder why the mere mention of India would be enough for that, but that is just what India is like.

I could put reasons to it, but sometimes reasons aren't needed.

If I had to name it, I'd say it's something like being comrades in arms.

Survive India's harsh conditions and enjoy every bit of it, and that alone does it: respect for each other comes on its own, along with a feeling that you belong to the same tribe.

## Tales of Daring

I had always considered my own India trip about as intense and as much fun as travel gets, but H's stories blew me away.

H was a rock 'n' roller, which meant he lived in constant confrontation with a philosophical question: What is rock?

It showed in the way he traveled, too. His whole trip had been rock 'n' roll.

For him at that point, rock meant diving into the deep end without fear.

## The Rainbow Gathering

The story that caught my attention was about an event near Varanasi called the Rainbow Gathering.

It was an event I would later become deeply involved in, and everything that followed grew out of the curiosity his story sparked in me that day.

According to H, it was a kind of huge get-together of hippie travelers, and seasoned travelers, the really intense kind, had come from all over the world.

Those travelers interested me, but what intrigued me even more was the way the event was run.
