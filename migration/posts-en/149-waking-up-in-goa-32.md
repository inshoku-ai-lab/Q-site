## The New Year's Party

The party closing out the century was at Paraiso, with Goa Gil DJing.

The thrill of Christmas had not worn off yet, and we were overjoyed at the chance to experience another Goa Gil party.

This one, on top of that, would run twice as long as the last.

Last time he had DJed for twenty-four hours straight; this time, word was, he would go for forty-eight.

Long past anything a human being should be able to do.

The night of the third was a full moon, so the New Year's party doubled as a full moon party.

The more reasons to celebrate, the better.

We finished our New Year's Eve soba hours before the year actually turned, and set off for the party at dusk.

An enormous crowd had gathered already, the roads were solid with motorcycles, and there was no way a car was getting through.

We pushed into the party through the blasting sound and the din.

Everyone was dancing, beautifully lit by the setting sun, and they all looked like they felt good.

Watching people dance like that feels good in itself.

Joy and happiness were scattered all around us.

As always we headed for the chai stall, drank chai and smoked a joint and got ready to dance.

At the Christmas party I had taken a heavy dose of LSD and danced straight through, but this party ran twice as long.

The short-sprint tactic was not going to work.

This time I decided to start with a bite of an ecstasy tablet and then take the LSD in eighths of a tab, the right amount for me, dose after dose.

My guess was that this way I would not get so high that things went wrong, and could hold a good state for a long stretch.

Lion, who danced at the center of every Goa party, apparently kept going on quarter tabs, one after another, washed down with Coca-Cola he had shaken until the fizz was gone.

## Session 2

The strategy of taking a little at regular intervals worked beautifully.

I danced twelve hours straight and then went home for a while.

The dose had been right, so I fell asleep at once, slept twelve hours, and got my strength back.

The party was still nowhere near over.

Coming back to the dance floor after a solid rest, I found it still at the same miraculous pitch, not faded in the slightest.

Take LSD continuously and you build up a tolerance and it stops working, so this time I went with quarter tabs.

That turned out to be the right amount too, and I danced on without my body locking up.

Eighteen hours this time, and I made it to the very end of a party that ran until the sun went down into the sea.

Thirty hours of dancing in trance, all told.

Dancing flat out for that long is far outside anything normal, but at twenty it was a natural thing for me to do.

It was twice as long as the Christmas party, and the depth of the journey it took us through consciousness was unreal — it worked a far bigger shift in people's awareness than Christmas had.
