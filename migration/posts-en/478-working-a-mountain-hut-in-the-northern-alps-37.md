## Before Dawn

I got up even earlier than usual that day.

The whole point was to see the peak burn red in the morning sun, and once the sun was up, it would be too late.

The night sky was already turning pale, a sign that dawn was close.

I was still dozing in the tent when the sun began to rise.

Its deep red light caught the very tip of the peak.

Little by little the light set the whole summit ablaze, and then the whole mountain.

The burning summit was mirrored upside down on the ice-cold surface of the pond.

The peak on fire and its upside-down twin in the water came together into something beyond spectacular.

It was a brilliance that lasted only a moment, fading away before my eyes.

I fumbled frantically with my camera, trying to capture it.

As time went on, the red peak lost its color bit by bit, until at last it was simply a summit shining in the daylight.

No wonder the mountain veterans had recommended it so eagerly. It was a magnificent sight.

## Home Again

With that sublime view, my mountain vacation came to an end.

Work was waiting for me again that afternoon.

I packed up the tent, erased every trace of my campfire, and headed back to the hut.

It was the first real climb of my life, and it had me hooked on the mountains in one go.

Somewhere inside me I'd been carrying an image of mountain climbing as grueling, sweaty, dangerous, and boring, and this one experience painted right over it.

## Acting Manager

When I got back from my time off, it was the manager's turn to take his.

Chances for a break were few, so we rotated them, one person at a time.

With the manager gone that day, K and I played the part of manager between the two of us.

Not that it was much different from the usual work, but with no manager there to carry all the responsibility and give us that underlying sense of security, I felt a twinge of unease.

K greeted the guests and led the wine toast.

I took care of everything to do with the generator and made the scheduled check-in over the radio.

On my mountain vacation I'd felt the independence of being alone in nature, and back at the hut I got a different kind of independence: a mountain hut with no manager.

Both were experiences of standing on my own in the wilds of nature, gentle and terrifying at once and beyond any human power, and both shook the very foundation of my existence as a physical being.
