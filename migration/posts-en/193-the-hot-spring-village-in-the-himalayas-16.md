## World Class

C wasn't the only world-class Japanese there.

G, the relentlessly positive guy I wrote about in an earlier post, had come up to Vashisht a few days before and was at this party too.

He was in his usual African getup, moving to a wild, earthy rhythm and handing out smiles all over the party.

The shine coming off him was unreal.

Not to be outdone by personalities of that class, every last one of us danced like mad.

The Israelis who were throwing the party danced hardest of all.

A notch below on personality, but on stamina they matched anyone.

They danced on army-built endurance and youth, both at full output.

## Sunlight

The party kept building steadily, and we met the sunrise.

As the light came up, we could see exactly how beautiful the landscape was that we had been dancing in all this time.

Far off stood the snow-covered range of the Himalayas, glowing pink where the morning sun struck it.

LSD blows your pupils wide and sharpens everything you feel, and between the two, the beauty in front of my eyes went past anything normal.

Everyone was inside the same rush, roaring, dancing in pure joy.

The Section Chief, N, came over to where I was sitting and resting and delivered his famous line: "So... we meet again..."

I'd heard it a few times at parties in Goa; to N it was a toast to a fated meeting across time and space.

He only ever came out with it when he was completely gone on LSD and shaking with feeling at how miraculous reality was.

His eyes would be a little wet.

Among us it was treated as a running gag, but when it came out, everyone lit up.

## Afternoon

The party went on, and by a few hours after the sun was up plenty of people were worn out and sitting it out.

Through all of it, the ones still dancing were the world-class ones I wrote about in the previous post.

The beauty who looked like a model, the karate men, the ninja, and the African, all of them out in the sunlight, still exploding with personality.

A few of the ex-army Israelis mixed in with them, and I worked my way in too, with the stamina I still had from scaffolding work.

Honored to be dancing with the world class, I kept on expressing nothing but the pulse of life itself.
