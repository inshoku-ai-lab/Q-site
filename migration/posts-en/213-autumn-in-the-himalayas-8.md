## The Hot Springs

I had danced myself soaked at the party, and a hot spring after a party feels better than just about anything, so I headed for the baths alone, still a little high on the LSD.

The other two, deep in their bad trips, were in no shape for a hot spring.

Rinsing the sweat off felt good, and I enjoyed the water.

But the two of them were on my mind, so I kept it short and went back to the guesthouse.

Unfortunately I had been right to worry: they still had not come out of it.

F had made the party baba angry, and he was in full panic, genuinely believing the man was coming to the guesthouse to kill him.

An open door left him unable to settle, and he kept begging me to close it and lock it.

B, who until recently had been a star of the rugby team at a certain well-known university and was built like a slab of muscle, was not afraid of the baba; instead he had fallen into a bad trip of pure mortification, sorry that their own stupidity had cut my night at the party short.

Once you have taken LSD enough times, you learn that a bad trip like that comes from letting your thoughts run too far into the negative, and, out of the plain need to be able to play all the way through, you learn to steer them — but on a first trip there is nothing to be done.

Since nothing was going to help until the drug wore off in its own time, I locked the door to put F at ease, and told B, over and over until it finally took, that his leaving the party early did not bother me in the least.

So the three of us holed up in the room and spent the whole day smoking cannabis and listening to mellow music.

By night the LSD had worn off, and around the time F understood that the baba was not coming to kill him, I sent them back to their own room and went to bed.

## The Next Day

The next day, with the whole situation finally clear to them, the two of them saw how stupid they had been, and learned how frightening it is when LSD runs your thinking too far in one direction.

These two, jocks who ran at full volume all the time, had dropped into deep reflection mode and gave off the air of athletes serving a suspension they had handed themselves, and even so they took me out to a restaurant to apologize and to thank me.

What they bought me was a sizzler, Chinese food as India interprets it.

I have never seen it in any other country.

Meat and croquettes and vegetables go on an iron plate and are cooked until they are scorching, and the waiter carries it to your table hissing and crackling so loudly that the whole restaurant turns to look — a dish equal parts delight and embarrassment.

It was my favorite thing to eat in Vashisht, and any time there was a reason to celebrate, a sizzler was what I had.
