## S's Work

S had nothing to do with the everyday work of a mountain hut, like cooking meals or folding futons.

He was a mountain specialist: besides maintaining the trails, he took on the mountain work that amateurs weren't up to, like rescues in an emergency.

Over those forty years, he had carried out countless rescues and seen countless climbers die.

## His Stories

Stuffing himself with the best food J could make and washing it down with the best liquor, S told us a few stories of his own.

Out in the wilderness of the mountains, in a world where people's lives are at stake, strange things happen once in a while.

One day, S was walking along a trail when a beautiful butterfly landed right in front of him.

As he walked up to it, the butterfly flew off and landed again a little farther away.

He walked up again, and again it flew off and landed a little farther away.

The butterfly just kept doing this.

It never flew away for good, only kept its distance, never too close and never too far, and S began to find it strange.

## Guided

After a while of this, the butterfly turned off the trail and came to rest off to one side.

It had been flying on ahead of him the whole way, and now it had left the trail, so S, puzzled, decided to follow it.

For an ordinary climber, climbing down off the trail there would have been too dangerous, but S was in a class of his own.

He kept following the butterfly down the treacherous slope.

And there at the bottom, of all things, lay a human corpse, dried out and shriveled.

## Afterward

A few days later, S came back with the mountain rescue team and brought the body up.

It turned out later that the body belonged to a climber who had gone missing two years before.

S had been part of the rescue effort back then too, he said, but no matter how hard they searched, they couldn't find the missing climber, and in the end the search was called off.

The way S saw it, the body, left lying out there all that time, had wanted someone to find it and lay it to rest.

That longing, he said, must have been what moved the butterfly...
