## Up at Three

We got up at three, when the alarm we had set went off.

The sleeping pills had put us into a deep sleep, and we woke up clearheaded.

The plan had worked, apparently.

The first thing I saw when I got up was the pillow — about half of it burned away, charred and soaking wet.

Once our heads were finally working properly again, we remembered how idiotic the night before had been and howled with laughter.

Getting to set off for the Christmas party laughing was, I suppose, a small mercy.

## The Venue

The Christmas party was supposed to be at Paraiso, along the beach, but the police shut it down, so a different party sprang up in the jungle of South Anjuna.

And then came the shock.

The organizers were charging admission.

Putting on an event and charging for it is perfectly normal, and you may be wondering what there was to be shocked about.

The appeal of Goa is that the place runs on a completely different set of dynamics from ordinary society.

The same went for the parties: until then, every one of them had been put on for free.

More often than not the organizer was a large-scale drug dealer, and the setup was this: a free party drew a crowd, the crowd meant drugs sold well, and the sales paid for the party.

So the people who came to a party were, in a sense, members of the mafia family, the hippie family, and taking money from family was simply not done.

Organizers and guests were nearly all criminals, but they each carried a pride of their own, something like an outlaw's code of honor, along with a rebellious spirit set against the whole social system, so seeing Goa's parties invaded by commercialism made them angry and low.

If this was the first Goa party you had ever come to, you would take it in without a second thought, but those who had known the old glory were disappointed.
