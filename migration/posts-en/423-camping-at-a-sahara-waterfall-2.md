## Mountain Man

Back in Japan, Y had shared a place with his best friend from his Canada days.

That roommate worked for a company that dealt in climbing gear, which gave Y close ties to people in the mountaineering world.

He often helped out at the clearance sales during the year-end rush, apparently, and through those connections he could get climbing gear cheap.

For me, this was the first time I'd been camping since elementary school, and for I-chan it was very nearly the first time ever.

So between us we knew next to nothing about camping, and we were going to have to figure out everything on the spot.

As for gear, we had a tent and sleeping bags, a pot and a knife, and that was about it; beyond the barest basics we had nothing, and we had no idea what we should have had.

The one who became our guide was Y.

## What Y Knew

Y was several years older than us, and he didn't just have more life experience; where he really stood out was in how much he knew.

He was smart to begin with, and a real thinker, enough of one to major in philosophy in college.

Add the well-read side that came with his job as a writer, and he had built up a huge store of knowledge.

What he knew about camping and living outdoors was especially impressive, and I learned a lot from him.

## Mountain Gear

Thanks to his connections, Y could buy mountain gear for a fraction of the price, so he was well equipped with high-quality stuff.

To people who climb, this kind of gear is nothing special, but to anyone who doesn't know it, it looks astonishingly convenient.

And that mountain gear turned out to be excellent travel gear, too.

Back then I knew none of this, so I just used whatever I could get cheap.

That was the only way I knew to travel well.

What I learned from Y would serve me well on the road later on.
