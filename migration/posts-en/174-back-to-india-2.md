## Delhi

The moment I came out of the airport, taxi touts and pushy vendors and beggars came surging at me in a pack.

Only a few hours out of the calm air of Japan, I had plunged completely into another world.

Their eyes had a hard glitter, brimming with how serious they were about living.

I had missed that desperate air, and their passion for living came flowing into my heart.

In that instant my soul got its shine back, and it felt like I had returned to myself.

I savored the joy of it — I had come home to India.

The sheer force of them coming at me was annoying, but the familiarity of it loosened my cheeks into a grin.

My senses were still tuned to the air of Japan, and I nearly got led away, but I pulled myself together — this is India — and stopped where I stood.

I pulled my cheeks tight again, looked away, fired off NO after NO, and walked away fast.

It isn't the attitude I want to take, but let your guard down here and you get stripped of everything, roots and all — that is what India's life force does.

This was no field on a high plateau; the place had the energy of a tropical jungle.

From the very first minute you have to throw your whole strength into the back-and-forth, and that desperation about staying alive set my heart dancing.

"This is it, India!" — the shout inside my head.

For anyone with a normal set of nerves, I imagine, this kind of back-and-forth is enough to break the spirit, but to a traveler who has taken India in all the way, it was something like a stimulant, a charge that livened up the whole business of being alive.

## Haggling 1

I push the beggars aside, speak to the driver of one of the three-wheeled motorbikes they call auto-rickshaws, and bargain with him to take me where I'm going.

Here too, of course, there is the back-and-forth.

Whether the price he names is a fair one I have no way of knowing, since this is my first time in the city, but I know very well that nearly every auto-rickshaw driver in India opens at about four times the fair price.

So I put on the attitude that says, "I've been coming to India for years and I know the fair price. You're going to try a number like that on a man like me? Don't talk nonsense," and I meet the figure he has named with a look of shock, then of exasperation, then a laugh through the nose.

An act: no interest in haggling at all, just briskly moving on to look for the next decent auto-rickshaw.

The driver sees this, comes after me, and names half the price.

Without dropping the exasperated-face act, I demand half of that half.

And I still don't drop the posture of a man about to stroll off.
