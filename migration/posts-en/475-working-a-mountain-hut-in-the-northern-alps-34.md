## Nature's True Face

A mind honed sharp by a matter of life and death brings out the true face of nature.

Of course, a mountain with none of the dust of human society on it would have been literally teeming with life to begin with.

Add a sharpened mind to that, and the green takes on an even brighter glow.

My eyes were probably blazing.

I was beside myself with excitement over my first real taste of mountaineering.

Climbing usually means hauling a heavy pack and suffering for it, but after months of working at the hut, the hard part didn't bother me in the least.

I was just enjoying the climb, pure and simple.

## The Mountain Hut

After hours of walking, I came to a signpost pointing the way to the hot spring.

Beaten by the sun, the wind, and the rain, the signpost was falling apart, but I could just about make out what it said.

At the very bottom of the hollow stood an old mountain hut.

Like the signpost, the hut was falling apart, crushed under the weight of winter snow.

Its middle had caved in, sagging in the shape of an arch.

The roof sagged, and so did the floor.

Roof, walls, paint, all of it was in tatters, and it wouldn't be much of a stretch to call the place a ruin.

You don't normally get to see a building that far gone in Japan.

Hardly any climbers make it out to that hut, so there's probably no money coming in, not even enough to lay a new floor.

It was a world away from our hut, which was flush with cash.

But that hut had something ours didn't: a deep kind of love, and a character all its own.

It even had a sort of sacred air about it, like the holy tree that stands at the heart of a village.

Among the staff at mountain huts everywhere, there's a sort of custom: any hut worker can stay at any hut for free.

But it's more of an unspoken understanding than a hard-and-fast rule.

Each hut decides for itself.

I held back and didn't take advantage of it.

I felt shy.

Looking back now, I really should have.

I'm sure it would have led to some wonderful encounters and experiences.
