## Behind the Miracle

People were overjoyed at the first rain to fall on the desert in four years, but this miracle of weather brought more than joy.

There wasn't much of it, but it was still enough to bring down the temperature of the desert in the dead of winter.

And what happens when the temperature drops in some places and stays the same in others? The wind blows.

And what happens when that wind blows across a dead-flat desert with nothing at all in its way? It turns into gusts full of sand.

Those gusts blew away every structure being built for the festival.

## Gandalf's Gloom

The day after the gusts, Gandalf came to the hotel looking grim.

Three days before the festival was due to open, everything built so far had been blown away.

For someone responsible for organizing the festival, it was the worst disaster imaginable.

All the desperate work up to that point had come to nothing, and there was no time to rebuild.

About the only thing anyone could do was clear away what had blown down.

To help Gandalf out, we decided to go to the festival site and pitch in with the cleanup.

## Where Disaster Struck

At the festival site, lots of people had gathered and were taking apart the wrecked structures.

It looked like they were going to use what they salvaged to build the bare essentials again, like a stage.

They had built a beautiful stage, but it was gone now.

Every large structure had been wrecked by the gusts, but the small, heavy things were completely untouched.

The make-believe Japanese garden that I-chan and I had built hadn't budged an inch.

The rocks we'd lined up on the desert were still just rocks, same as ever.

Everything the others had worked so desperately to build was gone, while the thing we'd thrown together was still standing, and that made me glad and a little guilty at the same time.

But what had happened had happened.

All anyone could do was keep going with what was left.

The gusts seemed to have pulled the volunteer workers together.

They had been working under a heavy load of stress to finish in time, and suddenly all of it had blown away.

No ideals, no pretenses, nothing but getting the bare minimum built before the festival started.

That emergency brought everyone together, and they pushed on toward opening day.
