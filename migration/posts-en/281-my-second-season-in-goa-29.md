## Inside Another Dimension

There is no way to describe the other-dimensional world I experienced on DMT, but I want to try anyway to translate it into the Japanese of the three-dimensional world.

As I went deeper into that dimension, dazzling, beautiful geometric patterns spread out in front of me.

But it felt less like watching them from the outside than like being a part of that world.

A strange sound rang through it, pleasant and unpleasant at the same time.

A quiet sound, and at the same time a sound with the feel of twisting.

What came back to me later was that it was exactly like the sound I had been hearing in Vashisht, the time I smoked brown sugar and left my body.

I was floating there, in a sea of that strange drifting sound and those beautiful geometric patterns.

After a while I began to feel that this other-dimensional world was a being with substance.

There was no substance there the eye could see, no outline to make out, and yet that presence was really there, and I felt it with a clear, palpable certainty.

What it was, I couldn't say, but something was clearly there.

It was a feeling like that, and there is no explaining it.

## Back to the Real World

How long I stayed in that world, I have no idea.

It feels like it was two hours, and it also feels like it was three minutes.

It was a world that was at once infinite and a single instant.

When I came to, I was looking at a mandala of blood-vessel patterns spreading across the inside of my eyelids.

Little by little the feeling of my body came back, and I understood as well that I was lying down.

And then it came back to me that I had been gone on DMT.

But the experience had been so overwhelming that I couldn't move right away, and I lay there a while longer, waiting for my mind to settle.

At last I opened my eyes and met the eyes of S and I-chan.

The whole thing, they said, had taken only about twenty minutes.

From the outside, apparently, nothing much had seemed to change.

I sat up slowly and handed the next turn over to I-chan.

At her request, I made a point of saying nothing about what the trip had been like.

Say the wrong thing and I would only stoke her fear.
