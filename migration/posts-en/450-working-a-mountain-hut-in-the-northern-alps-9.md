## K

The one I found it easiest to get close to was K.

Most of the seasonal staff had come from all over the country, but he was a local, from Nagano.

His parents were apparently former hippie travelers who, after wandering Japan and the world, had settled down in a hippie village deep in the mountains of Nagano.

These days they seemed to live fairly conventional lives, but K, raised by such free-spirited traveler parents, still had something of that free spirit in him.

He was only eighteen, but his upbringing had left him mature in many respects.

He was especially mature emotionally, and the sight of him playing guitar and singing with real feeling was a comfort to the rest of us working at the hut.

## Personality

At first I hadn't noticed, but as time passed, I came to realize that the people working at this mountain hut all had quite strong personalities.

Maybe strong personalities are simply drawn here, but more likely, they adapt easily to the unusual environment of mountain life, which is why so many of them keep working here year after year.

Communal life at a mountain hut was never easy, and there was hardly any private time.

For someone who loved nature, loved being around people, and loved working, it was the perfect workplace.

This was something travelers in India had in common as well: when people drawn to a place full of character spent time meeting other people with character, their personalities seemed to amplify and reinforce one another.

## Regulars

That strength of personality stood out especially in people who had worked at the hut for years.

I was amazed by this crew of distinctive characters.

The man who ran the cable-car relay hut — somewhere between "uncle" and "big brother" in age — had a wonderful character of his own, and the manager who had climbed the mountain together with us stood out just as much.

The longer someone had worked at the hut, the stronger that quality seemed to grow.

Everyone presumably had their own mix of extroverted and introverted traits to begin with, but it felt like the experience of working at a mountain hut intensified each person's personality, whichever direction it already leaned.
