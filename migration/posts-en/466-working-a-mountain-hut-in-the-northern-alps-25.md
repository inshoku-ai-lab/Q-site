## The Source

We climbed the cliff carrying a section of the pipe and the tools for joining it.

As we went up, we let the pipe trail down below our feet, laying the line that would carry water down to the valley.

Coming down the mountain into the valley, we'd used the trail, but there's no trail up the slope where the water springs out, so we scrambled up over a steep heap of rubble.

Frankly, it was pretty dangerous, but neither J nor N seemed to care much, and they charged straight up.

For people who live in the mountains, this kind of thing is probably just part of life.

When we reached the source, there was a big plastic container sitting there.

It had apparently been put in years ago.

## The Work

Next came the job of fitting the pipe to the outlet near the bottom of the container.

The water collected in this bucket at the source would run through the pipe all the way down to the valley. That was the idea.

But this part would not go smoothly.

The spot was cramped, the footing was bad, and we had to fit the pipe onto an outlet with water gushing out of it.

Getting the pipe on meant pushing back against the force of the water, and that was what gave us so much trouble.

Once we finally had the pipe fixed firmly at the source, it was back down the cliff to the valley.

We had to check whether the connection was working.

## The Valley

The connection seemed to have worked: water was shooting hard out of the end of the pipe.

Next we got ready to run the water arriving in the valley into an oil drum and, from there, pump it up to the hut.

From behind a huge rock in the valley, a good five meters (sixteen feet) tall, we dragged out the gasoline pump and started the engine.

The last time anyone had started it was a full eight months earlier, and it had been left sitting out in the snow ever since, so getting the engine going took a considerable amount of effort.

Still, the thing had been built tough to begin with, and the engine did start.

With the engine confirmed running, the next job was connecting the pipe up toward the hut.

I stayed behind alone in the valley, and J and N connected the pipe as they made their way back up to the hut.

When the pipe was all connected, they would call me on the walkie-talkie, and I would run the engine and send the water up. That was the plan.
