## The Christmas Party

Thanks to the magic LSD D had given us, we had a fantastic time on Christmas night.

D, E, and the rest of the crowd were funny and cool, and they told us all sorts of great stories from their travels.

One of the people there, a Swiss woman named M, had kept her travels going by working as a hostess in Roppongi, Tokyo's nightlife district.

Winters she spent having fun in Goa, and summers she made her money hostessing in Roppongi.

She had us laughing with her broken Japanese.

Seasoned travelers that they were, they knew exactly what it's like to get by in broken language, and once they saw that our English wasn't great, they spoke slowly, using surprisingly simple, easy words, so that even we could enjoy a conversation in English.

## Noise

That night's party went on until nearly dawn.

Unfortunately, the room next door had ordinary tourists in it, people who had come to see the desert, and they came over to complain about the instruments we were playing, but a tough-looking French bassist glared at them until they left.

The Tough Guy insisted that at Christmas, people have a right to party.

The poor travelers apparently got the hotel to give them a different room after that, which I do feel bad about.

## The Next Day

The Christmas Eve party was over; the next day we slept until well past noon, and around evening, just as we were slowly starting to get up, the Christmas party's second wave arrived.

The festival staff team had heard that we travelers had thrown ourselves a party on Christmas Eve, and saying they wanted a party too, they had dropped their festival work and rushed over.

The four of them were on magic mushrooms, and with nowhere else to go, they piled into our room.

They all seemed stressed out from their volunteer work, and they were tripping with a bit of anger in them.

It wasn't a very pleasant vibe, but we'd been wrecked ourselves and caused other people trouble plenty of times, so we couldn't exactly throw them out.

In the traveler's world, you look after each other.

In the end we let them use the room until nearly midnight, but it felt less like friends wanting to have fun together and more like people using our place because they had nowhere else to go, and the day ended with me not feeling very good about it.

For better or worse, it was a Christmas that taught me what goes around comes around.
