# The Way F Lives

At the risk of belaboring it, I want to stay with F, the homeless guy, a little longer.

F has no notion of earning money whatsoever, and not a speck of him wants to live a normal life, or to go back to Japan and rejoin society.

While I'm at it, he has also thrown away any interest in romance, any involvement with women — and beyond that, even real friendship, even any relationship built on mutual trust.

Being that kind of man, he gets cursed out by travelers who have met him and gotten reasonably friendly with him: "a disgrace to the Japanese," "a dog that tags along after you begging for a meal."

And F doesn't care about any of those opinions in the slightest.

Because he rejects social life this completely, he can get the opposite reaction from India-struck travelers who approve of the world-renouncing values of a Hindu ascetic: respect, and now and then a donation toward his way of living.

He squirrels that money away, and through the winter high season he lives off begging from the tourists coming in, enjoying his days, while through the summer rains, the off-season, he buys food with what he has saved little by little and waits out the rain alone in a half-built building where construction stopped.

The one opinion all the travelers who met him shared was this: "A hundred times happier than being homeless in Japan."

## On the Nature of Things

I said before that, in my own view, what LSD does is send every piece of information to the brain without sorting any of it out.

That view has no evidence behind it, but it is also what nearly everyone who has taken LSD feels to be true.

Not sorting the information, put another way, connects to the Buddhist idea of seeing things exactly as they are, and it also means holding the perspective of a genius artist, who sees the beauty a thing has in itself.

Because that unusual, widened perspective was there to be had, many artists were drawn to LSD during the hippie movement of the sixties.

It isn't hard to imagine: see the nature of a thing as it actually is, and spiritual inspiration follows, artistic inspiration follows, and in the end inspiration for life as a whole.

Being able to get that inspiration — that is the bait that pulls people in.

People who don't know LSD tend to picture it like any other drug, something you take for the pleasure, but that isn't it.

The fact is, taking LSD gives you no pleasure at all.

LSD works like an amplifier for information: what information comes in — the outer world — combined with how you receive it — the inner world — is what makes the experience pleasant or unpleasant.

That comes through well in the title of a book by the English writer Aldous Huxley, The Doors of Perception.

I am a big fan of his, though unfortunately he was part of the Cabal too.

LSD is the key that opens those doors of perception.

Not a big machine that pries the door open — a small key you slide into the lock.

All LSD does is interfere with the part of the brain that sorts and discards information.

So the dose is a tiny one, around a hundred micrograms.

Being that small, it has no effect on the body at all, and no physical side effects at all.

There is no direct pleasure, and in its place, no addictiveness either, and it has properties completely unlike drugs such as cocaine, heroin, and meth.
