## The Japanese Guy Who Loved Dub

There was a Japanese guy I ran into at just about every dub night I went to.

He had an afro and a pair of round eyes.

Seeing him that often, we naturally fell into talking.

He was a didgeridoo player, he told me, making his own music and putting out CDs.

His artist name was Goma da Didgeridoo.

The name came from those eyes, apparently — they looked like the eyes of Goma-chan, the baby seal in the cartoon.

When I got home and told I-chan about him, it turned out she had been a big fan since back in Japan and even owned his CDs.

Put a didgeridoo to his lips and nobody could touch him — a genius, she said, who had taken first place in a contest in Australia.

## Playing on the Street

Then one day I-chan was out walking through town, and there was Goma, playing on the street.

On a corner in our own neighborhood, she said, blowing the didgeridoo and selling CDs.

I was at work and not home, but I-chan worked up the nerve to invite him back to the house and set him up with tea and cake.

That connection took us to a few of his shows later on.

We never got close, though, and by the time we left London we had drifted out of touch.

## What Became of Goma

Just recently it occurred to me to wonder what Goma was up to these days, and when I looked him up, what I found was staggering.

In 2009 he was in a major accident, lost every one of his memories, and was left with higher brain dysfunction — the lasting cognitive damage of a brain injury.

After that, while keeping up his rehabilitation, he began making dot paintings.

The didgeridoo and dot painting are both Aboriginal traditions, so there is probably some deep connection there — past lives, or something along those lines.

Lately I find myself turning his strange fate over in my mind, and it leaves me with an odd feeling.

His story since then is told here.
