## The Notting Hill Carnival

N was into reggae, and there was one event she looked forward to every year; she told us about it more times than I could count.

It was the Notting Hill Carnival, and the whole stretch of west London where we lived would turn into one big festival, reggae booming out at deafening volume.

It had started out in Notting Hill itself, a small neighborhood where a lot of Jamaican immigrants lived, but it was fun enough that other neighborhoods began joining in, until a fairly large area with no connection to Jamaican immigrants at all was putting on the carnival.

The way N told it, intersections all over the place were closed off to traffic, enormous speakers were set down in them, and the whole place turned into a wild party — but looking at our usually quiet streets, it was hard to picture.

## The Day Itself

What the carnival would actually be like, I could not imagine — but when the day came, I was made to feel it whether I liked it or not.

That morning I woke to the sound of the window glass in our room buzzing.

For a second I had no idea what it was, and then it clicked: speakers pounding out reggae bass were shaking the panes.

I couldn't hear the reggae itself, but the low end rolling out of those giant woofers was shaking the air over the whole neighborhood.

We jumped straight out of bed, hyped, and went out the front door.

At an intersection about ten meters (thirty feet) from the house stood two enormous speakers, each roughly three meters (ten feet) square, blasting reggae.

Loud enough to explain the shaking glass.

It had never crossed my mind that cheerful reggae would end up playing right in front of our house.

A small intersection in a normally quiet residential neighborhood.

Delighted, we went back inside, got ourselves ready, and set out into the town to enjoy it.
