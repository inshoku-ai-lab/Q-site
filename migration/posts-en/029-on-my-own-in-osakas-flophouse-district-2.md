# The Rebellious Phase That Came Late

Now that a free life on my own had begun and I could express myself, the rebellious streak that had been so violently suppressed in my junior high years, never able to find an outlet, began to surface.

Sublimated at the right time and in the right form, that rebelliousness would have helped my ego become independent, but having been suppressed and then arriving late, it came out all the sicker and more warped.

There were no parents or teachers close at hand to rebel against, so it emerged as rebellion against society.

Having no one to rebel against directly may have suited a coward like me.

It was not the kind of antisocial nature that leads to actual crime, and it was not the transparent rebellion of a junior high student either; being eighteen, it was rebellion in the form of antisocial thought.

Perfectly aware that I could not opt out of society, that I could not live without relying on people and on society, I had still grown sick of the taboos and the deceit and the hypocrisy and the genteel masks people wore.

![A white dove with spread wings flying past dark silhouetted birds under a hazy sun](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog29-1.jpg)

In keeping with the degree of suppression and brokenness and sickness in how I was born and raised, conditions like being a teenager suddenly freed from the discipline of the Jehovah's Witnesses and living in Nishinari Ward must have come together.

Antisocial thought struck me as cool, and it became part of my style.

# Abunai Ichigo

Around this time, browsing in a bookstore, I found a mook — a Japanese magazine-book — called Abunai Ichigo, "Dangerous No. 1," whose tagline was "a skull-splitting book dedicated to the scumbags of all Japan."

It was extreme and vulgar and depraved beyond measure, and the first time I saw it the shock felt like my skull literally splitting open.

It disgusted me, but morbid curiosity won out and I decided to buy it.

A book of sleazy perverts, of the underworld, of crime and drugs.

The exact opposite of the Christian values and the environment I was raised in.

It was an outrageous book, one you really could call a forbidden text of devil worship.

The unknown, suspect worlds written about in that book set my imagination fiercely alight.

# Digital Hardcore Recordings

As my interests and inclinations changed, my taste in music changed along with them, and I came to listen to quite extreme hardcore punk techno.

My theme song at the time was a track called "Fuck All" by the German underground band Atari Teenage Riot.

Destroy everything, fuck it all, smash it, start a riot —

a destructive song that just keeps screaming that.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o8Fkpbt82Dc

The artists on this label, Digital Hardcore Recordings, mostly had a similar flavor, and I liked them enough to collect every release.

I wonder now how I ever listened to music that crazy, but perhaps, with the violent warping I carried in my heart, the wavelengths matched, and that song was healing music to me.

It filled my heart and relaxed me.

It was precisely because of that period that I felt such a strong connection to a French film I saw in a theater, La Haine, released in Japan as Hatred.

It depicts young people living in a public housing project in the slums of Paris who get caught up in a riot, and it mapped onto the environment I was born and raised in; I felt as though I had become the film's protagonist.

![Close-up of a bearded man with a shaved head gripping a chain-link fence, seen from behind](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog29-2.jpg)

Out of admiration for the film's protagonist, and to psych myself up, I shaved my head into the same near-skinhead buzz cut.

It meant a break with my old self, the good little Jehovah's Witness boy raised in a cage.

Things like blond hair were against the rules at my part-time job, but a near-skinhead buzz cut broke none of them, so it became my own way of expressing rebellion against society.

I was psyched up, but as bad luck would have it I had cut my hair on New Year's Eve, and the icy wind suddenly hitting my head gave me a cold.
