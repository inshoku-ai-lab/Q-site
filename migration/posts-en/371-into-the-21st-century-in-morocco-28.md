## The Real LSD

But the LSD I'd known until then had apparently been giving me a fake version of the experience.

LSD usually has all sorts of things mixed into it, and it seemed those additives had been blocking what LSD is really like.

The LSD D gave us, he said, came straight from a lab in Switzerland.

LSD was first developed in Switzerland, so maybe there are chemists or labs there that have kept it as pure as it was back then.

Normally, taking LSD makes you jittery, or gives you a tight feeling in your chest, or makes your tongue feel strange, but D's stuff did none of that.

It felt so much like normal that at first I didn't even notice it working.

## The Effect

But about an hour in, I-chan and I looked at each other in amazement.

That was how naturally the sense of another dimension that LSD brings had slipped into ordinary life.

There was nothing forced or unreal about it; before we knew it, the everyday had been upgraded into a kind of super-everyday.

Nothing felt foreign or unpleasant at all, and the beauty I'd find in any everyday view grew in a natural way, and the beauty I'd find in any everyday music grew in a natural way.

Every emotion and every sense had sharpened, yet at the same time I felt relaxed and full of peace.

It was the first time LSD had ever relaxed my body.

If I'd wanted to sleep, I probably could have.

## D's World

This, we realized, was the world D was living in, and it made complete sense to us.

It felt like passing through a four-dimensional tunnel with stillness, beauty, and comfort all held together.

D's extraordinary gravitas, his depth as a human being — it all seemed to show through in the quality of his LSD.
