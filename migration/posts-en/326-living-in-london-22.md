## Dhaka

We were waiting for D to arrive in London, expecting him any day now, but no further word came.

I figured he would surely make it before the festival started, but the day of the festival came and went with no sign of him, and then an email arrived saying he was still in Dhaka, the capital of Bangladesh.

In the end, his flight to London wasn't settled until a good week after the festival was over.

He got himself as far as a station near us, and we went to pick him up.

We let him unpack, gave him tea and time to settle, and then asked what on earth had kept him in Bangladesh for two whole weeks.

Asking by email had gotten us nothing but "I'll tell you when I get to London," so we had been waiting right up until this moment.

## The Reason

D's plan was to fly out of Thailand, change planes in Dhaka, and come on to London.

On that first flight, he said, he put his passport in the pocket on the back of the seat in front of him, the one they keep the magazines in, and fell asleep.

And while D slept, his passport vanished.

Where it went, nobody knows, but stolen is the reasonable guess.

Anything could have happened.

Losing your passport inside an airplane is not something you hear about every day.

He had already cleared immigration out of Thailand, and now transit was out of the question — he could not so much as enter the country.

After a very long wait and the formalities, he finally got out of the airport.

But it was a temporary, special sort of permit to stay, so he couldn't move around freely and had to remain in Dhaka.

He contacted the Japanese embassy and spent about two weeks in the city doing the most tedious things there are — documents, procedures, waiting.

## Shrimp

Dhaka had nothing in it to see as a tourist, and by his account the city was simply poor and the conditions bad.

There was nothing to do to fill the endless dull waiting, so he just walked around the city.

And in the middle of all that, what he found was a shrimp farm.

Bangladesh sits at the lower reaches of the Ganges, so it has a great deal of wetland, which apparently makes it good country for farming shrimp.

Prices there are extremely low to begin with, and frozen shrimp, he said, can be had for very little.

Looking a little pleased with himself, he asked whether I wanted to go into the import business with him, but unfortunately I had no interest at all.
