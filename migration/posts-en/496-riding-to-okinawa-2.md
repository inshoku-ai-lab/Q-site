## The Journey Begins

The next day, I left my friend's house early in the morning.

I was going to ride my bike from Himeji to my mother's place in Osaka, an eight-hour trip.

Of course, I could have just taken the train home again, but someone about to bicycle all the way to Okinawa had no business getting cold feet over a distance like this.

Down the line, this was a bike journey that even had the African continent in its sights.

True to its 150,000-yen price tag, about twelve hundred dollars, the bike let me pedal along at astonishing speed.

The difference in quality was so great that it made me wonder what the bikes I'd been riding until then had even been.

With a bike this good, I felt confident that riding to Okinawa wasn't out of the question.

The route was flat, which helped, and I made it back home to Osaka with surprising ease.

After my days of grueling physical labor at the mountain hut, it was hardly much of an adventure.

## Nouveau Riche, in a Small Way

Now that I had the bike, I threw myself into preparing for the journey in earnest.

I had about 800,000 yen saved from the mountain hut, around sixty-five hundred dollars, and since I had never held that much money in my life, I splashed out on travel gear.

It was the mindset of the nouveau riche, in a small way.

After years of scraping by on the road with the bare minimum of gear, I swung the other way, and on top of that, Y, whom I'd met in Morocco, had shown me how wonderful mountain gear could be, and I wanted to know more. So I went on a spree, buying up the very best travel gear there was.

That mindset, nouveau riche in a small way, had me buy a 55,000-yen rain jacket, about $450, and a 40,000-yen tent, about $320.

Looking back now, I know I could have gotten products just as good for a third of the price, but that mindset, nouveau riche in a small way, wanted the ego boost of buying top of the line.

In the end, 300,000 yen of my 800,000 in savings, around twenty-four hundred dollars, went on travel gear.

Whether those prices were reasonable or not, my satisfaction and my confidence in the journey were at an all-time high.

As long as I had a body toughened by life in the mountains, the knowledge and experience I'd picked up at the hut, top-of-the-line camping gear, and a bike, I felt I could keep traveling no matter what.
