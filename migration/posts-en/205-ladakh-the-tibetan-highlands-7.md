## The Local Bus

For the trip back from Leh to Manali I traveled by local bus.

The way up from Manali had been on a long-distance bus meant for tourists, so it had come with some comfort, but the local bus stood for the reality of India.

The seats were narrow and the whole thing was filthy, but it was overwhelmingly cheaper, needed no reservation, and made no overnight stop along the way.

The bus set out early in the morning and kept going all day with barely a break.

I wanted to watch the landscape slide past outside, but the windows were small and clouded, not built for enjoying the view.

That's the difference between a tourist bus and a local bus.

I gave up on the view and listened to music instead, sleeping and waking and sleeping again, until we reached the last stop.

This was still the dry high country, but I had come as far as a town called Keylong, close to Manali.

From there Manali was half a day away.

The trip up had ended up taking three days and two nights, and the way back looked like it would take half that, which was a small relief.

## Friends

In Keylong I met a hippie-looking Japanese couple, and we hit it off right away; they were out looking for a room too, so we decided to look together.

They lived in Okinawa, apparently, and told me how good life there was.

Okinawa is Japan and yet not Japan, they said, and Japanese people who have been traveling too long to settle back into Japan wash up there.

It was the part of Asia where Japanese still worked, they said.

I made up my mind to go to Okinawa the next time I was back in Japan.

That's how it goes: travelers listen to stories from every corner of the world and dream of a journey with no end.

It was already late at night, so most of the guesthouses were full, and all we turned up in the end were two beds in a dormitory.

Since they were a couple, they said one bed would do for them, and the two of them slept holding each other on the narrow bed.

The sight of two people wrapped around each other in a single bed made me envious, but it also looked impossibly cramped, and the way they accepted it without a thought made me feel the size of the love between them.

Watching them, I thought of I-chan, my girlfriend back in Japan, but at that age I had no room in me to spend on romance, and all I had in my head was getting every last thing out of the adventure in front of me.

We were still in touch, but set against the excitement of living each day on the road moment by moment, it faded in importance whether I wanted it to or not.

Looking back now, that was a cold-hearted way to be, but maybe that's just what boys around twenty are like.
