## Overdose

T had overdosed on LSD at this party.

He had dropped three times the usual dose, and he was flying, and then flying some more.

And luckily for him, it arrived as a ferociously good trip.

For this party he had come in costume as the god Shiva.

T held a certain Osaka set of values, the one where whoever dresses the most outlandishly and stands out the most is the coolest, so standing out in his Shiva getup had him feeling like the hero of the party.

Then he took a huge amount of LSD on top of that and went flying, and his values and his convictions went into overdrive all at once, until he was possessed by the delusion that he had taken the throne of London's psytrance scene.

That conviction combined with his extreme performer's temperament, and at last he began to shout.

"I've taken the throne! I've taken a huge amount of LSD and I'm completely wrecked!" — that kind of thing, at the top of his lungs, in Japanese, and he kept it up for about an hour.

## A Married-Couple Comedy Act

He seems to have held off inside the club, at least, but once he came outside he went on and on, shouting in Japanese.

The shouting was so relentless that his Japanese girlfriend tried to rein him in, and to anyone from Kansai that could only look like a married-couple manzai act — the two-person stand-up comedy Osaka runs on.

The childishness of him preening in that outlandish getup, and the sight of him flying too high on LSD and screaming, had been a little cringeworthy and a little frightening, but once it started to look like a married-couple manzai, the whole thing snapped at once into the frame of a gag manga.

It was a pretty cringeworthy sight, no question, and yet the sheer distance he had flown blew all of that away; there was something endearing in it, and it drew laughter from the people around him.

T mistook that laughter for warm smiles and worked himself up even further, a Don Quixote of a man, cringeworthy in a way that went clean through to the other side — and both I-chan and I found ourselves looking at him with astonishment, and with a kind of respect.
