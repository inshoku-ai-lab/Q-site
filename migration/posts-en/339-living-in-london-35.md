## Longing for Morocco

When I went over to visit my friend J, whose IQ was abnormally high, he gave me a flyer for a wonderful event.

It was for a psytrance party in Morocco to celebrate New Year's at the end of this year, 2000 — and, by extension, the dawn of the twenty-first century.

It looked like this.

![The festival flyer on a tabletop: "Morocco 2001 — A Universal Tribal Gathering," a photo of a low island in a still blue lake with desert hills behind, and the words "On a desert island — Tawrirt N'Zbib, Lake El Mansour Eddahbi, Ouarzazate"](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/morocco.jpeg)

A three-day festival on an island in the middle of an oasis in the Sahara Desert.

The island where the event would be held had abandoned, ruined buildings on it, which made the strange atmosphere of the place all the stronger.

"Sahara Desert." "Oasis." "The turn of the century." "Psytrance." One keyword after another seized my attention in an instant, and just like that I had to go, no matter what.

## Pushback

But here I ran into resistance I hadn't expected.

I-chan did not like it one bit that I had seen the flyer, gotten all worked up, and decided I was absolutely going.

We were living together and traveling together, sharing one life, and for me to leave her opinion out of it and choose our next destination on nothing but my own interests was just too much. That was her point.

We were a couple, partners, so it was a perfectly reasonable thing to say, but back then I had neither the thoughtfulness nor the room in my heart to take anyone else's opinion into account.

The curiosity right in front of me was everything, and whatever caught my interest, I charged at it headlong.

I-chan pushed back, but words like the Sahara, oasis, and the turn of the century seemed to appeal to her too, and after we talked it over a few times she came around, and we agreed to celebrate the turn of the century together in Morocco.

## The End of the Visa

By then it was already autumn, and the weather had started turning cold.

Our visas for England would run out in mid-November, six months after we had entered the country, so something had to be done about it.

To stay on in England, the choices were things like enrolling in a language school to get a student visa, or leaving the country for a while and then coming back in.

I was enjoying life in London, and I was glad to be earning money, a little at a time but steadily.

I-chan, though, wanted to go back to Japan and get serious about a career in the theater, so she was thinking of going home when the visa ran out.

Against that background, the Morocco plan was also a sensible alternative: a way to set the real, serious questions aside for a while and go after the pleasures of travel.
