## Hofmann

What I took on the motorcycle was a variety of LSD called Hofmann 2000.

The name comes from Dr. Albert Hofmann, the man who developed LSD, and the paper it comes on is printed with a motif out of the story of his own first LSD trip — flying off to the moon on a bicycle.

Up until then the variety in circulation had been Hofmann 1943, but that year Hofmann 2000 started going around.

What my friends all agreed on was that the 2000 had less speed in it, and forced the body into gear less than the old one had.

The year in the name marks a different type of high, but what the two have in common is that they run about twice as strong as ordinary LSD.

So for someone whose right amount is one tab of ordinary acid, half a tab of Hofmann does it.

## Overdose

At the last party of the season, I wanted to fly out as far as it was possible to go.

Doing that let me live the present moment to the maximum, and living the present to the fullest brought with it a total sense of rightness, as if it affirmed every choice of my whole life up to that point.

Taking more LSD doesn't necessarily add up to enjoying a party more, but for me back then, raising the dose meant the same thing as giving it everything I had.

So when I took half a tab of Hofmann on the motorcycle, what went in was eight times the right amount for me.

My body is a very sensitive one, so an eighth of a tab of paper acid is normally about right.

At that dose I can dance all night without my body seizing up, my consciousness still opens up properly, and the party is as good as it gets.

Any more than that, and the body can't keep up with the speed of consciousness; it stiffens and locks in place.

But this time I took eight times the dose as if it were the natural thing to do.

Right from the start, I opened at Kaio-ken times eight — the manga move that multiplies your own power and tears your body apart at the high numbers.
