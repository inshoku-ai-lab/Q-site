## The Standoff

The cop who had pulled up right beside me got off his motorcycle and spoke to me.

From the first moment, he was leaning on me with everything he had.

He put me against the wall and set the motorcycle in the middle of the road so I couldn't move.

One cop stood in front of it and one behind, and that closed off any way out.

It was checkmate.

In clumsy English, the cop told me to let him search my bag.

There was nothing I could do, so I did as I was told and showed him what was in the bag.

I prayed he wouldn't find the hash I had stashed in a snack container, and in under a minute he had dug it out.

He was clearly used to searching bags.

## The Wall

The moment that small piece of hash came out, their attitude changed completely.

They got even more overbearing, ordering me to face the wall and put my hands up.

It was already past midnight, and there was no one on the street at all.

The instant I turned toward the wall, a wave of disgust went through me.

The entire wall was covered in cockroaches, a swarm of them clinging to the surface.

There was no way to guess how many.

Cockroaches filled a wall about two meters (six and a half feet) high, packed edge to edge.

The wall ran several hundred meters (a thousand feet or more), and I couldn't begin to guess how many tens of thousands of cockroaches were on it.

Staring at that wall of horror, I got patted down.

Fear wrapped around my whole body and my knees started to shake, but if the cops caught that they would only push harder, so I did what I could to look calm.

The cops were saying something about taking me to the station.

I knew that was not what they really wanted — it was a show, and what they were actually after was a bribe — so I started negotiating to pay them off and have them let it slide.

My English at the time was broken English so bad it didn't even qualify as broken English, and the cops' English wasn't much better.

But my own safety was on the line, so I kept at it, pressing my case as hard as I could.
