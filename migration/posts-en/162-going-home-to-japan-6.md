## Walking Around

First thing, I headed for the front of the speakers.

Japanese gear was in a different class from anything in Goa — far louder, far cleaner.

The DJ was blending the newest tracks smoothly, and it was easy to dance to.

Japan does quality well, I thought, and still something was missing.

What I felt was missing was the edge.

There was none of that whiff of danger I'd felt in Goa — the sense that once you set foot in the world of Goa trance, you could never come back to society again.

A party in Japan felt like fun with a clean line drawn around it: you play on the weekend, and Monday morning you go back to work.

There was none of the excitement of Goa, where people shouldered real risk and played all out with their lives on the line.

Between seasoned travelers who had thrown their lives into the road and spent years crossing the world, and Japanese kids out for a weekend before going back to work or school, of course there was a difference. Obvious enough, once you say it out loud.

## The Goods

After dancing for a while, I ran into the friend who had told me about the party.

Several other friends from Goa had gathered as well, and he said they had connections for LSD and ecstasy.

Getting to connections like that was part of why I had come to this party in the first place, so I went straight over to their camp and picked up everything I needed.

A few of them I had not seen in a long time, and not one of them had the glow they'd had in Goa.

I had thought Goa was where the bright, interesting travelers ended up; apparently it worked the other way around.

Goa's magic had been what lit up each of their personalities, and living inside the frame of Japan, they had already lost that glow.

Not the LSD, not the ecstasy, not the Goa trance — none of it made them shine.

## Comparison

There was no point complaining, so I bit off a chip of the ecstasy I'd just picked up and took an eighth of a tab of LSD.

Say what you like about the place, dancing to Goa trance while wrecked on LSD was fun.

Every little thing set off another comparison between the parties in Goa and this one, another critical thought, but I kept dancing, focused on the sound and on myself.

The advice from the leatherworking travelers, who had been on the road far longer than me — don't expect too much from a Japanese party — turned out to be useful.

Holding a party in Japan to the standards of Goa, the most out-of-its-mind town in the world, was ridiculous, but Goa was the only party I had ever known, and it had become my baseline.

Without that advice I might have picked the Japanese party apart all night and never enjoyed it; because of it I could stand back a step, and the night turned out fun enough.

Still, if one Japanese party was going to cost me about fifteen thousand yen — a hundred and twenty dollars or so — then the same money spent on two weeks in Goa was better by a mile.

For now I would go without the fun, save the money, get back to India as soon as I could, and enjoy myself efficiently there — that settled it.
