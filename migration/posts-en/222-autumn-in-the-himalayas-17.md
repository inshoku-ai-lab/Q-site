## The World of Travelers

The Parvati Valley isn't known to beginners, so the travelers who come here are a notch more seasoned than the ones up in Manali.

Put it this way: travelers with a single digit of years behind them gather in Manali, and travelers with double digits gather in Parvati.

Most people reading this blog don't know the travel world, so I imagine the reaction is surprise — "Are there really that many people out there with double digits of years on the road?" — but there are.

And a fair number of them, at that.

But travelers like that have no interest in writing blogs on the internet, so word of them almost never gets out; even so, people who have been on the road ten, twenty, thirty, forty years really are out there in considerable numbers, and they have formed a society of their own.

That society reflects the wisdom those travelers have collected from all over the world, and there are plenty of fascinating examples in it.

## Parties

All over India there are places that get called hippie holy ground, and psytrance parties run at some of them.

In northern India the famous ones are Manali and Parvati, and there is always a party going in one town or the other.

When Manali has no party, word goes around that Parvati must be having one right about now, and when Parvati has no party, word goes around that it must be Manali.

That was the state of things when a rumor about a party in Parvati reached those of us staying in Vashisht.

Every one of us traveled with the resolve to get through any hardship if a psytrance party was at the end of it, so on the day a rumor like that came in, not going wasn't an option.

We stuffed our backpacks, got ready for the party, and headed for Parvati.

The entrance to Parvati is about six hours along a beautiful winding mountain road.

We weren't planning to stay long, but a new town to look at always lifts the mood.
