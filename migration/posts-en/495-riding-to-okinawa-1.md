## The Bike

One of my coworkers at the big hut was a young guy who was crazy about outdoor sports.

He did all kinds of sports, but one of his hobbies was bicycle touring.

He would work at the hut, save up, and buy yet another new bike.

Each one an upgrade, of course.

The oldest of them he no longer rode, so he'd said he could let it go cheap.

"Old" was relative: it was a high-end road bike that had cost around 150,000 yen a few years earlier — about twelve hundred dollars — and he'd agreed to sell it to me for 30,000 yen, around two hundred forty dollars.

As a way to get started with bicycle touring, I couldn't have asked for better.

I wanted to travel around Japan, to get around cheaply by bicycle, and to keep up the body the mountain hut had built.

On top of that, I wanted a trip to get over a breakup, to heal a heart left in a mess by everything with I-chan, and when the offer of this high-end road bike came along on top of that, the plan got under way.

## To Himeji

I took trains and buses to Himeji, west of Kobe, where my friend lived.

What surprised me when I got to his house was how grand it was.

I'd grown up poor, and my own home and my classmates' homes all fell within a certain range of poverty.

The travels that came after took me around countries like India and Morocco, what people called the Third World, so I'd never come across a rich family there either.

But this friend's father ran a real estate company and owned a mansion.

Working alongside him at the hut, I'd never have guessed, but he was rich on the level of Suneo, the rich kid in *Doraemon*.

The mansion was about fifty times the size of the apartment in the public housing project where I was born and raised, and it floored me.

It was bigger than most mountain huts.

Wealth on a completely different scale made me uncomfortable.

Everything felt unnatural, and I couldn't shake a strange sense that something was off.

But that cushion of money was exactly what let my friend sell me the bike so cheap.

The price, too low to believe, finally made sense.
