## Reunion

When I finally made it to Anjuna, Y was there to meet me.

It was the first time we had seen each other since Vashisht, a few months back.

After that he had gone home to Japan, put every last preparation perfectly in place, and come back to India.

The preparations he meant were for shooting a film.

He made his living as an assistant director on films, and he dreamed of one day shooting his own.

Then Goa, the year before, had handed him a shattering experience, something close to an awakening, and he had set his mind on getting this traveler's world onto film no matter what.

Over the past year he had saved the money, put together the camera gear, and on top of that brought A-san, a younger colleague from the same industry, along as his assistant.

And I-chan — my girlfriend, who wanted to be an actress — was arriving in a few days to appear in the documentary.

Our separate dreams were tangling together, about to surface as something real.

## A Smoke

At Y's house, A-san, the younger man from the film business, and D, who lived next door, were both there to welcome me as well.

I unpacked, caught my breath, and then showed off the top-grade hash I had carried down from the Himalayas hidden in my luggage.

Nine out of ten people staying in Goa were cannabis lovers, and they had a weakness for the good stuff.

Having good hash on you meant, in effect, a place high up in the Goa caste system.

It sounds a little absurd, but carrying the real thing genuinely got you invited into the chillum circles of seasoned travelers and interesting people, and out of that came still more interesting experiences.

For a traveler, interesting experience was the whole point, the wealth, the thing wanted above all else, so good hash, the door that opened onto it, was worth every bit of what it cost.

What I had brought was a piece so fine that even Y, a film man with more than ten years of smoking behind him, had never seen its like, and everyone there could see what it was worth.

We rolled a joint straight away and passed it around.

I had taken on that risk deliberately to bring it here, and watching everyone get wrecked made me proud.

Everyone in the room was delighted with what I had brought, and buyers turned up immediately.

In that one moment most of it was sold, and I made back what I had paid.

Which meant that for the next several months, everything I smoked myself was free.

The risk had paid off, and it was a moment of happiness I could barely contain.
