# The Straw Gentleman

There was a good reason we ended up calling this old guy who turned up out of nowhere the Straw Gentleman.

By the time we got to the bar, he was already good and drunk.

In one hand, a small bottle of whiskey.

For a man that far gone, his back was straight, and his clothes were proper too: a pressed dress shirt and slacks.

In Goa, where it is mostly shorts, T-shirts, and flip-flops, that was an unusual outfit.

The clothes were proper, but they were coated in sandy dust, dirty in the particular way that makes you think a man has been sleeping on the street.

For a drunk he stood straight, and for a man dressed politely he was filthy.

Anyone who knows Binbotchama-kun — the broke kid from the manga Obotchama-kun — should picture him here.

Binbotchama-kun in the flesh: the Straw Gentleman kept a whole bundle of straws in his breast pocket, and he stuck straws into our hot chai for us.

He seemed to be drinking his beer through a straw as well.

We fell apart laughing at this Straw Gentleman who had appeared from nowhere and made no sense whatsoever.

He looked like he was doing it in complete earnest, and he also looked like he was doing it for laughs.

By then it had to be around four in the morning. On a weekday.

What he was doing out there was anybody's guess.

Who he really was stayed a total mystery, but we could not stop laughing at how far off the rails he was.

After kindly setting the straws in our chai, he began his speech.

Sharply dressed and delivering it with force, he looked like Hitler — no, like Chaplin doing his mocking impression of Hitler.

My English was almost nonexistent, so I had no idea what he was saying, but none of the ridiculous comedy of it was lost on me.

For all I know it was not English at all, but a speech in Hindi.

In the end we never found out who he was, but we had met a man whose comedy came from another dimension, and we went home more than satisfied.

A few days later, the same crew was having a meal at our usual restaurant.

Someone rolled a joint, as always, and it went around the table.

One of the dangers of LSD is the effect called a flashback.

Days, months, even years after you take it, some small trigger brings the effect back.

To people like us, who were used to it, a free trip counted as good luck, but for someone not used to it, it can get confusing and dangerous.

E's case was one of the bad ones.

The cannabis in the joint pulled the trigger, and the LSD came back up.

E sank into her brooding mode, and the air around her started to turn strange.

Then, all at once, she turned to R, who was at the same table, and came out with, "Huh? You're X, aren't you? What happened — weren't you dead?"

When we got her to explain, she said R had started to look like X, who had committed suicide years earlier.

Talk to E after she had settled down and she understood things perfectly well, but once she was in one of those fixed-idea panics she was too rattled to take anything in.

In the end, we agreed E was better off away from Goa and away from LSD, and she and S, the couple, left Goa and went home to Japan.

After that, whenever we met someone in Goa with that delicate, easily twisted temperament, we would say they were another one of E's kind — a dark joke we kept running — and at the same time stay on guard and take care not to let them break.

As a postscript: a friend who ran into them about ten years later says they went back to normal once they left Goa, blended into life in Japan to a reasonable degree, and travel abroad now and then.

S has since qualified as an acupuncturist, and is said to be living in Japan, working in the healing arts.
