## The Accident

I was standing stock-still in the middle of the dance floor, my mind in a daze.

Then someone slammed into me out of nowhere and knocked me a little to one side.

Something went streaming past the spot where I had been, at tremendous speed.

In that moment I had no grasp at all of what was happening, but apparently someone had come riding a skateboard straight into the middle of the dance floor.

I was plenty far gone myself, past making sense of anything, but a person who rides a skateboard into a dance floor is plenty out of his mind too.

The person who slammed into me, it seemed, had kept me from being hit by the skateboard guy.

Normally something like that would have made me angry, but right then I wasn't linked to the actual situation at all, and I was a long way from any sense of my own body.

The sensation was something like "my body was suddenly moved, and something came flying through the spot where it had been."

No judgment about it at all.

## The Chai Stall

Any concept of dancing along to the music had left me long before; I was more like something just floating in space.

Even so, I understood that I was at a party with my friends and my girlfriend, and that I was wrecked on LSD.

Knowing there was no way I could dance in that state, I wobbled off toward the chai stall where my friends were gathered.

At the stall everyone was smiling and talking, and the whole place was filled with warmth.

## U

Then U, who had traveled with me in Vashisht and in Thailand, came over to say goodbye.

That evening he was catching the train to Delhi, he said, and flying on to Europe.

After he walked away, a big realization came to me.

What I saw was that U's presence had been a source of stress for me.

U preferred a thoroughly radical, edgy, outlaw way of living, and he often took a critical view of the Japanese in Goa, myself included.

I had taken that as part of who he was, but his critical eye was stifling, and distance had opened up between us on its own.

It was never anything I consciously registered, but on the level of energy it had been working on my unconscious.

With U leaving Goa, that unconscious wall dissolved on its own and left me with something like a sense of release.

It surprised me, because I hadn't known I carried any such feeling about him.

I had become extremely sensitive to energy, and that, it seemed, was what let me see movements of feeling like that inside me so clearly.
