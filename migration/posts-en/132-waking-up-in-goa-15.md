## The Gap Between Sensation and Body

Things were going well and I was enjoying myself.

The trick of taking a small dose of ecstasy first and dropping the LSD after had clearly worked, and I could dance and feel good with no worry about a bad trip.

But two hours or so into the dancing, my body couldn't keep up with the sensations in my head and began to lock up.

Even a quarter tab of LSD seemed to be too much for me.

I lay down at the chai stall and waited it out.

This state — too much LSD, a body that wouldn't follow — forced a kind of meditation on me.

Every kind of emotion and thought and inspiration came rushing in.

For that stretch I could do nothing at all, and so I was made to learn an attitude of acceptance, taking everything exactly as it was.

By this point it was something like my tenth time on LSD, and round by round I settled further into the sensations and the values that came with it.

Putting the thinking and the perspective of an LSD experience together with the thinking and the perspective of ordinary life, I was starting to see things from more angles, in three dimensions, and each time I took LSD and went through those sensations I felt myself grow a great deal.

A few hours after taking it the effects began to ease off and my body could move again, so I headed back to the dance floor.

## Spy Suspicions

I had been dancing flat out for hours without a break, but I was finally getting tired, so when the music came to a lull I went back to the chai stall to rest.

At that same moment all of my friends came back to rest too.

We drank water, ordered chai, rolled a joint.

The party was as fun as it gets, and everyone was riding high.

Conversation with the new friends came easily too.

The one who happened to sit down next to me was an older guy named S, from Kansai, and as we talked it came out that he had grown up near my family's house.

As the conversation picked up, he started asking me all kinds of questions.

Do you go to parties a lot?

Do you go to events like this in Japan too?

Where did you get this LSD?

Which beach do you live on?

For two Japanese meeting in Goa these were natural questions, and I understood the urge to ask them.

But that friendliness peculiar to people from Kansai, and how far into my business he was willing to step, planted doubt in a head the LSD had spinning at high speed.

Why was he firing questions at me like this?

Could this be an undercover cop, infiltrating the place as a spy?

Most of the travelers who came to Goa again and again put real care into how they dressed, and at parties in particular everyone showed up decked out.

But S was wearing a fluorescent orange windbreaker and faded shorts, an outfit that by the standards of 1998 was dorky.

He looked like nothing so much as some middle-aged guy from Osaka's old downtown, which only strengthened my suspicion that he was an undercover cop.

Still eyeing him with suspicion, I put his questions off with vague answers, and at the same time watched N answer those same questions of his smoothly and cheerfully, my stomach in a knot.

Maybe they are all being fooled, this is dangerous — thinking things like that, I went back to the dance floor.
