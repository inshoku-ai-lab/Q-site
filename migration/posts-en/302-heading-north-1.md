## Heading North

My girlfriend I-chan and I said goodbye to our friends in Goa and boarded a train for Delhi.

A full day and a half on the rails lay ahead of us.

Almost all of my train travel until then had been alone, which meant staying on guard the entire time.

Inside a station I kept my bags wedged between my feet so they could not be stolen, and on a train, when I needed the toilet, I would always turn to several of the passengers around me, ask them to keep an eye on my things, and only then go.

The reason for asking several was that a single person might be the one to steal them, whereas several would keep an eye on each other, which was a comfort.

You might think that is taking suspicion a little too far, but this was the reality of India.

## Traveling as a Pair

This time, though, I was not traveling alone — there were two of us.

I-chan had done a fair amount of traveling herself, which made her a dependable partner to have along.

The hassle of getting tickets was cut in half, and we could take turns going to the toilet.

There was always someone to talk to, so there was no getting bored.

The biggest problem for a woman traveling alone in India is groping on public transportation.

What is rampant among Indians is not violent rape but the same sort of furtive groping that goes on in Japan.

And for some reason, it is Japanese women who get targeted.

Japanese women are small compared to Western women, and reserved by nature, so it is easy enough to see why they are picked out — but Indian men in far-flung parts of the country grope Japanese women as though they had all agreed on it beforehand.

I-chan said that traveling with me meant none of that was a worry, and that made her feel safe.

## Delhi

On the train it was chai, then curry, then sleep in the berth, and after a few turns of that cycle we reached the end of the line at Delhi.

Traveling as a pair, when both of you know the road, is an easy thing.

In Delhi we decided to stay at the Bright Guest House, the same as last time.

We had passed through often enough by now that the staff seemed to remember our faces, and they gave us a warm welcome.

The plan was to rest here for one night and catch the night bus for Manali the next day.

The days of nonstop moving would go on, but there was not much to do in Delhi anyway, so it was probably the safe call.
