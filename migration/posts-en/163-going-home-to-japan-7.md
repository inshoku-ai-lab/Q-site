## Acceptance and Criticism

The party in Gifu had beautiful country around it and the music of cutting-edge DJs coming through high-fidelity speakers, and it was fun, but what it left behind was a flat feeling.

Once you've been to the real thing in Goa, it seems anything else is bound to feel thin.

But maybe "thin" is not a strong enough word for it.

What I was feeling was closer to a hunger that nothing could ever fill.

The kind of fun I was after, the kind you bet your life on, could not exist anywhere but Goa.

It stood on two things: Goa, that peculiar patch of ground inside the continent that is India, and the history of European and American hippies there, running back to the sixties.

I was tormented, caught between an acceptance that said "this is just what parties in Japan are" and a flat, total condemnation that said "Japan is lame."

## The Ride Home

I started home with that criticism kept quiet in my chest, and on the way something happened that drove it in deeper.

I had doubts about the driver, a guy I found through the party website, as early as the drive out; on the drive back, with everyone worn out from the party, his selfishness went off.

Nothing you could point to actually went wrong between us, but sharing a car with someone so completely self-centered was not a pleasant way to pass the hours.

The shape of it — a driver who owned the car, and a traveler who could not get home unless he depended on that car — was probably what accelerated the dynamic between us.

## A Traveler's Rite of Passage

In India there had been no shortage of hassle, locals cheating me or trying to steal from me, but never once on the road had a person's own character been the thing that turned a situation into a hassle.

That, together with the party falling a little short of what I had hoped for, planted a critical feeling about Japan in me.

The criticism of Japan that started there would go on for several years.

That this kind of critical feeling toward your own country is a rite of passage every traveler wandering the world goes through in the first few years, I would only learn much later.
