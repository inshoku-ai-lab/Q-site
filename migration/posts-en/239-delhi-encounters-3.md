## The Bright Guest House

The guesthouse we were staying in saw a steady stream of seasoned travelers, and the main reason was that inside it you could smoke cannabis without the slightest trouble.

According to J, the owner had connections high up in the police, and by paying them off at regular intervals he kept the place clear of any crackdown.

We smoked joints and chillums every day, and one feature of cannabis culture in India is that manners require you to pass whatever you are smoking to everyone present.

At the same time, it is equally good manners to smoke as much as you can of whatever comes around to you.

The people you smoke with are not only fellow travelers you have never met — the staff working at the guesthouse smoke with you too, on the footing of friends.

When work got genuinely busy, or when the owner was in the building, it was apparently a different story, but as a rule the staff went about their jobs smoking cannabis with the travelers from the middle of the day on.

The place was a hidden liberated zone in the middle of Delhi, somewhere everyone could live at ease.

## D

A few days after we got to Delhi, D showed up.

He was a friend of J's from way back, and U had met him briefly in Goa as well.

Like J, D had lived for years in the world of travel, but he did it running drugs from country to country — half traveler, half yakuza.

Plenty of travelers make their money in ways that involve drugs, but what set D apart was the type and the scale of it.

He was a heroin addict himself, and he traveled carrying it in bulk, so in exchange for enormous risk he moved around the world freely with a great deal of money on him.

People who live at that level have had some wire in the head snap clean through; the way they face fear and the nerve they carry are on another level entirely, and it leaves them with a kind of enlightened calm and a devilish charm.

And then this man told us he had heroin of the very top grade, and invited us over.

U and I were twenty-one at the time, fearless and full of curiosity, and we took him up on it without a second thought.
