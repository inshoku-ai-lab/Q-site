## T

A lot of the people I met in London had strong personalities, and the most outlandish of them all was T.

He was a familiar face to the psytrance party crowd in London, and among the people going to psytrance events there back in 2000, there can't have been many who didn't know him.

Why was he so famous? Because his outlandish getup was outlandish in a way nobody else came close to.

A certain amount of outlandishness can still be called personality shining through, can still be called cool, but once it crosses a line, what people start calling it is cringeworthy, or scary.

In his case, unfortunately, it was the latter.

And more unfortunately still, he was from Osaka, the same as I-chan and me.

This Osaka brand of outlandishness that goes past the line is, to another Osaka native, embarrassing, and something you would rather not have to look at.

But at the same time it carries the freedom and the humor that only something past the line can have, so it isn't purely unpleasant either.

We chose not to get to know him, and to enjoy him from a distance.

## The Outskirts of London

What made us start really paying attention to him was an underground trance party held on the outskirts of London.

We had spotted him before that, but had written him off as just another eccentric.

The party was in a club converted from an old factory along the Thames, a place that held underground events on a regular basis.

Nobody lived in the area around it, and it wasn't in central London, so the whole atmosphere was freer, and nobody cared if the sound came blasting out of the club.

As long as you weren't causing trouble for anyone, they left you alone — that was the good thing about the Metropolitan Police.
