## Y's Clothes

The first thing that surprised me was what Y was wearing.

It looked like an ordinary sweatshirt, but it was made of some special material — thin, light, tough, and above all, seriously warm.

I knew nothing about that kind of thing, so I'd been fighting off the cold by layering twelve T-shirts, but once I saw Y's gear, I badly wanted some good mountain gear of my own.

The reason I owned twelve T-shirts in the first place was a pretty pathetic one: with that many, if one got dirty, I didn't have to wash it right away, because there was always another.

Y's special fabric, on the other hand, dried fast after washing, and even half-dry, it would finish drying on his body without chilling him. It was next-generation stuff.

He had that kind of gear top and bottom, and although he traveled light, it got him through desert nights as cold as the dead of winter.

## Y's Pots

The pot we had was a cheap aluminum one we'd bought at the market in town.

The reason was simple: it was cheap, and it was big enough to cook plenty of food in.

Y's pots were titanium — light, strong, with no metallic smell and nothing harmful in them.

On top of that, they nested inside one another, so even though he had several, they fit in a corner of his backpack. Clever stuff.

## Camping Gear

Then there was a French folding knife, a folding mat made of special material, a tarp of thin, tough fabric — every last thing he had was refined.

All that preparation didn't just help Y. It helped us too.

The tarp most of all: without it, there was no surviving the desert sun.

Y hadn't only brought us knowledge. He was a real, practical help.
