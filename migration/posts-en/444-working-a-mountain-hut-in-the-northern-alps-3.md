## The Staff Room

As soon as we got to the hut, we were assigned staff rooms.

We had arrived as a couple, but couples got no special treatment, and we were put in separate rooms.

For now, I was just grateful for the chance to rest.

My body was well past its limit.

There was nothing I could do but rest.

The staff room was an attic about the size of two tatami mats, a little over three square meters (thirty-five square feet).

At one end I could stand up straight; in the middle I could only sit, and at the other end I couldn't even do that.

They were pretty rough quarters, but not having to pay rent was a blessing.

## Dinner

After I'd rested a while, it was time for dinner.

A meal in a hut on a mountaintop didn't promise much, so it was a pleasant surprise when a proper, solid meal came out.

There was warm rice, freshly made miso soup, side dishes loaded with vegetables and meat, and plenty of pickles and the like.

On top of that, the drinks were apparently all you could drink.

According to the person sitting next to me, there was also a snack at ten and another at three, and complaints about the food were rare.

The work was hard physical labor, so they seemed to take good care of us when it came to food.

## Age

At dinner, we were introduced to everyone.

They were all young, and a good bunch.

The oldest was the thirty-four-year-old manager who had climbed the mountain with us.

Almost everyone else was in their twenties.

There's something exciting about a group of young people coming together to live under one roof.

About ten people were working there in all.

Heading into summer, more part-timers would gradually join, and at the summer peak there would be more than thirty.

There were only about fifteen climbers that day, roughly the same as the number of staff, but at the very height of the season, more than eight hundred people apparently came up the mountain.

That was far beyond anything I could picture; I couldn't even imagine that many people fitting into this hut in the first place.

We had only just arrived, but we were starting work the very next day, at eight in the morning.

Eight in the morning felt awfully early to me, but apparently that counted as a late start.

Some people started their day at four thirty in the morning.
