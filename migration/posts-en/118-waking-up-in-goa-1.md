# The Party Next Door

The December full moon party took place on the grounds of a restaurant, a three-minute walk from the guesthouse where I lived.

Locals lived right there in the neighborhood, and for them the whole thing was a real nuisance, but a party poured money into the local economy, so supply and demand stayed balanced.

A breezy restaurant with nothing but a roof and walls about a meter (three feet) high, the tables and chairs hauled out, big speakers and DJ gear lined up, black lights rigged up — a bare-bones setup.

We were too impatient to wait, so we walked onto the grounds while the crew was still setting up and waited there.

We drank chai, rolled a joint, and waited, taking it easy.

At any party in Goa, someone was carrying some kind of drug with almost one hundred percent certainty, so if the police came in, the place would be shut down in one stroke.

Around here bribery ran wild on the dirty arrangements of the adult world — the rich locals and the mafia, who prospered whenever there was a party, paid off the police, and on party nights the route of the patrolling officers shifted to another district.

## The Start

We had been smoking joints and talking for a while when the sound came on.

We took that as the signal and dropped the LSD.

I would not learn this until some time later, but the right dose of LSD differs from person to person, and for me an eighth of a tab was plenty.

Which meant that a whole tab would send me into a trip eight times as strong as a normal person's.

The seven or so trips I had taken up to then were all weak, poor-quality stuff, so nothing came of them beyond a good time, but what I took that night was fresh and high-quality, straight from Amsterdam, and the trip was of a completely different order.

The effects started almost as soon as I took it, and the Goa trance and the movement of my body synced up at an incredible level.

The rhythm and melody that put the brain into a trance kept me dancing with no end to it.

I kept dancing hard, at the pace of a dead sprint, and when I got thirsty I chugged cold water in one go.

That ordinary act — moving, sweating, drinking water — carried my trip into its next stage.

## Stage Two

By that point I was already far past the line into overdose.

Take a certain amount of LSD and your consciousness accelerates, and dancing at the speed of that consciousness is what makes the trance feel good, but this time the speed of my consciousness had gone too far.

I was dancing to keep up with that speed, but the body has limits.

Chugging cold water chilled my stomach all at once, and the speed of my body dropped all at once with it.

Here is what happens when consciousness alone accelerates and the body slows down: the body cannot catch up to a consciousness moving that fast, and you can no longer dance.

And when consciousness alone accelerates while you cannot dance, thought alone keeps spinning at extreme speed.

This time I had taken eight times the normal amount.

My thinking was pulled into a whirlpool of thought spinning at that extreme speed.

## The Questions

I think this is the fate of being human: keep the brain turning past a certain speed, keep the thoughts going, and you run straight into the philosophical questions of life.

What does it mean to live?

What is good, what is evil?

Is there a God?

What happens to a person after death?

Questions like these torment people deeply and send them running to religion.

For the first time in my life, I came face to face with a philosophical question.

Until then I had known there were people who thought about things like that, but it had never crossed my mind that I would be the one facing them.

Since escaping, at seventeen, a home life that was visibly too much to bear, my days had been empty-headed and free of worry, and having no worries at all was something I was proud of.

But here, now, a large existential question had risen up in front of me.
