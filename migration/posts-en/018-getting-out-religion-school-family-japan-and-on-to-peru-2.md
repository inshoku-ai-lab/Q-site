# My Sister's Wedding and the Move

Around the same time I quit the Jehovah's Witnesses and high school, plans were moving forward for my sister to marry a Peruvian man and move to Peru.

At the beginning of October she would go to Peru and hold a Peruvian-style wedding in her husband's hometown.

And to coincide with that, the whole family would go to Peru — the biggest event in our family's history.

Back then flights were not as cheap as they are now, and I believe a round trip cost more than two hundred thousand yen (about eighteen hundred dollars).

Shut away in the house, passing my days going nowhere, I was encouraged to go to Peru, in what felt like an attempt to get rid of me.

It was not that I particularly wanted to go to Peru, but since I had nothing to do and was just rotting away, I raised no objection and decided to go.

I had managed to take the one step of quitting the Jehovah's Witnesses and high school, but I could not take the next step of going out into the world.

Putting distance between myself and my mother was, I think, a good decision for both of us.

My sister told me this much later: there was apparently a hidden intention behind my mother sending me off to Peru.

Among Jehovah's Witnesses there is a rule that a man and a woman must not spend time alone together until they are married.

It is an absolute rule, and breaking it means being expelled from the Jehovah's Witnesses.

But as things stood, my sister had to go to Peru alone, and if she spent time with her husband-to-be before the marriage, there was a danger of premarital relations — sex before marriage.

![A black-and-white photograph, seen over a man's shoulder, of a couple sitting on a wooden bench holding hands](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog18-1.jpg)

To keep that from happening, my mother sent me out as a watchdog.

At the time I of course knew nothing of this, and my sister must have found me quite a nuisance.

# Preparing to Leave for Peru

A month after my sister left for Peru, it was my turn to go.

I could not imagine what kind of life was waiting for me, but having quit both high school and the religion and been left dangling, I found it was all the same to me.

In a situation like this a person would normally get anxious and make all sorts of preparations, but although I had become free, my heart had still not grown out of its half-dead state, so in a sense I was stable.

I bought a three-month round-trip ticket, but had no plan to come back on the return date, and no idea what I would do once I got back.

Put kindly, I was living in the present.

Put unkindly, I was not thinking about anything.

In truth, it would be more accurate to say that I was not capable of thinking about anything.

As for preparations for Peru, all I did was buy a few books and one notebook.

Having never lived any life other than religion and school, I had no idea at all what preparations to make.

Into a backpack went a change of clothes, the books, and the notebook.

Being poor to begin with, I did not own much of anything, and the idea of buying something never occurred to me.

More or less all I bought was shampoo that does not need to be rinsed out with water.

My sister and mother said that if I brought a handheld game console I would just play games the whole time, so I should leave it behind, and I saw their point and left it.

With no idea what sort of life I would live once I arrived, I simply headed there.

I figured the same aimless life I had then would just continue over there.

# Departure

I was going to Peru alone.

Family and relatives came to see me off, but once inside the airport I was on my own.

It was my first time away from my family, my first time going abroad, and my first time on an airplane.

Here too, perhaps because my emotions were dead, I worked matter-of-factly through what I could do, without fear.

![An airport cargo terminal at dusk, lit loading bays with trucks, planes on the tarmac beyond under a blue and pink sky](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog18-2.jpg)

The first flight went from Kansai International Airport to Los Angeles.

My sister's husband was a foreigner, and I had seen other foreigners before, but being surrounded by them was a first in my life.

I understood nothing about departure procedures or transit or any of it, but when I arrived at the Los Angeles airport and showed my ticket to someone who looked like a staff member, they gestured to show me where to go.

Since I genuinely understood nothing, every time something came up I found a staff member and showed them all of my tickets, and that solved it.

From Los Angeles I changed planes for Miami, then changed planes again for Lima, Peru.

The planes got smaller with each transfer, which made me slightly uneasy.

Having steeled myself just two months earlier for being thrown out of the house, I felt no fear about the possibility of the plane going down and killing me.

To someone who has flown many times this sounds ridiculous, but the first time you board a plane, you prepare yourself to die.

At the airports I got lost here and there, but there were no major problems, and I made it there safely.
