## Stomach Pain

Take too much LSD or cannabis and, more often than not, your stomach starts to hurt.

If you have eaten nothing before taking the LSD it is no problem, but that day, late in the afternoon, I had eaten my fill at a Christmas lunch with friends.

Then the overdose of LSD set my body purging, and the pain came on fast.

Paraiso, the party venue, was an actual building, so it came equipped with toilets and there was no need to run off into the jungle.

But the line for them was enormous, and there was no way, as wrecked as I was, that I could hold the pain in and wait.

## A Magnificent View

Dragging my reeling mind and body along with me, I headed for the top of an empty, desert-like hill.

Up there, other people who couldn't wait for a toilet either were finding open ground and relieving themselves.

I found a decent spot of my own and squatted down.

From the top of the hill I looked out at the half moon glowing in the dark.

Down below, the surface of the sea was catching the moonlight.

Having just lived through the best Christmas Goa had to offer, as wrecked as a person can get, I took in a magnificent view and relieved myself in the middle of nature.

The joy of being free of the pain and the awe at all that natural beauty came together, and my spirits shot up.

On top of that, expelling the excess LSD in my bloodstream along with the waste brought the speed of my consciousness back down to the speed of my body.

With that, mind and body both burst into peak condition all at once.

## Lion

I came down the hill at a quick walk and headed for the dance floor.

Lit by the half moon, the wooden floor, so easy to dance on, was swaying.

Dancing as I went, I worked my way through the crowd bit by bit toward the front of the speakers.

It was the hottest spot in the whole party.

A giant of a white guy with dreadlocks, the one who had been at the center of the hottest spot at every party before this too, was dancing at twice the speed of the already fast Goa trance, pulling the dancers along with him.

We called him Lion.

With his skin burned dark from travel and parties and his blond dreadlocks, he looked like a lion, and his size, his powerful dancing, and his charisma made him something like the king of beasts at the top of Goa's whole crowd of freaks and oddballs.

The most fired-up travelers packed in around him, dancing like lunatics.

Half-naked barefoot hippies, guys with mohawks and tattoos and piercings who could have walked out of the manga Fist of the North Star, middle-aged women dancing in folk dress: every one of them blowing their own peculiar character wide open, going at it full force, on and on without stopping.

I stepped into the circle and let my own individuality out at full blast.

What set me apart there was overwhelming youth.

Most of the crowd were experienced travelers in their thirties and forties who had gathered from all over the world, and I was a young Asian guy with no experience at all, skinny as a rail, head shaved.

I was probably in the poorest bracket there financially as well.

That was exactly why I could throw myself into getting high with nothing held back, and with a delicate sensibility and a purity, what I brought to the party was fearless young energy.

Shorts filthy from the road, flip-flops, no shirt, a shaved head, and about three hundred yen — two and a half dollars — in loose change in my pocket: my whole look spoke for the drive that only the young have.
