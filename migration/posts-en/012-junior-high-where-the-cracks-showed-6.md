# High School Entrance Exams

In the second term of my third year of junior high, having woken up to the pleasure of studying, I saw my test scores rise dramatically, but high school entrance depended not only on test scores — my overall record as a student up to that point was factored in as well, so I could not apply to a high school that matched my scores.

Looking back now, if I had stood firm and insisted on applying to the school I wanted, my wish probably would have been granted, but on my mother's strong recommendation I ended up sitting the exam for a technical high school a little distance from home.

My mother pushed this technical high school for the usual Jehovah's Witness reasons.

At the high schools in the district where I grew up, boys were required to take about two hours of judo once a week.

But Jehovah's Witnesses do not participate in anything violent, so martial arts are forbidden.

Which means the children of Jehovah's Witnesses have to refuse to take part in judo class.

At some high schools, though, sitting out judo can keep you from moving up to the next grade.

The high school I applied to, meanwhile, would let you sit out judo with no trouble at all if you said you wanted to, and it made no difference to moving up a grade.

For that reason, sons of Jehovah's Witnesses came to this school from a wide area around it.

Officially the school was coed, but in practice it was ninety-nine percent boys, which suited Jehovah's Witnesses, who want to avoid contact between the sexes as much as possible.

It was also a pretty dumb school, and I think the absence of any prospect of going on to university suited Jehovah's Witnesses too.

![Two horizontal strands of barbed wire silhouetted against a clear blue sky](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog12-2.jpg)

My motive for applying to this high school was not only my mother's strong urging — the deciding factor was that its exam date fell about two months earlier than the other schools'.

Why this one school held its exams earlier than everywhere else, I do not know, but for me, wanting to be free of exams sooner and to play games, it was convenient.

There was a game at the arcade I was hooked on, and I wanted to free up time and play it to my heart's content.

An extremely stupid motive on which to base a life decision, but for me, with no hope for my life at all, the vacation right in front of me mattered more.

There was also, I think, a slave mentality in me that obeyed my mother's strong push without thinking.

Living by my own will was not something I had learned.

This technical high school was far below my academic level, so I cleared the exam easily, without the slightest trouble.

Academically it was around the level of a second-year junior high student.

Having had my high school place settled earlier than the other students at my junior high, I was at last free to throw myself into games.

# Preparing for High School Life

For an ordinary student about to start high school, preparation would mean buying a bag and stationery, or a new bicycle, that sort of thing.

The preparation I made was buying a judogi, so I could take part in the judo my mother had forbidden, without her finding out.

![Two children in white judo uniforms grappling on a mat while other children sit watching](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog12-1.jpg)

By this point my mother had clearly noticed that I hated being a Jehovah's Witness, and she was saying that if I ever left the Jehovah's Witnesses, she would throw me out of the house.

She must have felt anxious about a son who was not turning into the zealous Jehovah's Witness she had in mind.

Not understanding much about how to relate to people, my mother could see no way other than threatening to throw me out, I suppose.

If it came out that I had secretly bought a judogi and was taking part in judo class at high school, I might be thrown out of the house, so this decision was an enormous one for me.

I went to the neighborhood sporting goods store to buy a judogi.

They happened to have none in stock, so I had to place an order.

When it came in they would phone the house, but since I did not want my mother finding out, I gave them a fake phone number, went to the store at around the time it should have arrived, and got my hands on the judogi.

The reason I had chosen this high school was that I would not have to take judo, so the whole thing was backwards, but my heart leapt at being able to hide from my high school classmates the fact that I was a Jehovah's Witness.

In the school life about to start, almost nobody would know I was a Jehovah's Witness.

For me, dreaming of an ordinary life in which I was not a Jehovah's Witness, high school life was the first step toward it.

To my mother the judogi was a tool of the devil for taking part in acts of violence, but to me it was a symbol of the freedom to make my dream come true.
