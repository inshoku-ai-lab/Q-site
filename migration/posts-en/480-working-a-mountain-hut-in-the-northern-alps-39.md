## Getting Ready to Close

Life at the hut moved steadily toward closing.

We worked through the checklist in the "closing notebook" one item at a time.

The notebook was written with a lot of thought, so that any of the part-timers, who change every year, could follow it.

That knack for organizing the work at a mountain hut probably came from the president, a true believer in the company creed.

The whole thing is set up so that one experienced person and the notebook are all it takes for everything to go smoothly.

Closing a hut is much easier than opening one.

Day after day, we quietly got through the work.

## Closing the Hut

For the last few days before the hut closes, there are no climbers.

We hauled every futon up into the attic and fastened every storm shutter tight, and last of all we nailed a shutter over the front entrance. That was it.

I'd been working there since the hut opened for the season, so finishing this job stirred up a lot of feelings.

It was only a few months, but I met all kinds of people and learned all sorts of things at a tremendous pace.

I came to know how hard life in the mountains is, and the know-how that gets people through it, and I picked up the strength to get through tough situations.

## Coming Down

Time to thank the hut that had kept us going all season, and head down the mountain.

The plan was to walk to the town at the foot of the mountain, where a car would be waiting.

Life in the mountains teaches you a lot, and I was taught plenty on the way down, too.

That know-how would come in very handy later, in my life on the road.

The most useful part was how to pack a backpack.

There are a number of rules, and following them saves a great deal of strength.

Heavy things go high and close to your back. Light things go low and away from your back.

That moves the center of gravity of the load forward, so the pack rides lighter and walking forward gets easier.

On top of that, cinching the hip belt and the shoulder straps tight makes the load lighter still.

The key is to carry most of the weight on your hips.

Your shoulders are there to keep the load balanced.

I packed everything the way I was taught, tightened the straps, and the pack got so light it startled me.

It made me wonder what all that struggling under heavy packs up to then had been for.
