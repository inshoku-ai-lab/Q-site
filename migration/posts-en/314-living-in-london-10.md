## A Friend in Common

Meeting A-kun, who was making his living doing fire shows in London, reminded me of another friend I had met some time before.

D, who I ran into about a year earlier in Calcutta, India, used to earn his money doing fire shows in London.

Carrying that same momentum, he had kept the fire shows going to pay his way as he traveled east overland, and he had come to a stop in Calcutta, which was where the two of us met.

He had thick dreadlocks as well — a fire performer who used to live in London, and a traveler.

On the off chance, I asked A-kun whether he knew a guy named D, and the answer came back at once: "Of course!"

## Traveler Connections

To my astonishment, D was like a big brother to A-kun, a kind of mentor who had handed down to him all sorts of wisdom about living as an outsider.

When I told him how I had met D in India, and about D's street performances in the Middle East, he was delighted and listened intently.

Travelers of our particular kind are a tiny fraction of the traveling population, very few in number.

But the flip side is that each of us has a circle of friendship spread across the world, hundreds of people apiece, so a chance connection like this one is not rare at all.

The traveler's world is also a place where the hypocritical slogan that a friend of a friend is a friend works as plain fact, so the two of us both felt good about meeting that day.

## Trance Style

Among hippie travelers like these, the ones who love a psytrance party, there is one shared ritual: smoking cannabis together, passing it around the circle.

Cannabis is basically illegal in London, but in practice nobody much cares.

Inside a trance party, all the more so — it is a completely lawless zone, and you can smoke wherever you like.

I had brought a chillum and some Indian charas to this party, so to celebrate meeting A-kun, we drank a toast with the chillum.
