## How the Rainbow Gathering Runs

The event had an anti-capitalist philosophy, he said, and ran entirely on donations and volunteers.

Gatherings were apparently held not just in India but all over the world, yet there was no organization behind them; volunteers came together on their own, as individuals, to put each one on.

So rather than an event run by a particular group, he said, it was more accurate to call it a style of event, one that followed a particular way of doing things.

## Native Americans

I was hooked, and I pumped him for every last detail.

A gathering began on the day of the new moon, peaked at the full moon, and ended on the day of the new moon.

For a whole month, people camped and lived outdoors, celebrating how wonderful communal life in nature could be.

The way he described it sounded a lot like the way I had camped at the oasis in Morocco.

Maybe Gandalf, who had led us there, had been influenced by the Rainbow Gathering.

According to H, the way the gathering was run drew on Native American traditions.

Since everything was done camping-style, there was practically nothing to pay for except food, which was apparently why donations alone were enough to keep it going.

## The Food Circle

Because the whole thing rested on a philosophy of not killing, the food they bought was completely vegan, with no meat, no fish, and no eggs, which, he said, kept costs down and cut the risk of food poisoning.

The donations that came in paid for the food, which everyone cooked together and ate together.

Twice a day there was a meal called the food circle.

People formed a big ring and sang and prayed before they ate.

When the meal was over, people apparently came around singing and dancing to collect donations.

The donations gathered that way bought more food, and so on... a cycle that repeated for the whole month.
