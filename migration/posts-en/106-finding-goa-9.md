# F's Bicycle Journey

Four years before I met him, he had already spent something like four years riding a bicycle through India and the other countries of Asia.

Since I met him in '98, that puts the journey between 1990 and 1994.

Back then, going abroad might mean Hawaii or Guam, but the number of people heading for India was overwhelmingly small.

There was almost no information, actually getting there was a feat in itself, and traveling by bicycle was harder still — keeping that up for four years is not something an ordinary person does.

The feeling of spending several years riding a bicycle through India and the countries of Asia is nearly impossible to grasp for anyone living a normal life in ordinary society, but to put it simply, you turn tremendously wild and tough.

At the same time the body grows frighteningly strong, in muscle and in immunity, and maybe because of that strength, your sense of what counts as clean shifts a long way as well.

Years of bicycle travel hand you an immune system that will not let you fall ill no matter how filthy you are.

In short, ride a bicycle for years through a country like India and you end up looking wild enough to surprise even yourself.

# The Incident

In that wild state, F had his passport and his cash stolen.

There was no internet in those days, and when something goes wrong in a country like India, it turns into a serious problem.

F managed to make it to the Japanese embassy — literally pedaled his way there — and asked them for help.

The Japanese embassy in India exists to help Japanese people in India.

Asking for help was only natural, and being helped should have been just as natural.

But the embassy staff took one look at how filthy F was, recoiled, and sent him away without hearing him out.

Being sent away did not change the fact that he had no other options, so F went back to the embassy several times to try to get someone to listen, but the staff, long since worn down by the persistent begging of India's homeless, treated him the same way they treated beggars.

After asking for help over and over and being brushed off and turned away, F blew up — "Enough! I'm not taking your help. I'm never letting the Babylon System take care of me again!" — and declared his escape from society.

Since then, having declared himself out of society, he has been living in Goa as a homeless man, or so I hear.
