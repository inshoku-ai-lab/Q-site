## Tired

The day before, I'd walked for about eight hours straight, and my legs and back were worn out, but the joy of being in the mountains outweighed all of that, and I was riding high.

I let that high carry me and pushed on and on.

Best of all, there wasn't a person or a single man-made thing in sight.

Just me and the wild ranges, rolling on for kilometers, tens of kilometers (miles and miles) in every direction.

It was a strange feeling, like standing right on the border between outer space and inner space.

## A Break

Partway along, I came across a stream running down a gully and stopped for a rest.

I was a little hesitant to drink straight from the stream, but when I tried it, it tasted pure and clear, with nothing else in it.

Water this good had to be fine, I figured, and gulped it down.

Soothing a climber's thirst with water that flows straight out of the mountain deepens your sense of being one with nature and lifts your spirits even higher.

With the stream water, I cooked some of the food I'd brought on my gas stove.

It was just a simple pot of rice cooked with dried vegetables, but made with fresh water, it tasted on a whole different level.

I had a little time to spare, so I pulled my camping mat out of my pack and took a nap.

Walking, eating, napping. Simple things, but doing them in the middle of such ridiculous scenery made them feel many times more special, and it felt wonderful.

## The Beautiful Pond

After an hour or so of napping, my fatigue was gone.

Brimming with energy, I kept tramping hard.

After a while, I reached the pond, earlier than planned.

It lived up to all the praise.

The scenery was reflected in its mirror-like surface exactly as it was.

Maybe it was the lay of the land, but no wind blew in, and the water stayed perfectly still.

The peak reflected in it was every bit as beautiful as the real thing, and once the morning sun set the summit aglow, the beauty would simply double.

That day, instead of using the gas I'd brought, I decided to build a campfire and cook over it.

Campfires and camping are actually banned at this spot, but I invoked my local's privilege as a mountain hut employee and decided to declare it "just fine."
