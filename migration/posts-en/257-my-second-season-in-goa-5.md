## The Christmas Party

A Christmas party in Goa is very different from what the world at large calls Christmas.

There is no tree, no green and red decorations, nothing with Santa on it.

For the psytrance travelers who gather in Goa, Christmas is simply a reason to hold a party; presents, the birth of Christ, a holy night, romantic encounters between couples — none of it has any bearing at all.

What Goa people think about is one thing only: how to set up the best possible conditions, get wrecked on LSD, and have a good time.

We were no exception to what passes for common sense among Goa people, and what we focused on was how to get as wrecked as we could and keep dancing.

And so Christmas dinner becomes a sideshow, an amusement on the way to enjoying the party.

## The Dinner

We gathered at Y's house and set out a lot of candles to make the place feel like Christmas.

Then we deep-fried a fresh, just-butchered chicken bought in town and enjoyed a dinner that was Christmas through and through.

That day we ate deliberately early, so that we could give the party afterward our full attention.

The reasoning was that a full stomach weakens the LSD when you take it and keeps you from concentrating on the dancing.

Having finished eating early, we planned to sleep early, get up around three in the morning, and head for the party.

But the approaching party had us too excited to sleep, so we cheated.

We crushed up the sleeping pills we had bought at a pharmacy and took a small amount.

That way you fall asleep right away, and you are set for the party.

We blew out most of the candles lighting the room, left a few burning, and fell asleep in the half-dark.

## The Incident

This is where the incident happens.

The sleeping pills had dropped me into a deep sleep, but even down inside it I felt uncomfortable.

I could not tell what it was, but on the far side of my fogged consciousness I could feel something hot.

Or rather, my face was hot as if it were on fire.

My head was not working at all because of the sleeping pills, but the heat was so intense that I woke — and for reasons I could not begin to work out, the pillow I had been sleeping on was roaring with flame!

Looking closer, one of the candles up on the wall, the ones we had lit before bed, had melted down and dripped, still burning, onto the pillow I was sleeping on, and set it alight.

I jumped up in shock, but the pills had my head in a fog.

Even so, I managed to get water from the sink and throw it over the pillow, putting the fire out.

I-chan scrambled up in a panic too, but the two of us were far too sleepy, and we shoved the soaked pillow aside and went straight back to sleep.

In India, and in Goa above all, outrageous things happen one after another, and the sense of surprise had probably gone numb in us.
