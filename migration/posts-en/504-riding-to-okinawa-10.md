## The Landslide

A little farther on, just as the signs had warned, the cliff above had come down and blocked the road.

A mound of dirt and rock about five meters (sixteen feet) high sat on top of the asphalt.

Sure enough, the road was closed by a landslide.

But while it was impassable for a car, for a bicycle it wasn't quite impossible.

I had no intention of turning back after coming this far, so I decided to get over to the other side in two trips, carrying my bags and then the bike on my shoulder.

Once I'd made it safely across, I felt a rush of gladness that I hadn't let the road-closed signs turn me around.

I strapped my bags back onto the bike and started pedaling again.

## The Road Gives Way

The road climbed a steep slope, and after a stretch of uphill it made a U-turn and kept heading higher.

Coming out of the U-turn, I realized I was now directly above the landslide I'd just crossed.

The dirt I'd climbed over a moment ago was the road in front of me, which had broken off and fallen.

This time it wasn't a closed road. The road itself had collapsed down the cliff, and there was no road left to take.

All that remained was the drainage channel along the inside edge.

The channel was about forty centimeters (sixteen inches) wide, just barely enough that getting across wasn't out of the question.

The collapsed section was only about four meters (thirteen feet) long, so I had a decent chance.

Looking down, I could see the mound I'd just climbed over, about ten meters (thirty-three feet) below.

A fall probably wouldn't kill me, but I'd likely break a bone or two.

And if I broke something here, I'd have to pedal back the way I'd come on my own.

On a closed road, there was no chance of someone happening by to rescue me.

Everything was entirely on me.

The sun was going down, too, so I had to decide fast.
