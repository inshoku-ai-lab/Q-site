## A Party in Japan

Right after I got back to Japan, a friend from Goa got in touch to say there was a Goa trance party deep in the mountains of Gifu, in the center of the country, and that I should come out for it.

I never thought I'd find a party in Japan this soon, so it was a happy surprise.

Some of the other friends from Goa were coming too, he said.

In Japan itself, apart from a school trip in junior high, I had never been outside the Kansai region.

Traveling around Japan sounded like fun too.

Through the party's website I found a spot in a carpool.

I didn't have any camping gear, but for a single night I figured I would manage.

## To Gifu

I traded messages with the driver, met him near the expressway, and got in.

We talked as the car headed for Gifu.

About an hour after we set off, I started having doubts about the driver's character.

There was something oddly self-centered about him, as though he couldn't see the people around him.

Every traveler I had met in Goa had been wonderful, and from that I had assumed, with nothing whatsoever to back it up, that anyone who went to Goa trance parties had been woken up by LSD and had grown as a person along with it. That expectation collapsed on the spot.

Nothing about him carried the sacredness or the depth of meaning I had felt at the parties in Goa; what came through the words he spoke was something cheapened, a drug party and nothing more.

## The Mountains of Japan

The party was at a campground up in the mountains.

Japan's mountains, thick with new green, were beautiful.

Unlike the dry red earth of Goa, these green mountains, cooled a little by the damp, had a shine of an entirely different kind.

The parking lot held a good crowd of party people got up in flashy outfits, and it moved me to find that Japan had a scene like this too.

None of the earthiness of the travelers in Goa — everybody neat and clean, heavy on the fluorescent colors, which felt very Japanese to me.

Walking the party grounds with the driver I'd come with, the sound blasting, I ran into a couple I used to hang out with in Goa.

They had been traveling the world for years by then, covering their living costs by making leather goods, dyed-in-the-wool hippie nomads.

Later they would launch a leather brand, turn it into the top name in the Goa trance world, and get seriously rich, but at this point, just two deft leatherworkers on the road.

We relaxed at their tent and traded stories from the road.

The way they told it, Japan had a few Goa trance parties, all of them fun enough in their own way, but you couldn't bring the Goa mindset to them.

Goa parties were fun on another dimension entirely, so if you looked at a Japanese party through Goa eyes, the sound quality of the speakers and the famous artists DJing were beside the point. The sheer character of the people who turned up, how interesting they were, the energy and the momentum of the whole thing were another thing altogether, so better not to expect too much. Those were their words.

Still, they added, it was fun enough in its own way.

With their words tucked in a corner of my mind, I decided to wander around the party.
