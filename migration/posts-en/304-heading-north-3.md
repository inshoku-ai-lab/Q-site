## To London

To get to London, I slipped fast through the brutal heat of Delhi and headed for the plane.

I had booked the London ticket a long time earlier, and it went by way of Turkmenistan, a country that made no sense to me at all.

I'd taken it because it was the cheapest route, but I liked that Turkmenistan made no sense.

It was a former Soviet country sitting on top of Iran and Afghanistan, and how unknown it was stirred my urge to travel.

In London, my close friend N would be waiting for me.

N was twenty-nine, eight years older than me, and last year in Goa she had doted on me like a little brother.

Our funny bones and our senses of humor were very much alike, and once we started riffing off each other the laughter would spin up and never stop.

This year N had gone to Bali instead of Goa, so we hadn't had any time together, but from here it looked like we would be cutting loose in London to our hearts' content.

## Takeoff

I had a plan for this flight.

The hash and the squares of LSD paper I'd bought in Vashisht last year were all sold off or used up, but I had held back the smallest bit of LSD and ecstasy.

The reason was that I wanted to take in the view above the clouds while wrecked on LSD and ecstasy — a fly-as-high-as-you-can idea.

LSD by itself might turn me paranoid in the cabin and leave me confused.

I might even come down with something like claustrophobia.

Ecstasy by itself and I wouldn't be able to savor how staggering the view was.

Too strong a dose and I wouldn't be able to sit still on a plane — for that combination of reasons, I settled on a small amount of LSD and ecstasy.

I kept the two of them tucked in my pocket and tossed them into my mouth right before going through customs.

Risky, if you want to call it risky, but by any normal reckoning there was no way it would actually put me in danger.

I enjoyed that kind of danger — outrageous, brimming with mischief.

Taking risks strictly inside a range that is absolutely safe.

That finely judged sensation was what I played around with and enjoyed.

## The View at Dusk

I'd been thinking about this plan for a while, so I had even lined the flight up to fall at dusk.

The plane took off smoothly, and right on schedule, when I looked out the window, the setting sun was dead center.

And by good luck, a sea of clouds stretched away beneath it — the spectacle of all spectacles, spread out in front of me.

I was grateful for my own luck, getting to enjoy a view like that wrecked out of my mind.

There is something deeply satisfying about laying out a careful plan and having it go exactly to plan.

I was thoroughly pleased with myself — with my plan, with the nerve to carry it out, and with the playfulness that let me enjoy something so ridiculous.
