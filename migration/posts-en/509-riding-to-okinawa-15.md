## Over the Pass

From here I was heading from Miyazaki to Kagoshima, and to get there I had to cross a mountain pass.

It was more of a low hill, really, but on a bike trip even a low hill burns through a lot of energy.

I expected a tiring ride, so I set out early in the morning.

When I started up the mountain road, mandarin orange groves spread out all around me.

And to my delight, there were unattended roadside stands selling mandarins everywhere.

Bags stuffed with about two kilos (four and a half pounds) of ripe mandarins were going for just 100 yen, about eighty cents.

It was cheaper than buying a bottle of water, and I could eat all the ripe, delicious mandarins I wanted. For someone traveling by bicycle, it doesn't get better than that road.

I took frequent breaks, eating mandarins to top up on energy, water, and vitamins.

## The Sea

Past the pass through the mandarin groves, the sea opened up ahead, stretching as far as I could see.

I flew down the mountain road, looking out over the water.

I'd been through this pattern many times by now, but a long downhill after a hard climb up a pass is the best thing about traveling by bike.

Drive up a mountain in a car and you don't feel much of anything, but the struggle of pedaling uphill builds up the joy.

## An Active Volcano

Across the water, I could see Sakurajima.

It seemed to be giving off a faint wisp of smoke at the summit.

The first volcano I had ever seen in my life felt like a living thing.

The earth is literally alive, and volcanoes are active.

As I got closer to Sakurajima, its raw, powerful presence took my breath away.

The road at its foot was called the Lava Road, and a name that fitting only made the trip more exciting.

## The Hot Spring

I made it to Ryujin Onsen, the hot spring that was my destination for the day.

From what I'd read beforehand, it was a hot spring right on the sea, where you could take in the vastness of the ocean and the life of the volcano at the same time.

It cost more than other hot springs, but with a view like no other, I had to try it.

When I went inside the building, they handed me a set of white robes.

The bath was mixed bathing, so apparently you had to wear white robes to get in.

Once I was in the water, I saw that the other guests were all in white robes too, and it felt like I'd stepped onto the set of a samurai period drama.

I wrapped up the day in a perfect hot spring, then decided to camp on a nearby beach that night.
