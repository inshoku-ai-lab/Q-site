# Another Traveler

A few days into my stay on the beach, another Japanese traveler turned up.

He too had apparently timed his trip to the full moon.

M, too, seemed quite a seasoned traveler, a type of person I had never encountered before.

Where in Japan would you have to go to meet someone like this?

He looked to be in his late twenties, the same as the other two travelers.

His hair, which he had started growing out, was tied back.

Like the other two, he was tanned dark, impossibly easygoing and friendly, and dressed in loose, casual clothes.

Whether travel turns people into that sort of person, or whether something about Thailand as a country makes them that way, I could not tell, but they had something in common.

The difference was that the other two were solidly built, with a masculine sort of presence, while M wore glasses and gave a vaguely androgynous impression.

I had assumed only rugged men could travel, but ruggedness seemed to have little to do with it.

# The Traveler's Story

He was planning to travel in India for about half a year after this, and had apparently come to a southern island of Thailand for a vacation, a last break before the grueling journey ahead.

He talked passionately about his past travels and his longing for India.

![The Taj Mahal's white marble dome and four minarets seen through green foliage under a hazy sky](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog37-1.jpg)

Up to then, travel to me had meant three days and two nights, a week at the very most.

In fact, the school trip was three days and two nights, and even summer camping with relatives ran five nights at the longest.

Once I started working part-time at Tsutaya, I discovered the idea of traveling for several weeks, and thought I was pretty cool, leaping past everything I had known to go backpacking for a whole three weeks.

Even so, that was a matter of weeks; traveling for months was beyond anything I could picture.

In the Saruganseki story they had hitchhiked over several months, but that was television, unrelated to reality.

Yet with a person right in front of me who was about to travel for half a year, I had the sensation that the values I had held until then were collapsing.

Reset everything you have done up to now and travel in India for six months?

What about rent? What about your things?

Was such a thing even possible?

Listening to M, I thought it might not be simple, but it did not seem impossible either.

He was an ordinary guy, with no special skills, not born into a wealthy family.

I had always thought of myself as raised poor and living poor, but go to another country and the difference in prices makes you quite rich.

If you used that price gap well, I came to think, traveling for half a year was possible.

Around this time I began to think that someday I would make a long trip to India the way he was doing.

It was not that I had any particular interest in India.

If anything, my curiosity covered an extremely narrow range, confined to urban life in central Osaka, but the experience of traveling in India itself interested me.

An adventure into a completely unknown world.

Through that experience, I imagined, the possibilities of my life might open up enormously.

# The Campfire

As the full moon drew near, more and more travelers gathered.

The bungalows all filled up, and the nearby guesthouses seemed to be almost completely full as well.

Once people started gathering, the atmosphere lifted and a sense of closeness grew.

When night came, someone collected firewood from the jungle and started a bonfire.

People came from every corner of the guesthouse and crowded around the fire.

![A group of people sitting on logs around a campfire at night, tents and dark trees behind](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog37-2.jpg)

Everyone was talking in English, so I had no idea what they were saying, but the enjoyable mood came through.

After a while a white guy brought out sticks, poured gasoline on them, and set them alight.

He seemed to be planning some kind of show.

It was a form of juggling called fire devil sticks, a street performance where you hold one stick in each hand and spin a third stick in the middle.

I did not understand the principle behind it, but he spun the sticks deftly and performed a fire dance.

It is hard to explain in words, so [here is a video.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOt0YiVEK_I&t=5s)

He put on a circus act unlike anything I had seen before, and we were all overjoyed.

It was not the full moon yet, but the moon shone brilliantly, and the white beach glowed in the moonlight.

As I stood on the beach, the night wind felt good.

The vastness of the sea on one side and, on the other, the sight of the trees and the bonfire and the travelers exuded a beauty and a freedom I cannot put into words, and I was utterly content.
