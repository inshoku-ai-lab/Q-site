## Escape

In late May, the day I had been waiting for finally came: the start of our job at a mountain hut.

After all that wild nature in Morocco, I had come back to live in Osaka, and I was fed up with modern life among the concrete.

I wanted to practice the way of living in harmony with nature that Gandalf had taught me, but in Osaka that was hard to pull off.

Just the chance to get out of the city had me excited.

We had our gear properly sorted and were ready to work hard.

The plan was to hole up in the mountains until the beginning of November.

## Nagano

We made our way to Nagano Prefecture on a string of overnight buses.

I had traveled abroad, but I had barely been anywhere in Japan; the only time I'd gone farther north than Osaka was a junior high school trip to Tokyo.

Parts of Japan I had never seen were waiting for me.

A journey into the unknown.

First thing in the morning, we gathered at the office in Matsumoto and were driven to the trailhead.

Apparently we would be heading up to the hut by tagging along on a guided climb led by the hut's manager.

I wasn't all that confident in my stamina, so it was a relief to be climbing with the elderly people on the tour.

Better still, as a staff perk, we got to have our luggage carried partway up the mountain by cable car.

The cable car was apparently for work use only, and the rule was that it couldn't carry guests' luggage.

## The Climb

At the trailhead, I was introduced to R, who would be working with us.

R was twenty and had come from Tochigi, a quiet guy with a sharp, hard stare.

With months of working together ahead of us, that hard stare and sullen face were not exactly welcome, but I couldn't just brush him off.

The conversation never really got going, but we chatted about this and that as we climbed.

Climbing the mountain wasn't as hard as I'd expected.

At this rate, the job wasn't going to be much of a struggle.

Walking alongside the old folks, I started to feel pretty good about my own stamina.

And they were carrying their own packs, so they were slow.

After an hour or two of walking, we reached the cable-car relay hut, where the cable car ended.

Nothing to it.
