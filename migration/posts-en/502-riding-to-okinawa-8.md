## Cape Muroto

I kept riding along the southern coast of Shikoku and passed Cape Muroto, the sharp point at the island's lower right.

...Or I must have, but sadly, as I write this now, I have no memory of Cape Muroto at all.

I remember the kamaboko, the henro pilgrims, and the hot springs, but maybe scenic spots just aren't the kind of thing that stays with you for years.

What has stayed with me, at least, isn't scenery or places or tourist sights, but strong emotions, the moments that moved me, surprised me, or hurt, and whatever was running through my head at the time.

## Along the Coast

It felt great to race along the seaside roads on my bicycle.

Breathing air that had crossed the Pacific to reach land felt like breathing in nature itself, fresh and untouched by the grime of cities, and somehow I felt as if I'd come out ahead.

Living with Gandalf, the ecology researcher, in Morocco had left me with views you could call nature fundamentalism, or even an ecology cult.

It was a way of thinking that revered nature above everything else and saw anything made by people, anything artificial, as tainted.

So to me, wind and sea that had never touched the human world were also symbols of something sacred.

## Runner's High

Every day I pedaled from morning to night, and each night I soaked in a hot spring and camped nearby to sleep.

The routine was monotonous, but the monotony itself brought a kind of contentment, and it helped heal a heart hurt by the breakup.

Back then, my head was a swirl of bitter grievances, heartache, and longing over my romance with I-chan.

On a bike trip, where there's nothing to do but keep pedaling, all of that was always sprawled out in my head and my heart.

On the other hand, pedaling for hours on end was forcing a runner's high on my body, and my body was forcibly hauling my sick heart back up.
