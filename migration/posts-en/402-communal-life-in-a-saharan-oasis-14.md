## Travel Gear

D and E both seemed to be seasoned travelers, but the gear they'd brought was nothing like a traveler's.

D had a huge djembe, an African drum, that must have weighed around ten kilos (22 pounds), and E had brought an equally huge didgeridoo, the Aboriginal Australian instrument, that looked like another ten kilos.

On top of that, E had brought a professional video camera that had to weigh about five kilos (11 pounds).

D set a giant piece of quartz he'd brought along, a kilo (about two pounds) or so, beside the fire and unhurriedly began to play the djembe.

E set up the camera to frame D, the fire, and the quartz, then stepped into the shot himself and started playing the didgeridoo.

## Jam Session

D laid down a rhythm that was quiet but groovy, and E made the didgeridoo undulate along with it.

The rest of us joined the two of them with shakers and clapping.

Some danced, some sang, some listened, gazed into the fire, played along.

We kept playing for a long time, on and on, and slipped into a kind of trance.

At a desert oasis out in the remote wilderness, friends gathered around a fire and shared the time together.

The hours drifted by, and they felt good.

## The Full Moon

As the sun sank in the western sky, the full moon began to rise in the east.

A full moon always sits on the far side of the Earth from the sun, so it rises as the sun sets and sets as the sun rises.

On nights without a moon, the desert is swallowed in total darkness and the whole sky blazes with stars.

But tonight the full moon had come up just as the sun went down, so it stayed fairly bright.

We were about 1,000 meters (3,300 feet) up, so the air was relatively thin, and sunlight, starlight, and moonlight all shone brighter than usual.

The unusually bright moonlight and the glow of the blazing fire reflected all around, lighting us up as we played our instruments, sang, and danced.
