## Down from the Mountain

It was time to end my long stay in Vashisht.

I had already been there close to four months, and I had enjoyed life in the Indian Himalayas to the fullest.

The plan from here was to go to Thailand, get a new Indian visa, and head back to Goa.

For me, any option other than Goa was unthinkable.

Goa the year before had turned my life completely upside down and woken me to a world I had never known, but that world went so deep that, for all that it had fired up my curiosity, I had not gone far enough into it to feel satisfied.

Having glimpsed that world, there was no going off to another one without facing it.

It felt like my destiny.

## To Delhi

U was coming down the mountain at the same time I was, so we decided to travel together.

Two is more fun than one, and it puts your mind at ease in all sorts of ways.

U was carrying a large quantity of drugs to make money with too, and I was glad we were after the same thing.

To hide it all, we used chyawanprash, an Ayurvedic health food.

It is something like a jam, all sorts of fruits and spices that are good for you kneaded together, pitch black and thick and sticky, and once something is buried in the jar there is no telling from the outside.

Run it through an X-ray and it might show up, but inside India, unless someone opened the lid and dug through the contents, nothing was going to be found.

We bought two one-kilo (two-pound) jars of chyawanprash and hid five hundred grams (about a pound) in each.

Then we glued the inner seals back down so the jars passed for brand new — exactly what a tourist would buy as an Indian health-food souvenir.

Even if the police did get suspicious, they were hardly going to open a sealed jar of food, and in Indian culture, putting your hand into food is a rude thing to do.

As long as we did nothing suspicious and carried nothing connected to cannabis, I figured we could brazen it out even if the police did take an interest.

LSD is nothing but a scrap of paper, so I stuck a big sticker on the inside of a notebook and hid it in there.

I had no feeling whatsoever that this crime would be found out.
