# All Kinds of Friends

December came to Goa and the crowds started thickening fast.

Plenty of them were Japanese — of the several thousand foreigners around Anjuna, maybe three percent.

Japanese travelers back then were far more intimidated by English than they are now, so they stuck together constantly.

The one who moved in next door to me was Japanese too: U, twenty-one, a year older than me.

Most of my friends were around thirty, so having someone close to my own age was a relief, and we hit it off right away.

Even at that age U was thoroughly antisocial, with long hair in dreadlocks like Bob Marley's and a silver piercing through his lower jaw.

Dreadlocks and chin piercings aren't much of a rarity now, but this was 1998, and nobody looked like that.

U was another one who had caught wind of Goa somewhere and come all this way.

Every last one of us had been called to Goa the way we were meant to be, and pulled in.

# The Groups of Girls

Around the same time I met a pair of Japanese girls.

They were twenty-one as well, a year older than me, and they turned out to be from a place about twenty minutes by bicycle from my parents' house.

We were starting to become friends, but they had no room in their schedule and left for their next stop before the friendship could take.

A few days later a trio of girls turned up.

They had been living in London for a few years and were traveling through Asia together, heading home to Japan.

In London the three of them had worked at the same Japanese restaurant and shared a house.

One had an Israeli boyfriend within a week of reaching Goa and moved to another beach, and that was the end of hanging around with her, but with the other two I built a friendship that would last for years.

Those two, T and N, had opposite personalities, an odd couple.

T was twenty-nine, quiet, sharp, delicate, the artistic type.

N was twenty-eight, a talker, a total idiot, oblivious, and funnier than anyone.

Both were serious beauties, and they turned heads everywhere.

They doted on me like a little brother, more than eight years younger than they were.

## Aftermath of the Party

It's such a stupid story that I hesitate to share it here, but it's funny, so I'll swallow my shame and tell it.

On the day of the bad-trip party I was completely wrung out, and still the LSD wouldn't let me sleep.

I stayed up until nightfall before finally going under, and then, worn out in body and mind alike, slept until the following evening.

Twenty hours straight, by my arithmetic.

My brain needed that much rest.

Waking in the evening didn't fix it either; a few hours later I went to bed at a normal hour and got up the next day at a normal hour.

All told, it took about thirty hours of sleep before I was fully back.

Maybe during all that sleep the nerve tissue in my brain, damaged from being driven too hard, healed and came back stronger.

## The Worry Resolved

The first thing on my mind when I woke that morning was this: had I taken the plastic wrap off that ecstasy tablet or not?

Worry mode was completely over, but the question still hadn't been answered.

On the off chance, I went to the toilet and took a shit.

Poking at it with a tree branch lying nearby, I turned up the ecstasy, still wrapped in plastic.

Beautifully sealed, not dissolved in the slightest.

Which meant I had ridden out that storm of worry mode with no help from the ecstasy at all.

I had taken eight times the normal dose and come through a bad trip, so feeling reborn and shaving my head is no great mystery.

Any weakness or warp in my mind and it could have broken right there.

Looking back on it now, I see how dangerous a game I was playing.

I pulled the wrapped tablet out and washed it in water.

The shit caked on it came away, but the smell stayed in the plastic.

I opened the wrap and took out the tablet, and it was pristine, as if nothing had ever happened to it, no smell at all.

That was good enough for me, so I rolled it into a fresh piece of plastic and saved it for the next party.

It's a filthy, crude story, but to me at the time a tablet of ecstasy worth ten days of rent was valuable enough to dig out of my own shit.

## A Party on Ecstasy

At a party at a beach bar a few days later, I took the ecstasy I had set aside.

My body is sensitive to ecstasy as well, and a quarter of a tablet would really have been enough, but I knew nothing of that then and swallowed the whole thing.

The fun and the happiness came on strong, and since I was already having fun and already happy, it wasn't much of a jolt.

What it confirmed was that the deep perceptual experience of LSD, the visions and the voices, interested me overwhelmingly more.

Ecstasy, for me, wasn't the main drug of a party — it was backup, so that LSD wouldn't turn into a bad trip. That seemed to be the size of it.
