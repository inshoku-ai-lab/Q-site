## What Became of A-kun

I thought A-kun and I would run into each other again before long, but in the end years went by without it.

What reached me instead was rumor on the wind: that he was out in South Africa making a name for himself as a fire performer, and that he had worked the weapons of Japan's old martial arts into circus juggling, and that the props themselves had caught on with performers all over the world.

Still we never crossed paths, and then, ten years later, we met by chance.

That year the biggest juggling convention in Europe was being held in Finland, and he was there performing as an invited act.

Delighted to see him again after ten years, I went over and started talking. He did not remember me at all.

Well, that's how it goes...

## J

In London I had several interesting encounters with other Japanese.

J, whom I met at the same psytrance club night where I met A-kun, was another seriously interesting human being.

He was living in the city with his Japanese girlfriend, K, the two of them living for the fun of it.

He was, I think, one of the highest-IQ people I have ever met, and you could see that abnormal intelligence in the way he looked.

The crown of his head bulged out a little, like a Grey-type alien, enough to make you suspect that the brain itself was simply larger than other people's.

His eyes were big and blazing, and he had a handsome, sharp-featured face that all but brimmed over with intelligence.

He was twenty-two at the time, a year older than me, but mentally he must have been somewhere in his mid-thirties.

At that age his hair was already thinning, and something about it made you think his brain ran at too high a spec for the nutrition to reach his hair.

Even then he had the air of a man who had already worked it all out, and he lived one step back from society, drifting.

He basically never worked a real job; he was forever turning up some way or other of making money, and living for the fun of it.

When I met him he was saying that the next time he went to Germany for fun, he would pick up a paid job in a clinical trial while he was there.

Clinical trial work means handing your body over to medical experimentation on human beings and taking a large payment in return.

Thinking about it now, it is something I would never want to do, but at the time I envied J for finding strange openings like that and turning them into money.
