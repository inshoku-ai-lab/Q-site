## I-chan

I saw I-chan again, for the first time since we had parted in Morocco.

Her family home wasn't far from mine, so it was easy to get together.

As I saw it, we'd had a big parting, then a big shift in the flow had brought us back together, and there was something fated about that. It felt good.

But I-chan, seeing me again after so long, wasn't all that happy about it.

She had just made a clean break and was ready to focus on her own path, so my turning up again seemed to take the wind out of her sails.

What she'd apparently had in mind was a fated reunion years down the road, after we had both grown.

## Plans

She was working toward becoming an actress, and with her theater company's performance coming up in a month, she was busy rehearsing every day.

Once the performance was safely over, she wanted to go work at a mountain hut — the staffed lodges on Japan's high trails — and she asked if I'd come along.

I'd already figured I couldn't make any moves until my grandfather's condition settled one way or the other, better or worse, so a job at a mountain hut sounded like a good plan.

A close friend she'd made traveling had apparently worked at one, and those stories had fired I-chan up; she was set on trying hut life for herself.

But we had no idea how to go about finding work at a mountain hut, so for a start we looked through the seasonal work section of a part-time job listings magazine.

There was exactly one listing, for part-time work at a hut in the Northern Alps of Japan, so without really knowing what we were getting into, we decided to call.

The people on the other end were apparently desperate for help, because things moved along fast, and soon they were asking us to send in résumés.

A few days after we sent them, the phone rang, and just like that we were hired, without so much as an interview.

Apparently we'd work at the same hut for the first month and the last, but spend the three and a half months in between at different huts.

It would have been nice to work together the whole way through, but it couldn't be helped.

It was an astonishingly quick hire, but I was more than happy: for now, I'd get to live out in nature and earn money doing it.
