## Juggling

Around this time I came across a form of juggling called devil sticks.

You hold a slender stick in each hand, bat a third stick through the air, and keep it spinning without letting it drop.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_38vtmlIwSU

I borrowed a set R had picked up at the flea market, started messing around with it, and got hooked.

Once I was hooked, it was too much fun to stop.

There was a pleasure in syncing my body to the flow of the stick — like riding a wave, like narrowing my focus until I dropped into the zone.

Every day I played and practiced for hours.

I probably had a talent for it.

After about three weeks of concentrated practice, I could keep a stick going with one hand alone for thirty minutes without dropping it.

I learned to spin it in rhythm too, and in the end I could dance while spinning it in time with music.

Even I was surprised by that.

Since childhood I had been a sickly, frail, unathletic, quiet kid, and I took that to be simply who I was.

In the school distance races I always finished somewhere near last, and everyone — me, my family, my friends — had settled on the verdict that I had no athletic ability.

I believed the verdict too and built my character on it, and now, of all things, a new theory was surfacing: the "actually, he can move" theory.

That was how far my devil stick technique had come.

Everyone who saw me perform was surprised, and I was surprised by their surprise.

## A Lull

After all those back-to-back parties over the New Year, once mid-January came around the parties simply stopped.

We had only just woken up to how much fun parties were, and we could not wait to do it again.

But wait as we might, no party came.

Word was that the local schools had gone into exam season, so no parties would happen until February.

It was a reason we had to accept, so we gave up on parties.

Apparently this was common knowledge among people who knew Goa, and the ones who had been here many times before used the timing to travel to other towns.

We spent our days taking it easy in a Goa gone quiet again, the way it had been back in November.

We bought fresh seafood in town and enjoyed cooking it.

We smoked joints, played cards with music on, shot the breeze, and let the days go by.

Drifting through those ordinary days pushed our friendship up to the next level, and we came to see each other as family.
