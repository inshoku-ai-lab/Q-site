# My Sister's Escape

Among young Jehovah's Witnesses, going abroad on a homestay to do missionary work was in fashion.

The reason was that it let you fulfill your own dream of living in a foreign country while still being in keeping with your activities as a Jehovah's Witness.

Living alone within Japan was not encouraged, since it exposed you to worldly temptation, but going abroad to do missionary work in a country that needed the truth the Jehovah's Witnesses preached was encouraged.

For children raised in Jehovah's Witness households, this was one of the few escape routes from their parents.

I think there were two sides to it: the front you showed your parents and the other Witnesses, that you wanted to spread God's teaching to the world, and the real motive, that you wanted to get out from under your parents' control and spread your wings.

For that reason among others, studying foreign languages caught on with the young Jehovah's Witnesses who wanted to get away from their parents.

Just as I had felt dissatisfied with growing up in a religious household, my sister, who was suffering under a way of life that allowed her no freedom, seemed to have found her way out in going abroad.

I do not know when she first formed that plan, but she had been studying Spanish steadily for quite a long time.

Around the time I entered my third year of junior high, a Chilean Jehovah's Witness my sister knew was going back to Chile for a while, and she went along.

That was how my sister managed to escape, temporarily, from the curse of our household.

# Waking Up to Comedy

The effect of my sister going abroad, as far as I was concerned, was that I got the room to myself.

The first room of my own in my life.

Around the same time a new, largish television arrived at our house, so the small fourteen-inch set we had been using was handed down to my room.

![A woman in denim overalls leaning on an old television showing static, beside a briefcase and a suitcase](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog10-1-1.jpg)

Now a junior high boy with a room to myself, I started watching the dirty late-night programs that aired on Saturday nights, the ones my classmates were talking about.

But what struck me was not those dirty programs — it was the comedy show that started after they ended, around two in the morning, "Nichome Wachacha TV," recorded in a small theater with the young comedians of the Yoshimoto agency.

Fresh from their debuts and appearing on no television program besides this one, the Chihara Brothers, Tomonori Jinnai, Savanna, Sekai no Nabeatsu, Kendo Kobayashi, Kenji Tamura — comedians who are all doing well on television now — gathered in one place and did funny things.

They were young, they had momentum, and they were afraid of nothing.

It was a late-night slot on a small regional station, so there was probably little regulation coming down from upper management.

The edgy way they cut loose was no joke, and their young, driving sensibility hit my junior high self hard.

For me, born and raised in Kansai, comedy was part of daily life.

Ours was an ordinary Kansai household where Saturday meant watching Yoshimoto Shin-Kigeki over lunch, but up to then I had never taken any special interest in comedy.

The sharpness of the laughter on this program, though, cut straight into me, and let my thoroughly repressed heart experience a sense of release.

After that experience, I grew interested in comedy as a culture and started to follow it deliberately.

The free air of late-night television and late-night radio in particular got its hooks in me, and I began to experience a fresh world unlike anything I had known before.

This was the first passion I had experienced in my life.

Until then I had coasted along on inertia, doing whatever my parents said, but now I was taking an active interest of my own and chasing after it.

I think it was around this time that my own individuality began to grow.

# Waking Up to Studying

Around the second term of my third year of junior high, I suddenly began to feel that if I went on like that without studying, things would turn out badly.

I do not remember what set it off, but for some reason the thought just arrived.

Perhaps, with my individuality starting to grow, I had begun to think for myself.

Having decided to try studying, I went to a bookstore and bought a thin, easy-to-follow study guide that covered every subject evenly.

It was thin and simple, but since I had not studied at all until then, it was exactly right for me.

I had a good head on my shoulders to begin with and liked using it, so studying became something I enjoyed, the way you enjoy solving a puzzle or a riddle.

![A boy reading an open book in a dark room, his face lit by light from the page](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog10-2-1.jpg)

After a few months of studying, test day came.

The studying paid off, and the answers came to me.

Until then, not knowing the answers had stressed me out, and violent stomach pains had hit me at every single test, but this was the first time I got through a test without them.

By the end of the second term I was scoring over 350 points total across the five subjects.

Before that my total on each test had averaged around 200 points, so it was a big jump.

Those few months of the second term became the only period in which I did schoolwork properly.

I think what I wanted was the experience of doing schoolwork.
