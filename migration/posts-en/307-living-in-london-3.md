## First Day on the Job

Tuk, the owner's Thai wife, explained how the job worked, in Japanese.

Not that there was much to explain, since the job was washing dishes, but I was genuinely grateful to have all of it laid out for me in Japanese, start to finish.

The explanation was barely over when the dishes started arriving.

I washed them with everyone watching closely and lined them up on the drying rack.

Judging by their reactions, I had passed, more or less.

The dishes kept coming, one after another, and I kept washing without a break.

Somewhere around the point the evening rush had passed, Onee-san made me a staff meal.

In her Thai-accented English she told me, "Food, eat."

English even I could understand.

After I had finished the staff meal I cleared the last of the dishes, and the shift was over.

The first day ended without any trouble at all.

## House Hunting

The dishwashing job carried on without any particular trouble, one day much like the last.

Since the work started in the evening, I-chan and I put our days into house hunting.

This was the year 2000, back before the internet had developed to the point where you could look for a place to live on it.

The procedure was to go through a local listings magazine N had pointed us to, call, and ask to be shown the place.

And here too, N made the calls and moved things along for us.

We really did owe N for every last thing.

In the end, after looking at several rooms, we rented one at the next station over from N's place.

I-chan and I shared a room of about eight tatami mats, thirteen square meters (140 square feet).

The rent was about fifty-six thousand yen a month — some four hundred and fifty dollars — which came to twenty-eight thousand each.

Given how low the pay was and the fact that I-chan wasn't working, it was not an easy rent, but weighed against the experience of living in the middle of London it wasn't bad at all.

Our landlords were a Black family, and the father was a blind music teacher.

He was like Stevie Wonder, I thought, and I liked him on the spot.

The music lover in me was grabbed right by the heart.

He lived there with his daughter, who was in high school, while the mother lived somewhere else and came by the house about once a week.

The father had a very gentle, warm way about him, and I had the feeling this was a house we could live in without worrying.

And so, with a job and a place both found, the foundations of a life were coming together.
