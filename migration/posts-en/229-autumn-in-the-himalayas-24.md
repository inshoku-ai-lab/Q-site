## Brown Sugar

In Vashisht the rainy season ended, and as one clear day followed another, winter was coming on.

As the cold gradually set in, a kind of opium called brown sugar started going around among the travelers.

Going around didn't mean anyone was hooked on it — it was strictly a game among friends, curious travelers amusing themselves with something dangerous.

My own curiosity ran just as high, and although drugs like opium — the kind that leave your body heavy and slack — held no interest for me at all, I had no objection to satisfying that curiosity.

## Trying Opium

In my friend's room next door, we mixed brown sugar into the cannabis joint we always smoked and passed it around.

Compared to the usual cannabis high, it felt even more relaxed, the mind quieter.

I could see why everyone had started smoking brown sugar as winter drew closer.

Everyone went quiet, though, so smoking together wasn't much fun, and it was already late at night — I went back to my own room to fall asleep high on the brown sugar.

I lay down on the bed and listened to music.

In that kind of quiet, relaxed state I wanted quiet sounds, so I lay on the bed listening to experimental music, something like ambient.

## Something Strange

I have no idea how long I had been lying there.

Maybe I had fallen asleep, or maybe I was half awake.

When I came to, the drifting sounds of the experimental music were playing pretty loudly from the music player I'd set at my feet, so I propped myself up and stopped it.

I thought I had propped myself up and stopped the music, but the next thing I knew I was still lying on the bed.

Whether I had actually moved my body and propped myself up, or only my consciousness had slipped out and it merely felt that way, or the whole thing was a dream, I couldn't tell.

The music, though, was still playing.

I went to get up again, but this time my body wouldn't move at all.

Sleep paralysis had set in.

It was the first time in my life.

However hard I tried to move, my body stayed where it was, and the strange drifting sound went on and on — at my ear, or inside my head, I couldn't tell which.

There was a vague sense of something pressing down on my legs, too, but since I couldn't get up, I couldn't see down to my feet.

A small panic came over me, but at the same time — the opium, I suppose — I was relaxed and calm.

After a while of lying there unable to move, I dropped back into sleep as though blacking out.

In the morning I told my roommate about it, but apparently no music had been playing in the night.

I never did find out what it was, but I think of it as something like leaving my body, brought on by the opium.
