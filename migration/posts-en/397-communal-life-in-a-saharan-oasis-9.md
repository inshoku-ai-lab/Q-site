## The Weight of a Water Jar

I doubt many people have ever fetched water with a water jar, but frankly, it's pretty heavy.

A single jar weighed about ten kilos (22 pounds), and you filled it with roughly fifteen liters (four gallons) of water and carried it.

Unfortunately, the shape made it awkward to carry with both hands, so you ended up carrying one jar in each hand.

That meant walking around with fifty kilos (110 pounds) in all, so of course it was brutally heavy.

Even so, it was somehow easier than carrying a single jar with both hands, which, perversely, only made the whole thing more of a pain.

I filled the jars and then took twenty minutes to walk back over what was a ten-minute walk one way.

Add the walk there and the time spent drawing the water, and it came to about an hour of labor.

## Precious Water

This water carried a different weight of value than water that pours from a faucet.

It carried the weight of fifty kilos of water jars.

That fifty kilos' worth of preciousness, along with the desert's fierce heat and dryness, made me twice as grateful for water.

On top of that, the water was full of the earth's minerals and already tasted good, and once evaporation from the jar chilled it ice-cold, its flavor swelled many times over.

I can't think of any situation that would let you taste how good water is more fully than this; it was the most delicious water imaginable.

It also contained just the right amount of salt, so it soaked beautifully into a sweaty body.

It was a work of art by Mother Nature, and its name was "the right thing in the right place."

## An Everyday Scene

By the time I finished hauling the water, the people who had been sleeping in the hut would start getting up.

Built of earth and brick, the hut blocked the sun during the day and kept the inside cool.

Then, at night, it released the heat the bricks had soaked up during the day, keeping the inside warm.

The hut's door shut tight, so it was comfortable in there, sheltered from both the heat and the cold.

Which is why the people sleeping in the hut naturally drifted into going to bed late and getting up late.
