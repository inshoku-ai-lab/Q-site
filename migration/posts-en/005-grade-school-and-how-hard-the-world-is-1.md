# Starting Elementary School

Up until I started elementary school, there had been all sorts of things going on — family problems, life in the religion — but I myself was firmly protected, and nothing much had been hard on me.

The first time I faced the harshness of the world was after I entered elementary school.

For the first time in my life, I encountered what people call a bully, someone who would do me harm.

Up until then, raised in a gentle environment and at the same time having my defiance suppressed by loving discipline, I was ideal prey for a bully.

Looking back now, nothing that serious was done to me, no more than a mean elementary-school kid picking on me, but that was enough to turn my already quiet nature further inward.

The kind of child who spent recess alone in the classroom, reading.

My teachers were surprised at how bright I was, but told me I did not work well with others.

# No Good at Sports

After starting elementary school and coming into contact with a culture of comparison, I learned that I was bad at sports.

Since I had never done anything you could call exercise before that, it may have been only natural.

Every elementary schooler but me practiced softball in the daytime on Sundays and built up their athletic ability, but on Sundays I had to go to the Jehovah's Witness meeting, so I could not join softball practice.

Because the feeling that I was bad at it was so extreme, I came to convince myself that the truth was not that I was bad at sports but that I did not like sports.

Since I did not like it, I came to avoid all forms of exercise, my athletic ability did not develop, and that only fueled the belief that I was hopeless at sports.

This belief that I was bad at sports did a great deal to shape my character.

Bad at sports, smart, a suppressed personality, introverted, no ability to cooperate or socialize, a Jehovah's Witness and divorced-household background.

These elements, the beliefs included, piled up on one another, and in class I became a quiet oddball with little standing.

# The Restrictions of Being a Jehovah's Witness

Spending your elementary school years as a Jehovah's Witness child is pretty hardcore austerity, and I feel I finished off a lifetime's worth of austerity in childhood.

Once I was home from school, Tuesday and Thursday nights were the meeting, Saturday afternoon was field service, and Sunday was field service and the meeting.

The only time I could play was after school on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday.

Having little time to play is naturally hard on a child, but harder still is having television you cannot watch.

There were no VCRs or anything back then, so the only way to watch TV was in real time, and the anime and such that aired while I was at the meeting were impossible to see.

Anime with violent depictions were forbidden as well, so there were many times I could not keep up with the conversations of elementary-school boys.

Here too my character was warped, and I talked myself into believing that it was not that I could not watch TV, only that I had no interest in TV.

At the time I was frustrated that I wanted to watch and could not, but now I am deeply grateful to my mother for keeping me away from violent television programs.

It is hard to be an elementary-school boy with the violent elements stripped out, but as an adult who grew up without taking in much violence, I think it was for the best.

# Bullying

Even so, the violence latent inside me had not disappeared, and rather than being used to resist the bullies, it came out in the form of bullying someone weaker still.

I was spitefully cruel to a child with an intellectual disability.

Thinking about it now, elementary-school society too was a perfect miniature of pyramid society.

The kids who were tough in a fight and the popular ones rose to the top, and the further down you went, the weaker and more exploited your position.

Those below exploited those still further below.

The exploitation here was not material but mental, a matter of energy.

What I did and what was done to me were both truly trivial elementary-school harassment.

It was a far cry from the violent incidents you see in the news, but even so there was a clear hierarchy there, and there was abuse of power.

Within that class society, my life sat at the very bottom.
