## Outside the Known

The strange old guy was completely outside the frame of common sense as I understood it.

The way he cut loose was like a young comedian clowning as hard as he could, but nothing about it was forced; you could see that he had lived his whole life this way.

Through dancing to Goa trance he had gotten hold of something and become certain of it, and he had let go of everything.

He was far beyond anyone I had ever seen in how completely he had let go, and just by being there dancing, his presence had an enormous effect on everyone around him.

## Worry Mode

Deep in worry mode, in the middle of the vortex of thought, I watched him and it shook me.

If I kept going to Goa trance parties like this and kept taking LSD, wouldn't I let go the way he had and never be able to come back?

Or maybe he actually wanted to live an ordinary life, but he had let go too far to return, and since nothing could be done about it, he had no choice but to keep dancing like a crazy man?

Or maybe he looked like he was enjoying the dance, but the truth was that being unable to take any other road was painful, and he was dancing that hard to forget the pain?

I was sitting there thinking like that when O came over beside me and said, "Ahh, I'm beat. This is so much fun, but I'm too tired to dance any more," swaying her body with a huge smile on her face, and started in on her water.

Watching her, I decided that O was really suffering too, and was working hard at pretending to have fun so as not to make me sad.

From the overdose — eight times the LSD I needed — my power to convince myself of a thing had grown hundreds of times stronger than usual.

My conviction that everyone was actually suffering and pretending to have fun purely out of concern for each other was working perfectly.

The party as I saw it through my own eyes was passing through the filter of a conviction that every last person there had to be on a bad trip.

## The Solution

At this rate the bad trip would go on until morning; I had to do something.

So I found N, my Japanese friend, and got him to sell me an ecstasy tablet.

He too had been traveling the Goa trance world for a few years already, picking up drugs cheap from somewhere and selling them in Goa, making the small money that kept his travels going.

The ecstasy cost about the same as ten days of rent, insane money by my sense of prices, but this was no time to worry about money.

Why an ecstasy tablet: I had read in a book that ecstasy contains a compound called MDMA, which forces the symptoms of depression to lift, so that taking it makes you feel happy right away and gets you out of a bad trip.

Having believed myself onto a bad trip and thereby actually gotten one, I hurried the tablet I got from N down with water.

Now I would be all right; the bad trip was supposed to let me go.

I waited a while, and no effect came.

My thoughts went on spinning around and around, and the mood would not clear.

My body was heavy too, nowhere near able to dance.

## Plastic Wrap

That was when something occurred to me.

In the dark, in that panic — had I swallowed the tablet with the plastic wrap still around it?

There was no way it had been handed to me without the wrap, and I had no memory of taking the wrap off.

The LSD had my memory badly scrambled, but you would think unwrapping a tablet was the kind of thing that would stay with me.

I hunted N down in the party and asked whether the ecstasy tablet had been wrapped in plastic, and he said it had been wrapped up tight.

With that fact in hand, I plunged into a bad trip deeper still.
