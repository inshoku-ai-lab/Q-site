## Kochi

When I reached Kochi Prefecture, the mood shifted a little again.

There was something more polished about the place.

The air felt brighter, or maybe the atmosphere was just lighter.

The prefecture wraps around a south-facing bay, so maybe the natural environment there is simply warmer and gentler.

Kochi City was small, and right past downtown the road ran into fishing villages surrounded by nature.

The sea was calm and clear.

As I sat by the water eating lunch, I noticed something colorful under the surface.

Believe it or not, it was coral!

I wasn't far from the city, and I was only a few days' ride by bicycle from Osaka Bay to begin with.

I never imagined I'd see coral in a place like this.

Running into so many different kinds of nature made me happier than I could stand.

## Drunk Driving

From Kochi, I decided to head for a port at the far northwestern tip of Shikoku, taking a national highway through the mountains.

I was sticking to national highways for the whole bike trip, but the ones along the coast and the ones through the mountains get very different amounts of use.

Once you're on the mountain roads, the countryside gets a lot more rural all at once.

At one point I saw an old man in a kei truck, one of those tiny Japanese pickup trucks, driving with a beer in one hand.

I never expected to see things this loose in Japan, and it shocked me, but looking around at a place with no houses for kilometers in any direction and no other cars on the road, I could sort of see it.

## The Shortcut

As I pushed deeper into the mountains, a sign appeared: "Road closed ahead due to landslide."

This mountain road was the only way through, and turning back would mean a huge detour, several days of wasted time.

In a car or on a motorcycle, turning back might not be such a hassle, but when you've spent hours pedaling up mountain roads, it's a different story.

Figuring whatever happens, happens, I kept pushing on.

About an hour of pedaling past that sign, I came to another one, "Road closed due to landslide," with a rope strung across the road so no one could get through.

The second warning.

I ducked under the rope and kept going.

Having come this far, I wanted to see a landslide with my own eyes and find out what it actually looked like.
