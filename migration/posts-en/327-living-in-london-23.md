## D Takes It Hard

D had planned carefully, spent the money and the effort, and come all the way to England for the Glastonbury Festival he had dreamed about. One stupid mistake, and instead of the festival of his dreams he had spent those days at a shrimp farm in the wetlands. He was down on himself for being an idiot.

Well, of course he was.

D wanted to find some way to make coming to England mean something.

D had heard that a Japanese DJ named DJ Tsuyoshi, famous in the Goa trance world, ran a regular event in London and that it was on that weekend, and he said he had to be there, whatever it took.

Word was that it was a lot of fun.

This, surely, would be enough to send D home to Japan satisfied.

## The Weekend

So we invited N, my Goa friend from last year, and along with D, my Goa friend from this year, the four of us decided to go to the Goa trance event that weekend.

We got together on Saturday night and headed for a neighborhood in south London called Brixton.

What surprised me when we got there was that the clubs were packed into this one neighborhood, for whatever reason, and music of every genre imaginable was blasting out from all sides.

It was as if the whole neighborhood were throwing a carnival for the weekend.

Eccentrics dressed up in outrageous style were everywhere.

You could tell more or less what kind of music event a person was headed to just from what they had on.

At a funk party we passed, young people with Afros were hanging around.

That looked fun too, but we made for what we had come for: Return to the Source, the event DJ Tsuyoshi organized.

## The Event

There were so many clubs in this neighborhood that we couldn't find the one we wanted.

N, the only one of us who could really speak English, stopped a young guy going past and asked him where it was.

Oh, that one, he said, and pointed — it's in the basement, right here.

What luck.

The very first person we asked, and we had found the place.

We went down the stairs in high spirits, bought our tickets, and dove out onto the dark dance floor.

The music playing on the floor was far too slow to call Goa trance.

It was probably still early, and they would build it up from here.

The music itself sounded good, so we set our bags down and started dancing.
