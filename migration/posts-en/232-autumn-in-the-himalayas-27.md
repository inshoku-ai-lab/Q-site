## The Himalayan Hut

Life in the hut, set a little apart from the center of the village, was quite comfortable.

I'd get up in the morning, light a fire, and make chai.

While it boiled I'd roll a joint, and the day began with that first smoke of the morning.

Tea in hand, I made breakfast, and once I had eaten, went down to the hot springs for a morning bath.

Back at the house, I cooked on the stove.

Days went by, smoking joints and yakking with O about travel.

## Drug Running

A lot of the friends from my Goa days were starting to get into the hash-running trade.

Goa had hit us too hard for any of us to fit back into Japanese society properly, and we had no way of earning money while we traveled; for beginners on the road like us, no other way of staying out there came to mind.

For most of the travelers who had been partying in Goa it came down to two choices: grind out money in Japan at hard short-term jobs, or come up to Manali, buy hash, carry it back to Japan, and make a killing.

Most of the men went into physical labor, and most of the women into the nightlife business.

For a traveler who was young and short on life experience, that was what making money fast came down to.

I had made mine in short, concentrated bursts of scaffolding work in Japan, but carrying hash for money was always there in the back of my mind.

It was always tempting, and it always carried the huge risk of losing every freedom I had.

Even if carrying hash into Japan paid big, the odds of getting caught were overwhelmingly high — conceivable as a desperate last resort, maybe, but not a risk I wanted to take.

What I had in mind instead was moving drugs inside India, where a bribe could take care of it if I ever did get caught.

And now, with the autumn harvest over and hash starting to move in quantity, and my own departure from Vashisht drawing close, it was the perfect time to buy.
