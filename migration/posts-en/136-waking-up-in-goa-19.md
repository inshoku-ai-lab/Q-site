## Party Days

The closer Christmas came, the more travelers poured in, the numbers doubling by the day.

The pace of it was genuinely startling, and it lifted the whole town into a festival mood.

Out on the street, travelers in eerie, strange, flashy getups were romping around.

As the crowds swelled, the parties grew in number and in size.

Around this time a big outdoor party went off about once every four days.

The next one was at a place called Disco Valley, in the gully between two hills near the sea.

It was a big site, and apparently only serious, full-blown parties were thrown there — anyone who had been to Goa before would light up at the name alone.

Of course, we did everything we could to get ready for it.

During the day we took it easy in the shade of a tree, saving our energy.

We didn't treat partying as a pastime; we went at it with our lives on the line.

## The Current

Night came, and as if that was what we had been waiting for, we banded together and swung onto our motorcycles.

Even on the way there, you could already feel the buildup.

Motorcycle after motorcycle ran beside us, all of them headed the same way.

I didn't know exactly where Disco Valley was, but following the stream of motorcycles made it obvious.

The way streams merge one after another and turn into a great river, motorcycles came out of every side lane and joined a single current.

The current kept swelling, and then, once we got close to a certain point, it did the opposite and began to clog.

Ahead of us was a long jam of motorcycles, and past that, an absurd number of them parked.

A booming beat was pounding away in the distance, rattling our hipbones.

## Chaos

Of course there was no parking attendant where people left their motorcycles — total chaos.

Park in the middle of that and you wouldn't just be unable to get out, you wouldn't even be able to find your motorcycle again.

So we could get out easily, and so we wouldn't lose track of them, we parked under a tree a little way off.

This whole area was a plateau, with the beach below a sheer cliff.

Twenty meters (sixty-five feet) or so, I'd guess.

The party site, too, was down at the bottom of that cliff.

Looking down from the top, the party was darkness with the kerosene lamps of the chai stalls glittering in it, giving off a light all its own and a smell all its own.

And into that, a hard beat and an eerie melody came ringing.

Set down in a valley as it was, the sound echoed back on itself and doubled the strangeness.

## Disco Valley

First we looked for a chai stall to sit at.

After we had walked around a while, S — the man I had taken for an undercover cop at the last party — spotted us and waved with a huge grin.

When I told my friends about my undercover-cop theory after that party, they howled with laughter all over again and set me straight: I get too fixed on my own ideas.

The others had talked with him far more than I had, and they told me there was no way he was a cop, so relax.

We let S pull us in and joined him at the chai stall.

We ordered chai and smoked a joint.

This time we had rolled the joints in advance.

When the LSD comes on, big movements like dancing or running are fine, but fine work with the hands and fingers becomes difficult.

Rolling a joint in the dark with the sea wind blowing, wrecked on LSD, is nearly impossible, and I had struggled with it many times before.

But when the LSD was too strong you seized up, and it took the loosening effect of cannabis to work that out.
