## Bob Marley

While we were watching the performance up on stage, N, who I had arranged to meet there, turned up.

She had apparently been having plenty of fun herself with a different set of friends.

She boasted that this event was the whole reason she was in this city.

Her dream, she said, was to meet and marry a Rastaman who was half Black and half white, like Bob Marley.

Apparently she wanted a child with three races mixed together.

## The Café

We decided to head for a café and take a breather.

It was people, people, people in every direction, which made resting difficult, but being able to sit down in a chair at all was better than nothing.

The place was a local café about halfway between the house we lived in and N's, and on weekends, she said, all sorts of DJs came through to put on events.

N had been there the week before as well, she said, and had a good time at a music event.

What floored me as she talked was that the DJ the week before had been, of all people, DJ Vadim, a Russian hip-hop DJ — an artist I had spent years dreaming of seeing live someday.

That he had been playing at a little local café like this one was a happy surprise.

N did not seem to really get it, and said something like, "Mmm, yeah, it was pretty good."

To me he was a musician I worshipped as a god, so the gap between us was staggering.

The ticket had been cheap too, about fifteen hundred yen — twelve dollars or so — and here in London, it seemed, the musicians I worship as gods played their sets at prices anyone could afford.

## The Carnival Goes On

After that we went back out into the streets and cut loose at the speakers set up all over the city.

The carnival ran through the next day as well, and we enjoyed the last days of summer for everything they were worth.

Dancing full out in the streets of my own neighborhood was a rare thing to get to do.

I never heard anything about the Notting Hill Carnival after that, so I gave no thought at all to what had become of it, but looking it up online a moment ago, I found it is still running, and has apparently grown into the largest festival in Europe.

A map of the carnival came up while I was at it, so let me point out the address where I was living back then.

![](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/tfL-Notting-Hill-Carnival-map-1-2.png)

I lived on Lancaster Rd, just below Ladbroke Grove station, a little to the left of the center of the image.

So what, you might say — but it is a fond memory.
