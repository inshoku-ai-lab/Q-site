## The Festival Begins

At last the day we had been waiting for arrived: the festival was opening.

It would start on December 30, 2000, in the twentieth century, and end on January 2, 2001, in the twenty-first — a festival that crossed the turn of the century.

A chance to cross from one century into the next comes once in a lifetime, if that.

Travelers were coming from all over the world to celebrate this special moment.

And we had come from England to be part of it.

## Tourists

The town was filling up too, the number of people doubling by the day.

Talking to the new arrivals, I found that quite a few had flown in that day and would fly home four days later, right after the festival ended.

For people living in Europe, catching a couple of connecting flights to Morocco to enjoy the year-end holidays seemed to be nothing out of the ordinary.

It was probably like Japanese people going to Hawaii for New Year's.

We had spent more than a month traveling slowly to get here, so it felt strange to meet people who had been working in an office the day before.

## The Shared Taxi

Shared taxis were running back and forth between town and the festival site, one after another.

We got into one too and headed out to the site.

This time we hadn't come as volunteers; we had come purely as guests.

Seeing from the outside what we had been watching from the inside felt fresh.

At a glance, you would never guess that a few days earlier a gust of wind had blown everything away.

Ignorance really is bliss.

We went in and headed for the chill-out area we had helped decorate.

Most of our friends were bound to be gathered there.

Here, at last, the tent we'd brought along was going to earn its keep.

I hadn't been camping since elementary school, but just the idea of sleeping outdoors was exciting.

And this was camping at a turn-of-the-century festival on an oasis island in the Sahara, so I couldn't have been happier.

Giggling and goofing around, we pitched our tent.
