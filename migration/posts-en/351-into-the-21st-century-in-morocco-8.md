## Haggling

When I asked the price, he said a 200-gram (seven-ounce) block would be about 32,000 yen, roughly 260 dollars.

That was close to the amount we'd brought, and 160 yen a gram, about a dollar thirty, was cheaper than I'd expected.

In London I'd been paying around 1,500 yen a gram, twelve dollars or so, and in Japan it apparently goes for something like 8,000 yen, around sixty-five dollars, so 160 yen a gram was a very welcome price.

But if we jumped at it, he'd take us for pushovers, and if we happily paid up, the next traveler would be stuck paying more.

So I made a show of thinking it over, then tried to talk him down.

I threw in a bluff, too, saying we were looking into buying somewhere else.

This kind of back-and-forth is standard in India, where if you skip it, you get ripped off at whatever price they name.

## Deal

Saying we were looking elsewhere, and acting as if we were about to leave, must have done the trick.

The old guy took our offer and agreed to about 30,000 yen, around 240 dollars.

Had the bargaining I'd learned in India paid off? Or was this, too, part of the act, and were we getting ripped off even at the lower price?

Probably the latter, but I was satisfied that we had at least haggled before buying.

Now we wouldn't be the ones who pushed up the price of hash in this village and made things harder for the travelers who came after us.

We told him we'd go get the money and left his house for the time being.

On the outside we kept straight faces, but inside we were giddy at getting stuff this good at this price.

Celebrating now would ruin all our acting, though, so we scurried back to where we were staying, and scurried right back to the old guy's house.
