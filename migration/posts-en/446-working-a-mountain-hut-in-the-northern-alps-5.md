## Day One

Once the eerie chanting at the morning assembly and the "environmental upkeep" that felt like Buddhist monastic training were over, the real work began.

It was the first day for all three of us — me, I-chan, and R — so before doing any actual work, we started by learning how the work as a whole fit together.

How the hut was organized, and how the work moved along from one step to the next.

Everything, from how to walk on the stairs to how to take off your shoes, was governed by detailed rules, and the hut ran because everyone followed them.

I'd been traveling and living however I pleased, so I hated rules like that, but as long as I was on the payroll, there was nothing to do but follow them.

Each of those little rules had a solid reason behind it, one that made sense to me, so I decided to go along with them — but being managed this tightly, down to the last detail, was a far cry from the free breath of the natural world I'd been looking for in mountain-hut work.

## Lunch

After the rundown of how the hut worked, we were taught how to serve lunch.

Serving had its own detailed rules, too, and each of them had a solid reason behind it.

Even wiping a table had a carefully worked-out method: keep every crumb off the floor while moving your hands as fast as possible.

There were rules for which way the udon faced on the tray, and even for how the chopsticks were laid down.

Every last detail was spelled out, and it was stifling, but it also made clear to me that for people to work efficiently in such a cramped space, everyone had to move as one.

## Departments

By the time dinner started, everyone's department had been announced.

Since I'd be moving to another, smaller hut in three weeks, I was left unassigned at the big hut where I was now, and would help out with all sorts of work across the board.

I-chan was put in charge of the "café," picked for her feminine charm.

R was put in "facilities," which handled all the carpentry, on the strength of his background as a carpenter.

I'd wanted to learn how the working world runs by doing all kinds of jobs, so I was glad not to be tied to any one department and to get a taste of a bit of everything.
