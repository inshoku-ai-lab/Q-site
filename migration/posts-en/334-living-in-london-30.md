## Too Much Hospitality

We welcomed T in with a chillum ready and waiting, the thing every traveler in India loves.

We had charas laid in as well, the hash resin that comes from India.

We took him in with everything in place and gave him the full hospitality.

It seems, though, that we had things a little too well in place.

T ate an enormous dinner, smoked a strong chillum, got far too high, and went down.

For two hours or so he lay stretched out in something close to a half faint, and then all at once he was on his feet and at the sink, heaving it all up.

Our room was put together a little oddly, with a small sink inside it.

In a way, that may have been lucky for T.

There was no time for him to make a run for the toilet, after all.

For everyone but T, it was a nightmare.

His vomit clogged the sink and would not go down.

The smell of it filled that cramped eight-tatami room — thirteen square meters (140 square feet).

On top of that, every one of us was wrecked, and cleaning it up was not going to happen.

D had made himself small and kept repeating, "Sorry, I'm really sorry."

T was half unconscious.

I-chan and I were feeling sick ourselves — our guest had covered our room in puke — but there was no blaming T for it, and we just drifted along in that unnatural air.

Nothing could be done about it that day, so we opened the window to get away from the smell and got through the night a little cold.

A few days later T and D went back to Japan, and for D it had turned out to be a trip with one disaster after another.

He seemed to have enjoyed himself plenty all the same.
