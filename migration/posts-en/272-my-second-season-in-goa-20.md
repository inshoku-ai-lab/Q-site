## Friends in Goa

All kinds of Japanese travelers came to Goa.

In the area where we were, something like five percent of the people were Japanese, I'd guess.

They came in every type, but the only ones who became friends were the ones on the same wavelength — a small slice of the whole.

I was traveling India, going around to Goa trance parties inside the country and having a good time, so the people who became friends were all doing more or less the same thing.

And friends of one type pulled in more friends of the same type.

The comfortable circle of friends kept widening.

## Chillum Chelo

At bottom, our whole circle of friends belonged to the chillum circle.

We came together to smoke chillums, and built trust with one another through that chillum connection.

When you went over to a friend's house, the first thing you did was knead a chillum.

That was the courtesy you performed first on arriving at someone else's house.

Something like the small gift you are expected to bring to a house in Japan, I suppose.

And the host kneaded one of his own, as hospitality for his guest.

Once the chillums had gone back and forth and things reached a lull, we would put on music and talk.

In a house with a kitchen everyone would cook together, and that meant a chillum to get the cooking started, a chillum before the meal, and another chillum after it.

And at the very end, you smoked one more chillum on the way out the door.

That departing chillum had a name of its own — chillum chelo — after chelo, the Hindi word for taking your leave.

Without a chillum chelo the gathering never quite came to a proper close, and like the first chillum when you arrived, it was an important piece of the ritual of greeting.

## Why the Chillum Mattered

Since every bit of social life ran through the chillum like this, smoking good charas out of a good chillum mattered.

Goa society had an invisible class system: the higher up you went, the more years you had on the road, the better the charas you smoked and the better the chillum you smoked it from, the closer you were to the top of the Goa trance music business, the tighter your ties to the big drug dealers and the mafia.

At the same time, the higher up you went, the more intense the life you had lived, the more money you made, the more fun your days were, the better you did with the opposite sex, and the more interesting the friends around you.

And everyone belonged to that class system without knowing it, and without knowing it, everyone was trying to climb.
