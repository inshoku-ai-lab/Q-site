## ATM

After eating, I rode my bike to a bank nearby.

Unfortunately, it was Sunday, and the ATM wasn't running.

I'd been traveling by bike with no regard for the day of the week, so it never occurred to me that it might be Sunday.

Now there was nothing to do but wait until the next morning.

I'd wanted to get out of town that day and camp somewhere nice in the woods, but it looked like I'd have to camp somewhere in town instead.

I went back to the ramen shop, explained the situation to the manager, and left my driver's license as collateral, promising to come back the next day and pay.

The manager kept eyeing me with suspicion the whole time.

A few hundred yen short, just a few dollars, and I was stuck in town. What rotten luck.

## Sleeping Rough

This is one of the hard parts of traveling by bicycle: you don't want to go back over road you've already pedaled with your own legs.

If the spot I'd planned to camp at was an hour out of town, the round trip would waste two hours.

It was mid-December and getting colder by the day, so I didn't want to lose any more time.

I didn't want to spend extra money on a hotel because of a holdup like this, either.

So, with no other choice, I decided to sleep rough in town.

I found the big parking lot of a pachinko parlor in a spot with little foot traffic, and pitched my tent in the dark, out of reach of the streetlights.

If I left first thing in the morning, there shouldn't be any problem, and even if the police came by, they'd understand once I explained.

But camping like this felt as if the ramen shop manager's look, the way he'd eyed me like a beggar, was coming true.

All I'd done was ride my bike here under my own power, finally make it to Kyushu, and happily eat a bowl of champon. And for that, I was sleeping rough in a pachinko parking lot. It made me furious.

Even my light beer buzz started to feel like a bad drunk nursing a grudge.

It was only my first day in Kyushu, and I was already getting homesick for Shikoku.

The people of Oita had done nothing wrong, but this one ramen shop manager had painted my whole picture of Oita in ugly colors.
