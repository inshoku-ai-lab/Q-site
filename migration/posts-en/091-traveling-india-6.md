# Indian Trains

I'd heard that trains in India were something else, and I had wondered just how, but the rumors were right: for a man who had come here in search of "the chaos of the world," they satisfied my curiosity completely.

Travelers were not the only people at the station.

Some worked in and around the place; others were beggars and thieves prowling for travelers to target.

And people were not the only ones at the station either.

Cows, this being India, of course, but also stray dogs, stray cats, stray monkeys, pigeons, crows — the place stood open, equally, to every living thing that had a use for a train station.

The big-heartedness of the country showed in details like this.

The station formed a complete culture all by itself, and it would be no exaggeration to call it a town, or a village.

Out in the parking area beyond the station, a family of beggars gathered branches and cooked over them.

Skin-and-bone stray dogs fought vicious crows over the leftovers, life against life, while monkeys and thieves kept their eyes wide open for the first chance to take something off a traveler.

I thought of myself as a grubby, broke backpacker, but inside that station I counted as the top one percent.

Which meant I was a target for thieves, and I understood perfectly well that if I did not stay sharp, they would get me.

I carried the right amount of tension, but the fear was behind me, and the thought of throwing myself into the sea of chaos spread out in front of me had me excited.

![](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog91-1.jpg)

Luckily my backpack was on the small side and I carried little, so keeping it on my shoulder all day was no weight worth minding.

As long as it stayed on my shoulder, the odds of losing it to a thief were overwhelmingly lower.

When I set it down at my feet, I wedged it between my legs, so that some part of the bag was always touching me.

That right amount of tension worked on my appetite for adventure, and it made me happy.

# The Train Ride

On a three-day train journey across China I had sat on a soft seat the whole way and rubbed the skin off my backside, so this time, to be safe, I booked a sleeper car.

Most foreign travelers book the second-class sleeper, so I went with the same.

Getting ready before departure, I bought plenty of snacks and water.

Vendors come through during the ride, so there was no need to stock up, but I did it just in case.

This was India; anything could happen.

The train was already standing in the station.

My seat, or bed, was easy to find.

The top of a three-tier bunk.

The middle berth folded away during the day, so there was no lying down on it.

The bottom one had to be shared with other passengers in the daytime, and for reasons like that, all of my friends had told me to take the top.

Up there you could stay stretched out day and night and hold the space as your own.

And leaving my backpack on the bed would cut the risk of theft down a good deal.

The train began to move with a heavy, suspect creaking.

There was weight in the sound, and it lifted the mood of the journey.

Departure was at night.

We should reach Varanasi sometime after noon.

The bed was reasonably soft, reasonably clean, not bad at all.

No room for violent tossing and turning, but for one night alone it would do.

Wrapped in the thin blanket I had brought, I fell asleep.

# Morning

Morning came, the light grew, and I could feel people starting to get up.

But I wanted to stay lying down a little longer.

Against that wish entirely, someone shook my foot and woke me.

What on earth is going on — I shot upright, and a boy selling chai asked me, "Chai? Don't you want chai?"

India, of course: privacy, holding back, consideration for others, none of that fine-grained courtesy carried any weight here.

![](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog91-2.jpg)

The boy wanted to sell chai, so he woke me up.

That was all.

Being woken against my will made me angry, but I decided to forgive him on account of his innocence, and had him fix me a chai to start the morning with.

The price was, naturally, a rip-off, so I complained that it was too high and got it cut in half.

Waking up on a train in India and starting the day with a hot chai made fresh on the spot — that is as good as travel gets.

The day began with a thank-you to the boy who had forced me out of sleep.
