# Public Safety in Nishinari

My life in the doya district of Nishinari — Osaka's skid row, blocks of flophouses for day laborers — was already coming up on a year and a half.

The place was lawless to a degree that was honestly funny, and the punk kid in me enjoyed the thrill of living somewhere that dangerous.

My shift at Tsutaya, the video rental chain, ended at two in the morning; after that came beef bowls with the other part-timers, and I'd get home at three or four.

At that hour I would deliberately race through Airin, the heart of the doya district, on my bicycle.

The covered shopping arcade was where the vagrants slept, and the homeless who hadn't managed to get a room for the night sheltered under its roof from the wind and the rain.

In the parks during the day there were middle-aged meth heads, hard-faced yakuza, and small-time thugs whose faces had caved in from huffing paint thinner.

Morbid curiosity, or plain curiosity, or the adventuring urge you get from a role-playing game — whatever it was, I often prowled the danger zones on my bicycle.

The thugs and the yakuza weren't confined to Nishinari Ward — they were regulars at the small video store where I worked.

The thug with the ruined face made a habit of ranting at the top of his voice while renting the next installment of some yakuza movie series, and the hard-faced yakuza made a habit of bragging about himself and then lecturing me.

I hated thugs and I hated yakuza, but the weird thrill was fun, so I liked it.

# Turning Twenty

Summer was coming on, and my twentieth birthday — the age of adulthood in Japan — was a few months away.

For everyone, I think, the turn from nineteen to twenty carries enormous weight.

In my case, the weight came from a poet I loved, Uotake Hamada:

"If you want to accomplish something big in your life, take action before you turn twenty."

Those words had a powerful hold on me.

At seventeen I had steeled myself and walked out of both the religion I was raised in and high school, so I had no intention whatsoever of living a life of half measures.

I hoped that by taking some kind of action while I was still a teenager, I'd shed a skin and get to live a life that was truly my own.

Thanks to that line, I decided to leave on a long trip to India before I turned twenty.

That decision gave me an enormous amount of energy.

With a goal in the near future now settled, I was more alive than ever, brimming with curiosity and inspiration.

When I first started at Tsutaya I had wanted to work in film or in comedy, but from around the time I found Tortoise's music, my interest had shifted to making a living as a musician.

After the long trip to India, the plan was to move back into my family's house, keep my living costs down, and throw myself into making music.

I wanted to take what living alone in the city had given me, and what backpacking had given me, and turn it into music.

# The Last Days of the Tsutaya Job

Once it sank in that quitting was only a few months off, it sank in too that a chance like this — to watch movies and collect music at this volume — would not come around again easily.

I watched movies at an even faster clip and kept recording music onto MiniDisc.

Over that year and a half of living on my own, the total number of movies I had seen passed two thousand, and the number of albums I had recorded onto MiniDisc reached six hundred.

Some of those movies I had watched before I moved out, but the arithmetic still works out to an average of more than five hours a day on films while I was living alone.

And the MiniDiscs came in at better than one a day, which is not bad.

This was before YouTube existed, and getting your hands on that much good film and that much good music was close to impossible.

I had taken the Tsutaya job to watch all the movies and hear all the music I could, and I pulled it off completely, and the satisfaction ran deep.

Those days were, quite purely, work and movies and music and books.

Apart from the beef bowls after a shift, there was going over to T's place — a work friend close to my age — and now and then a club with A-kun, who had come to Thailand with me; nothing social beyond that, and a girlfriend was out of the question.

It amazes me now that I could focus that purely on nothing but culture, but I suppose that is what the energy of your teens is like.
