## Back to Vashisht

Once we had had our fill of Parvati Valley, we came back to Vashisht.

My old familiar room, the friends I had known a long time, and the scalding open-air bath all gave us a warm welcome.

Back at the guesthouse, I found that U — who for a while in Goa had been staying in the room next to mine — had come out from Japan.

With his dreadlocks and the piercing through his lower jaw, he had already fallen out of step with society before he set out the year before, and what he went through in Goa settled it: he was one of those travelers who had decided to live outside society altogether.

In Japan in the late nineties nobody wore dreadlocks, so his outlandish look drew eyes, and the resolve he had about his own life struck me as cool.

He was fond of the word *kabuku*, which meant something like expressing your own beauty as loudly and extravagantly as you possibly could.

I agreed with him and it rubbed off on me, and I had started growing my hair out to get dreadlocks like his.

## The Lupin Gang

I had shaved my head down to the skin about ten months before this, so it had not grown out much, but I had quit shampoo and washed with soap alone so the hair would tangle easily and dread up faster.

Right after the shave, people used to tease me that between the cropped head and my tall, skinny build I looked like Lupin the Third, out of the anime.

By now the hair had grown to medium length, and my chin beard was a few centimeters (an inch or two) long.

The teasing shifted: with that hair and that beard I looked like Daisuke Jigen, from the same Lupin the Third anime.

## Shanti

U was twenty-two, a year older than me, which still left him far younger than the rest of the travelers, and, as his look made obvious, he had an extremely rebellious streak.

He liked loud thrills and wanted to live a rock-and-roll life, but a while spent in Vashisht slowly began to change him.

Day after day of taking it easy with the mighty Himalayan peaks in view, warming up in the hot springs, and eating in easy company with friends at a restaurant where the food took about two hours to come out after you ordered — somewhere in all that, the sharp edges on him went soft.

By the time he had been in Vashisht a month, he had gotten to where he was running the other travelers down: rushing around from place to place is so stupid, who needs it.
