# A Leaning Toward Subculture

Life back when I was working at the pizza place was pretty monotonous.

Eight-hour shifts at the pizza place, seven minutes by bicycle from my parents' house, then straight home — that was the whole of it.

The rest of the time went to standing around reading in bookstores, watching movies and TV, playing games.

Outside of the pizza place I had not a single friend, and did nothing constructive — just killed time.

Among the books I came across in those days of reading on my feet was a subculture magazine called *Quick Japan*, which has been going for a full twenty-five years now.

I spotted the first issue at the bookstore where I always did my reading, and the cover caught my eye, so I picked it up — and the field the magazine covered, "subculture," pulled me in hard.

It presented underground culture from interesting angles — the sort of thing that never surfaces in the mass media — and with all this obscure, provocative information I had never seen or heard of before, my sense of the world seemed to widen.

The obscurity and the twistedness of what it covered overlapped with my own situation, and I felt a strange kinship with it.

Like coming face to face with the world I lived in.

# The Poet Uotake

One of the people I learned about through this magazine was a poet named Sandaime Uotake Nario Hamada.

He might be better known as the man who later married Nene Otsuka and then divorced her.

![A woman lying on a white fur blanket with eyes closed, holding a small white puppy](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog26-1.jpg)

He aimed at nothing but living out his life looking cool, and wrote no poems except ones praising himself.

The way he lived was the art he made.

His rock-and-roll values, leaping clean past common sense, hit me hard, and I became a fan all at once.

Reading his poetry collections and his autobiography, I longed for that independent set of values, that powerful sense of self-affirmation.

There were all kinds of influences, but the biggest came from these words:

"If you want to accomplish something big in your life, take action by the time you turn twenty."

For me at seventeen — sensitive, and out of the framework I was raised in — those words hit home hard.

It felt like a hand at my back, pushing me on in my choice to drop out of high school.

# Meeting My Brother

About half a year after I learned about Uotake, my brother and his wife invited me over for dinner.

The plan was to eat at their place and maybe watch a video.

My brother and I had lived apart since I was three, and we had met about once a year.

For the past few years we had been meeting weekly to rent videos, but we were still playing the roles of older and younger brother.

Once a month he would take me to a movie, but I rarely went over to his place.

This time too, I think it had been about a year since I last visited.

The first thing that caught my attention when I stepped into my brother's place was a framed piece hanging on the wall.

Something like calligraphy was written on it, so I read it.

It was, of all things, a Uotake poem I loved.

I had never imagined anyone I knew would know a poet this obscure, and for it to be my own brother — a huge fan at that, with the poem framed on his wall — was utterly unthinkable.

Of course my brother (twenty-four) had no idea a seventeen-year-old kid like me knew Uotake either, so he was startled and thrilled.

Talking about the poet we both loved, we hit it off immediately.

![Two boys standing back to back against a white background, both laughing with eyes shut](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog26-2.jpg)

As it turned out, my brother had a private love of subculture too, and had been buying *Quick Japan* and the like himself.

The conversation kept building, and when it was time to go he handed me a paper bag stuffed with manga.

All of it his recommendations — an invitation to come on into the world of the subculture nerd.

That dinner was the day of my "fateful encounter" with the brother I had lived apart from for fourteen years.

From that day on, my brother and I began to change from blood-related brothers raised differently into blood-related best friends raised differently but with similar tastes.
