# What Jehovah's Witnesses Are

This world was made by the god Jehovah, who had prophets write the Bible to hand the truth down to humankind.

God gave humans this world as a test, to decide whether to lead them to Paradise or cast them down into hell.

If you are a true believer in God, you can survive Armageddon and enjoy eternal life in the earthly Paradise.

To do that, you must keep firmly to the laws God laid down in the Bible.

To do that, you must interpret the Bible without error.

So believe the teachings of Jehovah's Witnesses — the only ones in this rotten world who interpret the Bible correctly, not misled by desire.

Every other interpretation of the Bible, every other religion, every other philosophy, was invented by the devil Satan to lead you astray.

So that you are not led astray, cut yourself off firmly from anything but the literature the Society publishes, and walk the road of truth with the right resolve.

Believe, and go to Paradise, or disbelieve, and be destroyed at Armageddon — it's one or the other. Don't get the choice wrong...

# My Mother's Choice

My mother believed the words of the true God and chose to go to Paradise.

To her mind it was the best of all the options available to her, and it was also a reassuring one: she'd get to keep walking the path of faith alongside her own older sister, whom she trusted with her whole heart.

From then on she would spend her entire life as a Jehovah's Witness, living as one who testified to the true God's works.

She worked at a factory, raised two children without receiving child support from their father, cut her living expenses to the bone to donate to the religious organization, and poured as much time as she could into her activities as a Jehovah's Witness.

There were stretches so poor, with no money left for food, that we lived off the ends of bread a bakery was throwing away.

Most of it was hard times, but she felt God's love close at hand, prayed every day, and thanks to the religion survived without her sense of self falling apart.

Many of the Jehovah's Witnesses around her were kind people too, and all of them shone with the joy of having learned the truth.

Without a shred of doubt, my mother pressed on down the path of Jehovah's Witnesses and led a happy life.

# Loving Discipline

Many of the adults who chose on their own to become Jehovah's Witnesses were happy people, but on the other hand, the children dragged along by their parents lived through days like hell.

A child's life was governed by whatever rules the parents believed in.

Loving discipline was recommended as one of the teachings grounded in the Bible.

Children were supposed to obey their parents, and a child who defied a parent was supposed to be corrected with the rod, or so it went.

At meetings, held three times a week, you had to sit still for over an hour, and if you acted up you'd be taken to the bathroom and hit on the backside with the rod.

It wasn't anything as gentle as a hand swat on the bottom — it was a homemade rod, a rubber belt of the kind used in car engines with a handle attached to make it easier to swing, and you'd be hit with it until you had welts.

Hearing a small child wailing from the bathroom during a meeting was an everyday occurrence.

# Necktie

For the children of Jehovah's Witnesses, worse than the rod was getting cold, disapproving looks from classmates.

Picture it: an elementary schooler in a suit and necktie, knocking on doors around the neighborhood to preach the Bible's teachings.

Among us, being seen by a classmate in a necktie was the ultimate humiliation, and we did everything we could to avoid it.

We'd take the tie off partway home, or wear a coat even when it wasn't cold, anything to cover it up.

To us, that was more embarrassing than getting caught taking a shit at school.

My classmates noticed something was off about my family, but I dodged it, lied, and played dumb to get through it.

# Brushing My Teeth

My mother, to put it kindly, was devout, or to put it unkindly, had a side to her that bordered on fanatical, and she believed whatever the religion told her without question.

Belief in the Paradise was one instance of that: since the end of the world was coming in the not-too-distant future, when every disease would be cured and we'd live forever, she saw no need to study at school or treat a cavity.

So I was never once told to study, and never once told to brush my teeth.

That's right — in elementary school nobody told me to brush my teeth, and my mother didn't brush them for me either, so months would go by without a single brushing, plaque piling up on my teeth until it got bad enough that scraping a fingernail across them would pack plaque under the nail.

The only time I brushed was the morning of the school dental checkup. Naturally, the results always came back riddled with cavities.

That was how things went until the upper grades of elementary school, when I started brushing my own teeth.

# Please Don't Judge

Reading this far you might be thinking, "that's awful" — but I'd rather you not make a one-sided judgment that it's simply bad.

Everything has a good side and a bad side; it's just that the good side worked in favor of my mother and the many other believers like her, while the bad side worked against their children.

I want you to understand that there were people for whom fleeing into a religion this extreme was the only way they had left to hold onto their sanity.

At the very least, I myself now feel nothing but gratitude for everything that happened, and hold no resentment or anger.
