## The Real Reason

The real reason for coming to Chaouen wasn't just to rest up from the road. The main purpose was to buy hashish (cannabis resin).

Chaouen was a source of Moroccan cannabis resin, commonly known as Moroccan hash, and the place was well known among travelers and hippies.

There had been villages like this in the Indian Himalayas too, and this one felt much the same: lots of travelers came here looking for cheap, good-quality cannabis.

The village had grown up alongside cannabis, and many of the villagers were involved in the cannabis business.

Not "business" in the sense of drug cartels smuggling it all over the world, though. More like local farmers, ordinary middle-aged guys, making a living selling cannabis to hippies.

There were probably plenty of drug cartels around too, but they never came to the tourist spots. My guess is they ran large-scale operations somewhere deep in the mountains.

## The Waiter

Once we were nearly done with our delicious meal, I asked the English-speaking waiter about hash.

In an ordinary town, walking up to a restaurant waiter out of nowhere and asking about cannabis would be completely deranged. Here in Chaouen, though, it was fine.

Sure enough, the waiter launched into the subject of hash as if he'd been waiting for it.

He'd probably been watching for the right moment to bring it up himself.

In Chaouen, a job as an English-speaking waiter came in very handy for a dealer.

He didn't grow anything himself; he seemed to work as a middleman.

He said he'd introduce us to a good place, and told us to come back tomorrow.

## The Trade

Buying drugs in a strange land through someone you've only just met looks far too risky, even foolish.

But for anyone traveling the hippie way, this sort of thing was an everyday affair, and for the village of Chaouen it was just part of daily life.

It had been going on without a break since the early 1960s. By treating travelers well and selling them good hash cheap, the people of Chaouen had built a trusted Chaouen brand.

Even for dealers, honest trade is what pays in the end.
