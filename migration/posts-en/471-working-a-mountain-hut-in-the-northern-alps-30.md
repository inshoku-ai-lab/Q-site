## Work at the Hut

The daily work went smoothly.

There was a lot to learn every day and nearly all of it was new, but J was good at teaching newcomers, so I settled into the job without much strain.

Within a month or so I could handle almost every job in the hut.

On weekdays, when there weren't many guests, I was the first one up and did all of it myself.

I got up at four in the morning, started the generator, cooked breakfast for the overnight guests, made box lunches for the climbers, and cooked breakfast for the staff.

Once the guests had set off, I folded the futons, cleaned, and got lunch ready.

I made lunch for the staff, served lunch to the climbers, and got ready for that night's guests.

I checked the guests in, then prepared dinner and served it.

I made dinner for the staff and cleaned up after the guests' dinner.

Last of all came the scheduled check-in with the company's other huts by satellite phone, and then I shut off the generator and the day was done.

The work was busy and the pay was low, but it was work worth doing.

## The Summer Part-Timers

In July, with summer at its height, the summer part-timers arrived, hired just for the busy season.

One was a fitness-obsessed college guy from Tokyo, and the other a cute college girl from Tochigi.

The fitness guy loved nothing more than moving his body, and back in Tokyo, apparently, he went running and practiced *bojutsu*, the martial art of the staff, on top of lifting weights.

Up in the mountains he handled the climbing with ease from day one, going far beyond anything you'd expect from someone new to the mountains.

He must have been a good student too, because he was sharp and picked up the work right away.

His ripped body and brisk, efficient way of working brought to mind the picture of the ideal man.

The girl seemed like a sweet, well-bred young lady from a good family. I had never met anyone who fit that picture so perfectly, so meeting her felt like an interesting encounter.

It wasn't just that she was cute to look at. What amused me was that even her name was exactly the kind a rich young lady in a manga would have.

I'll keep her real name out of this, but it was something like Reiko Shiratori — Shiratori means "swan."

Her good looks, her elegant name, and the good manners of a girl raised in a well-off family brought to mind the picture of the ideal woman.

The two summer part-timers, the guy and the girl both, had a first-class air about them, and they made me feel how much depth there was to the world of mountain hut work.

People of every imaginable kind come here from all over Japan.
