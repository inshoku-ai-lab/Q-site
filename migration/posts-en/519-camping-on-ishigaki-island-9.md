## A Friend from India

About a week into the new year, B showed up, a friend I'd met in Goa, India.

I'd only crossed paths with B very briefly and didn't really know him, but Y, who I'd hung out with in India, had met B later on his travels in Africa, and when B said he was heading to Okinawa too, Y put us in touch.

I'd been hoping to find work cutting sugarcane in Okinawa, and it turned out B had the same idea, so the point of teaming up was that we could help each other out.

## B's Personality

B ended up staying at the same campground, so we spent our days together out in nature.

With each passing day, more of B's larger-than-life personality came into view.

To sum it up: off-the-charts athletic, the nicest guy you could ever meet, and dumb on a level that shouldn't be possible.

If he were an animal, he'd be an adorable, dopey dog bursting with energy.

He was an unbelievable idiot, but his athletic ability was so extraordinary that his high school had made an exception for him, which was apparently how he got in and graduated at all.

His dumbness got in the way of everyday life, but he was such a genuinely good guy that everyone just shrugged and forgave him, apparently.

And when that dumbness did cause real trouble, he seemed to get through it on sheer physical ability.

Watching him reminded me all over again that happiness doesn't take brains. What matters is a healthy body and a bright smile.

## B's Cooking

When it comes to camp cooking, the classic is curry.

Anyone can make it taste decent, and it's almost impossible to screw up.

Even when it does go wrong, you can usually fix it, which is another reason it suits camp cooking.

B went into town, bought the ingredients for curry, and cooked it at the campground.

Until then he'd mostly eaten from what H and I cooked, so he apparently also wanted to make a big pot of curry to return the favor.

With that in mind, he put his whole heart into the curry.

But what came out of B as he tasted it wasn't a happy noise. It was a choking sound.

I was watching from a distance when B came over to me, half in tears.

No matter what he tried, it wouldn't taste good, he said, and he needed my help.

Wondering how anyone could make a curry that tasted bad, I tried a spoonful and was floored.

It was so bad it made me want to throw up.

A choking sound rose out of me all on its own.

## The Reveal

When I asked him about it, B said he'd once heard that adding milk makes curry taste better, so he'd dumped in an entire can of sweetened condensed milk.

Maybe he'd been picturing a drizzle of fresh cream over the finished curry, or a side of yogurt the Indian way.

Or maybe he'd been thinking of the apple-and-honey sweetness of Japanese boxed curry when he put in the condensed milk.

To fix a curry that had gotten far too sweet, he'd thrown in huge amounts of salt and spices, so what he ended up with was brutally sweet, and on top of that, hot and salty.

Even more horrifying, B had no idea how to control the heat of a campfire, so the bottom of the pot had burned, and the whole curry reeked of scorching. Nobody could have eaten it.

And for all that burning, it wasn't cooked at all. The potatoes and carrots were practically raw.

Scorched, sweet, hot, salty, raw potatoes.

That was the curry B had put his whole heart into.
