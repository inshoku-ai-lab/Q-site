## Flight

Our helicopter climbed steeply into the sky above the mountain, then pulled away from it in another near-somersault.

This time the somersault didn't seem necessary; apparently the pilot had thrown in a scare as a bonus, to welcome the new guy.

While we were tilted ninety degrees in the middle of those aerobatics, I could see the foot of the mountain through the space where a door should have been.

The helicopter had no doors, and without the seatbelt and the handholds, it would have been a straight drop to the bottom.

Honestly, it was insanely scary.

## The View

But the aerobatics were only at the start, and the helicopter soon settled into steady flight, gliding smoothly over the Northern Alps.

A view almost too spectacular to take in spread out before me.

The helicopters only flew on days with good visibility, so riding in one meant the same thing as getting a spectacular view.

Ranges of peaks around three thousand meters (10,000 feet) high stretched out beneath us.

Not in front of us. Beneath us.

And of course it wasn't just the mountains; the clouds were below us too.

The whole thrilling ride was over in barely ten minutes, and it was time to land.

A distance that takes eight hours on foot took ten minutes by helicopter.

## Landing

This time there was no one on the ground to guide the helicopter in.

But the pilot had done this many times, set the cargo down without any help, and brought the helicopter slowly down without any help.

We kept our heads low, jumped out, got clear to a safe spot, and watched it take off again.

The hut's manager gave the pilot a thumbs-up to say everything was OK.

The pilot saw the signal, flipped into another somersault, and headed back down the mountain.

The pilot was cool, and so was the manager.

I was thrilled by an experience unlike anything I'd ever had, and my heart leapt at the thought of all the unknown things I was going to experience over the next few months.
