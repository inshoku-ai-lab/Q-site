## A Sudden Turn

I hadn't been in London a week when everything turned around.

My computer had arrived from Japan, and I was about to start looking for a room and a job.

Then an email came from my mother: my grandfather had been rushed to the hospital.

He was her father, and I had lived with him until I left home.

He had fallen ill and been admitted, and word was he didn't have long.

## The Decision

I decided quickly.

I had only just arrived in London and was starting from a blank slate, so going back to Japan posed no problem at all.

If anything, I figured it would be easier than going back after building up a life here.

Besides, there was no telling when my grandfather might pass away.

The sooner I decided, the better.

My grandfather's condition was what I was thinking about, but my relationship with I-chan was on my mind too.

Having to rush home for my grandfather right after we'd broken up felt fated somehow, as if it had to mean something.

If my mother's message had come a week later, I might already have been renting a room in London, and my life could have gone somewhere completely different.

I suppose life is made up of one "what if" after another.

## Rushing Home

At that point I could still have kept the door open to coming back to London, but I chose to pull out of London for now.

I no longer remember why London dropped out of my plans for the future, but in the end I don't think I was really interested in living there.

Living so close to nature in Morocco had left me wanting, more than ever, to live in harmony with it.

A few days after my mother's email, I had a ticket to Japan.

I'd had no intention at all of going back to Japan, but once the ticket was booked, it made deep sense to me.

My last stay in Japan hadn't been easy, so I was a little nervous.

This one probably wouldn't be easy either, but a reason as compelling as my grandfather's emergency let me accept the nerves that came with going home.

Since I'd chosen not to come back to London, I decided to get rid of the bicycle and the few household things N had been keeping for me.

I couldn't sell the bike until right before I left the country. On my last day, I took it to a busy spot outside a station, taped a sign with the price on it, and just stood there. In about fifteen minutes, I had a buyer.

I was going home to Japan nearly broke and wanted to hold on to whatever cash I could, so leaving with even a little more money in my pocket at the very end was a real help.
