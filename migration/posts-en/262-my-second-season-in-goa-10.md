## Shopping

Y and I were both fixated on the culture of Goa, and right around then we made a big purchase.

A chillum.

For the people living inside the world of Goa, a chillum counted as part of looking presentable, and anyone who was a full-fledged member of that world had to own at least one.

Of course there was no actual rule that you had to, but that was the air that hung over my friends in Goa.

It was the same kind of thing as a middle schooler needing cool sneakers, or a high school girl needing a cute bag.

Among the travelers who believed in Goa and kept coming back to it, there was a culture they all shared.

Chillums had ranks, and even though this was Indian culture, the Indian chillum was looked down on.

The build really was crude, poorly suited to drawing out the flavor of charas, the hash resin.

The peak of the chillum world was said to be the Italian chillum.

That came down to Italy's ceramic tradition and a distinctive clay, which together could produce a chillum both sturdy and precise.

Pulling the finest smoke out of one took precise calculation, skill, and experience.

To hold that quality and that toughness, the story went, a finished chillum was tossed up above head height.

Only the pieces that survived the fall to the ground from that height were put up for sale — that was the level of obsession involved.

So for the chillum makers who turned out the high-quality work, demand ran ahead of supply, and the prices climbed on their own.

Y liked the top-of-the-line version of everything, so he bought a chillum by Alberman, the most famous craftsman of them all.

It ran about 20,000 yen — roughly a hundred and sixty dollars.

Measured against the cost of living in India that was an insane amount of money, but for life in Goa and for his pride it was an important item.

I liked things with a distinctive quirk to them better than the finest grade, so I bought one by a craftsman named Fabio — shaped like the muzzle of a gun, in a subdued gray.

The price was about 10,000 yen.

I had been living on very little, so this was a bold purchase, but it was the magic item I needed to live my life all the way through, and to do it faster.
