## The Book I Came Across

This is a story from back in 2000.

The internet had not developed as far as it has now, so when it came to information, there was nothing like today's freedom.

The main sources were books and magazines somebody had written, or word of mouth — information that reached you directly.

For someone traveling away from Japan, there were pretty much only two ways to get your hands on anything written in Japanese: go to an internet cafe and pay by the hour for access, or trade books with the travelers you met.

But London was a big city, and it turned the usual rules of travel upside down.

In central London there was a place called the Japan Centre, with Japanese food for sale in the basement and Japanese books on the floor above.

The food and the books both cost several times what they did in Japan, but demand was strong and it all sold steadily.

I'm a bookworm to the core — back in Japan I was at the library constantly, reading anything and everything — but living broke on a dishwashing job, I could not work up any desire to buy a book that cost three times the Japanese price.

## Takashi Tachibana

I had no intention of buying, but I went inside for a look around, and one book caught my eye.

It was a book by Takashi Tachibana called *Mastering the Brain*.

Until then I had not known who Tachibana was — the man people call "the giant of knowledge" — and academic books like this one had never interested me, but traveling in India and coming into contact with the inner world had left me curious about the brain, about consciousness, about the mind.

Being curious was one thing; on the road there was no getting at that kind of information right away, so the interest sat off to one side, on hold.

And that was when I came across this book.

A quick browse standing there showed it talking about the brain and about consciousness in ways I had never heard before, and I decided on the spot to buy it.

Three times the price or not, it made no difference — the book had my interest in a firm grip, and I headed home.
