## M's Decision

M had seen up close both sides of being broke: the trouble it made of R, and the strength it brought out in O. It must have given him something to think about.

One day, out of nowhere, he said he was thinking of going back to Germany.

His money had run out, but he planned to borrow the fare from a friend, go home, work, and pay it back.

Maybe O had shown him what freedom was, and then seeing R's reality had sobered him up.

He said he wanted to work, save up, and travel.

D and E had probably rubbed off on him too.

## Setting Off

Free as he was, M moved fast once his mind was made up.

He went into town and picked up the cash a friend had sent him.

A few days later, he was ready to go.

Following O's lead, he was going to hitchhike.

He shouldered his small backpack, which didn't have much in it, and stood by the road in the same clothes he always wore, worn out and grimy with desert sun, dust, and sweat.

All of us had come down to the roadside to celebrate his departure and give him a warm send-off.

Out here on Morocco's desert roads, cars hardly ever came by, but nearly every one that did would stop.

After about twenty minutes, luck was with him: a car came along and pulled over to pick him up.

## Goodbye

We gave each other big, heartfelt hugs, celebrated having met, and thanked one another for the special time we'd shared.

The positive change that had taken place inside him stood for what all of us had been through, and his joy and his growth lived inside us too.

Everyone was behind his choice: to go home, become part of society again and work, and then head off on his next journey.

The funny part is that his Moroccan visa had expired ages ago.

Of course, M couldn't have cared less.

He set off fully committed to his freedom, and that gave every one of us courage.
