## Time to Go Home

I had decided to squeeze every last bit of fun out of Goa until my money ran out, but I had never had much saved to begin with, so there was a limit to how far scrimping could take me, and by the second half of February I was going to have to head home.

It stung to be the only one leaving early while all my friends were still going, but once the money is gone, there is nothing you can do.

There was nowhere for me to borrow from, and I didn't want to borrow anyway — staying out of debt was the line I drew for myself in order to stay free.

Some people turned to running drugs or dealing them, but I had neither the nerve nor the capital for that.

The best road open to me was to accept things as they were, go home without a fuss, save up as fast as I could, and come back to India.

Even once the Goa season ended, there would be other parties going, up toward the Himalayas.

That would be fun in its own way.

## What Goa Taught Me

In those not quite four months in Goa, I went through something big enough to change my life from the ground up.

All of it was unknown to me, and all of it fit me perfectly.

Only a few months, but it feels like I took in forty years' worth of what ordinary life in society has to teach you, all at once.

I went through the deepest darkness of my life, and right after it, the greatest joy of my life.

LSD expanded my consciousness to many times what it had been, and looking back now, the person I used to be feels like someone else entirely.

I found a depth of friendship and a connection like family that I had never known before, and I learned to trust people past any weighing of profit and loss.

I smashed to pieces the shell of assumption that had wrapped me up — "this is the kind of person I am" — and understood that who you are as a human being is something you build for yourself.

The lucky part, lucky enough that you could almost call it fate, was that I came to see it at twenty.

Having grown up in a Jehovah's Witness household, I had less of the ordinary social conditioning in me to start with, and when I left the religion and quit high school I threw all of it away and was left empty.

Realizing, while I was empty like that, that I could consciously choose the direction I grew in — that was an enormous thing.

It amazed me that a person could grow this much as a human being in so short a time, and knowing that more experiences like it would carry me further still, the future ahead of me was full of hope.
