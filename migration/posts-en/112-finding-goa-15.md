# Love Triangle

The triangle between the couple, E and S, and C was getting more tangled by the day.

In truth it was less a love triangle than E falling for C of her own accord and S being jealous about it, but what worried us was the influence C — an eccentric of the first order — was having on her.

C, the couple, and the rest of us all lived in the same neighborhood, so we ran into each other anywhere and everywhere, and we made a point of keeping things as smooth and uneventful as we could.

That was the tangle we were in when we went to another party.

Last time it had been the weekly night at Shore Bar, a place right on the beach; this time it was Primrose, a bar inland.

This one, apparently, had sound going every day.

## Taxi

Primrose was about an hour away on foot, so the five of us split a taxi and rode over.

Taxi rides in India often mean getting overcharged or otherwise made miserable, and this one was no different.

Maybe because we were all Japanese and he had us pegged as easy marks, the driver tried to charge more than the price we had settled on before getting in.

We ignored him and pushed it back, saying he would get the agreed amount and nothing else.

At that he flipped the other way, apologizing, begging us not to mention it to the guys inside the bar.

Maybe he had tried to overcharge one of the hard-faced crowd in there once and paid for it.

The little scuffle with the driver aside, inside the bar things were nicely warmed up.

The fast, hard beat that belongs to Goa trance, with synths wobbling and undulating over the top of it, pulled the mood up.

## Group Session

We split the LSD we had into portions and everyone took theirs.

Up to the front of the speakers, bodies moving with the sound.

LSD was what interested me, and it was why I had come all the way to Goa; the music called Goa trance was not.

If anything, the techno world at large called Goa trance's overblown hype and lyrical melodies uncool, and dancing to it myself I felt the same — the exaggeration embarrassed me, and it did feel uncool.

But standing there in Goa itself, with LSD in me, dancing in front of the speakers, all that musical theorizing began to feel absurd.

Music you listen to through headphones living in a city, and music you dance to at full blast in front of a speaker, out in nature, with LSD in you — the two were not on the same plane at all.

Just as it was coming on gently and the dancing was starting to feel good, the sound stopped again.

This bar, it seemed, cut the sound at one in the morning.

Always something like this — but this is how you adapt to a place.

Unfortunately there was no chai stall at this bar, so when the sound stopped the bar was finished too, and it went dark and closed.

According to the taxi driver, there might still be somewhere playing at Baga Beach, further on past Anjuna.

The LSD had everyone up with no prospect of sleeping before morning, so we figured we might as well go take a look at Baga.

## Waves and Moonlight

We took the taxi out to Baga Beach and walked along the shore.

The full moon was coming, and the moonlight grew stronger by the day.

The foam along the edge of each wave threw the moonlight back hard and came flowing toward us.

The waves and the moonlight wove something between them — the ultimate beauty of nature, all of it in motion — and it left me stunned.

Were waves on the night of a full moon always this beautiful?!

Nature had been this beautiful all along, and I had spent my whole life in front of it without noticing!

Walking along the beach, staring blankly at the light coming off the water, I began to hear the sound of the waves as music.

To put it in technical terms, it sounded like a drum and bass beat with the low end cut and a flanger over it.

The light on the waves, already too beautiful, streamed past along with that easy beat.

What an extraordinary thing to be experiencing, I thought — and then it came to me.

Right. I was on LSD.

The hallucinations, in sight and in sound, were making it all look beautiful to an exaggerated degree.

But next to that sober piece of understanding, the spectacle of nature actually unfolding in front of me had an overwhelming presence, and I felt how much direct experience matters.

I learned that in an overwhelming experience there is a "something" that knowledge and reasoning cannot reach.
