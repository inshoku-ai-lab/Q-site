## Days in Vashisht

Life in Vashisht, the hot spring village in the Himalayas, could not have been going better.

Three friends I had been running around with in Goa were staying in the same guesthouse, and most of the other Japanese travelers there were close to us in one way or another — old friends, a friend's younger brother, that brother's best friend — which made it easy for trust and friendship to grow between us.

A restaurant a one-minute walk from the guesthouse became our hangout, and most of my time went to the guesthouse, the restaurant, or the hot spring: a comfortable life inside a very small radius.

Basically, I was doing nothing beyond taking it easy and enjoying myself.

Money earned and saved on a Japanese salary, spent on taking it easy deep in the mountains of India, where everything was dirt cheap.

Not what you would call productive, maybe, but it was a crucial experience.

Taking it easy in a village with a hot spring, among close friends, letting the friendships grow.

At twenty I had no sense of what taking it easy meant, and taking it easy struck me as ridiculous and lame.

I wanted every day packed with as much excitement as I could get, and kept pushing for more, but here in Vashisht, in that slow air, I was starting to understand how good it was to take it easy.

## Music

When I set out on my previous journey, I deliberately traveled without anything to play music on, then regretted it partway through and ended up buying speakers and cassette tapes on the road.

This time I did the exact opposite, and set out again with a complete set of music gear.

Back when I worked at the biggest Tsutaya in western Japan, I had gone through the tens of thousands of CDs in the store and picked out six hundred albums to copy onto MiniDisc.

Out of those I picked another hundred and forty-four, recorded them onto MiniDisc in mono, and ended up with seventy-two discs holding two hours of music each — a collection I had narrowed down and narrowed down again.

And I had Sony's newest battery-powered compact speakers, so I could listen to music properly anywhere, anytime.

Back then the internet was barely developed, a music collection was not an easy thing to put together, and nobody else had compact speakers at all, so my collection and my speakers were prized by my friends.

Looking out at the Himalayas in the distance, spending good days with friends, listening to all the music I loved for as long as I wanted — the height of luxury, I thought, extremely pleased with myself.
