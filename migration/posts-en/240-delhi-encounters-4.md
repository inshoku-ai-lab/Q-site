## The Good Stuff

We snorted top-grade heroin, crushed slowly and carefully into a fine powder.

It was the first time for both U and me, with no experience to go on, but we could tell right away that what we had just taken was good quality.

The noise-packed air of Delhi fell silent all at once, and my mind turned clear as water with not a breath of wind on it.

Calling it pleasant was not quite the right way to put it, but a certain sense of release from the everyday world did carry pleasure with it.

We relaxed in the room and talked about all kinds of things.

But the talk going back and forth between J and D, two seasoned travelers, was so overwhelmingly interesting that we rarely found an opening to cut in.

The gap in experience was too wide for a real conversation.

A story about buying a used bus cheap in Europe, driving it all the way to Central Africa, and selling it there for several times the price to keep the trip going.

A story about a secret drug-smuggling route through Nepal and Thailand, safe and cheap.

A story about a legendary marijuana called Malawi Gold that grows around Lake Malawi in Africa.

The talk was packed with insider stories like that, and one thrilling story after another kept us amazed.

## Goodbye to J

The day was coming up when we would fly to Thailand to renew our India visas.

Meeting J had been a huge jolt for both of us.

We had both woken up to the travelers' world in Goa, and we were full of curiosity about what lay on the other side of LSD and psytrance parties.

Looking at J, decades ahead of us in that world, I felt hope, and reassurance, and something like a sense of justice.

J, D, the two of us — nearly everyone in that guesthouse was breaking laws left and right, and still there was something in the place that felt like justice.

Looking back now, I suspect it felt like I was doing the right thing because that world was full of love.

Built on love and friendship and trust — an antisocial community of travelers.

Scattered all over the world, every piece of it connected to the rest.

J was one of that community's elders.
