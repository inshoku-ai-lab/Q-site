## The Rhythm of the Day

Desert life was simple to the core, and because of that, a daily rhythm took shape on its own.

First, when morning came and the sun began to rise, it got hot in no time.

A tent in direct sunlight, especially, turned as hot as a sauna.

This was what we called summer.

When summer came, I crawled out of the tent and threw off every piece of clothing I had bundled up in.

I was wearing twelve T-shirts, one on top of the other, so I pulled them all off in one go.

I had several pairs of pants layered on as well, so off they all came.

## The Water Jar

Our primitive, simple way of life always needed some kind of work done.

One of the most important jobs was fetching water.

I volunteered to take charge of it.

Every morning I carried the water jar to the spring, scooped up water with a small pot, and poured it into the jar.

What I want you to notice here is that this was no plastic bottle or canister.

What I hauled water in was a real water jar, made of kneaded clay and fired in a kiln.

The same jar you see drawn in pictures of Aquarius, the water bearer.

## The Doctor of Ecology

Why a water jar and not a plastic bottle? Gandalf's convictions had a great deal to do with that.

Gandalf had already been devoted to the idea of ecology for more than twenty years, and had spent most of his life working to protect the planet's environment.

The festival had been one part of that work.

To Gandalf, plastic stood for consumer society itself: burn it, bury it, or let it wash out to sea, and whatever you did, it fouled the environment — something close to the root of all evil.

On his word alone, we were trying to live without plastic.
