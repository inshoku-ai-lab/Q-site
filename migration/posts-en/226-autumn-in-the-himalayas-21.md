## Tosh Cream

He showed us his collection of creams, gathered from villages all over.

Each village had been making hash its own traditional way for as long as anyone could remember, and each one seemed to have a character of its own.

What he treated us to was one he had kneaded himself, in a village called Tosh.

It was rare, top-grade stuff, but he shared it out without a trace of reluctance.

For his part, he probably wanted to show off his prize piece.

As preparation for smoking the chillum, he dampened a scrap of cotton cloth.

There seemed to be a proper way to wring it out as well, and he did it with complete assurance.

Then he toasted a coarse tobacco with no chemicals in it — a brand called Panama — to drive off the nicotine and make it easier to smoke, and mixed it with the cream.

He drew circles around the mixture with the chillum, murmuring what sounded like an incantation.

When the chant was over he packed the mixture into the chillum and smoked it through the damp cloth held at his lips, and there was a proper form for that too: both hands wrapped neatly around the pipe so that lips and chillum never touched.

Putting the fire to the chillum was an important part of the form as well, and it had to be matches, not a lighter.

He was thorough about it, too — he struck the match, then scraped the chemical head off the tip, and only then brought the flame to the chillum.

## Bom

It took some doing, but he finally got it lit, and at that moment he shouted at the top of his voice: "Bom!!!"

In Japanese the word is written with the kanji *bon*, and it seems to mean something on the order of praise offered to Shiva.

We said "Bom" ourselves back in Vashisht when we passed a joint along, but his "Bom!!!" was in another league from ours, in volume and in force, and the sheer power of it blew me back.

It echoed off the valley and came back at us, loud enough to make me wonder whether I had ever in my life made a sound like that.

My own sense of it was that cannabis culture in India had been rooted there since ancient times and was accepted, but that it was still illegal, so you were better off smoking with at least a little discretion.

But as this village saw it, and as he saw it, smoking cannabis in a chillum was a sacred act of worship to Shiva, and shouting as loud as you possibly could was the right and proper thing.
