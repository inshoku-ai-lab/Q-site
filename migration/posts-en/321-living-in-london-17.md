## Carnival

The carnival had already started the moment we stepped out the front door.

There was something wonderful about watching people dance to music along the street we walked every day.

The dancers seemed to feel the same, every last one of them grinning from ear to ear.

A thoroughly un-English sight.

The English, by rights, ought to be under a cloudy sky with their brows furrowed, but on this one day they were under a blue one instead, swaying side to side to a cheerful reggae rhythm, thoroughly at ease.

Everyone seemed to be enjoying the gap between this and ordinary life.

## Another Street Corner

We kept walking, dancing as we went.

This was a carnival, not a festival, so the correct move was not to dance in one spot but to dance your way along.

Walking toward the neighborhood supermarket we always used, we came on yet another row of giant speakers, people dancing away in front of them.

Then we turned toward the station, and people were dancing there too — speakers really were lined up everywhere you looked.

And even so, we were only walking the streets right around our house.

At this rate, there had to be something like a thousand of these speaker setups across the whole town.

From what I heard, a million people were expected to take part in the carnival.

An event on an outrageous scale.

## The Center of Town

We headed for the big park in the center of town, where one of the major television stations had set up a stage and famous artists were apparently going to perform live.

All of it was free, but the number of people let inside the fence was capped, and it was already so far past full that there was no getting in, so we decided to watch from outside the fence.

People like us who had spilled out past the fence ringed the entire stage.

Every way you turned: people, people, people.

I had never in my life seen this many people packed together across that much ground.
