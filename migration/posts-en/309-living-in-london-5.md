## My Best Friend Y

About a month after we set up the new place in London, my best friend Y came out to see us.

It wasn't a carefully planned visit — he had come to Amsterdam to have a good time, and while he was at it he simply turned up, out of the blue.

Word came only about a week ahead of time, and it was a happy surprise.

But our new life had only just begun, and we were still running around in circles over this new life in London.

My nights were taken up with work, so we didn't get much time together.

In the end we went nowhere, could not put on much of a welcome, and after a few days he went home.

He said he had come to see what kind of life we were living, but as far as we were concerned that life had not started yet, and honestly, we would rather he had come once things had settled down.

## T

A while after Y left, I heard from T, a guy I had met in Vashisht before coming to London.

The message said the flow of his travels had changed and he had suddenly wound up living in London, so we should get together if the chance came.

T was half Swedish and half Japanese, born and raised in Japan.

He wore his hair in dreadlocks, dressed sharp, and put out an "I'm the cool one" aura at full blast.

I liked running with people who cut loose in a big way, but the strength of his ego seemed to snag at I-chan, and she hung back a little from the start.

Having a friend from the India days turn up made me happy, and since we were close in age, I was glad to go over to the house where he was living.

## Squat Houses

What he meant by the flow of his travels changing was that he had met a traveler living in a squat house in London.

A squat house — also called an occupation house, a building taken over without legal title — is a style of living where you occupy a derelict government-owned building or something like it, legally or semi-legally, and stay there for free.

Lately there are apparently some in Japan as well, but in Europe it has been part of the culture for a long time, and any number of artists have come out of squat house culture.

The rent, after all, costs nothing, so there is no grinding away at a job to cover it, and you can throw that much more of yourself into making art — which creates the kind of soil where succeeding as an artist comes easily.

Combine that with dumpster diving, a culture of getting food for free, and you have the lifestyle and culture known as freegan.
