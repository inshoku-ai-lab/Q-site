## Oita

The ferry brought me to Oita Prefecture.

Oita had never come up once in my life, and I'd never met anyone from there, so I had no connection to the place at all; it was completely unknown territory.

It was my first time in Kyushu, too, so it felt like I'd landed on a new continent, and I was full of anticipation.

After how rural Shikoku had been, I was surprised by how built-up Oita was.

Knowing nothing about either, I'd figured Shikoku and Kyushu were both just islands of about the same size and hadn't expected them to be all that different, but the cities were at completely different levels of development.

The ferry got in during the afternoon, still too early to camp, so for the moment my plan was to get out of town and camp somewhere with more nature around.

The whole point of this trip was to keep heading south, so from the ferry terminal I followed the national highway south.

There were wooded hills with nobody around not far from town, which looked like a good spot to camp.

## The Ramen Shop

The sun was starting to set, so I decided to stop at a ramen shop.

Big letters on the sign advertised champon, a Nagasaki-style noodle soup.

It was about as Kyushu as a sign gets, and it reeled me right in.

I don't really drink as a rule, but this time I was in a champon kind of mood, so I ordered a small bottle of beer to go with it.

Right around when the beer reached the table, I realized something important.

I had only a little money in my wallet, not enough to cover the beer.

I hurried to tell the manager and asked if I could cancel the beer, but he said it was too late, since he'd already opened the bottle.

The champon was already made too, he said, so that couldn't be canceled either.

On top of that, he seemed to suspect I was a traveler pretending to be broke so I could dine and dash.

In Shikoku, just passing through as a traveler had been enough to get me treated well, no questions asked, so this reception made me angry.

But walking into a restaurant without the money was my own fault, so I had no grounds to complain.

There was nothing else to do, so I decided to eat fast and go take out cash from a bank ATM.

The champon was probably delicious, but I was too angry at the manager's rigidity and his snide attitude to notice, and all I could taste was the awkwardness.
