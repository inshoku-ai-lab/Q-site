## Waterfall, Lake, Full Moon

We sat on a slightly raised shelf of rock above the waterfall, watching the sunset.

As the sun went down, the full moon rose from the horizon.

In the desert, there are no ceilings overhead and no high-rise buildings.

No mountains, no forests, not a single cloud, and when the moon rises, the whole of it appears right in front of you.

It was still light out, so the full moon was only faintly visible.

Even so, freshly risen and still close to the horizon, it looked strikingly large.

Little by little the light faded, signaling the start of the full moon celebration.

## The Universe

At the last full moon, we'd gathered around a fire while people played instruments and our friends sang and danced.

This time there was no firewood for a fire, no musicians to play, and no friends to sing and dance.

Just raw nature and the three of us.

It was getting dark and cold, but our preparations for winter were all done.

We lay down right on the bare ground and looked up at the sky.

Lie down and look up anywhere but the desert, and part of the sky is hidden by mountains or buildings. In the desert, everything in front of your body is sky.

Sky in front, ground behind. That's all there is.

The simplicity felt better than I could have imagined.

## The Self

With things that simple, there was nothing extra in the way, and what surfaced was my own inner world, just as it was.

I was going back over my memories of everything since I'd come to Morocco.

The chain of events that led to the festival, and the life at the oasis that grew out of it.

There had been all kinds of encounters, all kinds of experiences.

It was hard to believe that only a few months earlier I'd been living in the heart of London.

Until about a month and a half before, Y had been living in the very heart of Tokyo.

Now anything urban had fallen away from us completely, and we had settled in with the desert sand, the oasis water, and the moon floating in the sky.

We hadn't showered in a long time, either.

In the desert everything is dry, so if sand gets on you, you just brush it off, and if you sweat, your skin is dry and smooth again in no time.

We might smell of sand, but there was no body odor and nothing uncomfortable about it, so nobody gave showers a second thought.
