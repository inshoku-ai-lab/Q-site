## The Bus Ride

We were nervous about our first job as couriers, but our minds were already made up.

And without that much resolve, the way I saw it, you could never get everything there was out of the psytrance traveler's world, where your life is on the line.

Because at a psytrance party you get wrecked on LSD and play, and if even the smallest trace of fear is left in you, the acid amplifies it many times over into a bad trip, and there is no enjoying anything after that.

The joy of a psytrance party was something only the strong could reach, only those who could conquer their own fear.

Before we left, we got rid of every last piece of cannabis paraphernalia and made a point of boarding the bus completely clean.

We checked each other over and over, confirming that nothing had been missed.

We made it perfect to the level where, if we still got caught after all that, we could call it fate and let it go.

That was the duty of people who take risks to gain their freedom.

## The Checkpoint

The bus pulled out and started down the mountain toward Delhi.

One night of driving, and we would be in Delhi by morning.

A few hours after departure, we reached a checkpoint.

It sat a little way down the road from the Parvati Valley, the biggest holy land of cannabis in India.

The police, of course, knew exactly how cannabis worked in the Parvati Valley, and they were well aware that the travelers had a business in it.

The Parvati Valley is a Hindu holy site, so cannabis there was effectively left completely alone, but there was also a real determination that none of it was getting out past here.

At a checkpoint, the first ones targeted were foreign travelers.

To an Indian, a foreign traveler was basically a duck that walked in carrying the onions to cook it with, a bottomless cash box you could squeeze forever.

They carried rare machines you could not get in India, portable music players and the like, and they had valuables tucked away on them.

Find one weakness, and you could take everything they owned and get their cash on top of it.

That was exactly what the police wanted, and it was the privilege of an officer posted at a checkpoint.

Looking back now, I suspect that privileged post was itself something traded among the police, bought and paid for in bribes.
