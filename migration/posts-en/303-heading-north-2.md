## To the Himalayas

We decided to take the next night's bus up to Manali, a town in the Himalayas.

I wanted out of the overheated south of India as fast as possible.

In Goa the temperature had been running from forty to nearly forty-five degrees Celsius (104 to 113 Fahrenheit), and Delhi was much the same, except that with no nature anywhere close at hand, the heat got into you more.

We climbed onto the bus and celebrated getting out of the heat.

## Manali

A night on the bus, and we arrived in Manali, the Himalayan town.

The mountain air in the morning was cool, and my head began to clear.

As soon as we were in town we switched to an auto-rickshaw and headed up the valley to the village of Vashisht.

I had spent about four months in this village the year before, and it was full of good memories.

For now I wanted to enjoy the mountain life and soak in the hot spring until the tiredness of traveling in India came out of me.

In two weeks I was due to leave for England.

This would be the last time I could sink into the way time moves in India.

## Vashisht

Staying at the guesthouse from before crossed my mind, but in Goa a quiet house in the woods, out in nature, had suited me better than a guesthouse where travelers gathered, so in Vashisht too I picked somewhere quiet, a little away from the center of the village.

It was one of the old wooden buildings peculiar to the Himalayan highlands, the ceiling absurdly low, but the balcony was wide and it looked out on a magnificent view, and that was what won me over.

Straight ahead stood the mountain across from us, and below it the ranges running on down the valley.

A view like that in front of you for about 300 yen a night — around two and a half dollars — is something you get only in the mountains of India.

It was I-chan's first time in Vashisht, so I gave her the full tour of what the place had going for it.

Not that there was much to show: a small village with nothing to it but the hot spring, so the tour came down to a soak in the spring and a meal at a good restaurant.

We were only staying about ten days this time, so there was no relaxing all the way down into the bone the way I had before.

As long as that limited time sits in a corner of your head, the thought that you have to be enjoying yourself keeps echoing through it.

## The Waterfall

Sometime during the stay we went out to the giant waterfall, which to my mind is the one sight in Vashisht worth going to see.

Two hours of walking up the mountain trail, back and back into the hills, and the falls were, as always, the spectacle of all spectacles.

It has probably held that view for tens of thousands of years.

A triple rainbow hangs over the giant waterfall bursting out of the rock face.

It is a miracle of a waterfall: anyone who sees it is moved whether they like it or not, and comes away feeling as good as a person can feel.

Soaking in the hot spring and getting out to the falls — that alone felt like enough to have done what I came to Vashisht for.

Now I could leave India with no regrets.

In a few days I would be heading for London.

A whole new life would be starting, and it would be a good while before I came back to India.

I had decided to stay away for a while because India was too much fun; if I kept coming back only here, I would never get to see any other part of the world.

What I wanted was to experience not just India but all kinds of worlds.

And for that, I had to break out of a deep addiction to India.
