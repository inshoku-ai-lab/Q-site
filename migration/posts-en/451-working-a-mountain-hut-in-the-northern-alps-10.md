## The President

If anyone embodied the strong personality of these mountain people, it was the president, who was also the man at the head of the hut.

In an earlier post, I wrote about how this third-generation president had become a devotee of "the god of management" and was forcing an extreme management philosophy on his employees.

For the employees, that philosophy was hard to bear, but it was also having good effects in all sorts of directions.

By believing in it completely, the president had cut through his own doubts and was charging ahead in his chosen direction without hesitation.

## King of the Hill

He was effectively the president of an independent company, and that company's headquarters sat on the summit of a mountain, so he was living out "king of the hill" in the most literal sense.

Inside this hut, the president was the king, the head of state, and the colonel of a dictatorship, all at once.

The employees didn't necessarily like him, but he seemed to have deliberately chosen to play the bad guy.

That was also the way of the god of management he worshipped, and probably unavoidable for running the company.

His resolve to carry on as the one everybody disliked made his personality stand out even more.

That resolve gave him the thick skin not to care what anyone thought of him, and time and again, to anyone watching from the outside, he came across as seriously unhinged.

## Strange Behavior

Here I'd like to share one story that has become a legend among the longtime employees.

This mountain stands nearly 3,000 meters (9,800 feet) high, and it isn't one you climb on a whim.

But if climbers don't come, a mountain hut is out of business.

To solve that problem, the president put money and effort into improving the trails.

As a shrewd business strategy, the trail work was a resounding success.

Combined with advertising, promotion, and all sorts of other strategies, it made the mountain known as one even beginners could climb easily.

But easier trails don't make the mountain any lower, and they don't make your pack any lighter.

For older climbers new to hiking especially, it was a real challenge.
