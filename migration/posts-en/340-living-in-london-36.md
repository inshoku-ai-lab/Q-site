## Setting a Finish Line

Once we had settled on going to Morocco, life began to shine even brighter.

When you think a thing will be there forever you handle it carelessly, but once you know it is ending you suddenly hate to lose it.

The ordinary London days I had taken for granted began to feel precious once I thought about how few were left.

The usual weekend flea market, too, was now so unmistakably London that it became part of a special tour of the city.

Even my everyday bicycle route to work suddenly took on the air of travel — that was what it had come to.

## The Thai Restaurant

Just as we hated to lose our London days, the people at work hated to lose me.

Oneesan especially, the Thai chef who was my direct boss, seemed to see me and the son she had left behind in Thailand as one and the same, and grieved over my leaving in a big, theatrical way.

She had always given me special treatment, and to help us out — a broke traveling couple — she would pass along leftover ingredients and the like.

## Green Curry

Green curry, the most popular dish in Thai cooking, was cooked in one enormous batch at the start of the day, which was how the kitchen stayed on top of the orders when the place filled up.

But there is no telling how many people will walk into a restaurant — some days a crowd arrives all at once, other days not a soul comes in.

So there were times when a huge amount was left over, and nearly all of it went home with me.

Thai green curry, as anyone who has eaten it will know, has a peculiar flavor all its own, and to a palate used to ordinary Indian curry it is a little hard to warm to.

In the beginning neither I-chan nor I cared much for green curry, but it came to us as a gift so often that little by little it was taking over the freezer at home.

But that changed too — with time our tongues grew used to Thai flavors, and by the time we left London it had turned, quite naturally, into a dish we loved.

There is something quite pleasant about the process of coming to like what you originally had no taste for, and it remains a good memory to this day.

Twenty years or so on from that day, green curry is still a favorite of mine, and the feelings of that time still gladden my heart.
