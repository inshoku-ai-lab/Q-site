## The Truck

She and her daughters lived on the road, driving a huge black ten-ton truck all over Europe and Africa.

The truck was their home, and it was also equipped to put on festivals.

It had a huge generator and speakers, and enough gear to run a restaurant during a festival.

In summer, apparently, they made their money at festivals in Europe, selling food and throwing after-parties once the big festivals were over.

And this was no small truck; it was a full-size ten-ton truck.

It was insanely big, and it was painted black from end to end.

The sheer menace of it was something else; it had the presence of the king of the road.

The cab sat higher than an ordinary car's, and from up there, Janis Joplin, a big star ornament in her dreadlocks, looked down from on high.

Wouldn't your average border guard be shaking at the sight?

## The Party

We had planned to take LSD today, the second day of the festival.

Take it every day and you build up a tolerance until it stops working, but take it only once and it isn't enough.

The plan was to take it on day two, rest on day three, and get as high as we possibly could on the fourth and final day.

Best of all, we had some superb Swiss-made LSD we'd gotten from D.

With that, we were guaranteed the time of our lives.

## The Dance Floor

Crowds of people were dancing in front of the speakers, big smiles shining in the sunlight.

I joined in the dancing too, but this is where a bad habit of mine showed up.

I compare things to what I've experienced before.

I still hadn't broken free of my memories of the year before last, when I'd gone to Goa for the first time and enjoyed its trance parties.

At club parties in London, I'd accepted that that was just how they were, so I could enjoy them, but a turn-of-the-century party in the Sahara in Morocco sent my expectations soaring, and up came the trap the brain sets for itself: comparison.

I was definitely having fun, and the DJs and the crowd were great, but next to the parties in Goa in 1999, it just didn't measure up.

I had turned into the kind of wishy-washy guy who can't get over his ex.
