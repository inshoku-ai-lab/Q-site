## Chaos at the Festival

As the opening day drew closer, the confusion on the organizing team kept growing.

Some things were moving along fine; others had stalled.

Normally a big festival is built by a seasoned crew backing one another up, but this was the first electronic music festival in Moroccan history.

And not just any electronic music festival, either: a psytrance festival, a genre that feels like seeing light in the middle of chaos.

Leading this chaos-filled festival was a rich Moroccan man who had never organized a festival before.

He had come across psytrance festivals while traveling in Europe, decided he wanted to do the same thing in his own country, and apparently, with help from all sorts of people, had managed to get it off the ground.

For better or worse, he was the son of a very wealthy family, so the money for the festival was covered. But for someone who had never organized a festival, pulling off a big one right out of the gate was no easy job.

The whole thing had been a stretch from the start, and as one thing piled on another, the chaos kept picking up speed.

## Resentment

We had finished the Japanese garden we were in charge of and stepped away from the organizing team.

We had our tickets, a place to live, and a kitchen, so we weren't planning to help out any more than we'd enjoy.

We found out later that some of the staff thought we were volunteers without tickets. The way they saw it, we were supposed to be working a lot harder, and we were only drifting around having fun because Gandalf had taken a liking to us. Some of them resented it.

The same kind of resentment was apparently breaking out all over, and all sorts of rumors reached our ears.

## Escape

With less than a week left before the opening, one of the festival staff we had become friends with showed up at our hotel room.

Something had apparently happened that was infuriating beyond words. "I've done more than enough," our friend said. "I'm leaving."

"I'm going to forget the whole thing, go kitesurfing by the sea, and ring in the new century there." And with that, they breezed out the door.

To come all the way from Europe for this New Year's, help put it together, and then choose to walk away: something serious must have happened.
