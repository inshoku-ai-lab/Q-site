## Life in the Little Room

Life with I-chan was going well, on the whole.

Our relationship had turned around completely from what it had been in Goa — smooth now, and full of love and harmony.

In Goa there had been no room in me for it — I was so desperate to cut loose that I could not accept having a girlfriend around at all — but the settled days in London brought me around to being grateful for the joy of spending my time with the person I loved.

## Living Poor

For my part, a badly paid dishwashing job at the Thai restaurant, four hours a week.

I-chan was drawing down the savings from her office lady days, so the two of us were living pretty poor.

Being poor in England still meant a life far richer in material terms than the average traveler had in India, and since we put more weight on spiritual richness than material richness, it was not something that bothered us.

If anything, I took pleasure in it: here I was living abroad, still in the frame of mind of a traveler, and instead of spending money I was earning it.

Young and with no life experience at all, what I wanted above all else was experience that was new and exciting, so simply being able to go on traveling like this was more than enough.

## The Supermarket

A while into living in England, I came to see that the country held every kind of social class, and that the people at the very bottom of the pay scale had their own dirt-cheap supermarkets.

Back then, eating good food and staying healthy mattered less to us than how cheaply we could satisfy our tongues without spending, so the dirt-cheap supermarkets were a happy surprise.

The prices were astonishing: at our favorite, a gigantic dirt-cheap supermarket, two liters (about two quarts) of vanilla-flavored ice cream sold for around a hundred yen — eighty cents or so.

It was not vanilla ice cream but vanilla-flavored ice cream, with only the barest trace of milk in the ingredients, but we swarmed happily around ice cream that tasted good and came cheap.

There were potato chips at prices that made no sense, too, and for us — young, brimming with energy whether or not we gave health a thought — they were a welcome option.

Looking back on it now, it really was not what you would call a healthy diet, but I never caught so much as a cold, and health never once crossed my mind.

All my focus went to one thing: how to keep the days exciting and funny.
