## The Abyss

The party at Disco Valley ran wild until past noon, and ended in a storm of applause and whistling, with everyone sorry to see it stop.

Apart from walking back to the chai stall for water, I danced without a break all the way to the end.

The music had stopped, but my ears were so used to the volume that they were still ringing.

On the way back to the chai stall I ran into a friend and we talked.

He had apparently been through the dark abyss at this party, and he reported it to me dead serious, his face worn down to nothing: "I've done twenty-five years' worth of traveling."

Another friend had taken cold medicine in the evening, and it reacted strangely with the LSD he took at the party — his body locked up, and he spent a frightening few hours like that. It had been a fierce party, and it seems a few people had bad trips.

I had taken out insurance against a bad trip by biting off a tiny piece of an ecstasy tablet.

The dark abyss I had been through two weeks earlier was deep and terrifying, and if I could help it I never wanted to go through it again.

## A Spectacular View

Still carrying the good feeling of being as high as a person can get, I left Disco Valley.

I got on my motorcycle and rode with friends to a juice bar near our house.

It stood on top of a cliff that rose straight out of the shore, a café with a spectacular view of the horizon from that height.

We sat down and rolled a joint.

Cannabis is illegal in Goa too, of course, and if the police find you they take a bribe.

But the people running businesses in Anjuna are relaxed about all of it, and if the police come near they let you know early.

Feeling the pleasant heat in my body from hours of runner's high, smoking the joint while looking down at the horizon from up there, pouring a fresh fruit shake down a dry throat — it was the best feeling in the world.

The salt wind blowing in off the sea felt good.

It stroked the scalp I had shaved only two weeks before.

The LSD had made my senses finer, and I could feel every last part of that touch as the wind moved over my scalp.

That good feeling from the salt wind traveled over my whole body, and I felt an eye-opening bliss and an easy sense of being one with it all.

## Appetite

When the LSD has you, your stomach clenches tight and your appetite disappears.

Eat a big meal and you can turn nauseous, and the high gets muddy too, so most of the time I ate nothing for several hours before taking LSD.

Just picturing food could make me feel a little sick.

But after the joint and the fruit shake my appetite started to come back, so we set off for the restaurant we always went to.

We got back on the motorcycles and rode over there in a pack.

## Sublimation

I was still pretty far gone on the LSD, so I let a friend drive and rode on the back.

Passing under a big tree, I looked up: sunlight came down through the leaves, shining with an exquisite beauty.

And because I was on a moving motorcycle, the streaks of light slid smoothly past.

The LSD amplified that beauty many times over and drove it into my senses.

It was so beautiful that it shook me, and I felt, quietly and all the way through, what an incredibly happy time I was living through right then.

Feeling that happiness all the way to the bottom, I felt the trauma of my childhood suffering, still sitting in my body and my mind, being laid to rest and sublimated away.

Next to happiness on that level, the trauma I had been through counted for nothing at all.
