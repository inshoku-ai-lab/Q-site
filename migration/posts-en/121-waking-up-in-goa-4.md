## The Next Stage of Worry

Once N confirmed for me that the ecstasy tablet I had taken had been wrapped in plastic wrap, my worry mode moved into its next stage.

Maybe I had swallowed the tablet without taking the wrap off.

Or maybe I had taken it off properly.

I had no memory of removing it, but the moment I decided I hadn't, it started to feel like I had.

Confusion bred confusion, and that confusion bred more.

If I had taken the wrap off, I wouldn't be this confused by now — though I couldn't say the confusion wasn't starting to settle a little.

I had taken ecstasy once before, in Thailand, but I had no idea how strong this stuff was.

Maybe I actually had taken the wrap off, and it was already coming on.

Think it and it seemed true; think the opposite and that seemed true too.

I just drifted on and on inside an infinite loop of thought.

At some point the sky started to pale, and morning was coming.

## The Next Worry

I could see everyone dancing to the blasting music, smiling.

But to my eyes, every single one of them was gritting through something painful, forcing themselves to keep dancing.

Frazzled from all the worrying, I sat a little way off and did not move.

I wanted to talk to my friends about what was eating me, but I was too far gone on LSD to hold a real conversation.

While I was tossed around in that sea of thought, morning came, then midday, and the party ended.

In the end, apart from dancing hard at the start of the party, I spent the whole night worrying.

The party over, my friends gathered around, saying things like "That was so much fun."

Locked inside my delusion, I decided that even those words were forced.

They said they were heading to a restaurant for smoothies.

I was in no state for that, and the LSD was still strong enough that putting anything in my stomach would have made me throw up, so I turned them down and went back to my room to rest.

Lying on the bed in that narrow room, I tried to settle my spinning head.

Nothing bad had actually happened to me apart from not being able to dance, but because I was convinced that every last one of them was having a bad trip, I felt like I had looked into the abyss of human darkness.

I learned how deep worry goes, how deep self-consciousness goes, how deep the senses go.

The overdose of LSD had fired my brain up several times over, and it felt like my brain had come off its hinges.

My awareness had deepened; looking at the same thing was no longer the same, and feeling the same emotion was no longer the same.

For better or worse, I understood that I had become something different from who I had been, and that I could never go back to where I had been.

I had been through too deep a darkness to go back to my old self.

For better or worse, I understood that today I had been reborn, and that from here I would be living a new life.

## The Head-Shaving Ceremony

Right then, out of nowhere, an urge came over me to cut off all my hair.

Back in Japan I had always kept my head shaved punk-style, and I had kept the same haircut since hitting the road — but this time it wasn't a punk shaved head, it was a shaved head that stood for being reborn.

In other words, I was cutting it off with the resolve of a man taking monastic vows.

I went straight out, got on a bus, and headed for the nearby town of Mapusa.

At a barbershop, I asked them to shave my head.

The barber was startled and asked if I was serious, but my mind was made up.

Thirty minutes later every hair was gone and I was completely reborn.

I wrapped a towel around my head so the sun wouldn't scorch my scalp and headed home to Anjuna Beach, where I lived.

## Getting It Wrong

On the way home I looked into the restaurant we always went to, and there were my friends, hanging around.

I sat down at their table and showed them my shaved head.

During the party I had been too high on LSD to hold a real conversation, but the effects had worn off enough by now that I could talk.

I told them what had been going on in my head during the party — that I was sure every one of them was having a bad trip.

The whole table cracked up.

I was the only one sitting there, blank-faced.

Nobody, they said, had been having a bad trip.

Still unable to stop believing my own delusion, I asked them over and over whether that was really true.

It seemed I really had manufactured the whole bad trip out of my own conviction.

What a day. I thought I had looked into the human abyss, and the whole thing was a bad trip built out of a delusion.

Even so, the feeling that I had seen into the abyss of human darkness and the feeling that I had been reborn were both real.

Let me close this LSD awakening story with a parting shot from Y, delivered like a line out of Fist of the North Star, the manga.

"You will never again dance the way you once did."
