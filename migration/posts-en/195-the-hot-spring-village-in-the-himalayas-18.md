## The Hot Springs

We all shared auto-rickshaws back to Vashisht.

We were a pack of close friends with our energy running high, and that made every last thing fun.

It felt like a school trip that never ended.

The fun didn't stop once we reached the village either — we dropped our bags in our rooms and headed straight for the hot springs.

We washed off the sweat and the mud and sank into the scalding water.

The LSD had left my skin hypersensitive, and the fierce heat came at it like an attack.

It felt that much hotter, but past a certain line it crossed over and became something close to pleasure.

One pleasure after another with no gap between them, and every face was shining.

Here too, we never stopped carrying on.

## Music

Clean and refreshed after the soak, I finally made it back to my room and settled in to rest.

For me, what came next was just as much of a pleasure.

Smoke cannabis while the LSD is still working in you and the effect comes surging back — a flashback, people call it.

Listen to music in that state and you hear far more depth in it than usual.

Maybe that's the sound the musicians themselves are actually hearing, or maybe it goes past even that.

Everything from the instant the bass comes in to the faint tone of the hi-hat rings out with flawless, exquisite timing.

Adrift on the sea of what music really is, half asleep, I set out on another journey through space.

In Goa I'd had only about ten cassette tapes with me; this time I had 144 albums, picked and picked until only the best were left.

It was music I had listened to dozens, hundreds of times back in Japan, but hearing it through the combination of LSD and cannabis breathed new life into it.

Every time one of those albums played, fine details in the sound and the real meaning of the lyrics came through — things I had never noticed sober — and each one opened a world I had never been in before.

Music was everything to me — back in Japan it played from the second I woke up to the second I fell asleep — but what happened in Vashisht lifted my experience of it to another dimension.

Back then I still had a dream of becoming a musician, so listening to music in an altered state was a kind of study, or practice, or discipline.

In my own mind, I ranked that experience as the most important thing I had.
