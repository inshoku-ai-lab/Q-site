## Arriving on Ishigaki

Talking with H was so much fun that I lost track of time, and before I knew it we had arrived on Ishigaki Island.

The goal of this bike trip had been to reach Okinawa, and now that I'd done it, I had no particular idea what to do next.

In a vague way, I'd been thinking it would be nice to find work in the sugarcane fields.

Sugarcane cutting was famous among travelers: go to Okinawa in winter, the story went, and everywhere you looked they were hiring cane cutters, and anyone at all could make easy money, no questions asked.

H had been thinking along the same lines, and he filled me in on all kinds of information and strategy.

According to him, the cutting peaked around February, so the usual thing was to spend the weeks before that meeting people and making connections, then work without a day off from February through about April.

He was planning to camp for about a month at a campground on the far side of the island from the ferry terminal, and he asked if I wanted to come along.

## A Companion for the Road

I had nothing lined up, so this was a boat showing up just when I needed to cross, and I jumped aboard.

I rode my bike to the campground, and H took the bus.

I figured the bus would be faster, but with the wait for it, I got there first.

The campground was at a place called Yonehara Beach, with just enough trees and just enough sand to make it a comfortable place to camp.

I pitched my tent in a likely spot and set up the base where I would spend the next month.

While I was at it, H arrived and pitched his tent a little way off.

## Camp Life

Not that there was anything in particular to do, but even with nothing to do, camping always gives you plenty to do.

Resting in the shade, reading a book, listening to music.

Buying food and cooking for ourselves over a campfire.

Swimming in the sea, gathering shellfish and eating them.

At night, sitting around the fire with H, talking until late.

In Okinawa, a scene like this usually comes with awamori, the local liquor, passed around the fire, but we both wanted to save every yen we could, so we cut the unnecessary spending and made do with tea and water.
