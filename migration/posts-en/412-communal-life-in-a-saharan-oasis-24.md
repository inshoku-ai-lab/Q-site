## Setting Off

M's hitchhiking went smoothly, and within about a week an email came saying he had made it safely to Germany.

His expired visa had apparently been a problem at the border, but there was no fining someone who had no money, so they decided he wasn't worth the trouble and let him through.

M's departure was a bright moment for all of us, but for I-chan and me, the subject of leaving also weighed heavily.

Once this journey through Morocco was over, I-chan would go back to Japan and follow her dream of working in theater.

I still wanted to keep traveling the world, and had no interest in living in a Japanese city and throwing myself into something.

We had talked it through properly, so it was a conclusion we had both accepted, but neither of us wanted to split up, and it hurt.

## Overstay

And now our own visas were about to run out.

Before, we would have booked flights to line up with the day our visas expired.

But M's experience of staying in Morocco illegally had been a good jolt, and we settled on traveling wherever our hearts took us and not worrying if our visas ran out.

Still, going past a visa's limit was close to taboo, judging by what travelers had learned up to then.

We wanted to stay as long as we could and be together, but that didn't make our money limitless, and our plans ahead weren't going to wait for us either.

I-chan had been invited by friends from her theater company to join their next project, and rehearsals would start before long.

Either way, there was a limit.

## The Idea

The idea that would fill that gap was faking illness.

We made a plan: when we left at the border, we would lie our way out of it, saying we had fallen sick in some town and couldn't travel.

With a story like that, surely we could get away with being two weeks or so past our visa?

We wanted to be together, but there were limits, and at some point we had to strike a balance.

Even so, leaving right away would have been far too soon.

The gap that fit just right was the idea of a two-week overstay.
