## In the Night

We were sleeping soundly and comfortably, but in the middle of the night that peaceful sleep was interrupted.

The wind had picked up and started shaking the tent.

But we knew how frightening typhoons in Japan could be, and that made us confident. Not taking a desert windstorm seriously, we stayed in the tent and kept sleeping.

The wind kept gaining speed, shaking the tent harder and harder.

We stubbornly stayed put even then, until at last the wind got so strong that the tent collapsed flat.

It had no third dimension left. It lay there flat, like a frog that's been run over by a car and dried out in the sun.

## Escape

A flat tent is no use as a tent, and worried it might actually break, we tried to get out.

Once we were outside, we realized this was a really, really unbelievable windstorm.

Lying down, you couldn't tell, but the moment you stood up, you took the full force of it.

We pulled the poles out of the tent, laid it completely flat, and lined a bunch of big rocks on top so it wouldn't blow away.

Getting knocked around by the wind the whole time, we hurried into the hut and made it to safety.

## The Hut

The hut was made of stone and earth, and even winds of eighty meters per second (about 180 miles per hour) didn't budge it.

Even after we'd taken shelter, we could tell from the roar all around us that the wind was still getting stronger.

It's chilling to think what would have happened if we'd stayed in that tent.

Thanks to the sturdy hut, we spent the night safe, with the gale roaring and howling outside.

Something to be grateful for.
