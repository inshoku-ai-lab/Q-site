## Setting Out

At last the day of departure was getting close.

I stayed on the scaffolding job about four months and put away four hundred thousand yen — a little over three thousand dollars.

Fifty thousand yen a month went to my parents' household the whole time I was saving, but I held the line on everything else and hit the target figure exactly as planned.

I had given notice well in advance, so they took it gracefully and wished me well on the journey ahead.

## The Inside Story

A few days after I finished, I went to the office to collect my last paycheck, and the boss himself let me in on a secret.

It was about R, the junior on my crew: the man was, of all things, the head of a right-wing organization — one of Japan's ultranationalist groups.

Not the head's son, not a former head — really and truly the current one, the boss said.

He hadn't come to work for the money, apparently, but for the experience of ordinary working life.

So that was where the manners came from, I thought, and why he worked as seriously as he did.

The boss had been a delinquent himself and probably had ties to the yakuza, so it may have been through that channel that he was asked to keep an eye on R.

Which also explained why the boss had come to me in person and told me to look after him.

R kept the secret from everyone but the boss and his own underling, but to the boss he had apparently said, "If you ever run into trouble, call me any time. One phone call and every member comes out, and we take care of all of it."

The boss, for his part, was a powerfully built man; hardly any fool picked a fight with him, and even if trouble did start he could settle it himself, so he figured he would never have to ask one of his own workers for help.

## The Incident

Then came the incident.

He got into an argument with a yakuza over a parking spot at the office.

Reading it as bad news, the boss called R on the spot.

R's answer: "Understood. I'll handle it."

Ten minutes or so later, roughly fifty members of the right-wing organization were assembled in the parking lot.

They came so fast that even the boss lost his nerve.

The yakuza lost his as well, and after being stared down by fifty hard-faced men in black suits, he had no choice but to slink off.

Their job done, the fifty vanished in an instant, and the place went back to its ordinary day as if nothing had happened.

R left one line behind — "Send for me any time, if anything comes up" — and strode off, the boss said.

All of this happened during the days when R and I were working on site together.

Even the day after something like that, R showed up at the site like normal and worked away obediently while I yelled at him.

I knew none of it, and went right on throwing my weight around as his senior. Ignorance is bliss, and this was a textbook case.
