## Back Home

Back at my family's home in Osaka, I spent a while just recovering from the exhaustion of the mountain hut.

Not long after I got back, my grandfather's condition took a sudden turn for the worse.

The whole reason I'd left England and come back to Japan in the first place was to be with my grandfather at the end, and now that time had come.

I was ready for it, and so were my grandfather and everyone else in the family.

## My Grandfather's Last Days

By the very end, my grandfather had wasted away, his body shrunken.

Cancer was what killed him, but it would be more accurate to say he died of old age.

He was just short of eighty. A long life, well lived.

In his final days he was kept alive in the hospital, hooked up to all sorts of tubes, but once he understood there was no hope of living, he made the decision himself: he had the life support removed, and chose his own death.

Right up to the end, nobody told him he had cancer, or that he didn't have much time left.

But with me suddenly rushing home from England, I suspect he had some idea anyway.

## One Blow After Another

A few weeks before my grandfather died, we'd been given some shocking news, and it was kept from him to the very end.

My uncle — my grandfather's son, older brother to my mother and my aunt — had killed himself.

He was a regular downtown Osaka guy, friendly and funny, and as a kid I'd adored him.

As a child I took it for granted that adults were stiff and stuffy, but my uncle was sloppy and laid-back, someone who really understood how kids like to play.

But growing up in a doya district — blocks of flophouses for day laborers — came with its own fate: he grew up with gambling, and he lived with it.

For a gambler there are only two places to end up: as the one-in-a-million absolute winner, or in debt hell with everyone else.

That combination of gambling and debt, a one-way ticket to hell if there ever was one, kept eating away at my grandfather, my father, and my uncle.

But my uncle, who more or less had one foot in the yakuza world, seems to have gone in deeper than my grandfather or my father, with nothing to stop him.

He sank into illegal gambling with the yakuza and lost everything down to the shirt on his back, dragged into a hell where all he had left was an aging body and a debt he couldn't have paid off in a lifetime.
