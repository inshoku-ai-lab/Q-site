## D

D, who I'd been running around with in Goa earlier that year, was going to come and visit us at our place.

When Y came, we had only just arrived in London ourselves and had no real handle on the place, but a few months of living here had gone by since then, so we knew how things worked and were starting to fit into the city.

We even had the breathing room to host a friend.

We traded emails back and forth, checking where each of us stood.

## The Internet

This was 2000, and the internet was nowhere near as widespread as it is now.

We had no internet at home, so we did our emailing from an internet café in town, through Hotmail, which at the time you could call the only webmail there was.

This was before personal computers were common, so young people got online at the internet cafés in town, but the rates weren't exactly cheap and you couldn't just burn through hours online.

But London had a groundbreaking service that young people were all talking about: the rate changed with the time of day, and after midnight one pound — about a dollar fifty — bought you all the internet you wanted until morning.

The café was called easyNet, I think, and it was run by easyJet, a company that was something like the pioneer of budget air travel — for me, keeping a nomad life going on the cheap, easyNet and easyJet had both become necessities of life.

## Glastonbury

D's plan was to meet up in London with T, a friend from India, and head to Glastonbury Festival together.

Glastonbury Festival is a giant music festival that has been running since 1970, an international festival with history and tradition behind it, drawing people from all over the world to see top artists in every genre imaginable.

D had dreamed for years of going to Glastonbury one day.

With me living in London and T up for going too, D had started moving to make that dream happen.
