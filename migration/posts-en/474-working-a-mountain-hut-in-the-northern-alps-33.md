## Time Off

Once the brutal super-labor of peak summer was behind us, the climbers thinned out all at once in the second half of August.

The way their numbers swelled and then shrank had something of a bubble economy about it.

This was when we part-timers got to take our time off.

There were fewer guests, but the summer part-timers were still working, and by now they knew the job inside out.

Some people used the break to head down into town, but I took the chance to go on an adventure even deeper into the mountains.

## A Hot Spring Far, Far Back in the Mountains

I was aiming for a hot spring so remote that, whichever trail you took, you had to hike for at least thirteen hours to get there.

From our hut, it was "only" an eight-hour hike.

The hot spring sat in a hollow at about 2,000 meters (6,600 feet), ringed by peaks in the 3,000-meter (10,000-foot) class.

There was a single mountain hut right beside it, but apart from that, there was nothing within about five hours' walk.

It was the ultimate hot spring, sitting right in the heart of the wild.

## Setting Out

To get the most out of my three days off, I set out at sunrise on the first day.

Up near the summit of a nearby peak there were other climbers around, and I traded smiles and greetings with each one I passed, but once I got far enough into the mountains, people simply vanished.

In ridgelines rolling on as far as the eye could see, the only thing moving was me.

Not a single man-made structure came into view.

It was a purely one-on-one conversation between nature and me.

I'd been out in the wild before in my travels, but there had always been someone around.

Being truly deep in the mountains, completely alone, hours on foot from anywhere I might meet another person — that was a first for me.

If I twisted an ankle, no one would come to help, and if I fell off the mountain, my life would end right there.

Everything rested on my own responsibility.

Whether I lived or died was up to me.

One mistake, and five minutes later I would no longer exist in this world.

In the mountains it's a plain fact that you hold the power of life and death over yourself, and that fact honed my awareness to a razor's edge.

And that set every living thing I laid eyes on shining with light.

Brimming with life, I kept walking, step after steady step.
