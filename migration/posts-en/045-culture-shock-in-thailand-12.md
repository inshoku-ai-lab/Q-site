# Bangkok Again

The trip through the northern mountains had been a good one, and now it was over — I was back in Bangkok.

A few days left before the flight home to Japan.

Bangkok was as dusty and muggy as ever.

A long way from the comfort of the mountains.

Here I met back up with A-kun, who had been traveling separately.

Apparently he had gone to Ayutthaya, the old capital.

Pretty good, he said.

We traded travel stories.

We had only been apart about ten days, but both of us had been through a lot in that time.

Only a few days, and yet each of us looked a little more grown.

The jumpy, nervous air of the early days was gone, and our skin had gone dark enough that we blended in with the locals.

Both of us seemed more relaxed, more sure of ourselves.

# The Wednesday Market

Every Wednesday, back then, an enormous flea market opened up.

We headed there to buy souvenirs.

Back in Japan I used to go to the flea markets down in the old working-class parts of Osaka.

I liked how unpretentious they were, the goods laid out right on the ground.

Buying things cheap was part of the appeal, and so was the haggling and the back-and-forth with the sellers.

The Bangkok flea market was in a different league from Osaka's.

Not local Osaka size, but the international size of the gateway to Asia.

I couldn't tell you exactly, but it had to be some number of Tokyo Domes across — the yardstick Japanese people reach for when a thing is too big to picture.

Narrow lanes ran through a huge lot, packed with an unbelievable number of stalls.

And there were as many customers as there were stalls, spilling out through the whole market.

They sold everything here. Clothes and shoes and bags of course, but also groceries, plants, pets and fish.

Appliances, massages, tattoos, paintings, sculpture, and a giant street-food quarter — it was as if there were nothing they didn't have.

Maybe half foreign tourists, half Thais.

There were people everywhere, and the economy was spinning at a furious speed.

Since so many of the customers were foreign tourists, a lot of what was for sale had the souvenir trade in mind.

We set out to get all our souvenirs in one place and picked through everything.

We bought handmade soap, ethnic-style bags, and some secondhand clothes.

The secondhand T-shirts imported straight from America were far cheaper than they would have been in Japan — about two hundred yen apiece, something like a dollar sixty — so I stocked up on a pile of them.

# Chinatown

The next day we headed for Chinatown.

One street over, and every sign suddenly changed to Chinese.

The buildings shot up in height and density all at once, seven-story blocks running on and on.

Almost every one of them had an enormous Chinese neon sign hanging off it.

From the look of them, they were combined hotel-and-restaurant places, or shops that bought and sold gold and silver.

As on Khao San Road or at the Wednesday market, the shops were packed shoulder to shoulder and the tourists overflowed.

What set this place apart was that the Chinese were the main population, with local Thais and foreign tourists mixed in among them, and the mix gave off the strange heat of a real international city.

The foreign feel of it was cool, and with the whole scene looking like the near future out of a sci-fi anime, the two of us, techno fanatics that we were, were beside ourselves.

Tomorrow we would fly home to Japan.

We had a little money left over, so we ate at a nice Chinese restaurant.

Full in body and mind, comfortable, we came to the last day.

# Home to Japan

Those three weeks in Thailand were an enormous shock to me.

It wasn't my first time abroad, but it was the first time I had traveled on my own, and it woke me up to how much fun it is to move around freely, under your own direction.

The days on the southern island, where time didn't exist, called the hurried life of the city into question and planted a new set of values in me.

The biggest shock of all was the veteran travelers I met on Koh Phangan — people living by values completely unlike anything I had known, more than one of them, and getting to hear it directly from them became a real treasure to me.

By the time I boarded the plane back to Japan, what had hold of me was not nostalgia for the good trip I'd had in Thailand, but a burning thought: next time, India, and for a long time.
