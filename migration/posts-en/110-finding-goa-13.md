## The Genre Called Goa Trance

Back to the story...

I dove into the bar and kept dancing to the music.

The music in that bar, and at Goa's parties in general, came in exactly one flavor, and there was no choice in the matter: the genre people call psychedelic trance, or Goa trance.

Inside the broad frame of techno music there is a genre called trance techno, and this was the strain of it that had pushed furthest in one particular direction.

Goa's history runs roughly like this: from the sixties hippies gathered here and threw rock parties on the beach, and in the nineties techno took over.

Goa trance, you could say, is what came out of that music shifting and developing to suit the tastes of the travelers staying in Goa at the time.

Those tastes were fairly extreme: music built so that you could take LSD, keep dancing for hours out in nature, and arrive at a trance state.

Along with beats that set your body moving and melodies that move your feelings, the music mixes in ultra-high-pitched machine tones that repeat at regular intervals, and synthesizer lines that undulate in time with the sounds LSD puts in your ears.

It was made as music, but it was also a tool for the trance experience, put together meticulously and engineered to work exactly as intended.

This video may give you a rough sense of what a Goa trance party looked like back then.

It was shot after December, once the travelers had arrived in numbers, but it captures the atmosphere of the Goa I knew then perfectly.

## Satisfied

As I kept dancing in that bar, the crowd slowly grew.

The place was packed shoulder to shoulder and steaming with heat.

Strange travelers in strange outfits kept arriving, dancing freely with strange movements.

They danced like maniacs, as if how anyone saw them barely mattered to them.

I was thoroughly satisfied with this interesting event I had finally managed to find.

Meeting people this exciting made the long way from Japan worth it.

And fifth time lucky, I had finally gotten to experience LSD.

I stayed in that good feeling and kept dancing, and then, after a while, the music stopped.

It was still nowhere near dawn.

And the party had only just started to pick up.

I went out of the bar and down onto the beach, and there was Y, standing stunned.

He had been expecting to dance until morning too, and the sound cutting out so suddenly seemed to have left him confused.

From what we heard, the bar was legally required to close at four in the morning.

## The Chai Stalls

The bar had closed and the sound was gone, but the chai stalls on the beach stayed open.

Local mothers, known as chai mamas, come to the parties the foreigners throw, lay out their straw mats wherever they please, haul in their kerosene stoves and their pots without asking anyone, set out snacks and sweets, and open a chai stall.

Among them there are no licenses, no rules, no turf — the law of free competition governs everything.

I didn't know it then, but the chai stalls at these parties are part of Goa's traditional party culture, and for seasoned travelers who had come back to Goa many times the standard move was set: arrive at the party, go first to your favorite chai mama, drop your bags, order a chai, smoke cannabis while you drink it, and head for the dance floor only once you were ready.

We ordered chai at a stall and tried to roll a joint, but the LSD had taken away any fine, precise movement.

Swaying in the sea wind, I concentrated to the absolute limit and rolled one.

The misshapen joint my clumsy hands produced went out as soon as we lit it, and air came in through the gaps so it never smoked properly, but in fits and starts we managed to finish the whole thing.

LSD and cannabis go well together: the LSD amplifies your senses and leaves you tense, and the relaxing effect of the cannabis neutralizes it.

We talked about our first Goa party and about what the LSD was doing.

Neither of us understood LSD or Goa parties at all, but we both had the real sense that this was something seriously interesting, something we had never experienced before.

We sat on the beach until the sun came up, drinking chai and smoking joints and talking about this unknown experience.

The sun came up, and as the LSD began to fade we were finally ready to go home.

We had spent over an hour lost on the way there, but with the high worn off and the motorcycle running in daylight, we were home in a few minutes.
