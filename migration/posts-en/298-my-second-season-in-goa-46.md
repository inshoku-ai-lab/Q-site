## Collapse

Y lowered himself to the floor in the middle of the DJ booth, his face knotted up.

At first I could not tell what he was doing, but I had no doubt he would bring in the next track before this one ended.

The next track never came, though, and in the end the one playing ran out.

Y came to with a start and hurried the next track in.

And then down he went onto the floor again, with the same knotted face.

And again the track played all the way through with nothing to follow it.

A DJ's job is to keep the tracks running into one another without a break so the crowd keeps dancing.

Mixing can be smooth or clumsy, taste in track selection can differ, but as long as you keep the tracks connected, you have done the bare minimum a DJ has to do.

Y, though, stayed down on the floor and gave up on connecting the tracks altogether.

## Confusion

Something's off — the dance floor caught it and began to stir.

By this point the LSD I had taken was coming on for me too, and I was starting to lose the thread.

I was barely holding it together myself, but there was no way I could leave Y, my closest friend, hanging in a situation like that.

I ran up to the DJ booth and spoke to him, watching to see how he was.

He was muttering something, saying he could not DJ right now.

He had taken too much LSD, apparently, and gotten himself stuck deep in a labyrinth of thought.

About the worst place a person can get stuck.

Just then the owner of Nine Bar, who had noticed something was wrong, came rushing over.

Y kept telling him in English, "I know, I know," but there was no sign he was going to be able to keep DJing.

## Standstill

At the height of the Goa season the crowd at Nine Bar is full of DJs, and a replacement would have turned up right away, but by the middle of May they had all left for the Himalayas or gone home to Europe.

There was no one else there who could DJ.

Mercifully, everyone in the crowd had deep experience with LSD, and they understood the state you reach when you get too high, lose the thread completely, and come apart.

Most of them had been through something similar themselves, or had watched a friend go the same way.

Everyone understood that he had fallen into a situation nothing could be done about, and they understood perfectly well that pushing Y here would only give him trauma and would not turn anything around.

They had all given up and started smoking chillums, watching the sunset.

That ease in the face of a broken situation is probably the great thing about Goa, and about the people who travel in India.
