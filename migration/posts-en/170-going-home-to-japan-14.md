## The Scaffolding Days

I worked six days a week on the scaffolding crew and kept saving.

On the job I got to where I could carry fifty-kilo (110-pound) blocks of steel up and down stairs, and my twenty-year-old body was growing under the brutal labor a little more every day.

I think I put on about ten kilos (twenty-two pounds).

The build I had started with — tall, skinny, a matchstick — turned into something tall and thick with muscle, close to a giant.

And it wasn't only the build and the muscle that changed: my body had gotten supple, faster and more precise, and my stamina had gone up enormously.

No matter how hard I worked, I barely got tired, and one night's sleep had me fully recovered by the next day.

More than that, short sleep didn't wear me down either — where I used to sleep about nine hours, five was now enough.

## Culture Shock

Physical strength like nothing I had ever known was a turning point.

From the day I started grade school right up until recently, I had been convinced that my body was weak and sickly, that I had no athletic ability, that I was physically inferior — and now I wondered what the whole thing had been.

The belief had a real basis, since I genuinely couldn't do the exercises in grade school PE, and I had gone on reinforcing that experience with the belief itself, and then a few months of physical work changed it, and changed it easily.

A senior guy on the crew, an ex-*yankee*, told me, "The way you are now, you could fight anybody you see on the street and not lose" — his official seal of approval.

All through school I had been the weak one, and there was probably a part of me that went through life unconsciously watching the people around me, but now there wasn't a speck of that left.

Carrying the sense that I was overwhelmingly stronger than most of the people walking down the street was a serious culture shock.

The confidence of having come through heavy seas in China and India joined with the confidence of being physically tough, and I was in excellent condition.

## Boxed In

But on the other side of being at my peak, I was writhing under the rigidity of living inside the framework of Japanese society.

The suffering grew stronger by the day, a tight, cramped pain, like being left half-killed and never finished off.

Mentally I was in good shape, physically strong and healthy, the savings were piling up, things were good with my family and my girlfriend — nothing but good news — and still I was tormented by a sense of being boxed in like nothing I had ever felt.

Down at the level of the soul, something was screaming for freedom.

I understood that I was coming up on the limit of how long I could stay in Japan, and I booked a plane ticket back to India.

My soul needed the journey to India.
