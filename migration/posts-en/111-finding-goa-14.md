# New Friends

November was coming to an end, and travelers kept arriving.

More Japanese travelers were turning up too, and a couple, S and E, moved in nearby.

They had already been on the road about a year, through Southeast Asia, Nepal, and around India.

Goa was to be the last of it — after this the trip was over and they were going home to Japan.

We grilled out together at R's house, spent whole days playing cards in restaurants, and generally took it easy and enjoyed ourselves.

# A New Oddball Friend

Around that time we met an oddball of an older guy.

An eccentric on the order of F, the homeless guy.

His name was C, somewhere in his mid-fifties, a dozen years up on F and a different generation.

Like F, C was an old face here, coming back to Goa year after year.

He had about thirty years of travel behind him, hippie generation to the core.

He had lived his whole life outside society, and it showed in a personality that stood out, for better and for worse.

His experience of life ran deep, and he shared that knowledge and wisdom with the young travelers, who looked up to him.

But for some reason, both Y and I disliked C from the start.

Why Y disliked him I don't know; for me it was the way he talked down to people.

Ever since living on my own in Osaka I'd carried a punk attitude, defiant toward authority of any kind.

And if the authority was male and older, the defiance only picked up speed.

I had a policy of never using keigo — the deferential Japanese you speak to anyone above you — with a single person I met on the road.

In Japanese society, if you meet someone with a rank set between you, then depending on the situation you speak keigo — but travelers meet as equals, and one of them speaking keigo to the other was something I could never accept.

Anyone who couldn't get along with me because I didn't speak keigo was someone I had no need to talk to.

Y, about half again my age, accepted this and dealt with me as an equal.

From C's side — respected by everyone, spoken to in keigo by everyone — I imagine the youngest person in the place refusing him that was a nuisance.

He had two or three times my years and dozens of times my experience of life, so the high-and-mighty attitude of a brat like me must have gotten on his nerves.

For all that, I kept a little distance from C from the start, and never closed it.

# C and E

E, though — the girlfriend of the couple — wasn't like me, and seemed to take a liking to C and his store of knowledge and experience.

And from there, with her boyfriend S in the mix, a human drama began to unfold.

This is a Goa story through and through: E took LSD at a party, and the mental shock of it was too strong, and it left her in a violent state of confusion.

As I said two posts back, LSD takes a person whose thinking is already twisted and twists it further.

E was a textbook case of the bad outcome.

She was psychically sensitive by nature, and delicate.

She had an artistic side as well, and along with it a doubt about ordinary society.

With LSD firing up that twisted thinking and culture shock leaving her raw, E was at the far end of confusion.

Because she was so sensitive, even a minute dose of LSD was enough to fire her brain up to an extreme, and taking it at the last party had left her with a feeling of something like an awakening.

Y and I would both come to know that feeling of awakening later on, but at the time we met her, it was beyond us.

E flew a bit too far, maybe — that was about the extent of our thinking.

Neither we, her friends, nor S, her boyfriend, could make sense of that awakening of hers; the only one who could understand it and explain it was C.

C had a history with every kind of drug and every kind of spiritual search, and he could take the difficult business of the spirit world and lay it out for E in terms she could follow.

A man who could open up that unknown world with deep talk about the spirit was magnetic to E, and S, who couldn't understand the new world she had been through, may have started to look to her like an immature kid.

E closed the distance between herself and C.

The one who couldn't stand it was S.

Jealousy over E and C, confusion at a situation he couldn't understand, anxiety about where they were headed as a couple — every kind of negativity was eating at him.

Day by day, S grew more frantic and more worn down.
