## R's History with Addiction

When R came to Goa about ten years earlier, he had spent the whole summer hooked on brown sugar.

For all the fierce personality and energy he carried, he had a deeply shy, self-effacing side, and a great deal of repression inside him.

Part of it seemed to come from the fact that someone that full of energy had grown up in a closed-off country backwater, and it had left him with a kind of twist.

Brown sugar loosened that twist, and the release of it was his pleasure — which, it seems, is what led to six full months in Goa spent shut up in what amounted to an opium den.

He had declared outright that he would never go back to that life, so finding him caught in his old unhealthy pattern here in Vashisht was both strange and sad.

## How It Started

From what I heard, the one who set it off was the Section Chief — N.

It started with the Section Chief taking brown sugar for fun and, in a casual spirit, inviting R along.

The Section Chief had no addictive streak, so for him it was just travelers messing around with drugs — he took it now and then and shared it with whoever happened to be there.

To him, I think, offering brown sugar felt like setting out tea and sweets for someone who had dropped by.

But R was the addictive type, and once he had done it, that was that — the feeling from ten years before came back in an instant, and there was apparently no resisting it.

R knew his own character well, and he understood that as long as he stayed holed up in the mountains he would never get off brown sugar.

He knew the only way out was to go to another country, somewhere brown sugar simply wasn't available.

Having accepted all of that completely, he had made up his mind to spend the next few months on brown sugar.

## Free Will

Watching a friend destroy himself was hard to bear, but more than that, I respected the free will of the individual.

If he understood his situation and was destroying himself of his own free will, that was his freedom, and I understood that there was nothing I could do except stay his friend.

R had chosen, of his own free will, to go down into the dark.

Day and night had stopped meaning anything to him — he played video games the whole time he was awake, and when he got hungry he ate the Kit Kats he had bought by the box.

He ate all that sweet chocolate and never brushed his teeth, and his cavities kept getting worse, but he masked the pain by taking brown sugar.

It was endlessly ridiculous, completely pathological, and yet, strangely enough, R himself stayed as gentle and warm as ever.
