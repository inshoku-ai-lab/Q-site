## Tangier

The ferry pulled into Tangier, Morocco's northernmost city, at dusk.

Tangier is a port town on the sea, but it's also a hill town that climbs quite high.

The evening sun was falling on the buildings packed along those slopes, and they glowed a gorgeous gold.

Such a beautiful city, right after we'd set foot on the African continent, moved us deeply. It felt as if our arrival were being blessed.

## A Cheap Room

We had done our research beforehand, so we knew where the cheap guesthouses were.

We found one fast and secured a bed for the night.

In Morocco you can get by in Moroccan, Arabic, or French, but we couldn't speak any of them.

Six months in London had improved my English a little, but that effort didn't pay off here.

Still, credit where it's due: the travel experience we'd built up in India worked beautifully here in Morocco too. Not only did we find a cheap room without any trouble, we haggled relentlessly and got it for next to nothing.

Behind all that haggling was more than just our time in India; I-chan's desperate money situation was pushing it along too.

Never underestimate a desperate traveler from Osaka, I suppose.

## Tagine

The trip from London was more or less behind us and we had a place to stay for the night, so, feeling relieved, we went out for our first meal in Africa.

We walked the strip of restaurants along the main road facing the harbor.

They all looked about the same, so we picked one at random and ordered a tagine.

Tagine is the most iconic Moroccan dish: olive oil, spices, vegetables, and meat, stewed for hours in a clay pot.

We'd read up on this too, and we'd been told that the first thing to do in Morocco was eat a tagine.

The pot came with a triangular lid shaped like Snufkin's pointed hat, and apparently the lid does something that makes the food taste better.

The lid and the plate both seemed to be scorching hot, and the waiter who brought our tagine lifted the lid off with great ceremony.

The steam that billowed up carried the vapor of the vegetables and meat along with the scent of olive oil and spices, and the moment we smelled it, I-chan and I looked at each other, thrilled.

It smelled so good we knew it was delicious without tasting a bite.

That alone was enough to convince me that coming to Morocco had been the right call.
