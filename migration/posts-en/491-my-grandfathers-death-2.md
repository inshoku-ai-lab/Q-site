## My Uncle's Hardships

My uncle had yakuza debt collectors after him, and he'd been on the run, moving from place to place all over Kansai.

The debt collectors had apparently been calling my mother's place, too.

He'd divorced his wife some time before, and it seems he had no friends to turn to either.

After all that running, he finally had nowhere left to go, so he started crashing at the home of my mother's older sister and her husband.

He stayed there for about a year, never holding down a real job, doing nothing but eating and sleeping, day after day.

Have a freeloader like that around for a whole year, and anyone hits their limit, my aunt and her husband included.

Something small set off a huge fight, and my uncle was thrown out of the house with nowhere to go.

With that, his ties to his sisters, the only blood family he had, were cut too.

## The Last Choice

After that, apparently, he lived alone in the city of Osaka, working at a supermarket without telling anyone who he really was and hiding from the debt collectors.

But with no safe place to run to and nothing to hope for, he grew sick of a life of just getting old alone, and it seems he chose to kill himself.

He neatly got rid of everything he owned, got into his car, the one thing of value he had left, and drove up into the mountains.

It was the place our family and relatives used to go for picnics when I was still in elementary school.

I was in elementary school and my sister was in junior high, and the whole family would sit together, all getting along, eating the lunches we'd packed; it's a place I remember from the good old days.

It's a good memory for me, and it seems it was a good memory for my uncle, too.

In his last moments, he must have wanted to feel the one connection to family he had left as he passed on to the next world.

## What the Police Found

My uncle got rid of the car's license plates so no one could tell whose car it was, then doused himself from head to toe in gasoline and set himself on fire.

My mother thinks that after deep regret, he wanted to wipe out his entire existence completely.

The burned-out car and my uncle's charred body had been found months before.

There was nothing there to show who he was, so the police hadn't been able to identify him.

In the end, the key was his dental work, the places where his cavities had been treated.

The police kept following that trail, and after a long investigation, they apparently figured out who he was.

Since it could have been murder, they apparently worked hard on the case.

It really drove home for me what the Japanese police are capable of.

And that is how the news of my uncle's death finally reached my mother and the rest of the family.
