## T

Since D missed Glastonbury Festival, T, who was supposed to be enjoying it together with him, was left to enjoy the festival on his own.

Everything D had been in charge of preparing stayed behind in Dhaka, so T, who was already in England, had no way of getting hold of any of it.

There were all sorts of inconveniences, apparently, but T got past them on the traveler's spirit that came naturally to him.

All of his preparations had been blown away at the last minute, so he wound up going into the festival making it up as he went, and if anything the sheer desperation of that worked in his favor, and he had about as good a time at the festival as anyone could.

T was twenty-one, the same age as me, which is quite young for a traveler.

But while traveling in India he had fallen in with a group of Westerners and spent months on end with them, and that was how he had learned to speak English.

## The Festival

If you can speak English, then no matter what happens, you can get by in England.

All the more so at a festival, where free spirits gather and the place is full of the spirit of helping one another — there, everything works out.

On the spot he meets all sorts of people and explains: the situation is such and such, he has nothing with him and he is stuck, but he came all the way from Japan to have a good time anyway.

A story with that kind of festival soul in it is exactly what the people who come to an event with a heavy hippie feel to it, like Glastonbury Festival, eat right up.

Every last one of them gladly offers to help T out.

Just twenty-one, a young guy from an island country in the Far East, traveling with no money, his friend suddenly unable to come.

And on top of that, he speaks solid English.

Before he knew it he had met all sorts of people, one connection leading to another, and he had made a lot of friends.

Carried along by that, apparently, he kept it going even after the festival ended, hopping from one new friend's house to the next and having a fine time.

So even once D had arrived in London, T couldn't come to see him right away.

Then things finally settled down, and he was coming to our place to see D.
