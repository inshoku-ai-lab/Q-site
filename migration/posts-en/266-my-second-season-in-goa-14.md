## Y's Awakening

Y had planted himself at the chai stall, wandering the depths of his own consciousness.

That went on all night, and each time I came back from the dance floor to the chai stall, I caught the serious look on his face out of the corner of my eye.

Experience had taught me that the best thing to do with a person in that state is to leave him alone, so I let it run its course.

He stayed like that until the LSD began to weaken and his body started to move again — and right about then, the sun began to rise.

And then he rose like a phoenix out of the ashes and muttered just one thing: "I get it."

His eyes were glittering, unnaturally bright.

In a clear voice full of confidence he said, "I get it — completely! I can't explain it right now, but I completely get it!" and then walked straight to the dance floor, claimed the spot right in front of the speakers, and started dancing with tremendous force.

The force of it overpowered everyone around him, and the speed and strength of his movements were beyond anything normal.

His eyes blazed, and he danced like a man possessed.

He was a fighter to begin with, and when he went all out nobody could match his stamina.

And now, his senses doubled by that enormous dose of LSD and his consciousness cracked open, he had entered the zone and kept dancing as though something had descended into him.

## The End of the Party

Y kept firing off "I get it," but he could not explain what it was that he got.

I knew Y well, and having been through that kind of awakening myself, I could just about accept it — but I-chan had recoiled completely.

Our friends who were long used to Goa watched with a cool, detached eye: "Ah, that one again. Flies too high and comes out feeling enlightened. Carrying on like that — it's lame."

Eyes bloodshot, Y just kept repeating it: "I get it, I'm telling you."

He understood it on the level of consciousness, it seemed, but could not get it into words.

There was no end in sight, so we headed to Y's place to smoke a chillum and unwind.
