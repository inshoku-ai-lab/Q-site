## The Hike

One sunny day, we decided to drop LSD and go for a hike.

Two hours up the mountain trail, they said, there was a huge waterfall with a rainbow permanently hanging over it.

LSD sharpens the senses and deepens the emotions, which makes it perfect for hiking through beautiful country with friends.

After breakfast at our usual restaurant we left the guesthouse, met up with a friend who lived somewhere else, and started up the mountain trail.

The LSD came on as we walked, and our strength rose with it, so we kept going without getting tired.

Some pushed ahead briskly at their own pace, others walked slowly, caught by the beauty of the grasses and flowers along the way.

About an hour in, we came to the foot of the slope downstream of the falls, and the sound of running water reached us.

The water was running along the bottom of the ravine, about fifteen meters (fifty feet) below the trail we were on.

I looked down over the edge and got a shock!

The karate men I wrote about in an earlier post were down there, actually doing their training!

A small waterfall came down beside them, and they stood on the rocks throwing one kick after another.

I had heard the rumor, but I never believed they were really training at a waterfall, and it had never crossed my mind that I would see it with my own eyes.

And in the middle of being wrecked on LSD, looking at a reality gone dreamlike, no less.

The whole thing was straight out of a manga, and we were overjoyed.

## The Way to the Waterfall

We kept climbing.

The slope steepened, and green plants took over more and more of it.

Proof, probably, that we were getting close to the falls.

As we walked on, the sound of the water grew steadily louder.

This is hiking deep in the mountains of India, and it is nothing like hiking in a developed country.

There is no well-marked trail, and signposts are out of the question.

You guess the rough direction and just keep walking toward the falls.

Every one of us was high on LSD, and I had been a little worried that we might not get anywhere at all and end up lost — but once we could hear the falls, there was no getting lost anymore.
