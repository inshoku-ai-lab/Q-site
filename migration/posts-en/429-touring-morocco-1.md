## Farewells, and Setting Out

The time to say goodbye had finally come.

Goodbye to Y, goodbye to Ouarzazate, the desert town where we'd ended up staying about two and a half months, and goodbye to all the memories tied up with the festival.

It was also the start of a journey toward saying goodbye to my girlfriend, I-chan.

We were heading north to Marrakesh, the old cultural capital of Morocco.

Y said he was going to explore the desert a little longer.

There was no telling when we'd meet next, but we were both Japanese, so we were bound to run into each other again someday.

Looking forward to that reunion, we wished each other well on the road ahead.

## Marrakesh

We got on a bus and headed for Marrakesh.

The city was about 100 kilometers (60 miles) away, just over the mountains, which isn't much of a distance.

After about four hours of bouncing along on the bus, we reached Marrakesh.

The mountains we crossed that day were the High Atlas, which divide Morocco into a desert half and a green half.

Both halves are hot and dry, but the green land to the north is less parched, with patches of green scattered across it.

Marrakesh was a big city.

Apparently it was one of the largest in Morocco.

For the first time in ages, we plunged into a crowd.

## Fast-Forward

The markets in Marrakesh were fun, we ate good food, and we had a satisfying time.

We enjoyed our sightseeing.

But a fun sightseeing story doesn't necessarily make an interesting vagabond's chronicle to read.

In Morocco we also stopped in a surfer town on the coast, among other places, and had a great trip, but it wouldn't make an interesting post, so I'll skip over it.

We made our way around the country and finally came back to Tangier, the gateway to Morocco.

It was the first town we'd arrived in, and it would be the town where we parted.

It was also where Gandalf and F lived.

We'd said goodbye to them at the oasis about two weeks earlier.

I was looking forward to seeing them again after all that time.
