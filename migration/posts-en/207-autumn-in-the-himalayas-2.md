## Cannabis Culture

In Manali I smoked hash every day.

Mostly we rolled joints, but whenever somebody had a chillum, we smoked out of that.

The room K and I shared was the biggest one in the guesthouse, and it turned into the place our friends naturally drifted to.

Music going all day, cannabis going all day.

People started showing up a little before noon, and after a smoke we would head over to the Tibetan restaurant in the building next door for lunch.

## The Tibetan Restaurant

Inside that restaurant cannabis was as good as legal, so the smoking simply carried on at the table.

The owner and his son would join the circle themselves and take their turns passing the chillum around.

Chillum culture belongs, properly speaking, to the Hindu sadhus, but to a father and son raised in a village hippies had been coming to since the sixties, it was just one more ordinary part of the day.

The owner was already around fifty, but he had grown up hanging around Western and Japanese hippies from a young age, and though he looked like any other Tibetan, he shared something of a traveler's way of seeing things.

By his own account, he had smoked so much hash over the years that he had lost track of how many tens of kilos (well past a hundred pounds) it added up to.

Not that he was an addict or anything — just a restaurant owner going about an ordinary life.

This wasn't only true of that place: restaurants where travelers hung around were, as a rule, absurdly slow to bring the food out.

Two-hour waits, three-hour waits, nothing out of the ordinary.

It wasn't so much that people were hanging around as that the wait was so long everyone was still sitting there — which comes to the same thing.

In exchange for that drawback, the inside of the place was as comfortable as a room can be: the big main room of a traditional wooden Manali house, carpets and mattresses laid end to end, built Asian style so you sat directly on the floor.

You could stretch out and rest, and dropping off to sleep right there was normal.

A restaurant, in short, that also felt like a friend's house.

That looseness was part of why travelers liked the place.

## Chillum Culture

In Vashisht nearly every traveler was a long-term, hippie-ish sort, and nearly all of them smoked cannabis.

Heavily, at that.

Among hippies there was a kind of etiquette to it: when you smoked a chillum, you invited everyone present into the circle.

The idea being that you all take in the energy of Shiva together and connect through it.

So you would smoke a chillum with people you had never met, get cosmically wrecked together, and the walls came down and you got close.

That was how, in this village, the bonds between travelers kept growing stronger and a culture all its own kept taking shape.
