## Spectacular

The bus for Ladakh got off without a hitch, rocking its great bulk through the narrow mountain road.

The first real obstacle was a pass, about two hours north of the town of Manali.

The grade was good and steep, and the elevation was high.

Big buses and giant trucks can't get up a slope like that quickly, so we went up the S-curves slowly, slowly.

The higher we went, the farther we could see, and the green ranges running on and on were, in a word, magnificent.

Partway up the slow climb we passed a Western guy touring on a bicycle — or seemed to, until he caught hold of something on the back of the bus and started riding the whole way to the top for free.

Something about the way the cyclist traveled — playing it by ear, free to take whatever came — was just cool, and the whole bus erupted in cheers.

## The Other Side

The bus took about two hours to work its way up the pass road and reach the top.

This seemed to be the dividing line between the rain country and the dry country, and on the other side an entirely different landscape opened up.

The rain-soaked green forest was gone, and in its place lay yellow ground with low scrub growing thick in patches.

Beyond the pass the climate was dry and alpine.

The bare rock of the mountains lay open to view, and it felt like experiencing the earth raw.

The ecosystem had changed completely, of course, and the range of animal species there had to be far smaller.

## High Mountains

A while after the bus came all the way down off the pass, a few small restaurants stood in a row, serving travelers food and a place to sleep.

Our bus stopped there and we ate with the view in front of us.

The ranges on this side of the pass were a size larger than the ones on the Manali side, the peaks higher and the valleys wider by what looked like an order of magnitude.

Maybe the two were actually about the same size, and it only looked that way because this was desert country with no forest and no buildings, but what it felt like was an oasis sitting all alone in the middle of colossal desert mountains.

I traveled without a camera in those days, so there is not a single photograph left, but we have the internet now.

This is roughly what Ladakh looks like, so let it help you picture what I am describing.

Tibet looked much the same, but Ladakh feels to me like it has higher mountains and a bigger scale.

Spectacular does not begin to cover it.
