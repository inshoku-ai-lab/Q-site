## The London Music Scene

For whatever reason, England, and London in particular, draws musicians from all over the world.

In an environment where that many good musicians gather, an ordinary musician can grow into a good one.

Plenty of people argue about why the music that comes out of England is so interesting, but no one reason really stands out.

The most plausible explanation, to my mind, is the bad mood of the weather.

The gloomy overcast that hangs low over the city has become one of London's signature sights, and a dark atmosphere and a repressed society push people toward art.

While Australians drink beer and barbecue under direct sunlight doubled in strength by the thinning ozone, the English shut themselves in their rooms under that ill-tempered sky and throw themselves into music.

## Concerts

I made the most of this blessed musical environment.

Famous musicians and DJs you could not see in Japan without paying a steep entry fee; artists too obscure to bother coming to Japan at all, since a trip there would never pay for itself.

And those musicians I loved were putting on interesting events every weekend, at half the Japanese price, or even a third of it.

Back then information still lived in print rather than on the internet, so I scraped together event listings magazines and the flyers handed out at clubs, hunting desperately for the interesting ones.

## Dub

I made it to all kinds of events, but the ones I kept going back to were for a genre of music called dub.

Dub is a genre that grew out of reggae — the bass and the rhythm pulled out of a reggae track, run through extreme effects to bring out the psychedelic side of the music.

In London the culture of the Jamaican immigrants had fused with the psychedelic culture of the city's hippies, and an interesting dub culture had grown up out of it.

Entry fees and the rest were cheap, so when there was an event in the neighborhood I would tear over on my bicycle straight after my shift, dance to the music for a few hours, and then head home.
