# Techno Fever

Once I started composing "techno music" myself, the techno fever inside me picked up speed.

Most of the people reading this blog probably don't listen to techno, but techno — four-on-the-floor techno especially — requires a fairly particular sensibility before you can enjoy it as music.

In most cases, heard with ears accustomed to rock and pop, it turns into nothing but machine noise, of no interest whatsoever.

So how do you enjoy it? Since most four-on-the-floor techno is made as dance music, you have to catch the groove with your hips, not just listen with your ears.

Putting it into words doesn't really get it across, and it isn't the kind of thing whose appeal you grasp with your head.

For those who get it, it comes across as a feeling; for those who don't, it doesn't.

That is more or less how it goes with any specialized type of music.

My personal hit back then was a track called "Home Run" by HardFloor.

YouTube video

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5AGHtG6Htw>

I had bought it because it took first place by a wide margin in the contributor rankings of "TV Bros.," a magazine I liked for the interesting contributors it gathered, but for the first week or so I listened without really understanding it.

Then, all at once, as though a revelation had come down, I understood what made techno interesting, and from then on I was hooked, falling in deeper still once I was composing it myself.

# Clubs

As I started getting hooked on techno, I began going to clubs to chase the cutting edge of the techno scene.

The clubs I mean here are not school clubs like the baseball club or the soccer club, nor the kind of club in the nightlife districts where you drink with hostesses, nor the kind of club where people with a shared hobby gather.

![Black-and-white concert crowd in silhouette beneath bright laser beams fanning across a stage](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog33-1.jpg)

A disco is the closest description, but it wasn't about picking up girls and the crowd wasn't made up of party people; it was more like techno obsessives gathering and dancing in silence all night long.

Even after moving out on my own, I still had no friends besides my brother, and week after week I went to the club alone.

Compared with a year earlier, when I'd been invited to a house party in Peru and danced there in embarrassment, it was an enormous change to make in a single year.

From around eleven at night until around six in the morning, without a trace of embarrassment, I danced in front of the speakers and did nothing else.

The places I often went to played genres like "minimal techno" and "drum and bass" — music suited to dancing on and on in silence.

In all my club-going I never once talked with anyone or got friendly with anyone.

Simply bathing my whole body in the sound, becoming one with it, I kept dancing.

# A Techno Friend

Among the late-night shift at Tsutaya was a techno musician, A.

As my techno fever grew, we had more and more to talk about — gear and software for composing, and so on — and when a famous artist came over from abroad we started going to the club together.

![A smiling woman with blue headphones round her neck beside a man holding a microphone](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog33-2.jpg)

Having a proper friend, someone to go out and have fun with beyond just passing the hours at work, was a first in about five years.

The "power of music to connect people" really is great.

Like most people who compose techno, he was introverted and nerdy, the same as me.

The general image of the people dancing in a club is that a lot of them are party people, sociable, cheerful idiots, but the crowd changes a great deal depending on national character, the genre, and the feel of the music.

The Japanese techno scene seems to me to have an especially strong nerd streak, perhaps a matter of national character.

As representatives, so to speak, of that Japanese "nerd techno scene," we came to take an interest in the real techno scene abroad, where techno had actually come from.

As it happened, backpacking trips to Southeast Asia were in fashion just then among my coworkers at my part-time job.

We began planning to travel to Thailand and attend an outdoor techno event overseas.
