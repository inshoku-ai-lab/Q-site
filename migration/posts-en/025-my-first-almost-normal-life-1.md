# The Pizza Delivery Job

The pizza delivery job went well.

The manager there was an ex-*yankee* — Japanese for the delinquent type — or rather a current one, and the young wife who worked the same shifts was a yankee too, the two of them a blond married couple.

Whether it was the manager's personality or the delivery bikes drawing in people who loved bikes, I couldn't say, but the job attracted a lot of yankee types and plenty of easygoing, frank personalities.

Back in my school days I had been bullied plenty by people like that, but the manager held the place together like a gang boss, so there was no bullying at all and it was a warm, friendly, close-knit place to work.

Having nothing to do besides work, I put in shifts almost every day from opening until dusk.

In my school days I had hardly ever had a conversation with a girl, but at the job, conversation came perfectly naturally.

I got especially close with two I saw every day on the weekday daytime shift — a young married ex-yankee woman who loved movies, and a teenage girl who played drums in a band — and we talked and talked.

They were the first female friends I ever had, and the first friends I had made in the three years since middle school.

![Four people standing arm in arm, seen from behind, facing an open field at sunset](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog25-1.jpg)

What I realized then was that, having been raised in a gentle environment, I found it easier to spend time with female friends than with male ones.

Friends only in the sense that we talked at work — we never hung out after a shift — but even so, meeting people and mixing with them opened a heart that had been shut tight and gave it life.

I had chosen delivery work because I didn't want to talk to people, and what I enjoyed more than anything was mixing with people when I wasn't delivering.

# An Almost-Normal Life

At that job, remarkably, everyone got along with everyone.

The only person anybody disliked was the company president, who showed up now and then, and the president-versus-manager-and-staff arrangement seemed to strengthen our solidarity even further.

The president, it seemed, knew this and played the villain on purpose.

I didn't notice it at the time, but looking back, that closeness was of a rare order, and I have never once seen that degree of unity out in ordinary society since.

The upside of making a yankee the manager showed itself, I think, and the breezy, American image pizza has as a food probably had something to do with it too.

Given the nature of a pizza place, weekends were busy, so I started working nights as well as daytimes.

The Saturday shift ended past eleven at night, and afterward everyone would go to a late-night ramen shop, which I looked forward to.

Eating ramen after work and then heading home is an ordinary, commonplace story, I think, but for me it was the normal life I had dreamed of.

That stretch at the pizza place was, I think, the period when I learned the sociability inside me.

Until then it had taken everything I had just to keep my heart shut so as not to get hurt, but now I could mix with people and enjoy it, in an environment where expressing myself did not leave me hurt.

# A Change in the Environment

The change in that warm, friendly workplace came around the time the yankee manager quit.

Valued for the hard face and nerve peculiar to yankees, he changed jobs and became a debt collector.

I imagine an older yankee, one of his seniors, put in a word for him.

The one who became manager in his place was an eighteen-year-old freeter — a young guy living on part-time work — who, like me, worked weekdays.

He was smart and efficient, so he did the work well and the president liked him.

But he had none of the yankee manager's charisma, and he could not hold the rest of the staff together.

Wrapping himself in his competence and in the authority of the manager's title, he grew more and more high-handed toward the others, and the human landscape of the pizza shop shifted.

![A man in a backwards black cap with a hand on his forehead, frowning, plain background](/images/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Blog25-2.jpg)

A gang-boss character playing the gang boss was no problem, but when an upstart underling played the gang boss, nobody followed him in their hearts, and what had been president versus manager and staff turned into president and manager versus staff.

Since one of the two people who could work weekday daytimes on a regular schedule had become manager, I was the one left, and so found myself promoted into something like an acting-manager position for the weekday daytime.

Coming in before opening to raise the shutter, prepare the pizza dough, get the toppings ready — I came to be able to do the whole range of pizza shop work.

Being entrusted with the work at a part-time job is an ordinary story, but for me, carrying a complex about never having had a proper experience of life, being put in charge of a shop at seventeen was the kind of confidence-building experience I wanted to brag about.
