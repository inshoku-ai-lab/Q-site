## Autumn

Down in the world below, people were suffering through the lingering summer heat and the cicadas were wailing away, but up in the mountains summer was already over and autumn was on its way.

The temperature drops about six degrees Celsius (eleven degrees Fahrenheit) for every thousand meters (3,300 feet) of elevation.

So at our hut, at 2,800 meters (9,200 feet), it was roughly seventeen degrees (thirty degrees Fahrenheit) cooler than at ground level.

If the world below was sweltering at 32°C (90°F) in the late-summer heat, up at the summit it was 15°C (59°F) and autumn.

With summer over, the two summer part-timers, the handsome college boy and the beautiful college girl, went back to their hometowns.

Once again, it was just the original three of us.

Right after opening the hut, we'd had our hands full getting ready for the busy season; now that summer was over, the preparations for closing the hut began.

## After the Festival

The busy season is a kind of festival, so what came now was, you might say, the cleanup afterward.

With each weekend that went by, we cut back on the number of futons for the climbers.

We laid the futons out on the roof to air, then carried them up into the attic.

The bedsheets would go down on the next helicopter run.

Less and less food came up by helicopter, and more and more garbage went down to the world below in its place.

## Where Things Stood with My Girlfriend

As the small hut where I worked moved toward closing, the time was coming for me to work at the big hut again.

That meant I'd soon be seeing my girlfriend, I-chan, again.

I'd applied for the mountain-hut job because I wanted to work with her, but in practice we'd spent almost no time together.

This was before cell phones had caught on, so once we were working at different huts, there was no real way to stay in touch.

The huts could get on the internet through a satellite phone, but that was a special privilege, allowed only in exceptional circumstances.

At 100 yen a minute, about eighty cents, it wasn't something you could use to keep in touch day to day.

Oh well, we'd traveled together, so I figured a little time apart would keep things fresh for both of us.
